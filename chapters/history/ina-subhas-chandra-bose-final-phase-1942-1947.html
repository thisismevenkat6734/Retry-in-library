1. Historical Background | చారిత్రక నేపథ్యం

World War II (1939–1945) created a major opportunity and a difficult strategic setting for Indian nationalism. Britain involved India in the war without consulting elected Indian representatives. The failure of the Cripps Mission (1942), the Quit India Movement and the rapid Japanese advance in Southeast Asia intensified the crisis of British rule.

ద్వితీయ ప్రపంచ యుద్ధం సమయంలో జపాన్ మలయా, సింగపూర్, బర్మా ప్రాంతాల్లో వేగంగా ముందుకు వచ్చింది. అక్కడ బ్రిటిష్ సైన్యంలో పనిచేసిన అనేక మంది భారతీయ సైనికులు జపాన్ చేతికి యుద్ధ ఖైదీలుగా చిక్కారు. ఈ పరిస్థితి నుంచి భారత జాతీయ సైన్యం (INA / Azad Hind Fauj) ఏర్పడే అవకాశం వచ్చింది.

Important distinction: INA was not created by Subhas Chandra Bose from nothing. Its first phase began in 1942 under Captain Mohan Singh, with political support from Rash Behari Bose and the Indian Independence League. Subhas Bose later reorganised and greatly expanded it in 1943.

2. First Phase of INA (1941–1942) | INA తొలి దశ

  • Major Fujiwara Iwaichi of the Japanese army contacted Indian officers and nationalists in Southeast Asia.
  • Captain Mohan Singh, an officer of the British Indian Army who became a Japanese prisoner of war, helped organise the first INA.
  • Rash Behari Bose, the veteran Indian revolutionary living in Japan, provided political leadership through the Indian Independence League (IIL).
  • The Bangkok Conference (June 1942) discussed the organisation of an Indian liberation army and political movement.
  • Disagreements arose between Mohan Singh and the Japanese authorities over the INA's autonomy and role. The first INA was dissolved/disbanded in December 1942; Mohan Singh was detained by the Japanese.

Exam point: Mohan Singh = first INA phase; Rash Behari Bose = political leadership/IIL; Subhas Chandra Bose = reorganisation and leadership from 1943.

3. Subhas Chandra Bose and the Reorganisation of INA (1943) | సుభాష్ చంద్రబోస్

Subhas Chandra Bose escaped British surveillance in Calcutta in January 1941, travelled through Afghanistan and the Soviet route to Germany, and later reached East Asia by submarine transfer. He arrived in Southeast Asia in 1943. On 4 July 1943, Rash Behari Bose handed over leadership of the Indian Independence League to him at Singapore.

  • On 5 July 1943, Bose reviewed the INA and gave the call “Delhi Chalo”.
  • He reorganised the INA, encouraged recruitment among Indian prisoners of war and civilians in Southeast Asia, and raised funds from the Indian diaspora.
  • The INA included the Rani of Jhansi Regiment, a women’s regiment led by Captain Lakshmi (later Lakshmi Sahgal).
  • Bose’s slogans included “Give me blood, and I will give you freedom”, “Delhi Chalo” and “Jai Hind”. “Jai Hind” became a widely used national greeting.

తెలుగు: సుభాష్ చంద్రబోస్ INAకి కొత్త రాజకీయ దిశ, నాయకత్వం, ప్రజా మద్దతు, సైనిక పునర్వ్యవస్థీకరణ అందించారు. మహిళల భాగస్వామ్యానికి రాణి ఝాన్సీ రెజిమెంట్ ఒక ముఖ్యమైన ఉదాహరణ.

4. Provisional Government of Azad Hind (21 October 1943) | ఆజాద్ హింద్ ప్రభుత్వం

On 21 October 1943, Bose proclaimed the Provisional Government of Free India (Arzi Hukumat-e-Azad Hind) at the Cathay Cinema Hall in Singapore. Bose became its Head of State, Prime Minister and Minister of War. The government declared war against Britain and the United States and sought international recognition.

  • It was recognised by Japan and several Axis-aligned states.
  • It aimed to represent independent India diplomatically and to lead the struggle for liberation.
  • The Azad Hind Bank was established in Rangoon in 1944 to manage funds and donations.
  • The government adopted the tricolour with the springing tiger emblem; the INA used the motto “Ittehad, Itmad, Qurbani” (Unity, Faith, Sacrifice) in commonly cited accounts.

Important: The Provisional Government had limited practical control and depended heavily on Japanese military power. It should not be described as a fully sovereign government exercising effective administration over mainland India.

5. Andaman and Nicobar Islands | అండమాన్–నికోబార్

Japan occupied the Andaman and Nicobar Islands during World War II. On 6 November 1943, Japan announced that the islands would be transferred to the Provisional Government of Azad Hind. Bose visited Port Blair on 29–30 December 1943 and unfurled the tricolour on 30 December.

  • Andaman was renamed Shaheed and Nicobar Swaraj by the Azad Hind Government.
  • Japanese forces retained the decisive military and administrative control during the occupation; the transfer was therefore limited in actual authority.
  • The islands were liberated from Japanese occupation by Allied forces in October 1945.

తెలుగు గుర్తుంచుకోండి: Shaheed = Andaman; Swaraj = Nicobar. Bose’s flag-hoisting was symbolically important, but Japanese control remained substantial.

6. INA Military Campaign: Imphal and Kohima (1944–1945) | సైనిక యాత్ర

The INA fought alongside Japanese forces in the Burma–India theatre. The 1944 offensive aimed to enter British India through the northeast, especially the Imphal and Kohima sectors.

  • INA units participated in operations in the Arakan and Imphal sectors.
  • Japanese and INA forces crossed into Indian territory in the Manipur region. The tricolour was raised at Moirang in April 1944 by INA personnel, an important symbolic event.
  • The Japanese offensive at Imphal and Kohima failed because of Allied resistance, supply difficulties, disease, monsoon conditions and the weakening of Japanese logistics.
  • Retreat through Burma caused severe losses. In 1945, the Allied advance overwhelmed INA units fighting alongside the Japanese.

Assessment: The INA did not militarily defeat the British or liberate India. Its military campaign failed, but its political and psychological impact later became significant.

7. Rani of Jhansi Regiment | రాణి ఝాన్సీ రెజిమెంట్

The Rani of Jhansi Regiment was an all-women unit of the INA, established under Bose’s leadership in 1943. It was commanded by Captain Lakshmi Swaminathan (Lakshmi Sahgal).

  • It represented women’s participation in an organised military formation in the freedom struggle.
  • Members received military training; many also performed medical, nursing and support duties.
  • The regiment did not see major frontline combat as a formed unit.

ఈ రెజిమెంట్ మహిళలు కేవలం సహాయక పాత్రల్లోనే కాకుండా సైనిక శిక్షణ, క్రమశిక్షణ, జాతీయ ఉద్యమంలో నాయకత్వం వహించగలరని చూపించింది.

8. INA Trials at the Red Fort (1945–1946) | ఎర్రకోట విచారణలు

After the war, the British government decided to court-martial selected INA officers for waging war against the King-Emperor. The first major public trial began at the Red Fort in Delhi in November 1945.

  • The three prominent accused were Shah Nawaz Khan, Prem Kumar Sahgal and Gurbaksh Singh Dhillon.
  • Their different religious backgrounds became a powerful symbol of unity in the nationalist campaign.
  • The defence team included Bhulabhai Desai, Tej Bahadur Sapru and other lawyers; Jawaharlal Nehru also appeared in legal robes in support of the defence.
  • Public demonstrations, student protests, fundraising and press coverage spread across India.
  • The sentences were ultimately remitted by the Commander-in-Chief, but the trials had already generated widespread nationalist sympathy.

Significance: The trials helped turn the INA personnel into popular symbols of sacrifice and intensified doubts about the reliability of Indian personnel in the colonial armed forces.

9. Royal Indian Navy Mutiny (18–23 February 1946) | నౌకాదళ తిరుగుబాటు

The Royal Indian Navy (RIN) ratings’ revolt began on 18 February 1946 at HMIS Talwar, a shore establishment in Bombay. Immediate grievances included poor food, racial discrimination, abusive treatment and service conditions. Political slogans, including support for the INA and freedom, gave the revolt a wider nationalist character.

  • The revolt spread from Bombay to ships and shore establishments in other centres, including Karachi and Calcutta; actions and sympathy were reported in other cities too.
  • Workers and ordinary citizens expressed solidarity in several places, and Bombay saw strikes and street clashes.
  • Political leaders differed in their response. Sardar Patel and other Congress leaders urged the ratings to surrender; the Communist Party supported the strike more openly. The Muslim League also engaged with the situation.
  • The ratings eventually surrendered after appeals and negotiations; the revolt was not a successful seizure of power.

Exam caution: Do not say the RIN revolt alone caused independence. It was one important factor that increased British concern about the loyalty and discipline of the armed forces.

10. Why the INA and Mutiny mattered | ప్రభావం

  • National unity: The INA and its trials generated cross-community public support, although the wider political climate remained divided.
  • Armed forces question: British authorities became more concerned about nationalist sentiment among Indian soldiers, sailors and airmen.
  • Mass mobilisation: The trials created demonstrations and solidarity actions beyond established party structures.
  • Psychological impact: The myth of unquestioned loyalty within colonial armed forces was weakened.
  • Limit: The INA’s military campaign failed and depended on Japanese strategy; it was not the sole or direct cause of independence.

స్వాతంత్ర్యం అనేది దీర్ఘకాలిక ప్రజా ఉద్యమాలు, రాజకీయ చర్చలు, రెండో ప్రపంచ యుద్ధానంతర బ్రిటన్ పరిస్థితి, సైనిక అసంతృప్తి, అంతర్జాతీయ పరిణామాలు వంటి అనేక కారణాల సమ్మిళిత ఫలితం.

11. Transfer of Power: 1946–1947 | అధికార బదిలీ

The post-war political crisis led to renewed negotiations between Britain and Indian political parties.

  • 19 February 1946: British Prime Minister Clement Attlee announced that a Cabinet Mission would be sent to India.
  • 24 March 1946: The Cabinet Mission arrived in India. Its members were Lord Pethick-Lawrence, Sir Stafford Cripps and A. V. Alexander.
  • 16 May 1946: Cabinet Mission Plan proposed a Constituent Assembly and a Union with limited subjects, alongside provincial groupings. Congress and the Muslim League disagreed over interpretation and implementation.
  • 2 September 1946: Interim Government formed under Jawaharlal Nehru’s leadership.
  • 9 December 1946: Constituent Assembly met for its first sitting; Dr. Sachchidananda Sinha was temporary chairman. Dr. Rajendra Prasad was elected permanent President on 11 December.
  • 20 February 1947: Attlee announced the British decision to transfer power no later than June 1948.
  • 3 June 1947: Mountbatten Plan announced partition and transfer of power.
  • 18 July 1947: Indian Independence Act received Royal Assent.
  • 15 August 1947: India became independent; Pakistan came into existence on 14 August 1947.

Key distinction: Cabinet Mission Plan (1946) attempted to preserve a united India through a weak Union and groupings. Mountbatten Plan (3 June 1947) accepted partition. Indian Independence Act (18 July 1947) gave legal effect to the creation of the two Dominions.

14. Mains Answer Frameworks | మెయిన్స్ సమాధాన రూపకల్పన

Q1. “The INA failed militarily but succeeded in creating a powerful political impact.” Discuss.

Introduction: Briefly explain the two phases of INA and Bose’s leadership from 1943.

Body: Military limitations—dependence on Japan, logistics, defeat at Imphal–Kohima, retreat. Political impact—Azad Hind Government, Rani of Jhansi Regiment, Red Fort trials, cross-community sympathy, questions about armed-forces loyalty.

Conclusion: INA was not the sole cause of independence, but it significantly contributed to the final crisis of colonial authority.

Q2. Explain the significance of the INA trials and the Royal Indian Navy revolt.

Discuss popular mobilisation, nationalist unity, service grievances, political slogans, spread of the RIN revolt and British concerns about armed-force loyalty. Add a balanced conclusion that neither event alone explains independence.

Q3. Trace the constitutional developments from the Cabinet Mission to Independence.

Use a timeline: Cabinet Mission (March–May 1946), Interim Government (September 1946), Constituent Assembly (December 1946), Attlee statement (February 1947), Mountbatten Plan (June 1947), Independence Act (July 1947), Independence (August 1947).
