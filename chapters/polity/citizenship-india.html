1. Introduction – పరిచయం

Citizenship is the legal status of a person as a member of a sovereign country. It establishes the legal relationship between an individual and the State.

పౌరసత్వం అంటే ఒక వ్యక్తికి ఒక దేశంలో చట్టబద్ధమైన సభ్యత్వ హోదా. ఇది వ్యక్తికి మరియు దేశానికి మధ్య ఉన్న చట్టపరమైన సంబంధాన్ని సూచిస్తుంది.

Important features

  • India follows the concept of single citizenship.
  • India does not provide separate citizenship of a State.
  • Citizens enjoy certain political and constitutional rights unavailable to foreigners.
  • Citizenship is primarily governed by Part II of the Constitution and the Citizenship Act, 1955.

భారతదేశంలో ఒకే పౌరసత్వ విధానం ఉంది. అమెరికా వంటి దేశాల్లో కనిపించే విధంగా రాష్ట్రానికి ప్రత్యేక పౌరసత్వం భారత్‌లో లేదు.

Article 5 – Citizenship at commencement

A person was considered a citizen at the commencement of the Constitution if they had domicile in India and fulfilled one of the specified conditions: birth in India, either parent born in India, or ordinary residence in India for at least five years immediately before commencement.

రాజ్యాంగం అమల్లోకి వచ్చినప్పుడు భారత్‌లో domicile ఉండి, జననం, తల్లిదండ్రుల జననం లేదా గత ఐదు సంవత్సరాల సాధారణ నివాసం వంటి షరతుల్లో ఒకదాన్ని నెరవేర్చినవారికి Article 5 వర్తించింది.

Article 6 – Migration from Pakistan

It dealt with persons who migrated from Pakistan to India around Partition. Different conditions applied depending on whether migration occurred before or after 19 July 1948. For later migrants, registration and a prescribed residence period were relevant.

విభజన సమయంలో పాకిస్తాన్ నుంచి భారత్‌కు వచ్చిన వ్యక్తుల పౌరసత్వానికి సంబంధించిన నిబంధన.

Article 7 – Migration to Pakistan

Generally, persons who migrated from India to Pakistan after 1 March 1947 were not treated as Indian citizens under this Article. An exception existed for those who returned to India under a permit for resettlement or permanent return.

Article 8 – Indian origin persons abroad

Persons of Indian origin ordinarily residing outside India could be registered as Indian citizens by an Indian diplomatic or consular representative, subject to the constitutional conditions.

Article 9 – Foreign citizenship

A person who voluntarily acquired citizenship of a foreign State would not be a citizen of India under Articles 5, 6 or 8.

Article 10 – Continuance

Citizenship under the preceding provisions continues subject to laws made by Parliament.

Article 11 – Parliament's power

Parliament has the power to make laws regarding acquisition and termination of citizenship and all other matters relating to citizenship.

Exam Point: Article 11 is the constitutional basis for the Citizenship Act, 1955.

3. Citizenship Act, 1955 – పౌరసత్వ చట్టం

The Citizenship Act, 1955 regulates acquisition and determination of Indian citizenship after the Constitution came into force.

4. Acquisition of Citizenship – పౌరసత్వం పొందే మార్గాలు

The Citizenship Act, 1955 provides five principal modes:

1. By Birth – జననం ద్వారా

  • 26 January 1950 to 1 July 1987: A person born in India generally acquired citizenship by birth, subject to statutory exceptions.
  • 1 July 1987 to 3 December 2004: At least one parent had to be an Indian citizen at the time of birth.
  • On or after 3 December 2004: Both parents must be Indian citizens, or one parent must be an Indian citizen and the other must not be an illegal migrant, subject to statutory exceptions.

2. By Descent – వారసత్వం ద్వారా

Citizenship may be acquired by a person born outside India to an Indian parent, subject to the date-specific conditions and registration requirements under the Act.

3. By Registration – నమోదు ద్వారా

Specified categories of persons of Indian origin, spouses of Indian citizens and certain other eligible persons may apply for registration, subject to statutory eligibility and residence conditions.

4. By Naturalisation – సహజీకరణ ద్వారా

A foreigner may apply after satisfying the conditions in the Third Schedule, including the prescribed residence or service requirements, good character, knowledge of a language in the Eighth Schedule and intention to reside in India or serve an Indian-connected entity.

5. By Incorporation of Territory – భూభాగం విలీనం ద్వారా

When a territory becomes part of India, the Central Government may specify the persons who become Indian citizens by virtue of their connection with that territory.

Remember: Birth • Descent • Registration • Naturalisation • Incorporation of Territory

5. Loss of Citizenship – పౌరసత్వం కోల్పోవడం

1. Renunciation – త్యజించడం

A citizen of full age and capacity may make a declaration renouncing Indian citizenship. The declaration becomes effective according to the Act.

2. Termination – రద్దు

Indian citizenship terminates when an Indian citizen voluntarily acquires the citizenship of another country, subject to the statutory framework.

3. Deprivation – తొలగించడం

The Central Government may deprive a person of citizenship in specified circumstances, such as obtaining registration or naturalisation by fraud, disloyalty, certain unlawful dealings with an enemy during war, or other grounds specified in the Act.

గుర్తుంచుకోండి: Renunciation అంటే స్వచ్ఛందంగా త్యజించడం; Termination అంటే విదేశీ పౌరసత్వం స్వచ్ఛందంగా పొందినప్పుడు చట్టప్రకారం ముగియడం; Deprivation అంటే నిర్దిష్ట కారణాలపై ప్రభుత్వం తొలగించడం.

6. NRI, PIO and OCI – తేడాలు

TermMeaningImportant point
NRINon-Resident IndianAn Indian citizen residing outside India; remains an Indian citizen.
PIOPerson of Indian OriginFormer PIO card scheme was merged with OCI in 2015.
OCIOverseas Citizen of IndiaA statutory immigration/status scheme; it is not dual citizenship.

OCI privileges and limitations

  • OCI cardholders receive specified lifelong multiple-entry visa and other statutory benefits.
  • OCI is not full Indian citizenship.
  • OCI cardholders cannot vote in Indian elections.
  • They cannot ordinarily hold constitutional offices reserved for Indian citizens.
  • They do not have the same rights as Indian citizens to acquire agricultural land, plantation property or farmhouses, subject to applicable rules.

Important: India does not recognise full dual citizenship. OCI must not be confused with dual citizenship.

7. Citizenship Amendment Act, 2019 (CAA)

The CAA, 2019 amended the Citizenship Act, 1955. It provides a special eligibility route for specified communities: Hindus, Sikhs, Buddhists, Jains, Parsis and Christians from Afghanistan, Bangladesh and Pakistan who entered India on or before 31 December 2014 and meet the statutory conditions.

  • The Act provides a special exemption from the definition of illegal migrant for the specified groups, subject to its provisions.
  • For eligible applicants under this provision, the naturalisation residence requirement was reduced from 11 years to 5 years of aggregate residence or service in India.
  • The Act contains territorial exceptions, including specified Sixth Schedule tribal areas and Inner Line Permit areas.
  • The Citizenship Amendment Rules, 2024 operationalised the application process.

CAA 2019 అనేది పౌరసత్వ చట్టంలో చేసిన సవరణ. ఇది నిర్దిష్ట దేశాలు, మత సమూహాలు, ప్రవేశ తేదీ మరియు ఇతర చట్టపరమైన అర్హతలతో కూడిన ప్రత్యేక మార్గాన్ని కల్పిస్తుంది.

Exam caution: CAA does not automatically grant citizenship to every person belonging to the listed communities. Statutory eligibility and application requirements apply.

8. Important Exam Facts

  • Part II of the Constitution: Articles 5–11.
  • Citizenship Act: 1955.
  • Article 11: Parliament's power to regulate citizenship.
  • India follows single citizenship.
  • Five modes of acquisition are provided by the Citizenship Act.
  • Three modes of loss: Renunciation, Termination and Deprivation.
  • India does not provide full dual citizenship.
  • CAA was enacted in 2019; rules were notified in 2024.
  • OCI is not Indian citizenship.
