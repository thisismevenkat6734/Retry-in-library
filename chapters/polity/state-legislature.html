1. Introduction – పరిచయం

English: The State Legislature is the law-making body at the state level in India. It makes laws on subjects within the State List and Concurrent List, subject to constitutional limitations.

తెలుగు: భారతదేశంలో రాష్ట్ర స్థాయిలో చట్టాలను రూపొందించే వ్యవస్థను State Legislature అంటారు. రాజ్యాంగ పరిమితులకు లోబడి State List మరియు Concurrent List అంశాలపై రాష్ట్ర శాసనసభ చట్టాలు చేస్తుంది.

Constitutional Provision

Part VI of the Constitution deals with the States. State Legislature is mainly covered under Articles 168–212.

రాజ్యాంగంలోని Part VI రాష్ట్రాల గురించి వివరిస్తుంది. Articles 168–212 రాష్ట్ర శాసన వ్యవస్థకు సంబంధించినవి.

Types of State Legislatures

  • Unicameral: One House – Legislative Assembly (Vidhan Sabha).
  • Bicameral: Two Houses – Legislative Assembly (Vidhan Sabha) and Legislative Council (Vidhan Parishad).

ఒకే సభ ఉన్న వ్యవస్థను Unicameral అంటారు. రెండు సభలు ఉన్న వ్యవస్థను Bicameral అంటారు.

Important: Every state has a Legislative Assembly. A Legislative Council exists only in certain states.

Article 168 provides for the constitution of State Legislatures. The Legislature consists of the Governor and one or two Houses, depending on the state.

2. Legislative Assembly – శాసనసభ

The Legislative Assembly is the directly elected lower House of the State Legislature. ప్రజలచే నేరుగా ఎన్నుకోబడే సభ Legislative Assembly.

Composition – సభ్యుల సంఖ్య

  • Normally, the Assembly has not more than 500 and not less than 60 members.
  • Constitutional exceptions exist for certain states.
  • Members are directly elected from territorial constituencies.
  • Seats are reserved for SCs and STs as provided by the Constitution.

సాధారణంగా శాసనసభలో కనీసం 60 మంది, గరిష్ఠంగా 500 మంది సభ్యులు ఉంటారు. కొన్ని రాష్ట్రాలకు రాజ్యాంగం ప్రత్యేక మినహాయింపులు కల్పిస్తుంది.

Tenure – పదవీకాలం

  • Normal term is five years from the date appointed for its first meeting.
  • It may be dissolved earlier.
  • During a National Emergency, Parliament may extend its term by one year at a time.
  • Such extension cannot continue beyond six months after the Emergency ceases.

సాధారణంగా శాసనసభ పదవీకాలం ఐదు సంవత్సరాలు. ముందుగానే రద్దు చేయవచ్చు. జాతీయ అత్యవసర పరిస్థితిలో పార్లమెంట్ ప్రత్యేక నిబంధనల ప్రకారం కాలాన్ని పొడిగించవచ్చు.

Qualifications – అర్హతలు

Article 173 provides qualifications:

  • Must be a citizen of India.
  • Must take the prescribed oath or affirmation.
  • Minimum age for Assembly: 25 years.
  • Must satisfy other qualifications prescribed by Parliament.

Disqualifications – అనర్హతలు

Article 191 deals with disqualifications:

  • Holding an office of profit, unless exempted by law.
  • Being of unsound mind as declared by a competent court.
  • Being an undischarged insolvent.
  • Not being a citizen of India or voluntarily acquiring foreign citizenship.
  • Disqualification under a law made by Parliament.
  • Disqualification under the Tenth Schedule.

Quorum – కోరం

Article 189: The quorum is ten members or one-tenth of the total membership, whichever is greater, unless otherwise provided by law.

సభ సమావేశానికి సాధారణంగా పది మంది లేదా మొత్తం సభ్యులలో పదో వంతు – వీటిలో ఎక్కువ సంఖ్య అవసరం.

3. Legislative Council – శాసన మండలి

The Legislative Council is the upper House in states having a bicameral legislature. It is a permanent House and cannot be dissolved.

రెండు సభల వ్యవస్థ ఉన్న రాష్ట్రాల్లో శాసన మండలి ఎగువ సభ. ఇది శాశ్వత సభ; రద్దు చేయబడదు.

Composition – Article 171

  • Total membership cannot exceed one-third of the Assembly's total membership.
  • Ordinarily, it cannot have fewer than 40 members.
  • About one-third are elected by local authorities.
  • About one-twelfth are elected by graduates.
  • About one-twelfth are elected by teachers.
  • About one-third are elected by members of the Legislative Assembly.
  • The remaining members are nominated by the Governor.

Governor's Nominated Members

The Governor nominates members having special knowledge or practical experience in:

  • Literature
  • Science
  • Art
  • Co-operative movement
  • Social service

Tenure

  • Permanent House.
  • Approximately one-third of members retire every two years.
  • Individual member's normal term is six years.
  • Minimum age is 30 years.

Creation and Abolition – Article 169

Parliament may create or abolish a Legislative Council if the State Assembly passes a resolution by:

  • A majority of the total membership of the Assembly; and
  • Not less than two-thirds of members present and voting.

After this resolution, Parliament may pass a law to create or abolish the Council.

Exam Point: Article 169 is not an amendment under Article 368.

4. Presiding Officers – సభాధ్యక్షులు

Speaker – స్పీకర్

Under Article 178, the Legislative Assembly elects a Speaker and Deputy Speaker from among its members.

Functions of Speaker

  • Presides over meetings.
  • Maintains order and discipline.
  • Decides whether a Bill is a Money Bill, subject to constitutional provisions.
  • Allows members to speak according to House rules.
  • Exercises a casting vote in case of a tie.
  • Decides disqualification questions under the Tenth Schedule, subject to judicial review.

Removal of Speaker

The Speaker may be removed by a resolution passed by a majority of all the then members of the Assembly. At least 14 days' notice is required.

Deputy Speaker

The Deputy Speaker performs the Speaker's duties when the office is vacant or the Speaker is absent.

Chairman of Legislative Council

Under Article 182, the Council elects a Chairman and Deputy Chairman from among its members.

Governor's Role

  • Summons the House or Houses.
  • Prorogues the House or either House.
  • May dissolve the Legislative Assembly.
  • Addresses the first session after a general election and the first session of each year.
  • Acts on Bills under Article 200.

Articles 174, 175, 176 and 200 are important for these functions.

5. Legislative Procedure – చట్టాల రూపకల్పన

Ordinary Bill – సాధారణ బిల్లు

An ordinary Bill can generally be introduced in either House of a bicameral State Legislature.

  1. Introduction / First Reading.
  2. Consideration and discussion.
  3. Committee stage, where applicable.
  4. Voting and passage.
  5. Transmission to the other House, where applicable.
  6. Governor's consideration.

Council's Power over Ordinary Bills

The Legislative Council can delay, but cannot permanently block, an ordinary Bill passed by the Assembly.

SituationMaximum delay
First time sent to Council3 months
After Assembly passes again1 month

There is no joint sitting of State Legislature Houses to resolve a disagreement over an ordinary Bill.

Money Bill – ద్రవ్య బిల్లు

Article 199 defines a State Money Bill. It deals only with specified financial matters such as:

  • Imposition, abolition, remission or regulation of taxes.
  • Regulation of State Government borrowing.
  • Custody of Consolidated Fund or Contingency Fund.
  • Appropriation of money from the Consolidated Fund.
  • Charged expenditure.
  • Receipt, custody or issue of public money and audit of accounts.

Money Bill Procedure

  • Introduced only in the Legislative Assembly.
  • Governor's recommendation is required as constitutionally provided.
  • The Council can only make recommendations.
  • The Council must return it within 14 days.
  • The Assembly may accept or reject the recommendations.
  • If not returned within 14 days, it is deemed passed in the form approved by the Assembly.
Remember: Assembly has the final authority over a State Money Bill.

Governor's Assent – Article 200

When a Bill is presented, the Governor may:

  • Give assent.
  • Withhold assent.
  • Return a non-Money Bill for reconsideration.
  • Reserve the Bill for the President.

If a returned Bill is passed again by the Legislature, with or without amendments, the Governor shall not withhold assent under Article 200. Bills reserved for the President are governed by Article 201.

President's Consideration – Article 201

The President may assent or withhold assent to a reserved Bill. In the case of a non-Money Bill, the President may direct the Governor to return it for reconsideration.

6. Budget and Financial Procedure – బడ్జెట్

Article 202 deals with the Annual Financial Statement of a State.

రాష్ట్ర ప్రభుత్వ వార్షిక ఆదాయం, ఖర్చుల అంచనాలను Annual Financial Statement లేదా Budget అంటారు.

  • Annual Financial Statement: State Budget.
  • Charged Expenditure: Not submitted to vote, but may be discussed.
  • Demands for Grants: Expenditure demands submitted to the Assembly.
  • Appropriation Bill: Authorises withdrawal from the Consolidated Fund.
  • Vote on Account: Temporary expenditure authorisation.
  • Supplementary Grant: Additional grant for insufficient or new expenditure.

Consolidated Fund – Article 266

State revenues, loans raised and loan repayments received form part of the State Consolidated Fund, subject to constitutional provisions.

Contingency Fund – Article 267

The State Contingency Fund is placed at the Governor's disposal to meet unforeseen expenditure pending legislative authorisation.

7. Legislative Privileges – శాసనసభ హక్కులు

Article 194 deals with powers, privileges and immunities of State Legislatures and their members.

  • Freedom of speech in the Legislature, subject to constitutional provisions and House rules.
  • Immunity from court proceedings in respect of anything said or any vote given in the House, subject to constitutional limits.
  • Privileges of the House and its members as provided by the Constitution and law.

8. Legislative Committees – శాసన కమిటీలు

  • Public Accounts Committee: Examines government accounts and CAG reports.
  • Estimates Committee: Examines estimates and suggests economy in public expenditure.
  • Committee on Public Undertakings: Examines public undertakings.
  • Privileges Committee: Examines breach of privilege matters.
  • Petitions Committee: Examines petitions presented to the House.

Committee structure and membership may differ according to the rules of each State Legislature.
