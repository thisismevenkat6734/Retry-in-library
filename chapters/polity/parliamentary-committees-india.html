1. Introduction – పరిచయం

Parliamentary committees are groups of Members of Parliament (MPs) appointed or elected to examine specific matters in detail and assist Parliament in its work.

పార్లమెంటరీ కమిటీలు అనేవి నిర్దిష్ట అంశాలను లోతుగా పరిశీలించి పార్లమెంట్ తన విధులను సమర్థంగా నిర్వహించడానికి సహాయపడే ఎంపీల బృందాలు.

Why are committees important? Parliament has limited time to examine every issue in detail. Committees provide specialised, detailed and continuous scrutiny of legislation, expenditure and administration.

Functions – ప్రధాన విధులు

  • Examine government expenditure – ప్రభుత్వ ఖర్చులను పరిశీలించడం.
  • Scrutinise bills and policies – బిల్లులు, విధానాలను పరిశీలించడం.
  • Ensure executive accountability – కార్యనిర్వాహక వ్యవస్థను జవాబుదారీగా ఉంచడం.
  • Examine ministries and departments – మంత్రిత్వ శాఖల పనితీరును పరిశీలించడం.
  • Provide expert and detailed discussion – లోతైన చర్చకు అవకాశం కల్పించడం.

2. Classification of Parliamentary Committees

Standing Committeesశాశ్వత స్వభావం కలిగిన కమిటీలు. ఇవి నిరంతరంగా పనిచేస్తాయి; అవసరానికి అనుగుణంగా పునర్నిర్మించబడవచ్చు.
Ad hoc Committeesఒక నిర్దిష్ట పని లేదా అంశం కోసం ఏర్పాటు చేస్తారు. పని పూర్తయిన తర్వాత సాధారణంగా రద్దవుతాయి.

Examples

  • Standing: Public Accounts Committee, Estimates Committee, Committee on Public Undertakings.
  • Ad hoc: Select Committee on a Bill, Joint Committee on a Bill, committees appointed for a specific inquiry.
Standing Committee అంటే సభ్యులు జీవితాంతం అదే కమిటీలో ఉంటారని కాదు. కమిటీ నిరంతర సంస్థాగత వ్యవస్థగా కొనసాగుతుంది; సభ్యత్వం కాలానుగుణంగా మారుతుంది.

3. Public Accounts Committee (PAC) – ప్రజా ఖాతాల కమిటీ

The Public Accounts Committee examines government expenditure and reports of the Comptroller and Auditor General (CAG) to determine whether public money has been spent legally and for authorised purposes.

ప్రభుత్వ ఖర్చులు చట్టబద్ధంగా, ఆమోదించిన ప్రయోజనాల కోసమే జరిగాయా అని PAC పరిశీలిస్తుంది. CAG నివేదికలను పరిశీలించడం దీని ముఖ్యమైన పని.

  • Established in 1921 under the Government of India Act framework; it became an important parliamentary financial committee.
  • It consists of 22 members: 15 from Lok Sabha and 7 from Rajya Sabha.
  • Members are elected annually by their respective Houses through proportional representation by means of the single transferable vote.
  • A Minister cannot be elected as a member.
  • The Chairperson is appointed by the Speaker of Lok Sabha.
  • By convention, the Chairperson is generally from the Opposition.
  • Its term is one year.

Important functions

  • Examines appropriation accounts and finance accounts of the Union Government.
  • Examines CAG reports relating to expenditure.
  • Checks whether expenditure was authorised by Parliament.
  • Examines cases of excess expenditure and financial irregularities.
Exam point: PAC is a post-expenditure scrutiny committee. It generally examines expenditure after it has taken place; it does not itself authorise government expenditure.

4. Estimates Committee – అంచనాల కమిటీ

The Estimates Committee examines budget estimates and suggests economies, improvements in organisation and efficiency in public expenditure.

బడ్జెట్ అంచనాలను పరిశీలించి ఖర్చుల్లో పొదుపు, పరిపాలనా సామర్థ్యం మరియు మెరుగైన నిర్వహణకు సూచనలు చేస్తుంది.

  • First constituted in 1950 on the recommendation of John Mathai, the then Finance Minister.
  • It has 30 members, all from Lok Sabha.
  • Members are elected annually by Lok Sabha through proportional representation by means of the single transferable vote.
  • Rajya Sabha has no representation in this committee.
  • A Minister cannot be a member.
  • Its term is one year.
  • The Chairperson is appointed by the Speaker of Lok Sabha.

Functions

  • Suggests economies in public expenditure.
  • Examines whether money is well laid out within the limits of policy.
  • Suggests improvements in organisation and administrative efficiency.
  • May recommend alternative policies to bring about efficiency and economy.
Remember: Estimates Committee is often called a continuous economy committee because it focuses on economy and efficiency in public expenditure.

5. Committee on Public Undertakings (COPU)

This committee examines the reports and accounts of public sector undertakings (PSUs) and the reports of the CAG relating to public undertakings.

ప్రభుత్వ రంగ సంస్థల ఖాతాలు, నివేదికలు మరియు వాటి పనితీరును పరిశీలిస్తుంది.

  • Created in 1964 on the recommendation of the Krishna Menon Committee.
  • It has 22 members: 15 from Lok Sabha and 7 from Rajya Sabha.
  • Members are elected annually.
  • A Minister cannot be a member.
  • Its term is one year.
  • The Chairperson is appointed by the Speaker of Lok Sabha.

Functions

  • Examines reports and accounts of selected public undertakings.
  • Examines CAG reports concerning public undertakings.
  • Checks whether public undertakings are managed according to sound business principles and prudent commercial practices.
  • Examines autonomy and efficiency of public sector enterprises.
The committee does not generally examine matters of major government policy or day-to-day administration of a public undertaking. Its detailed scope is governed by parliamentary rules.

Easy memory trick: PAC = Past expenditure; Estimates = Proposed estimates and economy; COPU = Public sector undertakings.

7. Department-related Standing Committees (DRSCs)

Department-related Standing Committees examine the demands for grants of ministries, bills referred to them, annual reports and long-term policy documents.

మంత్రిత్వ శాఖల గ్రాంట్ల డిమాండ్లు, బిల్లులు, వార్షిక నివేదికలు మరియు విధాన పత్రాలను పరిశీలిస్తాయి.

  • Introduced in 1993.
  • The system was expanded and restructured in 2004.
  • There are 24 Department-related Standing Committees.
  • Each committee has 31 members: 21 from Lok Sabha and 10 from Rajya Sabha.
  • Members are nominated by the Speaker of Lok Sabha and the Chairman of Rajya Sabha, respectively.
  • A Minister cannot be a member of a DRSC.
  • The term of a DRSC does not exceed one year.

Main functions

  • Examine Demands for Grants of related ministries.
  • Examine bills referred to the committee.
  • Study annual reports of ministries and departments.
  • Consider national basic long-term policy documents referred to them.
Important limitation: DRSCs do not normally examine day-to-day administration, matters of major government policy or matters specifically assigned to other parliamentary committees.

8. Select Committee and Joint Committee

Select Committee

A Select Committee is constituted by one House to examine a particular Bill. Its membership is drawn from that House.

ఒక బిల్లును పరిశీలించడానికి సంబంధిత సభ ఏర్పాటు చేసే కమిటీ. ఆ సభ సభ్యులే ఇందులో ఉంటారు.

Joint Committee

A Joint Committee on a Bill consists of members from both Houses of Parliament.

లోక్‌సభ, రాజ్యసభ రెండింటి సభ్యులతో ఏర్పడే కమిటీ.

  • Both types examine the Bill and submit a report.
  • They are ad hoc committees in nature.
  • The House may accept, reject or modify recommendations through its legislative process.
  • The committee report does not automatically become law.

9. Other Important Parliamentary Committees

Business Advisory Committee

Recommends allocation of time for government and other business of the House. It helps organise parliamentary work.

Committee on Government Assurances

Examines assurances, promises and undertakings given by Ministers on the floor of the House and reports on their implementation.

Committee on Subordinate Legislation

Examines whether rules, regulations, bye-laws and other delegated legislation are within the powers delegated by Parliament.

Committee on Petitions

Considers petitions presented to the House and matters raised through them, subject to parliamentary rules.

Committee of Privileges

Examines questions involving breach of parliamentary privileges and related matters referred to it.

Rules Committee

Considers matters relating to the rules of procedure and conduct of business of the House.

Ethics Committee

Examines ethical conduct and matters relating to the conduct of members under the applicable House rules.

Committee on Absence of Members

Considers requests and matters relating to absence of members from sittings of the House.

10. Parliamentary Privileges and Ethics

Parliamentary privileges are special rights and immunities enjoyed by Parliament, its committees and members to enable them to function independently.

పార్లమెంట్ మరియు సభ్యులు తమ విధులను స్వేచ్ఛగా నిర్వహించేందుకు కల్పించిన ప్రత్యేక హక్కులు, రక్షణలను Parliamentary Privileges అంటారు.

  • Article 105 deals with powers, privileges and immunities of Parliament and its members and committees.
  • Article 194 deals with powers, privileges and immunities of State Legislatures and their members and committees.
  • Freedom of speech in Parliament is subject to the Constitution and rules of the House.
  • Committee of Privileges examines alleged breaches of privilege.
  • Ethics Committees examine ethical conduct under the relevant parliamentary framework.

11. Committee System: Strengths and Limitations

Strengths – ప్రయోజనాలు

  • Detailed scrutiny of complex issues.
  • Expert evidence and consultation.
  • Improves executive accountability.
  • Encourages cross-party discussion.
  • Helps Parliament manage limited time.
  • Improves quality of legislation and financial oversight.

Limitations – పరిమితులు

  • Committee recommendations are generally advisory and not automatically binding.
  • Reports may not always be discussed in detail in the House.
  • Some committees face time and research constraints.
  • Attendance and participation may vary.
  • Not every Bill is referred to a committee.
Reform suggestions: Refer more important Bills to committees, strengthen research support, improve attendance, provide adequate time and increase transparency while protecting appropriate confidentiality.

14. Mains Answer Framework

Question: Discuss the role of Parliamentary Committees in ensuring accountability and improving the functioning of Parliament.

Introduction: Parliamentary committees are smaller groups of MPs that undertake detailed scrutiny and assist the Houses.

Types: Explain Standing and Ad hoc Committees, with examples.

Financial oversight: Discuss PAC, Estimates Committee and COPU.

Departmental scrutiny: Explain DRSCs and their examination of demands for grants, bills and annual reports.

Importance: Better scrutiny, executive accountability, expertise and cross-party deliberation.

Challenges: Limited referrals, time constraints, attendance and advisory nature of recommendations.

Conclusion: Strengthening committee scrutiny can improve legislative quality, public finance oversight and democratic accountability.
