1. Introduction – పరిచయం

A Cooperative Society is a voluntary association of individuals who come together to achieve common economic, social or cultural objectives through a jointly owned and democratically controlled organisation.

సహకార సంఘం అనేది ఒకే విధమైన ఆర్థిక, సామాజిక లేదా సాంస్కృతిక లక్ష్యాల కోసం వ్యక్తులు స్వచ్ఛందంగా కలిసి, ఉమ్మడి యాజమాన్యం మరియు ప్రజాస్వామ్య నియంత్రణతో నిర్వహించే సంస్థ.

Core idea: Cooperation means working together for mutual benefit. సహకారం అంటే పరస్పర ప్రయోజనం కోసం కలిసి పనిచేయడం.

Important characteristics – ముఖ్య లక్షణాలు

  • Voluntary and open membership – స్వచ్ఛంద, అందరికీ అందుబాటులో ఉండే సభ్యత్వం.
  • Democratic member control – సభ్యుల ప్రజాస్వామ్య నియంత్రణ.
  • Member economic participation – సభ్యుల ఆర్థిక భాగస్వామ్యం.
  • Autonomy and independence – స్వయంప్రతిపత్తి.
  • Concern for community – సమాజ సంక్షేమం.
  • Service motive is central; profit is not the sole objective.

2. Constitutional Background – రాజ్యాంగ నేపథ్యం

Before the 97th Amendment

Cooperative societies were primarily within the legislative domain of States under Entry 32 of List II (State List), subject to constitutional provisions and other relevant entries.

97వ సవరణకు ముందు సహకార సంఘాలపై రాష్ట్రాలకు ప్రధాన శాసనాధికారం ఉండేది. ఏడవ షెడ్యూల్‌లోని State List Entry 32 దీనికి ముఖ్యమైన ఆధారం.

97th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2011

  • 97th Constitutional Amendment Act was enacted in 2011.
  • It came into effect on 15 February 2012.
  • It amended Article 19(1)(c).
  • It inserted Article 43B in the Directive Principles of State Policy.
  • It inserted Part IX-B, originally covering Articles 243ZH to 243ZT.
Exam point: The 97th Amendment is associated with three major constitutional changes: Article 19(1)(c), Article 43B and Part IX-B.

3. Article 19(1)(c) – Fundamental Right

Article 19(1)(c) guarantees citizens the right to form associations or unions or cooperative societies.

ఆర్టికల్ 19(1)(c) ప్రకారం పౌరులకు సంఘాలు, యూనియన్లు లేదా సహకార సంఘాలను ఏర్పాటు చేసుకునే హక్కు ఉంది.

  • It is a Fundamental Right under Part III.
  • It is available to citizens, not every person irrespective of citizenship.
  • The right is subject to reasonable restrictions under Article 19(4), on the grounds specified there.
  • The 97th Amendment expressly added cooperative societies to the wording.

4. Article 43B – Directive Principle

Article 43B directs the State to promote voluntary formation, autonomous functioning, democratic control and professional management of cooperative societies.

ఆర్టికల్ 43B ప్రకారం సహకార సంఘాల స్వచ్ఛంద ఏర్పాటు, స్వయంప్రతిపత్తి, ప్రజాస్వామ్య నియంత్రణ మరియు వృత్తిపరమైన నిర్వహణను ప్రోత్సహించాలి.

Voluntary formationస్వచ్ఛంద ఏర్పాటు
Autonomous functioningస్వతంత్ర నిర్వహణ
Democratic controlప్రజాస్వామ్య నియంత్రణ
Professional managementవృత్తిపరమైన నిర్వహణ

Article 43B is a Directive Principle of State Policy (DPSP), not a Fundamental Right.

6. Board, Elections and Accountability

Board composition and term – Article 243ZJ

The original Part IX-B framework provided for the composition and term of the board and office bearers. It included provisions concerning the maximum number of directors and reservation of seats for SC/ST and women, subject to the constitutional text and applicable judgment.

Election – Article 243ZK

It dealt with the conduct of elections to the board. The superintendence, direction and control of preparation of electoral rolls and conduct of elections were assigned to the authority specified under the provision.

Supersession – Article 243ZL

It dealt with supersession or suspension of the board and interim management, including safeguards and limits in the original constitutional framework.

Audit – Article 243ZM

It dealt with audit of accounts of cooperative societies. Audit helps ensure financial transparency and accountability.

Member rights

  • Article 243ZN – General body meetings.
  • Article 243ZO – Right of members to information.
  • Article 243ZP – Filing of returns.
  • Article 243ZQ – Offences and penalties.

8. Cooperative Movement in India – భారతదేశంలో సహకార ఉద్యమం

  • 1904: Cooperative Credit Societies Act, 1904 – cooperative credit movement received an early legislative framework under British rule.
  • 1912: Cooperative Societies Act, 1912 expanded the legal framework beyond credit societies.
  • 1919: Cooperation became a transferred subject under the Government of India Act, 1919.
  • 1935: Provincial autonomy continued the development of provincial legislative responsibility.
  • 1950: The Constitution placed relevant State cooperative matters mainly within the State List.
  • 2011: 97th Constitutional Amendment Act.
  • 2012: 97th Amendment came into effect on 15 February 2012.
  • 2021: Supreme Court delivered its judgment in Union of India v. Rajendra N. Shah.

9. Supreme Court Judgment – 2021

Union of India v. Rajendra N. Shah (2021)

The Supreme Court considered the constitutional validity of the 97th Constitutional Amendment, particularly Part IX-B.

97వ రాజ్యాంగ సవరణలోని Part IX-B చెల్లుబాటు గురించి సుప్రీంకోర్టు పరిశీలించింది.

  • The amendment affected the federal distribution of legislative powers concerning State cooperative societies.
  • Under the proviso to Article 368(2), an amendment affecting specified federal provisions requires ratification by at least half of the State Legislatures.
  • The required State ratification had not been obtained for the relevant amendment to Part IX-B.
  • The Court held Part IX-B invalid insofar as it relates to cooperative societies operating within a State.
  • Part IX-B was upheld in its application to multi-State cooperative societies and Union Territories.
Very important exam clarification: The Supreme Court did NOT strike down the entire 97th Amendment. The changes to Article 19(1)(c) and Article 43B remained valid. The ruling concerned Part IX-B's application to cooperative societies operating within a State.

10. Multi-State Cooperative Societies

Multi-State cooperative societies operate across more than one State. Their legal framework is distinct from societies operating only within one State.

  • Article 243ZR dealt with application of Part IX-B to multi-State cooperative societies.
  • Parliament has legislative competence over incorporation, regulation and winding up of cooperative societies with objects not confined to one State under the relevant constitutional framework.
  • The Multi-State Cooperative Societies Act, 2002 is an important central law.
