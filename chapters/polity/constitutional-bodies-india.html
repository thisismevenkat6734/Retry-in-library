Overview | అధ్యాయం అవలోకనం

Constitutional bodies are institutions established directly by the Constitution of India. Their powers, functions, composition and responsibilities are derived from constitutional provisions.

రాజ్యాంగంలోని నిబంధనల ద్వారా నేరుగా ఏర్పడిన సంస్థలను రాజ్యాంగబద్ధ సంస్థలు అంటారు. వీటి అధికారాలు, విధులు, నిర్మాణం రాజ్యాంగం ద్వారా నిర్ణయించబడతాయి.

Exam Focus: Articles, appointments, tenure, removal procedures, functions and differences between constitutional, statutory and executive bodies are important for UPSC, State PSC, SSC and other competitive examinations.

1. Constitutional, Statutory and Executive Bodies

Constitutional Body: Created directly by the Constitution.

Statutory Body: Established through an Act passed by Parliament or a State Legislature.

Executive Body: Created by a government executive resolution or order, rather than directly by the Constitution or a specific establishing statute.

Important: A body does not become constitutional merely because it performs an important public function. Its legal basis determines its classification.

2. Election Commission of India | భారత ఎన్నికల సంఘం

Article 324 provides for the superintendence, direction and control of elections to Parliament, State Legislatures, and the offices of President and Vice-President.

భారత ఎన్నికల సంఘం స్వేచ్ఛగా, నిష్పక్షపాతంగా ఎన్నికలు నిర్వహించడానికి బాధ్యత వహిస్తుంది.

Composition

  • Chief Election Commissioner (CEC).
  • Other Election Commissioners, if appointed.
  • The President determines the number of Election Commissioners, subject to law.
  • The CEC and other Election Commissioners are appointed by the President, subject to the applicable law.

Appointment law

The Chief Election Commissioner and Other Election Commissioners (Appointment, Conditions of Service and Term of Office) Act, 2023 provides the statutory framework for appointment and service conditions.

Under the 2023 Act, the Selection Committee consists of the Prime Minister, a Union Cabinet Minister nominated by the Prime Minister, and the Leader of Opposition in the Lok Sabha (or leader of the largest opposition party where applicable under the Act).

Tenure and removal

  • Under the 2023 Act, the term is six years from the date of assuming office or until attaining 65 years of age, whichever is earlier.
  • The CEC can be removed in the same manner and on the same grounds as a Supreme Court judge.
  • Other Election Commissioners cannot be removed except on the recommendation of the CEC.

Major functions

  • Conduct elections to Parliament and State Legislatures.
  • Conduct elections to the offices of President and Vice-President.
  • Prepare and supervise electoral rolls.
  • Recognise political parties and allot election symbols under applicable rules.
  • Monitor election expenditure and enforce the Model Code of Conduct through its election-related authority.
Remember: Local body elections are conducted by State Election Commissions under Articles 243K and 243ZA, not by the Election Commission of India.

3. Union Public Service Commission (UPSC)

Articles 315–323 deal with Public Service Commissions.

UPSC is a constitutional body that conducts examinations and performs recruitment-related functions for Union services.

Important Articles

  • Article 315 – Public Service Commissions for the Union and States.
  • Article 316 – Appointment and term of office of members.
  • Article 317 – Removal and suspension of members.
  • Article 318 – Regulations regarding conditions of service.
  • Article 319 – Prohibition as to holding of offices after ceasing to be members.
  • Article 320 – Functions of Public Service Commissions.
  • Article 323 – Reports of Public Service Commissions.

Appointment and tenure

  • Chairman and members of UPSC are appointed by the President.
  • They hold office for six years or until attaining 65 years of age, whichever is earlier.
  • At least half the members should have held office for at least ten years under the Government of India or a State Government at the time of appointment.

Removal

The President may remove a member on grounds and through the procedure specified in Article 317. In cases of misbehaviour, the President refers the matter to the Supreme Court for inquiry. The President may also remove a member in specified circumstances such as insolvency, paid employment outside duties, or infirmity of mind or body as provided by the Constitution.

Functions

  • Conduct examinations for appointments to Union services.
  • Assist in recruitment methods and principles.
  • Advise on promotions and transfers where applicable.
  • Advise on disciplinary matters referred to it.
  • Submit an annual report to the President.
UPSC advice is generally advisory in nature. The Constitution does not make every recommendation of UPSC binding on the government.

4. State Public Service Commission

State Public Service Commissions are provided under Articles 315–323.

  • Chairman and members are appointed by the Governor.
  • They hold office for six years or until attaining 62 years of age, whichever is earlier.
  • Removal is made by the President under Article 317.
  • They conduct recruitment examinations for State services and perform other functions assigned by the Constitution and law.
  • Their annual report is submitted to the Governor.

Joint State Public Service Commission

Under Article 315, Parliament may by law provide for a Joint State Public Service Commission for two or more States, if the concerned State Legislatures pass resolutions requesting it.

5. Comptroller and Auditor General of India (CAG)

Article 148 provides for the Comptroller and Auditor General of India.

CAG is an important constitutional authority responsible for auditing public accounts and promoting financial accountability.

Appointment and tenure

  • Appointed by the President of India.
  • Holds office for six years or until attaining 65 years of age, whichever is earlier.
  • Can be removed in the same manner and on the same grounds as a Supreme Court judge.
  • After retirement, the CAG is not eligible for further office under the Government of India or any State Government.

Important Articles

  • Article 148 – CAG of India.
  • Article 149 – Duties and powers of CAG.
  • Article 150 – Form of accounts of the Union and States.
  • Article 151 – Audit reports.

Functions

  • Audits expenditure from the Consolidated Fund of India and State Consolidated Funds, subject to constitutional and legal provisions.
  • Audits receipts and expenditure of governments and other bodies as provided by law.
  • Audits government companies and certain public sector entities under applicable laws.
  • Submits Union audit reports to the President.
  • Submits State audit reports to the Governor.
The CAG is often described as the guardian of the public purse. Audit reports are examined by legislative committees such as the Public Accounts Committee.

6. Finance Commission | ఆర్థిక సంఘం

Article 280 provides for the Finance Commission.

The President constitutes a Finance Commission every five years or earlier if considered necessary.

Composition

  • One Chairman.
  • Four other members.
  • Qualifications are determined by Parliament through law.

Functions under Article 280

  • Recommend distribution of net proceeds of taxes between the Union and States.
  • Recommend allocation of the States' share among the States.
  • Recommend principles governing grants-in-aid of State revenues.
  • Recommend measures to augment State funds for Panchayats and Municipalities, based on recommendations of State Finance Commissions.
  • Consider other matters referred by the President in the interests of sound finance.

Important points

  • It is a constitutional body.
  • Its recommendations are generally advisory.
  • It strengthens fiscal federalism.
  • The President lays the recommendations and an explanatory memorandum on action taken before Parliament.
Exam Tip: Finance Commission – Article 280. GST Council – Article 279A. Do not confuse these two bodies.

7. National Commission for Scheduled Castes (NCSC)

Article 338 provides for the National Commission for Scheduled Castes.

Composition

  • Chairperson.
  • Vice-Chairperson.
  • Three other members.

They are appointed by the President by warrant under his or her hand and seal. Their conditions of service and tenure are determined by the President through rules.

Functions

  • Investigate and monitor safeguards provided for Scheduled Castes under the Constitution and other laws.
  • Inquire into specific complaints relating to deprivation of rights and safeguards.
  • Participate in and advise on planning processes concerning socio-economic development of Scheduled Castes.
  • Present reports to the President.
  • Make recommendations for effective implementation of safeguards.

Under Article 338, the Commission has powers of a civil court while investigating certain matters.

8. National Commission for Scheduled Tribes (NCST)

Article 338A provides for the National Commission for Scheduled Tribes.

The 89th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2003 separated the earlier combined commission and created a separate National Commission for Scheduled Tribes.

Functions

  • Monitor constitutional and legal safeguards for Scheduled Tribes.
  • Investigate complaints relating to deprivation of rights.
  • Advise on socio-economic development planning.
  • Submit reports to the President.
  • Recommend measures for protection and development of Scheduled Tribes.
Article 338 – NCSC. Article 338A – NCST.

9. National Commission for Backward Classes (NCBC)

Article 338B provides for the National Commission for Backward Classes as a constitutional body.

  • The 102nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 2018 gave constitutional status to the NCBC.
  • The Commission consists of a Chairperson, Vice-Chairperson and three other members.
  • Members are appointed by the President.
  • It investigates and monitors safeguards for socially and educationally backward classes.
  • It advises on socio-economic development and submits reports to the President.

105th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2021

The 105th Amendment restored the power of States and Union Territories to identify and maintain their own lists of socially and educationally backward classes for their own purposes, subject to the constitutional framework.

Important distinction: Article 338B relates to NCBC. Article 342A deals with socially and educationally backward classes and the relevant lists.

10. Attorney General of India

Article 76 provides for the Attorney General of India, the highest law officer of the Union.

  • Appointed by the President.
  • Must be qualified to be appointed as a Judge of the Supreme Court.
  • Gives legal advice to the Government of India on matters referred by the President.
  • Performs other legal duties assigned by the President.
  • Has the right to speak and take part in proceedings of either House of Parliament and their committees, but does not have the right to vote.
  • Holds office during the pleasure of the President.

Advocate General of a State

Article 165 provides for the Advocate General, the highest law officer of a State.

  • Appointed by the Governor.
  • Must be qualified to be appointed as a High Court judge.
  • Advises the State Government on legal matters.
  • Has the right to speak and take part in proceedings of the State Legislature and its committees, but cannot vote merely by virtue of this office.
  • Holds office during the pleasure of the Governor.

11. Special Officer for Linguistic Minorities

Article 350B provides for a Special Officer for Linguistic Minorities.

  • Appointed by the President.
  • Investigates matters relating to safeguards provided for linguistic minorities under the Constitution.
  • Reports to the President at intervals directed by the President.
  • Reports are placed before Parliament and sent to the concerned State Governments.

12. Goods and Services Tax Council (GST Council)

Article 279A provides for the GST Council.

The President constitutes the Council by order. It is a key institution for cooperative fiscal federalism.

Composition

  • Union Finance Minister – Chairperson.
  • Union Minister of State in charge of Revenue or Finance – Member.
  • Minister in charge of Finance or Taxation, or another nominated Minister, from each State – Members.

Functions

  • Make recommendations on taxes, cesses and surcharges to be subsumed under GST.
  • Recommend goods and services that may be subject to or exempt from GST.
  • Recommend model GST laws and principles of levy.
  • Recommend threshold limits and special provisions for certain States.
  • Address issues relating to GST administration and implementation.

Voting

  • Quorum: one-half of the total members.
  • Decision requires at least three-fourths of the weighted votes of members present and voting.
  • Union Government vote has a weight of one-third.
  • States together have a weight of two-thirds.

13. State Election Commission

State Election Commissions are constitutional authorities responsible for elections to Panchayats and Municipalities.

  • Article 243K – Elections to Panchayats.
  • Article 243ZA – Elections to Municipalities.
  • State Election Commissioner is appointed by the Governor.
  • Removal conditions are protected by constitutional safeguards.
Do not confuse the Election Commission of India with State Election Commissions. Their election responsibilities are different.

14. State Finance Commission

State Finance Commissions are provided under Articles 243I and 243Y.

  • Under Article 243I, the Governor constitutes a Finance Commission every five years to review the financial position of Panchayats.
  • Article 243Y applies the relevant provisions to Municipalities.
  • It recommends principles for distribution of State revenues between the State and local bodies.
  • It recommends grants-in-aid and measures to improve the financial position of local bodies.

16. Mains Answer Framework | మెయిన్స్ సమాధాన నిర్మాణం

Question: Explain the role of constitutional bodies in strengthening Indian democracy.

Introduction: Constitutional bodies are institutions established by the Constitution to ensure accountability, fairness and effective governance.

Body:

  • Electoral integrity – Election Commission.
  • Merit-based recruitment – UPSC and State PSCs.
  • Financial accountability – CAG.
  • Fiscal federalism – Finance Commission and GST Council.
  • Social justice – NCSC, NCST and NCBC.
  • Legal advice to governments – Attorney General and Advocate General.

Challenges: Vacancies, delays in appointments, resource constraints, concerns about independence and implementation gaps.

Way Forward: Transparent appointments, adequate resources, timely reports, parliamentary oversight and stronger institutional independence.

Conclusion: Independent and accountable constitutional bodies are essential for protecting constitutional values and strengthening democratic governance.

17. Glossary | ముఖ్య పదాలు

  • Constitutional Body: A body established by the Constitution – రాజ్యాంగబద్ధ సంస్థ.
  • Statutory Body: A body established by legislation – చట్టబద్ధ సంస్థ.
  • Executive Body: A body created by executive action – కార్యనిర్వాహక సంస్థ.
  • Tenure: Period of holding office – పదవీకాలం.
  • Removal: Formal termination from office – పదవి నుంచి తొలగింపు.
  • Audit: Examination of accounts – ఆడిట్ / ఖాతాల తనిఖీ.
  • Fiscal Federalism: Financial relations between levels of government – ఆర్థిక సమాఖ్య విధానం.
  • Safeguards: Protective measures – రక్షణ చర్యలు.
  • Advisory: Giving recommendations or advice – సలహా సంబంధిత.
  • Superintendence: Overall supervision and control – పర్యవేక్షణ.
