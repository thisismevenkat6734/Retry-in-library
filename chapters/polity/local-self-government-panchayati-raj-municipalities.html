1. Meaning and Importance of Local Self-Government

Local self-government is a system through which local communities manage local public affairs through elected or otherwise constitutionally established local institutions.

Importance

  • Promotes grassroots democracy.
  • Encourages public participation.
  • Helps identify local needs and priorities.
  • Improves delivery of basic services.
  • Promotes leadership at the village and urban levels.
  • Strengthens accountability and transparency.
  • Encourages inclusion of women and disadvantaged communities.
  • Supports decentralisation of power.
Democratic decentralisation means transferring appropriate powers, functions and resources to institutions closer to the people.

2. Historical Background of Panchayati Raj

Ancient India

Village-level assemblies and community institutions existed in different forms in ancient India. Their structure and powers varied across regions and historical periods.

British Period

  • Lord Ripon's Resolution, 1882: Considered an important landmark in the development of local self-government in British India.
  • Royal Commission on Decentralisation, 1907: Examined decentralisation and local administration.
  • Government of India Act, 1919: Transferred local self-government to provincial administration under the dyarchical framework.
  • Government of India Act, 1935: Further developed provincial autonomy, with local government remaining an important provincial subject.

After Independence

  • Community Development Programme was launched in 1952.
  • National Extension Service was introduced in 1953.
  • The Balwant Rai Mehta Committee was appointed in 1957.
  • Rajasthan inaugurated the Panchayati Raj system at Nagaur on 2 October 1959.
Exam Fact: Lord Ripon's Resolution of 1882 is commonly called the Magna Carta of local self-government in India.

4. 73rd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1992

The 73rd Constitutional Amendment Act gave constitutional status to Panchayati Raj institutions.

  • Passed by Parliament in 1992.
  • Came into force on 24 April 1993.
  • Added Part IX to the Constitution.
  • Part IX contains Articles 243 to 243O.
  • Added the Eleventh Schedule.
  • The Eleventh Schedule contains 29 subjects.
24 April: National Panchayati Raj Day is observed in India to commemorate the commencement of the 73rd Constitutional Amendment.

Objectives

  • Constitutional recognition to Panchayati Raj institutions.
  • Regular elections to local bodies.
  • Reservation for Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes.
  • At least one-third reservation for women.
  • Constitution of State Election Commissions.
  • Constitution of State Finance Commissions.
  • Strengthening democratic decentralisation.

6. Three-Tier Panchayati Raj System

Article 243B provides for the constitution of Panchayats at village, intermediate and district levels.

LevelInstitutionArea
Village levelGram PanchayatVillage
Intermediate levelPanchayat Samiti / Mandal Parishad / Block PanchayatBlock or intermediate area
District levelZilla ParishadDistrict

Important exception

A State having a population not exceeding twenty lakh may not constitute Panchayats at the intermediate level.

The names of intermediate and district-level institutions vary across States. Their exact structure is determined by State laws within the constitutional framework.

7. Gram Sabha

Article 243A provides for Gram Sabha.

Gram Sabha consists of persons registered in the electoral rolls relating to a village within the area of a Gram Panchayat.

Functions

  • Discuss local development needs.
  • Participate in village-level decision-making.
  • Consider development plans and programmes.
  • Promote transparency in local administration.
  • Review implementation of public welfare programmes.
  • Encourage public participation in village governance.
  • Help identify beneficiaries under relevant schemes according to applicable rules.

8. Composition and Elections of Panchayats

Article 243C deals with the composition of Panchayats.

  • State Legislature determines the composition of Panchayats through law, subject to constitutional provisions.
  • Seats in a Panchayat are generally filled by direct election from territorial constituencies.
  • State laws may provide for representation of chairpersons of lower-level Panchayats and other specified representatives at higher levels.
  • Chairpersons at village level are elected in the manner provided by State law.
  • Chairpersons at intermediate and district levels are elected by and from among the elected members, as provided in Article 243C.

9. Reservation in Panchayats

Article 243D provides for reservation of seats in Panchayats.

Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes

  • Seats are reserved for SCs and STs in proportion to their population in the Panchayat area.
  • Reserved seats are allotted to different constituencies by rotation as provided by law.

Women

  • Not less than one-third of the total seats to be filled by direct election are reserved for women.
  • This includes seats reserved for women belonging to SCs and STs.
  • Not less than one-third of the total number of offices of chairpersons at each level are reserved for women.

Backward Classes

State Legislatures may provide reservation of seats and offices of chairpersons in favour of backward classes, subject to constitutional requirements and judicial decisions.

Many States provide 50% reservation for women in Panchayati Raj institutions through State laws. The constitutional minimum under Article 243D is one-third.

10. Duration and Disqualification

Article 243E – Duration

  • Every Panchayat continues for five years from the date appointed for its first meeting, unless sooner dissolved under law.
  • An election to constitute a Panchayat must be completed before the expiry of its duration.
  • If a Panchayat is dissolved, elections must generally be completed within six months from the date of dissolution.
  • If the remainder of the dissolved Panchayat's duration is less than six months, it is not necessary to hold an election for that short remainder.
  • A Panchayat constituted after premature dissolution continues only for the remainder of the original term.

Article 243F – Disqualifications

A person may be disqualified for membership under the grounds specified by the Constitution and State law.

A person is not disqualified merely because they are less than 25 years old if they have attained 21 years of age.

11. Powers and Functions of Panchayats

Article 243G empowers State Legislatures to endow Panchayats with powers and responsibilities necessary to function as institutions of self-government.

Major functions

  • Preparation of plans for economic development and social justice.
  • Implementation of schemes for economic development and social justice.
  • Agriculture and agricultural extension.
  • Minor irrigation and watershed development.
  • Animal husbandry and fisheries.
  • Rural housing.
  • Drinking water and sanitation.
  • Rural roads and bridges.
  • Education and health-related local services.
  • Women and child development.
  • Social welfare and poverty alleviation.
Article 243G does not automatically transfer every subject to every Panchayat. The actual devolution of functions, funds and functionaries depends on State laws and government action.

13. State Finance Commission

Article 243I provides for the constitution of a State Finance Commission by the Governor.

Constitution

  • The Governor constitutes a Finance Commission within one year from the commencement of the 73rd Amendment and thereafter at the expiration of every fifth year.
  • The State Legislature determines the qualifications of members and the manner of their selection by law.

Functions

  • Review the financial position of Panchayats.
  • Recommend principles governing distribution of State revenues between the State and Panchayats.
  • Recommend allocation of shares among Panchayats at different levels.
  • Recommend taxes, duties, tolls and fees that may be assigned to Panchayats.
  • Recommend grants-in-aid from the State Consolidated Fund.
  • Suggest measures to improve the financial position of Panchayats.
The State Finance Commission is different from the Union Finance Commission under Article 280.

14. State Election Commission

Article 243K provides for the State Election Commission for Panchayat elections.

  • The State Election Commissioner is appointed by the Governor.
  • The superintendence, direction and control of preparation of electoral rolls and conduct of Panchayat elections are vested in the State Election Commission.
  • The State Election Commissioner cannot be removed except in the same manner and on the same grounds as a Judge of a High Court.
  • Conditions of service of the State Election Commissioner cannot be varied to their disadvantage after appointment.
The State Election Commission conducts Panchayat and Municipal elections. The Election Commission of India conducts elections to Parliament, State Legislatures, President and Vice-President.

15. PESA Act, 1996

The Provisions of the Panchayats (Extension to the Scheduled Areas) Act, 1996 is commonly known as the PESA Act.

Purpose

PESA extends the provisions of Part IX of the Constitution to Fifth Schedule areas, subject to specified exceptions and modifications.

Important features

  • Recognises the role of Gram Sabha in Scheduled Areas.
  • Protects customary laws, social and religious practices and traditional management practices, subject to the Act.
  • Supports community participation in management of natural resources.
  • Provides for consultation with Gram Sabha or Panchayats in matters specified under the Act.
  • Recognises the role of Gram Sabha in safeguarding traditions and community resources.
  • Provides for ownership of minor forest produce to Gram Sabha or Panchayats at the appropriate level, subject to the Act.
  • Includes safeguards relating to land alienation and local development.
PESA applies to Fifth Schedule areas. It does not automatically apply to every tribal area or every village in India.

16. 74th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1992

The 74th Constitutional Amendment Act gave constitutional status to urban local bodies.

  • Passed in 1992.
  • Came into force on 1 June 1993.
  • Added Part IXA to the Constitution.
  • Part IXA contains Articles 243P to 243ZG.
  • Added the Twelfth Schedule.
  • The Twelfth Schedule contains 18 subjects.

Objectives

  • Strengthen urban local self-government.
  • Ensure regular elections.
  • Provide reservation for SCs, STs and women.
  • Promote decentralised urban planning.
  • Strengthen municipal finances.
  • Provide constitutional recognition to municipalities.

An industrial township may be excluded from the requirement of constituting a Municipality where the conditions under Article 243Q are satisfied.

19. Composition and Reservation in Municipalities

Article 243R

  • Municipal seats are generally filled by direct election from territorial constituencies called wards.
  • State law may provide representation for persons with special knowledge or experience in municipal administration, subject to constitutional restrictions.
  • Representation may also be provided for Members of Parliament and State Legislatures whose constituencies fall within the municipal area, as provided by law.

Article 243T – Reservation

  • Seats are reserved for SCs and STs in proportion to their population in the municipal area.
  • Not less than one-third of the total seats to be filled by direct election are reserved for women.
  • This includes seats reserved for women belonging to SCs and STs.
  • Not less than one-third of the total offices of chairpersons are reserved for women, subject to State law and the constitutional framework.
  • State Legislatures may provide reservation for backward classes.

20. Duration and Functions of Municipalities

Article 243U – Duration

  • Every Municipality continues for five years from the date appointed for its first meeting, unless sooner dissolved under law.
  • Election must generally be completed before the expiry of its term.
  • If dissolved, election must generally be completed within six months.
  • If the remainder of the original term is less than six months, it is not necessary to hold an election for that short remainder.

Article 243W – Functions

State Legislatures may endow Municipalities with powers and responsibilities to function as institutions of self-government.

  • Urban planning.
  • Regulation of land use and construction.
  • Roads and bridges.
  • Water supply.
  • Public health and sanitation.
  • Solid waste management.
  • Fire services.
  • Urban forestry and environmental protection.
  • Slum improvement and upgradation.
  • Urban poverty alleviation.
  • Public amenities and parks.
  • Registration of births and deaths.

  18. Regulation of slaughterhouses and tanneries.

22. Wards Committees

Article 243S provides for Wards Committees in municipalities with a population of three lakh or more.

  • Wards Committees are formed within municipal areas according to State law.
  • They help decentralise municipal administration.
  • They promote participation at the neighbourhood level.
  • They may assist in identifying local needs and monitoring civic services.

23. District Planning Committee

Article 243ZD provides for a District Planning Committee (DPC).

Purpose

To consolidate plans prepared by Panchayats and Municipalities in the district and prepare a draft development plan for the district as a whole.

Composition

  • State Legislature provides for composition and election of members by law.
  • Not less than four-fifths of the total members are elected by and from among the elected members of Panchayats at the district level and Municipalities in the district.
  • Representation is proportionate to the rural and urban population of the district.

Planning considerations

  • Common interests between rural and urban areas.
  • Sharing of water and natural resources.
  • Integrated development of infrastructure.
  • Environmental conservation.
  • Available financial and other resources.

24. Metropolitan Planning Committee

Article 243ZE provides for a Metropolitan Planning Committee (MPC).

A metropolitan area is an area having a population of ten lakh or more, comprising one or more districts and two or more Municipalities or Panchayats or other contiguous areas, as specified under the constitutional definition.

Functions

  • Prepare a draft development plan for the metropolitan area as a whole.
  • Coordinate plans prepared by Municipalities and Panchayats.
  • Consider common interests of urban and rural areas.
  • Coordinate infrastructure and environmental planning.
  • Consider available financial and other resources.

Composition

Not less than two-thirds of the members of an MPC are elected by and from among elected members of Municipalities and Chairpersons of Panchayats in the metropolitan area, in proportion to the population represented by them.

25. Municipal Finance

Articles 243X and 243Y deal with municipal taxation and financial arrangements.

Article 243X

  • State Legislature may authorise Municipalities to levy, collect and appropriate taxes, duties, tolls and fees.
  • State Legislature may assign certain taxes collected by the State to Municipalities.
  • State Legislature may provide grants-in-aid from the State Consolidated Fund.
  • State law may provide for municipal funds and their operation.

Article 243Y

The State Finance Commission constituted under Article 243I also reviews the financial position of Municipalities and makes recommendations concerning financial distribution and grants.

27. Challenges in Local Self-Government

  • Insufficient funds: Many local bodies depend heavily on State and Union grants.
  • Limited functions: Constitutional subjects may not be fully devolved by States.
  • Staff shortages: Local institutions may lack trained personnel.
  • Political interference: Local decisions may be influenced by higher levels of government.
  • Weak accountability: Social audits and public oversight may be inadequate.
  • Delayed elections: Delays weaken democratic accountability.
  • Capacity limitations: Elected representatives may require administrative and technical training.
  • Social inequality: Local power structures can limit participation of weaker sections.
  • Limited revenue: Local bodies may have weak tax collection systems.

28. Measures to Strengthen Local Self-Government

  • Effective devolution of the three Fs: Functions, Funds and Functionaries.
  • Regular and timely local body elections.
  • Strengthening Gram Sabhas and Ward Committees.
  • Improving local revenue collection.
  • Regular State Finance Commission reports and implementation of recommendations.
  • Training and capacity-building for elected representatives.
  • Transparent budgeting and public disclosure.
  • Social audits and citizen participation.
  • Use of digital platforms for service delivery and grievance redressal.
  • Greater participation of women and disadvantaged groups.
  • Better coordination between Panchayats, Municipalities and district planning institutions.
Important Concept: The three Fs are Functions, Funds and Functionaries. Effective decentralisation requires all three.

30. Mains Answer Framework

Question: Examine the role of the 73rd and 74th Constitutional Amendments in strengthening grassroots democracy in India.

Introduction: The 73rd and 74th Constitutional Amendments gave constitutional recognition to rural and urban local self-government institutions.

Major contributions:

  • Constitutional status to Panchayats and Municipalities.
  • Regular five-year terms and election provisions.
  • Reservation for SCs, STs and women.
  • State Election Commissions for local elections.
  • State Finance Commissions for financial review.
  • Eleventh and Twelfth Schedules identifying potential areas of devolution.
  • District and Metropolitan Planning Committees.

Challenges: Inadequate funds, incomplete transfer of functions, staff shortages, weak accountability and delayed elections.

Way Forward: Strengthen the three Fs, improve financial autonomy, support local capacity-building and promote citizen participation.

Conclusion: Effective local self-government is essential for inclusive development, democratic participation and accountable administration.
