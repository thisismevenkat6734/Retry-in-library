Overview | అవలోకనం

Political parties and pressure groups are important components of a democratic political system. Political parties seek political power through elections, while pressure groups attempt to influence public policy and government decisions without necessarily seeking to form a government.

రాజకీయ పార్టీలు ఎన్నికల ద్వారా రాజకీయ అధికారాన్ని పొందడానికి ప్రయత్నిస్తాయి. ఒత్తిడి సమూహాలు ప్రభుత్వ విధానాలు, నిర్ణయాలపై ప్రభావం చూపడానికి ప్రయత్నిస్తాయి. ఇవి ప్రజాస్వామ్య వ్యవస్థలో ముఖ్యమైన భాగాలు.

Exam Focus: Political party recognition, national and State parties, party systems, election symbols, anti-defection law, pressure groups, lobbying, social movements and their role in democracy.

1. Political Parties | రాజకీయ పార్టీలు

A political party is an organised group of people who share certain political ideas, policies or objectives and seek to influence or control government through constitutional and electoral methods.

సాధారణ రాజకీయ లక్ష్యాలు, సిద్ధాంతాలు లేదా విధానాలను పంచుకునే వ్యక్తుల సంఘటిత సమూహాన్ని రాజకీయ పార్టీ అంటారు. ఇవి ఎన్నికల్లో పోటీ చేసి ప్రభుత్వాన్ని ఏర్పాటు చేయడానికి లేదా ప్రభుత్వ విధానాలపై ప్రభావం చూపడానికి ప్రయత్నిస్తాయి.

Major characteristics

  • Organised membership and leadership.
  • Common political ideology or policy programme.
  • Participation in elections.
  • Desire to obtain political power or influence government.
  • Public support and political mobilisation.
  • Accountability to voters through elections.

Major functions

  • Contest elections and nominate candidates.
  • Form governments when they secure the required support.
  • Act as opposition parties when they are not in government.
  • Develop policies and programmes.
  • Educate and mobilise voters.
  • Aggregate different social and economic interests.
  • Recruit political leadership.
  • Connect citizens with government institutions.

2. Party System in India

A party system refers to the pattern of competition and interaction among political parties in a country.

Types of party systems

TypeMeaningExample / feature
One-party systemOne political party dominates or legally controls political competition.Limited political competition.
Two-party systemTwo major parties dominate electoral competition.United States is commonly described as a two-party-dominant system.
Multi-party systemSeveral parties compete and may form coalition governments.India has a multi-party system.

Features of India's party system

  • Multi-party competition.
  • Presence of national and State-level parties.
  • Regional parties play an important role.
  • Coalition governments have been an important feature of Indian politics.
  • Political competition takes place at Union and State levels.
  • Party alliances may change between elections.
  • Social, linguistic, regional and economic issues influence party politics.
India is generally described as a multi-party democracy. The importance of national and regional parties varies across elections and States.

3. National and State Political Parties

The Election Commission of India recognises political parties as National Parties or State Parties according to the Election Symbols (Reservation and Allotment) Order, 1968.

National party recognition criteria

A political party can qualify as a National Party through any one of the prescribed routes:

  1. It secures at least 6% of valid votes in four or more States in the relevant Lok Sabha or Legislative Assembly elections and wins at least four Lok Sabha seats.
  2. It wins at least 2% of the total Lok Sabha seats, with candidates elected from at least three States.
  3. It is recognised as a State Party in at least four States.

State party recognition criteria

A political party can qualify as a State Party through any one of the prescribed routes under the Symbols Order. Important routes include:

  • At least 6% of valid votes in the State Assembly election and at least two Assembly seats.
  • At least 6% of valid votes in the State in a Lok Sabha election and at least one Lok Sabha seat from that State.
  • At least 3% of the total Assembly seats or at least three Assembly seats, whichever is higher.
  • At least one Lok Sabha seat for every 25 Lok Sabha seats or fraction thereof allotted to the State.
  • At least 8% of total valid votes in the State in the relevant Lok Sabha or Assembly election.
Recognition criteria are governed by the Election Symbols Order. Do not confuse party registration with recognition. Registration alone does not make a party a National or State Party.

4. Registration of Political Parties

Section 29A of the Representation of the People Act, 1951 provides for registration of political parties with the Election Commission of India.

Important points

  • An association or body of individual citizens of India may apply for registration as a political party.
  • The application must comply with the statutory requirements and prescribed time limits.
  • The party's constitution or rules must include the required commitment to the Constitution of India and principles such as socialism, secularism and democracy.
  • The Election Commission examines applications and may register eligible parties.
  • Registration and recognition are different legal concepts.

Benefits and obligations

  • Registered parties may receive benefits available under election laws and rules.
  • Recognition as a National or State Party gives additional electoral privileges.
  • Political parties have obligations relating to accounts, contributions and disclosures under applicable laws.
  • Election symbols are governed by the Election Symbols Order.

5. Election Symbols and Party Recognition

The Election Symbols (Reservation and Allotment) Order, 1968 regulates the reservation and allotment of election symbols.

Types of symbols

  • Reserved Symbol: A symbol reserved for a recognised political party.
  • Free Symbol: A symbol available for allotment to eligible candidates according to the applicable rules.

Importance of symbols

  • Help voters identify candidates and parties.
  • Assist voters who may have difficulty reading candidate names.
  • Build party identity.
  • Provide consistency in election campaigns.

Party disputes

When rival groups claim to represent the same recognised political party, the Election Commission may decide the dispute under the applicable provisions of the Symbols Order.

The Election Commission's decision under the Symbols Order is subject to judicial review. The legal framework and facts of each dispute matter.

6. Major Political Party Ideologies

Political parties may be identified by their ideological positions, policy priorities and social or regional bases. Party positions can evolve over time.

Common ideological approaches

  • Socialism: Emphasises social and economic equality and public welfare.
  • Liberalism: Emphasises individual liberty, rights and constitutional government.
  • Conservatism: Emphasises continuity, tradition and gradual change.
  • Secularism: Supports equal treatment of religions by the State under the constitutional framework.
  • Regionalism: Focuses on the interests, identity and development of a particular region.
  • Social Justice: Focuses on reducing discrimination and inequalities.
  • Environmentalism: Emphasises environmental protection and sustainable development.
Exam preparation should distinguish a party's officially stated ideology from political analysis or public opinion about that party.

7. Coalition Politics in India

A coalition government is formed when two or more political parties come together to provide the required legislative support to form or sustain a government.

Reasons for coalition politics

  • Multi-party competition.
  • Regional diversity.
  • Different social and economic interests.
  • No single party securing a majority.
  • Pre-election and post-election alliances.

Advantages

  • Encourages representation of different regions and communities.
  • Promotes consultation and negotiation.
  • Can strengthen cooperative decision-making.
  • May prevent excessive concentration of political power.

Challenges

  • Policy disagreements among partners.
  • Risk of government instability.
  • Difficulty in coordinating common programmes.
  • Pressure from smaller coalition partners.
  • Possibility of frequent political bargaining.

Important historical context

  • Coalition politics became especially prominent at the Union level from the late 1980s and 1990s.
  • The National Front, United Front and National Democratic Alliance are examples of major coalition formations in different periods.
  • Coalition governments are also common in several States.

8. Anti-Defection Law

The Anti-Defection Law is contained in the Tenth Schedule of the Constitution. It was introduced through the 52nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1985.

Grounds for disqualification

  • A member voluntarily gives up membership of the political party on whose ticket the member was elected.
  • A member votes or abstains from voting contrary to a direction issued by the political party without prior permission, and the action is not condoned within the prescribed period.
  • An elected independent member joins a political party after the election.
  • A nominated member joins a political party after the permitted six-month period from taking the seat.

Exceptions

The merger exception applies when at least two-thirds of the members of the legislature party agree to the merger, subject to the Tenth Schedule.

Decision-making authority

  • The Speaker or Chairman decides disqualification petitions under the Tenth Schedule in the first instance.
  • The decision is subject to judicial review.
  • Courts may examine the decision on recognised grounds under constitutional law.

Important judgment: Kihoto Hollohan v. Zachillhu (1992)

The Supreme Court upheld the constitutional validity of the Anti-Defection Law while holding that decisions of the Speaker or Chairman are subject to judicial review.

Important judgment: Keisham Meghachandra Singh v. Speaker, Manipur Legislative Assembly (2020)

The Supreme Court discussed the need for timely decisions on disqualification petitions and suggested that Parliament consider an independent mechanism for deciding such matters.

The earlier one-third split exception was removed by the 91st Constitutional Amendment Act, 2003. The two-thirds merger exception remains.

9. Pressure Groups | ఒత్తిడి సమూహాలు

A pressure group is an organised group that seeks to influence government policies, public decisions or legislation without necessarily trying to capture political power or form a government.

ఒత్తిడి సమూహాలు ప్రభుత్వ విధానాలు, చట్టాలు మరియు నిర్ణయాలపై ప్రభావం చూపడానికి ప్రయత్నిస్తాయి. సాధారణంగా ఇవి నేరుగా ప్రభుత్వాన్ని ఏర్పాటు చేయడానికి ఎన్నికల్లో పోటీ చేయడం ప్రధాన లక్ష్యంగా పెట్టుకోవు.

Characteristics

  • Organised around common interests.
  • Seek to influence public policy.
  • May work at local, State, national or international levels.
  • May use peaceful demonstrations, petitions, research and lobbying.
  • May cooperate with political parties or remain independent.
  • Usually focus on particular issues or interests.

10. Types of Pressure Groups

1. Business and industrial groups

Represent the interests of businesses, industries, traders and employers.

Examples: FICCI, CII and ASSOCHAM.

2. Trade unions

Represent workers and seek better wages, working conditions, social security and labour rights.

Examples: INTUC, AITUC, CITU and BMS.

3. Farmers' organisations

Represent agricultural interests, including prices, subsidies, land rights, irrigation and rural development.

4. Professional associations

Represent professionals such as doctors, lawyers, teachers and engineers.

5. Student organisations

Represent student interests relating to education, employment, campus facilities and public policy.

6. Environmental groups

Work on pollution control, biodiversity, climate change, forests and environmental justice.

7. Human rights organisations

Work to protect civil liberties, equality, dignity and human rights.

8. Consumer organisations

Promote consumer awareness, product safety, fair prices and protection against unfair trade practices.

11. Methods Used by Pressure Groups

  • Lobbying: Communicating with public representatives and officials to influence policy.
  • Petitions: Submitting requests or demands to public authorities.
  • Public campaigns: Using media and public outreach to highlight issues.
  • Demonstrations: Organising peaceful public protests.
  • Research: Publishing reports and policy recommendations.
  • Legal action: Approaching courts through appropriate legal procedures.
  • Negotiation: Engaging with governments and other stakeholders.
  • Social media: Raising awareness and mobilising supporters.
  • Strikes: Organising work stoppages where legally permissible.
Pressure groups must operate within the Constitution and applicable laws. Violence, intimidation, corruption and unlawful coercion are not legitimate democratic methods.

13. Important Pressure Groups and Organisations

FICCI

The Federation of Indian Chambers of Commerce and Industry represents business and industrial interests and engages in policy discussions.

CII

The Confederation of Indian Industry works on industry development, competitiveness, policy advocacy and economic growth.

ASSOCHAM

The Associated Chambers of Commerce and Industry of India represents business and industry interests.

INTUC

The Indian National Trade Union Congress is a trade union organisation representing workers' interests.

AITUC

The All India Trade Union Congress is one of India's major trade union organisations.

CITU

The Centre of Indian Trade Unions is a national trade union organisation.

BMS

The Bharatiya Mazdoor Sangh is a major trade union organisation.

SEWA

The Self Employed Women's Association works to organise and support women workers in the informal economy.

Important distinction

These organisations have different legal structures and objectives. Not every organisation that influences government policy is formally a pressure group under a specific law.

14. Social Movements in India

Social movements are collective efforts by people to bring about social, economic, political or environmental change.

Important examples

Chipko Movement

A forest conservation movement associated with communities in the Himalayan region. People used non-violent methods to resist tree felling.

Appiko Movement

A forest conservation movement in Karnataka inspired by the Chipko Movement.

Narmada Bachao Andolan

A movement raising concerns about displacement, rehabilitation and environmental effects associated with large dam projects in the Narmada Valley.

Silent Valley Movement

A movement in Kerala focused on protecting the Silent Valley rainforest from a proposed hydroelectric project.

Bhoodan Movement

Initiated by Vinoba Bhave in 1951 to encourage voluntary land donations for redistribution to landless people.

Right to Information Movement

Grassroots campaigns, especially those associated with Rajasthan's Mazdoor Kisan Shakti Sangathan, played an important role in the development of the right to information movement.

Anti-corruption movements

Public campaigns have sought greater transparency, accountability and stronger anti-corruption institutions.

15. Civil Society and Democracy

Civil society includes voluntary associations, community organisations, non-governmental organisations, professional bodies, advocacy groups and other non-state organisations that participate in public life.

Role in democracy

  • Promotes public awareness and civic participation.
  • Raises concerns about government policies.
  • Supports vulnerable and marginalised communities.
  • Monitors public service delivery.
  • Promotes transparency and accountability.
  • Provides research and policy suggestions.
  • Encourages peaceful participation in public affairs.

Challenges

  • Funding transparency and accountability.
  • Political influence and conflicts of interest.
  • Unequal access to decision-makers.
  • Limited resources and organisational capacity.
  • Risk of misinformation or unrepresentative claims.

16. Role of Pressure Groups in Democracy

Positive contributions

  • Represent specialised and marginalised interests.
  • Provide information and expertise to policymakers.
  • Increase citizen participation.
  • Promote government accountability.
  • Highlight neglected public issues.
  • Help develop inclusive public policies.

Possible negative effects

  • Powerful groups may influence policy more than weaker groups.
  • Some groups may promote narrow interests over public welfare.
  • Unethical lobbying may weaken transparency.
  • Frequent disruptive activities may affect public services.
  • Financially powerful groups may gain disproportionate influence.
A healthy democracy requires transparency, equal opportunity for participation, lawful advocacy and accountability of pressure groups.

19. Mains Answer Framework

Question: Examine the role of political parties and pressure groups in strengthening Indian democracy.

Introduction: Political parties and pressure groups are important institutions of representative and participatory democracy.

Role of political parties:

  • Contest elections and form governments.
  • Represent public interests and develop policy programmes.
  • Recruit political leadership.
  • Provide opposition and legislative accountability.

Role of pressure groups:

  • Represent specialised interests.
  • Influence policy through research and lawful advocacy.
  • Mobilise public opinion.
  • Promote transparency and government accountability.

Challenges: Criminalisation, money power, internal party democracy, opaque funding, unequal influence, unethical lobbying and weak accountability.

Way Forward: Transparent political funding, stronger internal democracy, ethical lobbying, disclosure of interests, civic education and effective enforcement of election laws.

Conclusion: Political parties provide representation and government formation, while pressure groups enable continuous public participation. Both are essential when they operate transparently and constitutionally.

  • Electoral Symbol: Symbol allotted to a candidate or reserved for a party – ఎన్నికల గుర్తు.
  • Civil Society: Voluntary public organisations outside the State – పౌర సమాజం.
  • Advocacy: Public support for a cause or policy – హక్కులు లేదా విధానాల కోసం వాదించడం.
