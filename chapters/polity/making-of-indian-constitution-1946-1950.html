1. Background to Constitution-Making | రాజ్యాంగ నిర్మాణానికి నేపథ్యం

India's Constitution was framed during the transition from colonial rule to independence. The demand for a Constituent Assembly had developed over several decades. The idea was publicly advanced by M.N. Roy in 1934; the Indian National Congress formally demanded a Constituent Assembly elected on the basis of adult franchise in 1935. The British government acknowledged the principle in the August Offer of 1940, and the Cripps Proposals of 1942 suggested a constitution-making body after the war, but these proposals were not accepted by Indian political parties.

భారత రాజ్యాంగం ఒక్కరోజులో రూపొందలేదు. స్వపరిపాలన, ప్రజాస్వామ్యం, పౌర హక్కులు, సమాఖ్య వ్యవస్థ వంటి అంశాలపై జరిగిన దీర్ఘకాలిక రాజకీయ చర్చల ఫలితంగా రాజ్యాంగ సభ ఏర్పడింది.

  • Cabinet Mission Plan (1946) provided the framework for setting up the Constituent Assembly.
  • The Assembly was indirectly elected by the Provincial Legislative Assemblies, with representation from princely states to be nominated.
  • The Assembly first met on 9 December 1946.

2. Composition of the Constituent Assembly | రాజ్యాంగ సభ నిర్మాణం

The original strength envisaged under the Cabinet Mission scheme was 389: 292 seats for British Indian provinces, 4 for Chief Commissioners’ provinces and 93 for princely states. Members from the provinces were elected indirectly by the provincial legislatures through proportional representation by means of the single transferable vote. Representatives of princely states were to be nominated.

  • After Partition, the membership of the Assembly for India was reduced to 299.
  • Of these, 229 represented provinces and 70 represented princely states.
  • The Assembly was not elected by universal adult suffrage; it was indirectly elected.
  • It included lawyers, administrators, social reformers, freedom fighters and representatives of different communities and regions, though women and some social groups remained under-represented.

తెలుగు: మొదట రాజ్యాంగ సభకు 389 స్థానాలు ప్రతిపాదించబడ్డాయి. విభజన తర్వాత భారత రాజ్యాంగ సభ సభ్యుల సంఖ్య 299కు తగ్గింది.

3. First Sitting and Key Office-Bearers | తొలి సమావేశం–ముఖ్య పదవులు

  • 9 December 1946: First meeting of the Constituent Assembly in New Delhi.
  • Dr. Sachchidananda Sinha: Temporary Chairman/President of the first sitting.
  • 11 December 1946: Dr. Rajendra Prasad elected permanent President of the Assembly.
  • H.C. Mookherjee: Vice-President of the Assembly.
  • B.N. Rau: Constitutional Adviser; prepared an initial constitutional draft and studied constitutional systems.
  • Sir B.N. Rau was not the Chairman of the Drafting Committee.

రాజ్యాంగ సభ తొలి సమావేశానికి సచ్చిదానంద సిన్హా తాత్కాలిక అధ్యక్షత వహించారు. డా. రాజేంద్ర ప్రసాద్ 11 డిసెంబర్ 1946న శాశ్వత అధ్యక్షుడిగా ఎన్నికయ్యారు.

4. Objectives Resolution | లక్ష్యాల తీర్మానం

Jawaharlal Nehru moved the Objectives Resolution on 13 December 1946. The Assembly adopted it on 22 January 1947. It declared the resolve to establish India as an independent sovereign republic and laid down broad principles that later informed the Preamble.

  • India would be an independent sovereign republic.
  • Power and authority of government would derive from the people.
  • Justice—social, economic and political—would be secured.
  • Equality of status and opportunity and freedom of thought, expression, belief, faith, worship, vocation, association and action were envisaged, subject to law and public morality.
  • Safeguards for minorities, backward and tribal areas were recognised.

Exam link: Objectives Resolution = Nehru; moved 13 December 1946; adopted 22 January 1947; philosophical foundation for the Preamble.

6. Drafting Committee and Dr. B.R. Ambedkar | ముసాయిదా కమిటీ

The Drafting Committee was appointed on 29 August 1947. It had seven members, with Dr. B.R. Ambedkar as Chairman. The Committee worked from the Constitutional Adviser’s draft and the decisions taken by the Assembly and its committees.

  • Dr. B.R. Ambedkar – Chairman.
  • N. Gopalaswami Ayyangar.
  • Alladi Krishnaswami Ayyar.
  • K.M. Munshi.
  • Syed Mohammad Saadulla.
  • B.L. Mitter (later replaced by N. Madhava Rau).
  • D.P. Khaitan (after his death, replaced by T.T. Krishnamachari).

Important distinction: B.N. Rau was Constitutional Adviser; Ambedkar chaired the Drafting Committee. The Constitution was a collective product of the Assembly, its committees, advisers and public debate—not the work of one individual alone.

7. Debates, Amendments and Adoption | చర్చలు–ఆమోదం

The Draft Constitution was introduced in the Constituent Assembly on 4 November 1948 by Dr. Ambedkar. The Assembly debated the draft clause by clause and considered thousands of amendments. The final Constitution was adopted on 26 November 1949.

  • The Assembly held 11 sessions over approximately 2 years, 11 months and 18 days.
  • It sat for 165 days in total; a substantial part of this time was spent considering the Draft Constitution.
  • About 7,635 amendments were tabled and 2,473 were moved, according to commonly cited official summaries.
  • Members deliberated on parliamentary government, federalism, rights, minority safeguards, language, judiciary and emergency powers.

తెలుగు: ముసాయిదా రాజ్యాంగంపై విస్తృత చర్చలు జరిగాయి. అనేక సవరణలు పరిశీలించిన తర్వాత 26 నవంబర్ 1949న రాజ్యాంగాన్ని ఆమోదించారు.

8. Adoption, Signing and Commencement | ఆమోదం–అమలు

  • 26 November 1949: Constitution adopted, enacted and given to the people of India.
  • 24 January 1950: Members signed the Constitution; 284 members signed the final copies.
  • 26 January 1950: Constitution came into force; India became a Republic.

Some provisions came into force immediately on 26 November 1949, including provisions relating to citizenship, elections, provisional Parliament, definitions and commencement. The rest came into force on 26 January 1950.

Why 26 January? The date honoured the declaration of Purna Swaraj adopted by the Congress at its Lahore session in December 1929, with 26 January 1930 observed as Independence Day.

Do not confuse: Adopted – 26 November 1949; signed – 24 January 1950; came into force – 26 January 1950.

9. Sources and Influences of the Constitution | రాజ్యాంగ ప్రభావాలు

The Constitution drew on Indian constitutional experience, freedom-struggle demands and comparative constitutional ideas. Borrowing did not mean copying a foreign Constitution wholesale; provisions were adapted to Indian conditions.

Source / ExperienceImportant features associated with it
Government of India Act, 1935Federal scheme, office of Governor, public service commissions, emergency provisions and administrative details

Exam caution: Fundamental Duties were not in the original Constitution of 1950; they were added by the 42nd Constitutional Amendment in 1976.

10. Salient Features of the Original Constitution | అసలు రాజ్యాంగ లక్షణాలు

At commencement on 26 January 1950, the Constitution had 395 Articles, 22 Parts and 8 Schedules. These are the original commencement figures, not the present structure after amendments.

  • Written and detailed Constitution.
  • Sovereign democratic republic at commencement; the words Socialist and Secular were added to the Preamble in 1976.
  • Parliamentary form of government at Union and State levels.
  • Federal structure with a strong Centre and a distribution of legislative powers.
  • Fundamental Rights enforceable by courts.
  • Directive Principles of State Policy guiding governance and social policy.
  • Independent judiciary and judicial review.
  • Universal adult franchise for elections to the Lok Sabha and State Legislative Assemblies, subject to constitutional provisions.
  • Single citizenship and integrated services.
  • Emergency provisions.

11. Preamble: Original Text and Later Changes | ప్రవేశిక

The Preamble begins with “We, the People of India”, expressing popular sovereignty. It declares India to be a Sovereign Democratic Republic in the original text and promises Justice, Liberty, Equality and Fraternity.

  • 42nd Amendment Act, 1976: Added “Socialist”, “Secular” and “Integrity” (in the phrase “unity and integrity of the Nation”).
  • The Preamble is part of the Constitution, as held by the Supreme Court in the Kesavananda Bharati case (1973).
  • It is an interpretive guide and expresses the Constitution’s basic philosophy; it is not independently enforceable as a source of specific legal rights.

తెలుగు: “We, the People of India” అనే పదాలు రాజ్యాంగ అధికారానికి ప్రజలే మూలమని తెలియజేస్తాయి. Socialist, Secular పదాలు 1976లో 42వ సవరణ ద్వారా చేర్చబడ్డాయి.

12. Fundamental Rights and Directive Principles | హక్కులు–ఆదేశిక సూత్రాలు

Fundamental Rights: Located in Part III (Articles 12–35) in the original constitutional arrangement. They protect individual liberty and equality and are enforceable through courts. Article 32 provides the right to constitutional remedies in the Supreme Court.

Directive Principles of State Policy: Part IV (Articles 36–51). They are not enforceable by courts, but are fundamental in the governance of the country and guide the State in making laws and policies.

  • Fundamental Rights are generally justiciable; Directive Principles are non-justiciable.
  • The Constitution seeks harmony between individual freedoms and social-economic transformation.
  • Fundamental Duties are in Part IVA, inserted by the 42nd Amendment in 1976.

13. Language and National Identity | భాషా అంశం

The Constituent Assembly debated the language question intensely. The Constitution did not declare any language as the “national language”. Under Article 343, Hindi in Devanagari script was designated the official language of the Union, with international form of Indian numerals. English was to continue for official purposes for the initial period specified in the Constitution; Parliament later provided for its continued use through the Official Languages Act, 1963.

  • There is no constitutionally declared national language of India.
  • The Eighth Schedule originally listed 14 languages.
  • The Eighth Schedule now lists 22 languages, after later amendments.
  • Language policy was designed to balance national administration with India's linguistic diversity.

Exam caution: “Official language of the Union” and “national language” are not interchangeable terms.

14. Andhra and Telugu Context | ఆంధ్ర–తెలుగు నేపథ్యం

The language question and federal design were particularly important to Telugu-speaking regions. The demand for a separate Andhra province had a long history. Potti Sriramulu’s fast and death in December 1952 led to the creation of Andhra State on 1 October 1953, with Kurnool as its capital. This occurred after the Constitution came into force and is not part of the original 1950 constitutional commencement.

  • Andhra State was formed in 1953 from Telugu-speaking areas of the former Madras State.
  • Andhra Pradesh was formed on 1 November 1956 by merging Andhra State with the Telugu-speaking Telangana region of the former Hyderabad State, following the States Reorganisation process.
  • The States Reorganisation Commission (Fazl Ali Commission) reported in 1955.

Exam link: Constitution commencement = 26 January 1950; Andhra State = 1 October 1953; Andhra Pradesh formation = 1 November 1956.
