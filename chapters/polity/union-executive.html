1. Introduction | పరిచయం

The Union Executive is dealt with mainly in Part V, Chapter I of the Constitution (Articles 52–78). It consists of the President, Vice-President, Prime Minister, Council of Ministers and Attorney-General of India. India follows a parliamentary system: the President is the constitutional head, while the Prime Minister and Council of Ministers exercise real executive authority and are collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha.

తెలుగు: కేంద్ర కార్యనిర్వాహక వ్యవస్థ ప్రధానంగా Part V, Chapter Iలోని Articles 52–78లో ఉంది. రాష్ట్రపతి, ఉపరాష్ట్రపతి, ప్రధానమంత్రి, మంత్రిమండలి, అటార్నీ జనరల్ ఇందులో భాగాలు. భారతదేశంలో పార్లమెంటరీ విధానం ఉంది. రాష్ట్రపతి రాజ్యాంగపరమైన అధిపతి; ప్రధానమంత్రి, మంత్రిమండలి వాస్తవ కార్యనిర్వాహక అధికారాన్ని వినియోగిస్తారు. మంత్రిమండలి లోక్‌సభకు సమిష్టిగా బాధ్యత వహిస్తుంది.

2. President of India – Articles 52–62 | భారత రాష్ట్రపతి

Constitutional position and election

  • Article 52: There shall be a President of India. Article 53: Union executive power is vested in the President and is exercised in accordance with the Constitution.
  • The President is elected indirectly by an Electoral College consisting of elected members of both Houses of Parliament and elected members of the Legislative Assemblies of States, and of the Union Territories of Delhi and Puducherry. Nominated MPs and members of Legislative Councils do not vote.
  • The election uses proportional representation by means of the single transferable vote and a secret ballot.
  • The value of votes of elected MLAs is calculated with reference to the population and elected Assembly strength under the constitutional formula. The value of each elected MP’s vote is calculated by dividing the total value of votes of elected MLAs by the total number of elected MPs of both Houses.

తెలుగు: రాష్ట్రపతిని ప్రజలు నేరుగా ఎన్నుకోరు. పార్లమెంట్‌లోని ఎన్నికైన సభ్యులు, రాష్ట్రాలు మరియు ఢిల్లీ, పుదుచ్చేరి శాసనసభల ఎన్నికైన సభ్యులు కలిగిన Electoral College ఎన్నుకుంటుంది. నామినేటెడ్ సభ్యులు, శాసన మండలి సభ్యులు ఇందులో పాల్గొనరు.

Qualifications – Article 58

  • Must be a citizen of India.
  • Must have completed 35 years of age.
  • Must be qualified for election as a member of the Lok Sabha.
  • Must not hold an office of profit under the Union, State or specified local/other authorities; constitutional exceptions apply for the President, Vice-President, Governor and Minister.

Nomination requires the prescribed number of proposers and seconders who are electors, and the prescribed security deposit under election law.

Term, resignation and vacancy

  • Article 56: Five-year term, but the President continues until the successor enters office. Resignation is addressed to the Vice-President.
  • Article 57: Eligible for re-election; the Constitution does not prescribe a two-term limit.
  • Article 60: Oath is administered by the Chief Justice of India, or in their absence the senior-most available Supreme Court judge.
  • Article 61: Impeachment for violation of the Constitution. Either House may initiate; notice must be signed by at least one-fourth of the total membership of that House and given at least 14 days in advance. The resolution must be passed by a majority of not less than two-thirds of the total membership of each House.
  • Article 62: Election to fill a vacancy caused by expiry of term must be completed before the term expires. A casual vacancy must be filled within six months; the person elected serves a full five-year term.

3. Powers and functions of the President | రాష్ట్రపతి అధికారాలు

Executive powers

The President appoints the Prime Minister and, on the Prime Minister’s advice, other ministers. The President appoints Governors, the Attorney-General, Comptroller and Auditor-General, and other constitutional authorities in accordance with the Constitution and law. Union administration is formally carried on in the President’s name.

Legislative powers

  • Summons and prorogues Parliament and may dissolve the Lok Sabha.
  • Addresses Parliament and may send messages to either House.
  • Nomination to the Rajya Sabha of 12 persons with special knowledge or practical experience in literature, science, art and social service (Article 80). The former provision for nomination of two Anglo-Indian members to the Lok Sabha ceased after the relevant constitutional arrangement ended.
  • Prior recommendation is required for certain Bills as provided by the Constitution.
  • Article 111: May assent to a Bill, withhold assent, or return a non-Money Bill once for reconsideration. If Parliament passes it again, with or without amendments, the President shall not withhold assent. The President cannot return a Money Bill for reconsideration.

Ordinance power – Article 123

When both Houses are not simultaneously in session and circumstances require immediate action, the President may promulgate an Ordinance. It has the same force as an Act of Parliament but must be laid before both Houses and ceases to operate six weeks after Parliament reassembles, unless earlier disapproved or replaced. It is subject to constitutional limits and judicial review.

Financial powers

Causes the annual financial statement (Union Budget) to be laid before Parliament; Money Bills require the President’s recommendation; certain financial Bills also require recommendation under the Constitution.

Judicial and diplomatic/defence powers

Under Article 72, the President may grant pardons, reprieves, respites or remissions, or suspend, remit or commute sentences in specified cases, including court-martial cases, offences under laws within Union executive power, and all death sentences. The President is Supreme Commander of the Defence Forces, subject to constitutional and legal control. Ambassadors and diplomatic representatives are appointed, and treaties are made in the President’s name, subject to constitutional processes.

తెలుగు: రాష్ట్రపతికి కార్యనిర్వాహక, శాసన, ఆర్థిక, న్యాయ, రక్షణ, దౌత్య అధికారాలు ఉన్నాయి. అయితే పార్లమెంటరీ వ్యవస్థలో సాధారణంగా మంత్రిమండలి సలహా మేరకు వ్యవహరిస్తారు. Ordinance జారీ చేయడం, Billకు ఆమోదం, Article 72 క్షమాభిక్ష అధికారాలు పరీక్షలకు ముఖ్యమైనవి.

4. President and Council of Ministers – Article 74 | రాష్ట్రపతి–మంత్రిమండలి సంబంధం

Article 74 provides for a Council of Ministers with the Prime Minister at the head to aid and advise the President. The President must act in accordance with that advice. The President may require the Council to reconsider its advice once; after reconsideration, the President must act in accordance with the advice tendered.

The advice tendered by Ministers to the President cannot be inquired into by any court. Article 75 provides that ministers hold office during the pleasure of the President, but in the parliamentary system this operates in accordance with the confidence convention and constitutional responsibility to the Lok Sabha.

తెలుగు: Article 74 ప్రకారం ప్రధానమంత్రి నేతృత్వంలోని మంత్రిమండలి రాష్ట్రపతికి సలహా ఇస్తుంది. రాష్ట్రపతి ఒకసారి సలహాను పునఃపరిశీలించమని కోరవచ్చు. పునఃపరిశీలించిన తరువాత వచ్చిన సలహాను అనుసరించాలి.

5. Vice-President – Articles 63–71 | ఉపరాష్ట్రపతి

  • Article 63: There shall be a Vice-President of India.
  • Elected by members of both Houses of Parliament, including nominated members, by proportional representation through the single transferable vote and secret ballot. State Legislative Assemblies do not participate.
  • Qualifications: Citizen of India; at least 35 years; qualified for election as a Rajya Sabha member; must not hold an office of profit, subject to constitutional exceptions.
  • Article 64: Ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha. While acting as President or discharging presidential functions, the Vice-President does not perform the duties of Chairman of the Rajya Sabha.
  • Article 65: Acts as President during a vacancy or discharges presidential functions during absence, illness or other inability.
  • Article 67: Five-year term; resignation addressed to the President. Removal requires a resolution of the Rajya Sabha passed by a majority of all the then members and agreed to by the Lok Sabha; 14 days’ notice is required. This is not called impeachment.
  • Article 71: Disputes regarding the election of President or Vice-President are decided by the Supreme Court; its decision is final.

తెలుగు: ఉపరాష్ట్రపతిని పార్లమెంట్‌లోని రెండు సభల సభ్యులు (ఎన్నికైన, నామినేటెడ్ సభ్యులు) ఎన్నుకుంటారు. రాష్ట్ర శాసనసభలు పాల్గొనవు. ఆయన/ఆమె రాజ్యసభకు పదవీరీత్యా ఛైర్మన్. రాష్ట్రపతి పదవి ఖాళీ అయినప్పుడు లేదా రాష్ట్రపతి విధులు నిర్వహించలేని సందర్భంలో రాష్ట్రపతి బాధ్యతలు నిర్వహిస్తారు.

6. Prime Minister and Council of Ministers – Articles 74–75 | ప్రధానమంత్రి, మంత్రిమండలి

The Prime Minister is appointed by the President. Other ministers are appointed by the President on the advice of the Prime Minister. In practice, the leader who can command majority support in the Lok Sabha is appointed Prime Minister.

  • Article 75(1): Ministers are appointed by the President on the advice of the Prime Minister.
  • Article 75(1A): Total number of ministers, including the Prime Minister, shall not exceed 15% of the total strength of the Lok Sabha.
  • Article 75(1B): A member disqualified under the Tenth Schedule for defection is also disqualified from being appointed a minister for the period specified by the Constitution.
  • Article 75(2): Ministers hold office during the pleasure of the President.
  • Article 75(3): Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha.
  • Article 75(4): Oaths of office and secrecy are administered by the President.
  • Article 75(5): A minister who is not a member of either House of Parliament for six consecutive months ceases to be a minister.

Types of ministers

Common parliamentary practice distinguishes Cabinet Ministers, Ministers of State (with independent charge or attached to a Cabinet Minister), and Ministers of State. The Constitution refers to the Council of Ministers and does not itself set out this full political classification.

Role of the Prime Minister

  • Heads the Council of Ministers and allocates/coordinates ministerial work.
  • Communicates decisions of the Council to the President and furnishes information under Article 78.
  • Acts as the principal link between the President and the Council of Ministers.
  • Leads the government in Parliament and coordinates major policy.

తెలుగు: ప్రధానమంత్రి మంత్రిమండలి నాయకుడు. మంత్రుల నియామకానికి రాష్ట్రపతికి సలహా ఇస్తారు. మంత్రిమండలి లోక్‌సభకు సమిష్టిగా బాధ్యత వహిస్తుంది. మంత్రుల మొత్తం సంఖ్య లోక్‌సభ మొత్తం సభ్యుల సంఖ్యలో 15% మించరాదు.

7. Attorney-General of India – Article 76 | అటార్నీ జనరల్

The Attorney-General is the highest law officer of the Union. The President appoints a person qualified to be appointed a Judge of the Supreme Court. The Attorney-General advises the Government of India on legal matters and performs other legal duties assigned by the President.

  • Has the right to speak and take part in proceedings of either House of Parliament, a joint sitting and any parliamentary committee of which named as a member, but has no right to vote.
  • Holds office during the pleasure of the President; the Constitution does not prescribe a fixed term.
  • Is entitled to such remuneration as the President may determine.

తెలుగు: అటార్నీ జనరల్ కేంద్ర ప్రభుత్వానికి అత్యున్నత న్యాయ సలహాదారు. సుప్రీంకోర్టు న్యాయమూర్తిగా నియమించడానికి అర్హత కలిగిన వ్యక్తిని రాష్ట్రపతి నియమిస్తారు. పార్లమెంట్‌లో మాట్లాడవచ్చు, పాల్గొనవచ్చు; కానీ ఓటు హక్కు లేదు.

8. Parliamentary vs Presidential system | పార్లమెంటరీ–అధ్యక్ష విధానాలు

Parliamentary system (India)Presidential system (classic US model)
Executive drawn from legislatureExecutive institutionally separate from legislature
Collective responsibility to lower HousePresident has a fixed constitutional term; not dependent on legislative confidence in the same way
Nominal head and real political executive are distinctPresident is both head of State and head of government
Government may fall on loss of confidenceLegislature ordinarily cannot remove President merely by a no-confidence vote
Close executive-legislature relationshipStronger separation of powers

India’s parliamentary model is influenced by the British system but operates under a written, supreme Constitution with judicial review and federal features.

10. Quick revision | త్వరిత పునశ్చరణ

  • President: Articles 52–62; elected indirectly by elected MPs and elected MLAs of States, Delhi and Puducherry.
  • President’s minimum age: 35; term: 5 years; eligible for re-election.
  • Impeachment: Article 61; violation of the Constitution; special majority in each House.
  • Vice-President: Articles 63–71; elected by all members of both Houses of Parliament, including nominated members.
  • Vice-President is ex-officio Chairman of Rajya Sabha; removal is not called impeachment.
  • PM and Council of Ministers: Articles 74–75; collective responsibility to Lok Sabha.
  • Ministerial strength: maximum 15% of total Lok Sabha strength.
  • Non-MP minister: must become a member of either House within six months.
  • Attorney-General: Article 76; may speak in Parliament but cannot vote.
  • Ordinance: Article 123; generally ceases six weeks after Parliament reassembles unless earlier disapproved or replaced.
  • President’s pardon power: Article 72; Prime Minister’s duties to President: Article 78.

Q1. The Union Executive is primarily covered in which Part?

Answer: Part V. Part V covers the Union; Articles 52–78 deal with the Union Executive.

Q2. Article 52 provides for:

Answer: A President of India. Article 52 states that there shall be a President of India.

Q3. Union executive power is vested in the President under:

Answer: Article 53. Article 53 vests Union executive power in the President.

Q4. The President of India is elected by:

Answer: An Electoral College. The President is elected indirectly by the constitutional Electoral College.

Q5. Which members do NOT vote in the Presidential election?

Answer: Nominated MPs. Nominated members of Parliament are excluded from the Presidential Electoral College.

Q6. The Vice-President participates in the Presidential Electoral College as:

Answer: Not by virtue of being Vice-President. The Vice-President does not vote merely by holding that office; only if separately an eligible elected MP.

Q7. Presidential election uses:

Answer: Proportional representation by single transferable vote. It uses proportional representation by single transferable vote and secret ballot.

Q8. Minimum age for President is:

Answer: 35 years. Article 58 requires at least 35 years.

Q9. The President's normal term is:

Answer: 5 years. Article 56 provides a five-year term.

Q10. The President submits resignation to:

Answer: Vice-President. The President addresses resignation to the Vice-President.

Q11. The President's oath is administered by:

Answer: Chief Justice of India. The Chief Justice of India administers the oath, or the senior-most available Supreme Court judge in their absence.

Q12. Impeachment of the President is under:

Answer: Article 61. Article 61 lays down the impeachment procedure.

Q13. The stated ground for impeachment of the President is:

Answer: Violation of the Constitution. Article 61 specifies violation of the Constitution.

Q14. A Presidential impeachment resolution in each House requires:

Answer: Not less than two-thirds of total membership. Article 61 requires a majority of not less than two-thirds of the total membership of each House.

Q15. A casual vacancy in the office of President must be filled within:

Answer: Six months. Article 62 requires a casual vacancy to be filled within six months.

Q16. The President may return for reconsideration:

Answer: A non-Money Bill. Article 111 permits return of a non-Money Bill once.

Q17. The President's ordinance power is under:

Answer: Article 123. Article 123 concerns ordinances by the President.

Q18. An Ordinance generally ceases six weeks after:

Answer: Parliament reassembles. It ceases six weeks after Parliament reassembles, unless earlier disapproved or replaced.

Q19. President's power to grant pardon is under:

Answer: Article 72. Article 72 confers specified clemency powers on the President.

Q20. President's power under Article 72 includes:

Answer: All death sentences. The President may grant clemency in all cases of death sentence.

Q21. The President is the Supreme Commander of:

Answer: Defence Forces. The President is the Supreme Commander of the Defence Forces, subject to constitutional control.

Q22. Article 74 provides for:

Answer: Council of Ministers to aid and advise President. Article 74 establishes the aid-and-advice framework.

Q23. The President may ask the Council of Ministers to reconsider advice:

Answer: Once. The President may require reconsideration once.

Q24. The Vice-President is provided for in:

Answer: Article 63. Article 63 provides for a Vice-President.

Q25. The Vice-President is elected by:

Answer: Members of both Houses of Parliament, including nominated members. The Electoral College comprises all members of both Houses of Parliament.

Q26. State Legislative Assemblies participate in the election of:

Answer: President. State MLAs vote in the Presidential election, not the Vice-Presidential election.

Q27. The Vice-President is ex-officio:

Answer: Chairman of Rajya Sabha. Article 64 makes the Vice-President ex-officio Chairman of Rajya Sabha.

Q28. Removal of Vice-President requires a resolution initiated in:

Answer: Rajya Sabha. The resolution must originate in Rajya Sabha and be agreed to by Lok Sabha.

Q29. Removal of the Vice-President is:

Answer: A resolution procedure, not termed impeachment. The Constitution does not describe this process as impeachment.

Q30. Election disputes concerning President and Vice-President are decided by:

Answer: Supreme Court. Article 71 assigns these election disputes to the Supreme Court.

Q31. The Prime Minister is appointed by:

Answer: President. The President appoints the Prime Minister.

Q32. Other Union Ministers are appointed by the President on the advice of:

Answer: Prime Minister. Article 75 provides appointment on the Prime Minister's advice.

Q33. The Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to:

Answer: Lok Sabha. Article 75(3) states collective responsibility to the House of the People.

Q34. The maximum size of the Union Council of Ministers is:

Answer: 15% of Lok Sabha strength. Article 75(1A) caps it at 15% of total Lok Sabha strength.

Q35. A person who is not an MP can remain a minister for up to:

Answer: Six consecutive months. Under Article 75(5), they must become a member of either House within six consecutive months.

Q36. Oaths of office and secrecy to Union Ministers are administered by:

Answer: President. Article 75(4) provides for the President to administer the oaths.

Q37. The Prime Minister's duty to communicate decisions to the President is under:

Answer: Article 78. Article 78 specifies the Prime Minister's duties towards the President.

Q38. The Attorney-General of India is provided for in:

Answer: Article 76. Article 76 provides for the Attorney-General.

Q39. The Attorney-General must be qualified to be appointed as:

Answer: Supreme Court judge. Article 76 requires qualification for appointment as a Supreme Court judge.

Q40. The Attorney-General can vote in Parliament:

Answer: No. The Attorney-General may speak and participate but has no right to vote.

Q41. The President's power to seek advisory opinion of Supreme Court is under:

Answer: Article 143. Article 143 provides the advisory jurisdiction reference.

Q42. The classic Indian system of government is:

Answer: Parliamentary. India follows a parliamentary system.

Q43. In India, the real political executive is generally:

Answer: Prime Minister and Council of Ministers. The PM and Council of Ministers exercise real executive authority under parliamentary conventions and the Constitution.

Q44. The case Shamsher Singh v. State of Punjab is associated with:

Answer: Constitutional heads ordinarily acting on ministerial advice. The case explains the President/Governor as constitutional heads acting on aid and advice.

Q45. Which Article concerns the annual financial statement being laid before Parliament?

Answer: Article 112. Article 112 provides for the annual financial statement.

Q46. Which Article concerns the President's message and special address to Parliament?

Answer: Article 87. Article 87 concerns the President's special address.

Q47. The President nominates how many members to the Rajya Sabha?

Answer: 12. Article 80 provides for nomination of 12 persons with specified special knowledge or practical experience.

Q48. The President can dissolve:

Answer: Lok Sabha. The President may dissolve the Lok Sabha.

Q49. The Vice-President's normal term is:

Answer: 5 years. Article 67 provides a five-year term.

Q50. The President continues in office after five years until:

Answer: The successor enters office. Article 56 ensures continuity until the successor enters office.

Q51. Which statement about a minister who is disqualified under the Tenth Schedule is correct?

Answer: Constitution imposes a specified disqualification period. Article 75(1B) restricts appointment as minister for the constitutionally specified period.
