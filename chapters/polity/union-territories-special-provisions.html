Overview | అవలోకనం

India has a federal system with a strong Union. Union Territories are administered under the constitutional framework by the President through an Administrator, subject to special provisions for certain territories. The Constitution also provides special arrangements for certain States to address historical, cultural, geographical and administrative circumstances.

1. Union Territories – Meaning

A Union Territory is an administrative unit governed under the constitutional framework of the Union. Unlike States, Union Territories do not have the same constitutional status and legislative structure.

కేంద్రపాలిత ప్రాంతం అనేది కేంద్ర ప్రభుత్వ రాజ్యాంగ పరిపాలనా పరిధిలో ఉండే ప్రాంతం. రాష్ట్రాల మాదిరిగా అన్ని కేంద్రపాలిత ప్రాంతాలకు ఒకే విధమైన శాసనసభ లేదా మంత్రిమండలి ఉండదు.

Important features

  • They are administered by the President under Article 239.
  • The President may appoint an Administrator for a Union Territory.
  • The Administrator may be designated as Lieutenant Governor.
  • Parliament has legislative power over Union Territories under the Constitution.
  • Some Union Territories have legislatures and councils of ministers under special constitutional or statutory arrangements.
  • The powers and structure differ among Union Territories.

3. Administration – Article 239

Article 239 provides that every Union Territory shall be administered by the President acting, to such extent as the President thinks fit, through an Administrator appointed by the President.

Important points

  • The Administrator is appointed by the President.
  • The Administrator acts subject to the directions issued by the President.
  • An Administrator may be designated as Lieutenant Governor.
  • The constitutional arrangement differs depending on the Union Territory.
  • Parliament may make laws for Union Territories.
The Administrator is not necessarily a Governor. A Governor is the constitutional head of a State, while an Administrator represents the Union administration in a Union Territory.

5. Creation and Reorganisation of Union Territories

Parliament has extensive powers to reorganise States and Union Territories under the constitutional framework.

Article 3

Parliament may by law:

  • Form a new State.
  • Increase or diminish the area of a State.
  • Alter the boundaries of a State.
  • Alter the name of a State.
  • Separate territory from a State or unite territories in the manner provided by the Constitution.

The procedure under Article 3 includes reference of the relevant Bill by the President to the legislature of the affected State for expressing its views within the specified period. Parliament is not bound by those views.

Article 4

Laws made under Articles 2 and 3 may contain supplemental, incidental and consequential provisions. Such laws are not deemed constitutional amendments under Article 368 merely because they amend the First or Fourth Schedule.

6. Special Provisions for Delhi – Article 239AA

The 69th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1991 inserted Article 239AA and gave Delhi the status of National Capital Territory.

  • Delhi is called the National Capital Territory of Delhi.
  • The Administrator is designated as the Lieutenant Governor.
  • Delhi has a Legislative Assembly.
  • The Assembly may make laws on matters in the State List and Concurrent List, subject to constitutional exclusions and limitations.
  • Public order, police and land are excluded from the legislative competence of the Delhi Legislative Assembly.
  • Parliament retains power to make laws for Delhi.
  • A Council of Ministers headed by the Chief Minister aids and advises the Lieutenant Governor in matters within its competence, subject to constitutional provisions.

Important exclusions

Article 239AB

Article 239AB provides for action in case of failure of constitutional machinery in the National Capital Territory of Delhi.

7. Lieutenant Governor and Council of Ministers in Delhi

Delhi has a special constitutional arrangement involving the Lieutenant Governor, the Chief Minister and the Council of Ministers.

Key points

  • The Lieutenant Governor is appointed by the President.
  • The Chief Minister is appointed by the President.
  • Other Ministers are appointed by the President on the advice of the Chief Minister.
  • The Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the Legislative Assembly.
  • The Council aids and advises the Lieutenant Governor on matters within the Assembly's legislative competence, subject to constitutional provisions.
  • Where a matter falls within the constitutional category requiring reference, the Lieutenant Governor may refer it to the President.

Important Supreme Court developments

Government of NCT of Delhi v. Union of India (2018)

The Constitution Bench emphasised representative government, constitutional morality, collaborative federalism and the need for the Lieutenant Governor to act within the constitutional framework.

Government of NCT of Delhi v. Union of India (2023)

The Supreme Court considered the allocation of control over services in Delhi. Parliament subsequently enacted the Government of National Capital Territory of Delhi (Amendment) Act, 2023, establishing a statutory framework for services-related arrangements.

Delhi's services framework has statutory developments. Do not treat the 2018 or 2023 judgment in isolation without considering subsequent legislation and current legal position.

8. Puducherry – Article 239A

Article 239A empowers Parliament to create a legislature, a Council of Ministers or both for certain Union Territories, including Puducherry under the applicable constitutional and statutory framework.

Important points

  • Puducherry has an elected Legislative Assembly.
  • It has a Council of Ministers headed by the Chief Minister.
  • The Lieutenant Governor is appointed by the President.
  • The Government of Union Territories Act, 1963 provides an important statutory framework.
  • Parliament retains legislative power over Union Territories.
Article 239A is associated with legislatures and Councils of Ministers for certain Union Territories. Article 239AA contains the special constitutional provisions for Delhi.

9. Jammu and Kashmir Reorganisation

The Jammu and Kashmir Reorganisation Act, 2019 reorganised the former State of Jammu and Kashmir into two Union Territories with effect from 31 October 2019.

  • Union Territory of Jammu and Kashmir, with a Legislative Assembly.
  • Union Territory of Ladakh, without a Legislative Assembly.
  • The former State's special constitutional arrangement under Article 370 underwent major change in 2019.
  • The Supreme Court considered the constitutional issues in the 2023 Article 370 judgment.

Supreme Court judgment – 2023

In December 2023, the Supreme Court upheld the constitutional validity of the abrogation-related measures and directed that steps be taken for restoration of statehood to Jammu and Kashmir at the earliest, while also directing that Legislative Assembly elections be held by the specified deadline.

For current administrative and election status, verify the latest official notifications. The historical fact of reorganisation in 2019 is distinct from later political and administrative developments.

10. Article 240 – Regulations by the President

Article 240 empowers the President to make regulations for the peace, progress and good government of certain specified Union Territories.

Important features

  • Regulations may be made for specified Union Territories listed in the Article.
  • A regulation may amend or repeal an applicable law relating to that Union Territory.
  • Such regulations have the same force and effect as an Act of Parliament applicable to the territory, subject to the constitutional provisions.
  • The power operates within the scope of Article 240.

11. Article 241 – High Courts for Union Territories

Article 241 deals with High Courts for Union Territories.

  • Parliament may by law constitute a High Court for a Union Territory.
  • Parliament may declare an existing court to be a High Court for a Union Territory.
  • Parliament may extend or exclude the jurisdiction of a High Court to or from a Union Territory as provided by law.
  • The constitutional framework ensures judicial administration in Union Territories.

12. Fifth Schedule – Scheduled Areas

The Fifth Schedule deals with the administration and control of Scheduled Areas and Scheduled Tribes in States other than the tribal areas covered by the Sixth Schedule.

Important provisions

  • The President may declare an area to be a Scheduled Area.
  • The Governor has special responsibilities in relation to Scheduled Areas under the constitutional framework.
  • Each State having Scheduled Areas establishes a Tribes Advisory Council, subject to the Schedule's provisions.
  • The Governor may make regulations for the peace and good government of Scheduled Areas.
  • Regulations may relate to restrictions on transfer of tribal land, regulation of allotment of land and regulation of money-lending.
  • Such regulations require the President's assent.

Tribes Advisory Council

  • It advises on matters relating to the welfare and advancement of Scheduled Tribes in the State.
  • It generally consists of not more than 20 members.
  • As nearly as may be, three-fourths of its members are representatives of Scheduled Tribes in the State Legislative Assembly.

13. Sixth Schedule – Tribal Areas

The Sixth Schedule provides for administration of tribal areas in Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura and Mizoram.

Autonomous District Councils

  • Autonomous District Councils may be established in the tribal areas covered by the Schedule.
  • Autonomous Regional Councils may also be established in specified circumstances.
  • District Councils generally consist of not more than 30 members, with up to four members nominated by the Governor and the remainder elected, subject to the Schedule.
  • District Councils may have legislative, judicial, administrative and financial powers within the limits of the Schedule.

Major powers

  • Make laws on specified subjects such as land, forests other than reserved forests, shifting cultivation, village administration and inheritance, subject to the Schedule.
  • Establish village councils or courts for specified matters.
  • Manage certain local resources.
  • Collect certain taxes and fees.
  • Manage primary schools, dispensaries, markets and other local institutions within their jurisdiction.
Fifth Schedule and Sixth Schedule are different. The Fifth Schedule mainly concerns Scheduled Areas, while the Sixth Schedule provides autonomous council arrangements in specified tribal areas of four northeastern States.

16. Article 371 – Maharashtra and Gujarat

Article 371 provides for special responsibilities of the Governors of Maharashtra and Gujarat in specified matters.

Important areas

  • Establishment of separate development boards for specified regions.
  • Equitable allocation of funds for development expenditure.
  • Fair arrangements for technical education and vocational training opportunities.
  • Equitable opportunities in State services for people from different regions.

17. Article 371A – Nagaland

Article 371A provides special constitutional safeguards for Nagaland.

Parliamentary Acts may not apply to specified matters unless the Nagaland Legislative Assembly so decides.

  • Religious or social practices of the Nagas.
  • Naga customary law and procedure.
  • Administration of civil and criminal justice involving decisions according to Naga customary law.
  • Ownership and transfer of land and its resources.

Article 371A also contains provisions relating to the Governor's special responsibility for law and order and the Tuensang district arrangements.

Article 371A is particularly important for protection of customary practices and land-related rights in Nagaland.

18. Article 371B – Assam

Article 371B provides for a committee of the Assam Legislative Assembly consisting of members elected from specified tribal areas and other members as provided by the President's order.

  • Provides a special institutional arrangement for specified tribal areas.
  • Supports representation of tribal interests in the State Legislature.
  • Relates to the constitutional framework for Assam's tribal areas.

19. Article 371C – Manipur

Article 371C provides special arrangements for the Hill Areas of Manipur.

  • A Hill Areas Committee of the Legislative Assembly is provided for.
  • The Governor has special responsibilities concerning the administration of Hill Areas.
  • The Governor submits reports to the President regarding the administration of Hill Areas as provided by the Article.
  • The Union Government may give directions to the State Government in the specified constitutional framework.

20. Article 371D – Andhra Pradesh and Telangana

Article 371D provides special arrangements to secure equitable opportunities and facilities for people in public employment and education in the State or States concerned.

Major features

  • Provides for equitable opportunities in public employment and education.
  • Allows arrangements relating to local cadres and local areas.
  • Provides for local-area based admissions to specified educational institutions or courses.
  • Provides for an Administrative Tribunal under the constitutional framework.
Article 371D has been applied in the context of Andhra Pradesh and Telangana after the State reorganisation. Its operation must be read with the Andhra Pradesh Reorganisation Act, applicable Presidential Orders and subsequent legal developments.

21. Article 371E – Andhra Pradesh

Article 371E empowers Parliament to provide by law for the establishment of a University in the State of Andhra Pradesh.

This provision enabled the establishment of a Central University through parliamentary legislation. It does not itself establish a university automatically.

22. Article 371F – Sikkim

Article 371F contains special provisions relating to Sikkim following its integration with India as a State in 1975.

Important points

  • Provides special constitutional arrangements for Sikkim.
  • Contains provisions relating to the Legislative Assembly.
  • Provides for continuity and adaptation of existing laws.
  • Allows special arrangements for representation of different sections of the population.
  • Contains provisions relating to the Governor's responsibilities in specified matters.

23. Article 371G – Mizoram

Article 371G provides special safeguards for Mizoram.

Parliamentary laws relating to specified matters do not apply to Mizoram unless the Legislative Assembly of Mizoram so decides.

Protected matters

  • Religious or social practices of the Mizos.
  • Mizo customary law and procedure.
  • Administration of civil and criminal justice involving decisions according to Mizo customary law.
  • Ownership and transfer of land.

Article 371G also contains a provision regarding the minimum strength of the Mizoram Legislative Assembly.

24. Article 371H – Arunachal Pradesh

Article 371H provides special provisions for Arunachal Pradesh.

  • The Governor has special responsibility with respect to law and order.
  • The Governor exercises individual judgment after consulting the Council of Ministers, subject to the constitutional provision.
  • The President may issue directions regarding the discharge of this responsibility.
  • The Legislative Assembly of Arunachal Pradesh must consist of not less than 30 members.

25. Article 371I – Goa

Article 371I provides a special provision for Goa.

  • The Legislative Assembly of Goa shall consist of not less than 30 members.
  • This provision is important for understanding the constitutional arrangement of Goa.

26. Article 371J – Karnataka

Article 371J provides special provisions for the Hyderabad-Karnataka region, now known as Kalyana Karnataka.

Major features

  • Establishment of a development board for the region.
  • Equitable allocation of funds for developmental expenditure.
  • Reservation in educational and vocational training opportunities.
  • Reservation in State government posts and services for eligible local persons, subject to constitutional and legal provisions.

29. Important Constitutional Concepts

Asymmetric Federalism

Asymmetric federalism refers to a federal arrangement in which different States or regions enjoy different constitutional or administrative arrangements based on their specific circumstances.

Why special provisions exist

  • Historical circumstances.
  • Protection of tribal communities.
  • Preservation of customary laws and traditions.
  • Regional development needs.
  • Geographical and administrative difficulties.
  • Protection of local identity and interests.

Cooperative Federalism

Cooperative federalism refers to cooperation between the Union and States in governance, policy-making and development.

Regional autonomy

Regional autonomy allows specified regions to exercise powers in local matters within the constitutional framework.

30. Mains Answer Framework

Question: Explain the significance of special constitutional provisions for certain States in India.

Introduction: The Constitution provides special arrangements for certain States and regions to address their unique historical, cultural, geographical and administrative circumstances.

Major provisions:

  • Articles 371 to 371J provide special arrangements for specified States.
  • Fifth Schedule provides safeguards for Scheduled Areas.
  • Sixth Schedule provides autonomous council arrangements in specified northeastern tribal areas.
  • Article 371A and 371G protect specified customary practices and land-related interests.
  • Article 371D addresses equitable opportunities in education and public employment.

Significance:

  • Protects cultural and customary identity.
  • Promotes inclusive development.
  • Recognises regional diversity.
  • Strengthens participation in governance.
  • Supports national integration through constitutional accommodation.

Challenges: Implementation gaps, regional inequalities, administrative complexity and tensions between autonomy and uniformity.

Conclusion: Special provisions reflect India's commitment to unity through diversity and constitutionally accommodating regional needs.
