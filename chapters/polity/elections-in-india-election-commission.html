Overview | అవలోకనం

Elections are a fundamental mechanism of representative democracy. They enable citizens to choose their representatives and hold governments accountable.

Exam Focus: Articles 324–329, Election Commission of India, electoral systems, Representation of the People Acts, qualifications, disqualifications, Model Code of Conduct, EVM and VVPAT.

1. Meaning and Importance of Elections

An election is a formal process through which eligible voters choose representatives for public offices or decide specified questions through voting.

Importance of elections

  • Enable citizens to participate in governance.
  • Provide legitimacy to representative governments.
  • Ensure political accountability.
  • Allow peaceful transfer of political power.
  • Provide opportunities for political competition.
  • Encourage public debate on policies and development.
  • Strengthen democratic institutions.

Features of democratic elections

  • Universal adult suffrage, subject to constitutional and legal qualifications.
  • Regular elections.
  • Free and fair electoral competition.
  • Secret ballot.
  • Independent election administration.
  • Equal value of votes within the applicable electoral system.
  • Transparent counting and declaration of results.

3. Election Commission of India

The Election Commission of India is a constitutional body established under Article 324.

Article 324

Article 324 vests the superintendence, direction and control of the preparation of electoral rolls and the conduct of elections to Parliament, State Legislatures, and the offices of President and Vice-President in the Election Commission.

Composition

  • Chief Election Commissioner (CEC).
  • Other Election Commissioners, if appointed.
  • The President determines the number of Election Commissioners, subject to the constitutional framework.
  • The Commission may function as a multi-member body.

Historical development

  • The Election Commission was established on 25 January 1950.
  • Initially, it functioned as a single-member body.
  • It became a multi-member body in 1989 for a brief period.
  • It again became a multi-member body in 1993.
  • National Voters' Day is observed on 25 January.

4. Appointment of Election Commissioners

Article 324 provides the constitutional framework for appointment of the Chief Election Commissioner and other Election Commissioners.

Current statutory framework

The Chief Election Commissioner and Other Election Commissioners (Appointment, Conditions of Service and Term of Office) Act, 2023 provides a statutory framework for appointments.

  • The President appoints the CEC and other Election Commissioners.
  • The selection process under the 2023 Act involves a Search Committee and a Selection Committee.
  • The Search Committee is headed by the Union Minister of Law and Justice and includes two other members not below the rank of Secretary to the Government of India.
  • The Selection Committee consists of the Prime Minister, a Union Cabinet Minister nominated by the Prime Minister, and the Leader of Opposition in the Lok Sabha or the leader of the largest opposition party in the Lok Sabha, as applicable.
  • The Selection Committee recommends names for appointment to the President.
For examination purposes, distinguish the constitutional provision in Article 324 from the statutory appointment procedure under the 2023 Act.

5. Tenure and Service Conditions

Under the 2023 Act, the CEC and other Election Commissioners hold office for six years from the date they assume office or until attaining the age of 65 years, whichever is earlier, subject to the Act.

  • They are not eligible for reappointment.
  • A person who has held office as an Election Commissioner is not eligible for appointment as Chief Election Commissioner under the statutory framework.
  • The salary and service conditions are linked to those of a Supreme Court Judge under the Act.
  • Service conditions cannot be varied to their disadvantage after appointment, subject to the law.

6. Removal of Election Commissioners

Chief Election Commissioner

The Chief Election Commissioner can be removed from office in the same manner and on the same grounds as a Judge of the Supreme Court.

  • Removal is based on proved misbehaviour or incapacity.
  • The removal process requires a special parliamentary procedure.
  • The President issues the removal order after the required parliamentary address is passed.

Other Election Commissioners

Other Election Commissioners cannot be removed from office except on the recommendation of the Chief Election Commissioner.

The constitutional protection of the CEC is stronger than the protection provided to other Election Commissioners.

7. Functions of the Election Commission

  • Superintendence, direction and control of elections.
  • Preparation and revision of electoral rolls.
  • Conduct of elections to Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha.
  • Conduct of elections to State Legislative Assemblies and Legislative Councils.
  • Conduct of elections to the offices of President and Vice-President.
  • Recognition of political parties under applicable law.
  • Allotment of election symbols under the applicable legal framework.
  • Monitoring election expenditure under applicable rules.
  • Implementation and monitoring of the Model Code of Conduct.
  • Appointment of election observers and monitoring election arrangements.
  • Advising the President and Governors on specified disqualification matters under the Constitution.
The Election Commission of India does not conduct Panchayat and Municipal elections. These are conducted by State Election Commissions under Articles 243K and 243ZA.

9. Universal Adult Suffrage – Article 326

Article 326 provides that elections to the House of the People and Legislative Assemblies of States shall be based on adult suffrage, subject to constitutional and statutory qualifications.

Voting age

  • Originally, the voting age was 21 years.
  • The 61st Constitutional Amendment Act, 1988 reduced it to 18 years.
  • The amendment came into effect in 1989.
  • Every Indian citizen who satisfies the legal requirements can register as a voter.
Voting age = 18 years. The 61st Constitutional Amendment is important for competitive examinations.

Grounds for disqualification from registration

Article 326 mentions non-residence, unsoundness of mind, crime or corrupt or illegal practice, subject to laws made by the appropriate Legislature.

10. Article 325 – One General Electoral Roll

Article 325 provides that there shall be one general electoral roll for every territorial constituency for elections to Parliament or State Legislatures.

  • No person is ineligible for inclusion in an electoral roll on grounds only of religion.
  • No person is ineligible on grounds only of race.
  • No person is ineligible on grounds only of caste.
  • No person is ineligible on grounds only of sex.
  • No person can claim a special electoral roll merely on those grounds.

11. Electoral Rolls and Voter Registration

An electoral roll is the official list of eligible registered voters for a constituency.

Important points

  • Registration is based on eligibility and the applicable electoral laws.
  • A voter should ordinarily be registered in the constituency where they are ordinarily resident.
  • A person cannot be registered in more than one constituency at the same time.
  • Electoral rolls are periodically revised.
  • Eligible citizens may apply for inclusion, correction or deletion through prescribed procedures.
  • Election authorities conduct verification and update the rolls according to law.

Common voter services

  • New voter registration.
  • Correction of personal details.
  • Change of address.
  • Replacement or correction of voter identification details.
  • Checking name in the electoral roll.
Possession of a Voter ID card alone does not guarantee the right to vote if the person's name is not present in the electoral roll for the relevant election.

12. Electoral Systems in India

Different elections in India use different electoral systems.

First-Past-the-Post System (FPTP)

Under FPTP, the candidate who secures the highest number of valid votes in a constituency is declared elected. The candidate does not necessarily need more than 50% of the votes.

Used for elections to:

  • Lok Sabha.
  • State Legislative Assemblies.
  • Many other directly elected constituency-based offices under applicable laws.

Proportional Representation by Single Transferable Vote (PR-STV)

Under the Single Transferable Vote system, voters rank candidates in order of preference. Votes may be transferred according to the prescribed counting rules.

Used in specified elections such as:

  • Election of the President.
  • Election of the Vice-President.
  • Election of Rajya Sabha members by elected members of State Legislative Assemblies.
  • Election of members of State Legislative Councils from specified electorates.

13. Election of the President and Vice-President

President

The President is elected by an electoral college consisting of:

  • Elected members of both Houses of Parliament.
  • Elected members of the Legislative Assemblies of States.
  • Elected members of the Legislative Assemblies of the Union Territories of Delhi and Puducherry.

The election uses proportional representation by means of the single transferable vote and secret ballot.

Vice-President

The Vice-President is elected by an electoral college consisting of members of both Houses of Parliament, including elected and nominated members.

The election uses proportional representation by means of the single transferable vote and secret ballot.

Important difference: Nominated MPs do not vote in the Presidential election, but they do participate in the Vice-Presidential election.

14. Representation of the People Act, 1950

The Representation of the People Act, 1950 primarily deals with the allocation of seats and preparation of electoral rolls.

Major provisions

  • Allocation of seats in the House of the People.
  • Allocation of seats in State Legislative Assemblies.
  • Delimitation-related provisions within the statutory framework.
  • Qualifications of voters.
  • Preparation and revision of electoral rolls.
  • Registration of electors.
  • Constituency-related electoral roll arrangements.

15. Representation of the People Act, 1951

The Representation of the People Act, 1951 deals with the conduct of elections and related matters.

Major provisions

  • Qualifications and disqualifications for membership of Parliament and State Legislatures.
  • Notification of elections.
  • Nomination of candidates.
  • Scrutiny of nominations.
  • Withdrawal of candidature.
  • Election campaign and corrupt practices.
  • Election expenses.
  • Election disputes and election petitions.
  • Disqualification on specified grounds.
  • Offences relating to elections.
RPA 1950 = Electoral rolls and allocation of seats. RPA 1951 = Conduct of elections, qualifications, disqualifications and election disputes.

16. Qualifications for Membership of Parliament

Article 84 provides qualifications for membership of Parliament.

Lok Sabha

  • Must be a citizen of India.
  • Must be at least 25 years of age.
  • Must possess other qualifications prescribed by Parliament by law.

Rajya Sabha

  • Must be a citizen of India.
  • Must be at least 30 years of age.
  • Must possess other qualifications prescribed by Parliament by law.

State Legislative Assembly

Article 173 generally requires a person to be a citizen of India and at least 25 years of age, along with other prescribed qualifications.

State Legislative Council

A person must generally be at least 30 years of age, along with other prescribed qualifications.

17. Disqualifications for Membership

Article 102 deals with disqualifications for membership of Parliament. Article 191 deals with disqualifications for membership of State Legislatures.

Major grounds

  • Holding an office of profit under the Union or State Government, except an office declared by law not to disqualify.
  • Being of unsound mind and so declared by a competent court.
  • Being an undischarged insolvent.
  • Not being a citizen of India or voluntarily acquiring citizenship of a foreign State, or acknowledging allegiance to a foreign State.
  • Disqualification under a law made by Parliament.
  • Disqualification under the Tenth Schedule relating to defection.

Disqualification under the Representation of the People Act, 1951

Specified convictions, corrupt practices and other statutory grounds may result in disqualification under the Act.

18. Delimitation of Constituencies

Delimitation means redrawing the boundaries of electoral constituencies to reflect changes in population and ensure appropriate representation, subject to constitutional provisions.

Important points

  • Delimitation is carried out under parliamentary laws.
  • A Delimitation Commission may be established by law.
  • The Commission's orders have legal force according to the relevant statute.
  • Constituency boundaries and reserved constituencies may be determined through the delimitation process.
  • Delimitation is distinct from ordinary electoral roll revision.

Constitutional background

Articles 82 and 170 deal with readjustment of seats and territorial constituencies after each Census, subject to constitutional amendments and statutory provisions.

The constitutional freeze and readjustment provisions relating to delimitation have changed through amendments. For current seat-allocation and future delimitation questions, always consider the applicable constitutional text and current law.

19. Reserved Constituencies

Reserved constituencies are designed to ensure representation of Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes in elected legislatures.

  • Articles 330 and 332 provide for reservation of seats for SCs and STs in the Lok Sabha and State Legislative Assemblies respectively.
  • Reservation is based on population and constitutional provisions.
  • Reserved constituencies are identified through delimitation.
  • In a reserved constituency, only candidates belonging to the specified reserved category can contest, but all eligible voters in the constituency may vote.

20. Model Code of Conduct

The Model Code of Conduct (MCC) is a set of guidelines for political parties and candidates during elections.

Purpose

  • Promote fair electoral competition.
  • Prevent misuse of official machinery.
  • Maintain a peaceful election environment.
  • Prevent appeals based on prohibited grounds.
  • Promote responsible campaigning.
  • Protect voters from intimidation and inducement.

Important restrictions and expectations

  • Ministers should not use official machinery for election campaigning.
  • Public resources should not be used for partisan advantage.
  • Parties and candidates should avoid communal appeals and hate speech.
  • Voters should not be bribed or intimidated.
  • Campaign activities should follow the prescribed legal and electoral rules.
The Model Code of Conduct is enforced by the Election Commission through its constitutional powers and applicable laws. It is not itself a single parliamentary statute.

21. Election Campaign and Expenditure

Election campaigns enable candidates and parties to present their programmes and seek public support.

Important features

  • Campaigning must comply with the Representation of the People Act, 1951 and other applicable laws.
  • Candidates must follow prescribed expenditure limits and reporting requirements.
  • Election expenditure accounts must be maintained and submitted as required by law.
  • Paid news, bribery, intimidation and corrupt practices are subject to legal restrictions.
  • Campaigning must stop during the statutory silence period applicable to the election.

Corrupt practices

Section 123 of the Representation of the People Act, 1951 identifies corrupt practices, including bribery, undue influence and specified appeals or conduct prohibited by law.

22. Electronic Voting Machines (EVM)

Electronic Voting Machines are used in Indian elections to record votes electronically.

Important points

  • EVMs are used to record votes in elections where their use is authorised.
  • They help avoid invalid votes caused by certain ballot-paper marking errors.
  • They support efficient counting.
  • They are used with prescribed security and verification procedures.
  • Election officials follow safeguards for storage, transportation, commissioning and counting.

23. VVPAT

Voter Verifiable Paper Audit Trail (VVPAT) is a system that provides a paper-based verification record of the vote cast through an EVM.

How it works

  1. The voter presses the button corresponding to the chosen candidate on the EVM.
  2. The VVPAT system prints a paper slip showing the candidate's serial number, name and symbol.
  3. The slip is visible through a transparent window for a short period.
  4. The slip then falls into a sealed drop box.
  5. VVPAT slips are counted or verified according to applicable Election Commission and judicial directions.
VVPAT improves voter verification and provides a paper audit trail.

24. NOTA – None of the Above

NOTA allows a voter to indicate that they do not wish to vote for any of the listed candidates.

Important points

  • NOTA was introduced in Indian elections following the Supreme Court judgment in People's Union for Civil Liberties v. Union of India (2013).
  • It is available as an option on the EVM or ballot paper, as applicable.
  • It allows voters to express rejection of all contesting candidates.
  • Under the existing general rule for Lok Sabha and State Assembly elections, NOTA votes do not result in automatic cancellation of the election or a fresh poll.
  • The candidate with the highest number of valid votes among candidates is declared elected according to the applicable rules, even if NOTA receives more votes than any individual candidate.

25. Election Petitions and Judicial Review

Article 329 restricts judicial interference in electoral matters in the manner specified by the Constitution.

Important principles

  • Constitutional election disputes are generally raised through election petitions under the applicable law.
  • The Representation of the People Act, 1951 provides the statutory framework for election petitions.
  • Election petitions relating to Parliamentary and State Legislature elections are presented to the High Court under the Act.
  • The Supreme Court may hear appeals from High Court decisions under the statutory framework.
Article 329(b) provides that an election to either House of Parliament or to a House of a State Legislature shall not be called in question except by an election petition presented in the manner provided by law.

26. Political Parties and Election Symbols

Political parties play a central role in representative democracy.

Recognition of political parties

  • The Election Commission recognises political parties under the Election Symbols (Reservation and Allotment) Order, 1968.
  • Recognition as a National or State party depends on prescribed criteria.
  • Recognition criteria include electoral performance and other conditions specified in the Order.
  • Reserved symbols are allotted according to the applicable rules.

Election symbols

  • Symbols help voters identify candidates and political parties.
  • Recognised parties may receive reserved symbols.
  • Independent candidates and unrecognised parties may be allotted free symbols according to the rules.

27. Anti-Defection Law and Elections

The Tenth Schedule deals with disqualification on the ground of defection.

  • Added by the 52nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1985.
  • Provides for disqualification of members of Parliament and State Legislatures on specified grounds of defection.
  • The presiding officer of the concerned House decides disqualification petitions under the Tenth Schedule, subject to judicial review.
  • The 91st Constitutional Amendment Act, 2003 changed the merger-related requirements and removed the one-third split exception.
The anti-defection law is relevant to political representation and legislative stability, although it is not the same as the constitutional framework governing the conduct of elections.

28. Important Supreme Court Judgments

Mohinder Singh Gill v. Chief Election Commissioner (1978)

The Supreme Court recognised the broad scope of the Election Commission's powers under Article 324, particularly where legislation does not make specific provision.

Indira Nehru Gandhi v. Raj Narain (1975)

The case is important for the constitutional principles relating to free and fair elections and judicial review.

People's Union for Civil Liberties v. Union of India (2013)

The Supreme Court directed the introduction of the NOTA option to protect the voter's right to secrecy when expressing rejection of all candidates.

Lily Thomas v. Union of India (2013)

The Supreme Court invalidated the protection that had allowed convicted legislators to continue in office during the pendency of an appeal under the then-existing Section 8(4) of the Representation of the People Act, 1951.

Association for Democratic Reforms v. Union of India (2002)

The Supreme Court upheld voters' right to know relevant information about candidates, including criminal antecedents, assets and educational qualifications, under the constitutional framework.

Electoral Bonds judgment (2024)

In Association for Democratic Reforms v. Union of India (2024), the Supreme Court declared the Electoral Bond Scheme unconstitutional, emphasising the importance of voters' right to information about political funding.

29. Challenges in Indian Elections

  • Money power and excessive campaign expenditure.
  • Criminalisation of politics.
  • Misuse of government machinery.
  • Voter intimidation and inducement.
  • Disinformation and misleading digital campaigns.
  • Communal and divisive campaigning.
  • Low participation in some areas.
  • Concerns about transparency and political funding.
  • Challenges in maintaining accurate electoral rolls.
  • Accessibility barriers for elderly and persons with disabilities.

30. Electoral Reforms

  • Strengthen transparency in political funding.
  • Improve disclosure of candidate information.
  • Enforce expenditure limits effectively.
  • Strengthen election monitoring and enforcement.
  • Improve accessibility for voters with disabilities.
  • Improve accuracy of electoral rolls.
  • Promote voter awareness and participation.
  • Address disinformation during election campaigns.
  • Strengthen institutional independence and accountability.
  • Ensure timely investigation of electoral offences.
  • Improve transparency in political party functioning.

32. Mains Answer Framework

Question: Examine the role of the Election Commission of India in ensuring free and fair elections.

Introduction: Article 324 establishes the constitutional framework for the Election Commission of India and vests it with superintendence, direction and control over specified elections.

Major functions:

  • Preparation and revision of electoral rolls.
  • Conduct of elections to Parliament and State Legislatures.
  • Conduct of Presidential and Vice-Presidential elections.
  • Monitoring election expenditure and campaign conduct.
  • Implementation of the Model Code of Conduct.
  • Recognition of political parties and allotment of symbols under applicable rules.

Challenges: Money power, criminalisation of politics, misinformation, political interference allegations and enforcement difficulties.

Way Forward: Strengthen institutional independence, improve transparency, enforce electoral laws, improve voter awareness and strengthen campaign monitoring.

Conclusion: An independent and effective Election Commission is essential for protecting electoral integrity and strengthening Indian democracy.
