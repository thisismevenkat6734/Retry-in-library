1. Introduction – పరిచయం

Part XVII of the Indian Constitution deals with Official Language. It contains Articles 343 to 351 and is divided into four chapters.

భారత రాజ్యాంగంలోని Part XVII అధికార భాషలకు సంబంధించినది. ఇందులో Articles 343 నుంచి 351 వరకు ఉంటాయి.

Four Chapters of Part XVII

  1. Chapter I – Language of the Union (Articles 343–344)
  2. Chapter II – Regional Languages (Article 345)
  3. Chapter III – Language of the Supreme Court, High Courts and Acts (Articles 346–348)
  4. Chapter IV – Special Directives (Articles 350–351)

Exam Point: Part XVII = Articles 343–351

Article 343 – Official Language of the Union

  • Hindi in Devanagari script is the official language of the Union.
  • The form of numerals to be used for official purposes of the Union is the international form of Indian numerals.
  • For the first fifteen years from the commencement of the Constitution, English continued for the official purposes of the Union and for Parliamentary business, subject to the constitutional provisions.

Article 343 ప్రకారం దేవనాగరి లిపిలోని హిందీ కేంద్ర ప్రభుత్వ అధికార భాష. కేంద్ర అధికారిక అవసరాల కోసం అంతర్జాతీయ రూపంలోని భారతీయ అంకెలు ఉపయోగించాలి.

Article 344 – Commission and Parliamentary Committee

The President was required to constitute an Official Language Commission at the expiration of five years and again at the expiration of ten years from the commencement of the Constitution. The Commission makes recommendations regarding progressive use of Hindi, restrictions on English and other language matters. A Committee of Parliament examines the recommendations.

రాజ్యాంగం ప్రారంభమైన ఐదు సంవత్సరాల తరువాత, మళ్లీ పది సంవత్సరాల తరువాత అధికార భాషా కమిషన్ ఏర్పాటు చేయాలని ఈ ఆర్టికల్ పేర్కొంది.

Article 345 – Official Language of a State

Subject to Articles 346 and 347, a State Legislature may adopt one or more languages in use in the State, or Hindi, as the official language or languages of that State. Until such provision is made, English may continue for official purposes.

రాష్ట్ర శాసనసభ రాష్ట్రంలో వాడుకలో ఉన్న ఒకటి లేదా అంతకంటే ఎక్కువ భాషలను లేదా హిందీని రాష్ట్ర అధికార భాషగా స్వీకరించవచ్చు.

Article 346 – Communication between States and Union

The language authorised for use in the Union for official purposes is generally used for communication between one State and another and between a State and the Union. States may agree to use Hindi for communication between themselves.

Article 347 – Recognition of a Language

If a substantial proportion of the population of a State desires recognition of a language spoken by them, the President may direct that the language be officially recognised throughout the State or in a part of it for specified purposes.

Article 348 – Language of Courts and Legislation

  • Proceedings in the Supreme Court and High Courts are generally in English, subject to constitutional and statutory provisions.
  • Authoritative texts of Bills, Acts, Ordinances, rules and regulations are generally in English, subject to Article 348.
  • The Governor, with the previous consent of the President, may authorise Hindi or another official language of the State for proceedings in that State's High Court, subject to the constitutional exception for judgments, decrees and orders.

Article 349 – Special Procedure

During the first fifteen years from commencement, a Bill or amendment relating to certain language matters under Article 348 required the President's previous sanction and consideration of the Official Language Commission's recommendations and the Parliamentary Committee's report.

Article 350 – Language of Grievance Representations

Every person is entitled to submit a representation for redress of a grievance to an officer or authority of the Union or a State in any language used in the Union or the State, as applicable.

Article 350A – Mother-Tongue Instruction

Every State and local authority should endeavour to provide adequate facilities for instruction in the mother tongue at the primary stage of education to children belonging to linguistic minority groups.

Article 350B – Special Officer for Linguistic Minorities

The President appoints a Special Officer for linguistic minorities. The officer investigates matters relating to safeguards for linguistic minorities and reports to the President. These reports are laid before Parliament and sent to the concerned State Governments.

Article 351 – Development of Hindi

The Union has a duty to promote the spread of Hindi and develop it so that it may serve as a medium of expression for all elements of India's composite culture. It also refers to drawing vocabulary primarily from Sanskrit and secondarily from other languages.

హిందీ భాష అభివృద్ధి, వ్యాప్తి మరియు భారతదేశ సమ్మిళిత సంస్కృతికి అనువైన అభివ్యక్తి మాధ్యమంగా తీర్చిదిద్దడం కేంద్ర ప్రభుత్వ బాధ్యతగా Article 351 పేర్కొంటుంది.

3. Hindi and English – హిందీ, ఇంగ్లీష్

Important facts

  • Hindi in Devanagari script is the official language of the Union.
  • The Constitution does not declare Hindi as the national language of India.
  • English continued beyond the initial fifteen-year period under the Official Languages Act, 1963, as amended.
  • English continues to be used for Union official purposes and Parliamentary business under the applicable legal framework.
  • There is no single national language declared by the Constitution.

భారతదేశానికి రాజ్యాంగం ద్వారా ఒకే జాతీయ భాష ప్రకటించబడలేదు. హిందీ కేంద్ర అధికార భాష. ఇంగ్లీష్ కూడా చట్టప్రకారం కేంద్ర అధికారిక అవసరాల్లో కొనసాగుతోంది.

Official Languages Act, 1963

The Act provides for the continued use of English, in addition to Hindi, for official purposes of the Union and for the transaction of business in Parliament after the initial constitutional fifteen-year period. Its provisions were amended in 1967.

It also contains provisions concerning the language of communications between the Union and non-Hindi-speaking States.

4. State Official Languages – రాష్ట్ర అధికార భాషలు

Under Article 345, State Legislatures can adopt one or more languages in use in the State, or Hindi, as the State's official language or languages, subject to the Constitution.

ప్రతి రాష్ట్రం తన పరిస్థితులకు అనుగుణంగా అధికార భాషను చట్టం ద్వారా నిర్ణయించవచ్చు.

Telugu and Official Language

Telugu is an Eighth Schedule language. Andhra Pradesh and Telangana have adopted Telugu as their official language under their respective State laws. The use of English and other languages may continue as provided by applicable law and administrative rules.

Scheduled Languages

The Eighth Schedule contains 22 languages. Recognition in the Eighth Schedule is distinct from declaring a language the national language.

5. Important Commissions and Institutions

Official Language Commission

Constitutional basis: Article 344. It makes recommendations on progressive use of Hindi and related official-language matters.

Committee of Parliament on Official Language

Article 344 provides for a Parliamentary Committee to examine the Commission's recommendations and report its opinion to the President.

Special Officer for Linguistic Minorities

Constitutional basis: Article 350B. The officer investigates safeguards for linguistic minorities and reports to the President.

Official Languages Act, 1963

It provides the statutory framework for continued use of English along with Hindi for specified Union official purposes and Parliamentary business.
