Chapter Overview | అధ్యాయ పరిచయం

Emergency Provisions are special constitutional arrangements that allow the Union Government to take extraordinary measures during serious national, state or financial crises.

భారతదేశంలో తీవ్రమైన జాతీయ భద్రతా సంక్షోభం, రాష్ట్రంలో రాజ్యాంగ యంత్రాంగం విఫలమవడం లేదా ఆర్థిక స్థిరత్వానికి ముప్పు ఏర్పడినప్పుడు రాజ్యాంగం ప్రత్యేక అధికారాలను కల్పిస్తుంది.

Constitutional location: Part XVIII – Articles 352 to 360.

Learning Objectives

  • Understand the three types of Emergency.
  • Learn the constitutional procedure and parliamentary approval.
  • Understand the effect on Fundamental Rights and Centre-State relations.
  • Study the 44th Constitutional Amendment safeguards.
  • Understand Article 356 and the S.R. Bommai judgment.

1. Types of Emergency | అత్యవసర పరిస్థితుల రకాలు

The Constitution of India provides three major emergency arrangements.

TypeArticleSituationCommon name
National Emergency352War, external aggression or armed rebellionNational Emergency
State Emergency356Failure of constitutional machinery in a StatePresident's Rule
Financial Emergency360Threat to financial stability or credit of India or any partFinancial Emergency
Exam Alert: Article 355 is not a separate type of Emergency. It describes the Union's constitutional duty to protect States and ensure that State Governments are carried on according to the Constitution.

Why are Emergency Provisions necessary?

  • To protect India's sovereignty, unity and security.
  • To respond to serious threats to constitutional governance.
  • To protect financial stability and credit.
  • To enable coordinated action during exceptional circumstances.

తెలుగు: దేశ సమగ్రత, భద్రత, రాజ్యాంగ పరిపాలన మరియు ఆర్థిక స్థిరత్వాన్ని కాపాడేందుకు అత్యవసర నిబంధనలు ఉపయోగపడతాయి. అయితే ఈ అధికారాలు ప్రజాస్వామ్య హక్కులను ప్రభావితం చేయగలవు కాబట్టి రాజ్యాంగ పరిమితులు, పార్లమెంటరీ నియంత్రణ, న్యాయ సమీక్ష ముఖ్యమైనవి.

2. National Emergency – Article 352

Article 352 empowers the President to proclaim a National Emergency when the security of India or any part of its territory is threatened by:

  1. War: యుద్ధం.
  2. External Aggression: విదేశీ దాడి లేదా దురాక్రమణ.
  3. Armed Rebellion: సాయుధ తిరుగుబాటు.
Important: The 44th Constitutional Amendment replaced the expression “internal disturbance” with “armed rebellion” in Article 352. This made the ground for National Emergency more specific.

Who declares it?

  • The President of India issues the Proclamation.
  • The President acts on the written decision of the Union Cabinet.
  • The written decision must be communicated to the President.
  • Emergency may be declared for the whole of India or only a specified part of its territory.
  • It may also be proclaimed before the actual occurrence of war, external aggression or armed rebellion if there is imminent danger.

Parliamentary Approval

StageRule
Initial approvalBoth Houses of Parliament must approve within one month.
Majority requiredSpecial majority in each House.
Special majority meaningMajority of the total membership of that House AND not less than two-thirds of members present and voting.
Duration after approvalContinues for six months at a time.
ExtensionCan be extended every six months through parliamentary approval by the same special majority.
RevocationPresident may revoke it by a subsequent Proclamation.

Special situations in Parliament

  • If the Lok Sabha is dissolved when the Proclamation is issued, and the Rajya Sabha approves it, the Proclamation remains in force until 30 days after the first sitting of the newly constituted Lok Sabha, unless the new Lok Sabha approves it earlier.
  • If the Lok Sabha disapproves the Proclamation or its continuance, the President must revoke it.
  • At least one-tenth of the total members of the Lok Sabha may give written notice of intention to move a resolution disapproving the Emergency. A special sitting must be held within 14 days of receipt of the notice by the Speaker or President, as applicable.

Important distinction

Approval of a National Emergency requires a special majority. Do not confuse this with the approval requirements for President's Rule and Financial Emergency, which are different.

3. Effects of National Emergency – Articles 353 and 354

Article 353 – Effect of Proclamation

  • Executive power: The Union can give directions to States regarding the manner in which their executive power is to be exercised.
  • Legislative power: Parliament can make laws on matters that are not normally within the Union List, including matters in the State List, subject to constitutional provisions.
  • These powers may also extend to a State outside the area where the Emergency is in operation when the security of India is threatened by activities connected with the affected area, as provided in Article 353.

తెలుగు: జాతీయ అత్యవసర పరిస్థితిలో కేంద్ర ప్రభుత్వ అధికార పరిధి విస్తరిస్తుంది. రాష్ట్ర ప్రభుత్వాలకు కేంద్రం ఆదేశాలు ఇవ్వగలదు. పార్లమెంట్ రాష్ట్ర జాబితాలోని అంశాలపై కూడా చట్టాలు చేయగలదు.

Article 354 – Revenue Distribution

  • The President may modify the application of provisions relating to distribution of revenues under Articles 268 to 279 while a National Emergency is in operation.
  • Such an order cannot extend beyond the end of the financial year in which the Emergency ceases to operate.
  • The order must be laid before both Houses of Parliament.

Effect on State Legislature

National Emergency does not automatically dissolve State Legislative Assemblies. Parliament may legislate on State List matters during the Emergency under the constitutional scheme.

4. Fundamental Rights – Articles 358 and 359

This is one of the most important areas for competitive examinations.

Article 358 – Suspension of Article 19

  • Article 358 applies only when a National Emergency is declared on the ground of war or external aggression.
  • It does not apply when the Emergency is declared only on the ground of armed rebellion.
  • During the qualifying Emergency, Article 19 does not restrict the State's power in the manner specified by Article 358.
  • After the Emergency ends, a law protected under Article 358 ceases to have effect to the extent of its inconsistency, subject to the constitutional protection for things already done or omitted to be done.
  • After the 44th Amendment, the law must contain a recital that it is related to the Proclamation of Emergency to receive the protection under Article 358.

Article 359 – Suspension of Enforcement of Certain Fundamental Rights

  • The President may issue an order suspending the right to move courts for enforcement of specified Fundamental Rights during the Emergency.
  • The order may apply to the whole or a specified part of India.
  • The order must be laid before Parliament.
  • Articles 20 and 21 cannot be suspended through an order under Article 359.
  • Article 359 concerns the enforcement of specified rights, not a general cancellation of all Fundamental Rights.

5. President's Rule – Articles 355, 356 and 357

Article 355 – Duty of the Union

It is the duty of the Union to:

  1. Protect every State against external aggression and internal disturbance.
  2. Ensure that the government of every State is carried on in accordance with the Constitution.

Article 355 is a constitutional duty of the Union. It is not itself a proclamation of President's Rule.

Article 356 – Failure of Constitutional Machinery

If the President, on receipt of a report from the Governor or otherwise, is satisfied that the government of a State cannot be carried on in accordance with the Constitution, a Proclamation may be issued under Article 356.

Possible consequences

  • The President may assume functions of the State Government and powers vested in the Governor or other State executive authorities, subject to constitutional limitations.
  • The powers of the State Legislature may be declared exercisable by or under the authority of Parliament.
  • Necessary consequential provisions may be made to give effect to the Proclamation.
  • The President cannot assume powers vested in a High Court or suspend constitutional provisions relating to High Courts.

Parliamentary Approval and Duration

PointConstitutional rule
Initial approvalBoth Houses must approve within two months.
MajoritySimple majority of members present and voting.
Duration after approvalSix months.
ExtensionEvery six months, with parliamentary approval.
General maximumThree years, subject to special constitutional conditions for extension beyond one year.

Extension beyond one year

President's Rule cannot ordinarily continue beyond one year unless both of the following conditions are satisfied:

  1. A Proclamation of National Emergency is in operation in the whole of India or in the whole or any part of the State concerned; and
  2. The Election Commission certifies that continuation of President's Rule is necessary because of difficulties in holding general elections to the State Legislative Assembly.

Even where these conditions are met, parliamentary approval is required for each further six-month period, subject to the overall constitutional limit of three years.

Article 357 – Exercise of Legislative Powers

  • Parliament may confer the State Legislature's law-making powers on the President.
  • The President may authorise another authority to exercise those powers, subject to the Constitution.
  • Parliament may authorise expenditure from the Consolidated Fund of the State when the Lok Sabha is not in session, subject to constitutional provisions.

Article 365 – Failure to Comply with Union Directions

If a State fails to comply with or give effect to directions given by the Union under the Constitution, the President may consider that a situation has arisen in which the State Government cannot be carried on in accordance with the Constitution. Article 365 does not make President's Rule automatic; the constitutional conditions under Article 356 must still be satisfied.

6. S.R. Bommai Case (1994)

Case: S.R. Bommai v. Union of India (1994)

This landmark Supreme Court judgment placed important constitutional limits on the use of Article 356.

Key principles

  1. Judicial Review: A Proclamation under Article 356 is subject to judicial review.
  2. Relevant Material: The President's satisfaction must be based on relevant material.
  3. Floor Test: Where a government's majority is in doubt, the floor of the Legislative Assembly is generally the proper place to test it.
  4. Federalism: Article 356 must not be used merely for political reasons or because a different party is in power at the Centre.
  5. Exceptional Power: President's Rule is an extraordinary constitutional power and must be used sparingly.
  6. Assembly Dissolution: The Assembly should not be irreversibly dissolved before parliamentary approval of the Proclamation under Article 356(3).
Exam Point: S.R. Bommai (1994) is strongly associated with judicial review of Article 356, federalism and the floor test.

తెలుగు: ఒక రాష్ట్ర ప్రభుత్వానికి మెజారిటీ ఉందా లేదా అనే సందేహం ఉంటే గవర్నర్ వ్యక్తిగత అంచనా కంటే శాసనసభలో Floor Test నిర్వహించడం ముఖ్యమైన పద్ధతి. కేంద్ర ప్రభుత్వం రాజకీయ కారణాలతో Article 356ను దుర్వినియోగం చేయకూడదని సుప్రీంకోర్టు స్పష్టం చేసింది.

7. Financial Emergency – Article 360

Article 360 deals with a situation where the financial stability or credit of India or any part of its territory is threatened.

Procedure

  • The President may proclaim a Financial Emergency.
  • The Proclamation must be approved by both Houses of Parliament within two months.
  • Approval requires a simple majority of members present and voting.
  • If approved, it continues until revoked by the President. The Constitution does not prescribe a periodic six-month renewal requirement for Article 360.

Effects of Financial Emergency

  • The Union may give directions to States to observe specified canons of financial propriety.
  • Directions may include reduction of salaries and allowances of persons serving the State.
  • The President may issue directions requiring reservation for consideration of certain State Money Bills and other Financial Bills after they are passed by the State Legislature.
  • The President may direct reduction of salaries and allowances of persons serving the Union, including Judges of the Supreme Court and High Courts.
Important Fact: No Financial Emergency under Article 360 has been proclaimed in India so far.

తెలుగు: దేశ ఆర్థిక స్థిరత్వం లేదా క్రెడిట్‌కు ముప్పు ఏర్పడితే Article 360 కింద Financial Emergency ప్రకటించవచ్చు. ఈ సమయంలో రాష్ట్రాల ఆర్థిక వ్యవహారాలపై కేంద్రం ఆదేశాలు ఇవ్వగలదు.

9. Historical National Emergencies in India

India has proclaimed National Emergency on three occasions.

YearBackgroundGround
1962Chinese attack on IndiaExternal aggression
1971War with PakistanExternal aggression
1975Internal political crisis during the periodInternal disturbance (the expression used before the 44th Amendment)

Emergency of 1975

  • Proclaimed on 25 June 1975.
  • It was based on the then constitutional ground of internal disturbance.
  • It continued until March 1977.
  • The experience led to strong debate on civil liberties, democratic institutions and safeguards against misuse of power.
  • The 44th Constitutional Amendment later introduced important safeguards.
Do not confuse the 1975 ground of “internal disturbance” with the present Article 352 ground of “armed rebellion”.

11. Mains Answer Framework

Question: “Emergency provisions are necessary for national security, but their misuse can threaten federalism and democracy.” Discuss.

Introduction: Part XVIII of the Indian Constitution (Articles 352–360) provides extraordinary powers to address threats to national security, constitutional governance and financial stability.

Importance:

  • Protects sovereignty and territorial integrity.
  • Enables coordinated national response to grave threats.
  • Provides a constitutional mechanism for breakdown of State governance.
  • Protects financial stability during severe crises.

Concerns:

  • Centralisation of power can weaken federalism.
  • Misuse may affect civil liberties and democratic institutions.
  • Political misuse of Article 356 may undermine elected State Governments.
  • Excessive restrictions may weaken accountability.

Safeguards:

  • 44th Constitutional Amendment safeguards.
  • Parliamentary approval and special majority for Article 352.
  • Judicial review under the principles in S.R. Bommai.
  • Floor test to establish legislative majority.
  • Constitutional limits on duration and extension of President's Rule.

Conclusion: Emergency provisions should remain exceptional constitutional instruments. Their careful use, parliamentary accountability, judicial review and respect for fundamental rights are essential to preserve Indian democracy and federalism.

Practice Mains Questions

  1. Explain the grounds and procedure for proclamation of National Emergency under Article 352.
  2. Distinguish between Articles 358 and 359 of the Constitution.
  3. Discuss the safeguards introduced by the 44th Constitutional Amendment.
  4. Explain the constitutional limitations on the use of Article 356.
  5. Examine the significance of the S.R. Bommai judgment in protecting federalism.
  6. Discuss the effects of Financial Emergency under Article 360.
