1. Introduction | పరిచయం

Directive Principles of State Policy (DPSP) are contained in Part IV, Articles 36–51. They lay down goals and guidelines for the State to establish social and economic democracy and build a welfare state. They are not enforceable by courts, but Article 37 declares them fundamental in the governance of the country.

రాజ్య విధాన ఆదేశిక సూత్రాలు Part IV, Articles 36–51లో ఉన్నాయి. సామాజిక, ఆర్థిక ప్రజాస్వామ్యాన్ని సాధించి సంక్షేమ రాజ్యాన్ని నిర్మించేందుకు ప్రభుత్వానికి మార్గదర్శకాలు ఇస్తాయి. వీటిని నేరుగా కోర్టులో అమలు చేయించలేము; అయినప్పటికీ Article 37 ప్రకారం దేశ పాలనలో ఇవి మౌలికమైనవి.

Fundamental Duties are in Part IVA, Article 51A. They remind citizens of responsibilities towards the Constitution, the nation, society and the environment. They are generally non-justiciable by themselves, though laws may give effect to them.

ప్రాథమిక విధులు Part IVAలోని Article 51Aలో ఉన్నాయి. రాజ్యాంగం, దేశం, సమాజం, పర్యావరణం పట్ల పౌరుల బాధ్యతలను గుర్తుచేస్తాయి. ఇవి స్వతహాగా సాధారణంగా కోర్టు ద్వారా నేరుగా అమలు చేయించలేనివి; అయితే వీటికి అనుగుణంగా చట్టాలు చేయవచ్చు.

2. Constitutional background and purpose | రాజ్యాంగ నేపథ్యం

  • DPSPs were inspired substantially by the Irish Constitution, which in turn reflected elements of the Spanish constitutional tradition.
  • The idea was to supplement political democracy with social and economic democracy.
  • Fundamental Rights mainly protect individual freedoms and are enforceable; DPSPs guide the State in policy-making and are not directly enforceable.
  • Fundamental Duties were added later, influenced by the idea of civic responsibilities found in other constitutions, including the former USSR Constitution.

తెలుగు: DPSPలకు ఐర్లాండ్ రాజ్యాంగం ప్రధాన ప్రేరణ. రాజకీయ ప్రజాస్వామ్యంతో పాటు సామాజిక, ఆర్థిక ప్రజాస్వామ్యాన్ని సాధించడమే లక్ష్యం. Fundamental Rights వ్యక్తి హక్కులను రక్షిస్తే, DPSPలు ప్రభుత్వ విధానాలకు దిశానిర్దేశం చేస్తాయి.

3. Article 36 and Article 37 | Articles 36–37

Article 36: In Part IV, “State” has the same meaning as in Part III, unless the context otherwise requires.

Article 37: DPSPs are not enforceable by any court, but the principles laid down are nevertheless fundamental in the governance of the country. It is the duty of the State to apply these principles in making laws.

తెలుగు: Article 36లో State అనే పదానికి సాధారణంగా Part IIIలో ఉన్న అర్థమే వర్తిస్తుంది. Article 37 ప్రకారం DPSPలను కోర్టులు నేరుగా అమలు చేయించలేవు; అయినా చట్టాలు రూపొందించేటప్పుడు ప్రభుత్వం వాటిని అనుసరించాలి.

4. Classification of DPSPs | ఆదేశిక సూత్రాల వర్గీకరణ

The Constitution does not formally divide DPSPs into categories. For study and exam purposes, they are commonly grouped into three broad themes:

A. Socialist principles | సామ్యవాద సూత్రాలు

  • Article 38 – Promote welfare of the people and minimise inequalities.
  • Article 39 – Adequate means of livelihood; distribution of material resources for common good; prevent concentration of wealth; equal pay for equal work; protect workers and children.
  • Article 39A – Equal justice and free legal aid.
  • Article 41 – Right to work, education and public assistance within the State’s economic capacity and development.
  • Article 42 – Just and humane conditions of work and maternity relief.
  • Article 43 – Living wage and decent standard of life for workers.
  • Article 43A – Participation of workers in management of industries.
  • Article 47 – Raise nutrition, standard of living and public health.

B. Gandhian principles | గాంధేయ సూత్రాలు

  • Article 40 – Organise village panchayats and endow them with powers.
  • Article 43 – Promote cottage industries in rural areas.
  • Article 43B – Promote voluntary formation, autonomous functioning, democratic control and professional management of co-operative societies.
  • Article 46 – Promote educational and economic interests of weaker sections, especially SCs and STs, and protect them from social injustice and exploitation.
  • Article 47 – Prohibit intoxicating drinks and drugs injurious to health, except for medicinal purposes as constitutionally understood.
  • Article 48 – Organise agriculture and animal husbandry on modern and scientific lines; preserve and improve breeds and prohibit slaughter of cows, calves and other milch and draught cattle.

C. Liberal-intellectual principles | ఉదార–మేధో సూత్రాలు

  • Article 44 – Endeavour to secure a Uniform Civil Code for citizens throughout India.
  • Article 45 – Early childhood care and education for all children until they complete six years.
  • Article 48A – Protect and improve the environment and safeguard forests and wildlife.
  • Article 49 – Protect monuments and places/objects of national importance.
  • Article 50 – Separate the judiciary from the executive in the public services of the State.
  • Article 51 – Promote international peace and security, just relations, respect for international law and settlement of disputes by arbitration.

Exam caution: These are conventional academic groupings, not categories expressly created by the Constitution. Some provisions can reasonably fit more than one theme.

తెలుగు: రాజ్యాంగం DPSPలను అధికారికంగా మూడు వర్గాలుగా విభజించలేదు. చదువుకోడానికి సామ్యవాద, గాంధేయ, ఉదార–మేధో సూత్రాలుగా వర్గీకరిస్తారు. ఇది అధ్యయన సౌలభ్యం కోసం మాత్రమే.

5. Important Articles – detailed notes | ముఖ్యమైన ఆర్టికల్స్

Article 38 – Social order

The State shall strive to promote welfare by securing a social order informed by social, economic and political justice. Clause (2), added by the 44th Amendment, directs the State to minimise inequalities in income, status, facilities and opportunities.

Article 39 – Principles of policy

Includes adequate livelihood for men and women equally; distribution of community resources to subserve the common good; prevention of concentration of wealth; equal pay for equal work for men and women; protection of workers’ health and strength; and opportunities for healthy development of children.

Article 39A – Equal justice and free legal aid

Added by the 42nd Amendment. The State must ensure that opportunities for securing justice are not denied because of economic or other disabilities and provide free legal aid through suitable legislation or schemes.

Article 40 – Village panchayats

Organise village panchayats and endow them with powers and authority to function as units of self-government. The 73rd Amendment later gave constitutional status to Panchayati Raj institutions through Part IX.

Article 41 – Work, education and public assistance

Within its economic capacity and development, the State shall make effective provision for the right to work, education and public assistance in cases such as unemployment, old age, sickness and disablement.

Article 42 – Humane work conditions and maternity relief

Directs the State to make provision for just and humane conditions of work and maternity relief.

Article 43 – Living wage

Seeks a living wage, decent conditions of work, a decent standard of life, leisure and social and cultural opportunities for workers; also promotes cottage industries in rural areas.

Article 43A – Workers’ participation

Added by the 42nd Amendment; calls for steps to secure participation of workers in management of industries.

Article 43B – Co-operative societies

Inserted by the 97th Amendment; promotes voluntary formation, autonomous functioning, democratic control and professional management of co-operative societies.

Article 45 – Early childhood care

After the 86th Amendment, Article 45 directs the State to endeavour to provide early childhood care and education for all children until they complete six years. The 6–14 age group is covered by Fundamental Right Article 21A.

Article 48A – Environment

Added by the 42nd Amendment; directs the State to protect and improve the environment and safeguard forests and wildlife.

Article 50 – Separation of judiciary and executive

Directs the State to separate the judiciary from the executive in the public services of the State.

Article 51 – International peace

Promote international peace and security, maintain just and honourable relations, foster respect for international law and treaty obligations, and encourage settlement of international disputes by arbitration.

Article 31C current exam position: The protection associated with Article 31C applies to laws giving effect to Article 39(b) and 39(c), subject to judicial review. The 42nd Amendment’s attempt to extend it to all DPSPs was invalidated in Minerva Mills. In 2024, the Supreme Court in Property Owners Association v. Avoid assuming every privately owned resource automatically falls within Article 39(b).

7. Fundamental Rights and DPSPs | ప్రాథమిక హక్కులు – ఆదేశిక సూత్రాలు

Fundamental RightsDPSPs
Part III, Articles 12–35Part IV, Articles 36–51
Generally enforceable by courtsNot enforceable by courts (Article 37)
Primarily protect individual rights and freedomsGuide State policy towards social and economic welfare
Some rights are available to citizens; some to all personsAddress duties and policy goals of the State
Constitutional remedies include Article 32Implementation mainly through legislation and policy

Relationship: They are complementary, not enemies. Constitutional interpretation seeks harmony between individual liberty and social justice.

తెలుగు: ప్రాథమిక హక్కులు, ఆదేశిక సూత్రాలు పరస్పర విరుద్ధాలు కావు. వ్యక్తి స్వేచ్ఛను కాపాడుతూ సామాజిక న్యాయాన్ని సాధించేలా రెండింటి మధ్య సమతుల్యత అవసరం.

8. Landmark cases | ముఖ్యమైన తీర్పులు

CasePrinciple
State of Madras v. Champakam Dorairajan (1951)In case of conflict, Fundamental Rights prevailed under the then constitutional position; prompted the First Amendment and Article 15(4).
Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala (1973)Parliament’s amending power is limited by the basic structure doctrine; Article 31C’s first part was considered in this framework.
Minerva Mills v. Union of India (1980)Harmony and balance between Parts III and IV is part of the basic structure; extension of Article 31C to all DPSPs was invalidated.
Waman Rao v. Union of India (1981)Considered the constitutional position of laws placed in the Ninth Schedule before and after 24 April 1973.
Unni Krishnan v. State of Andhra Pradesh (1993)Recognised the right to education for children in the context of Article 21, helping pave the way for Article 21A.
Property Owners Association v. State of Maharashtra (2024)Clarified the meaning and scope of “material resources of the community” in Article 39(b); not every privately owned property automatically qualifies.

9. Fundamental Duties – Article 51A | ప్రాథమిక విధులు

Fundamental Duties were added by the 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976, following the recommendations of the Swaran Singh Committee. Initially ten duties were added. The 86th Amendment Act, 2002 added the eleventh duty concerning education of children aged 6–14.

Article 51A applies to every citizen of India. The duties are generally non-justiciable as standalone claims, but Parliament and State Legislatures may enact laws that support their observance.

తెలుగు: 42వ రాజ్యాంగ సవరణ (1976) ద్వారా మొదట పది ప్రాథమిక విధులు చేర్చారు. స్వరణ్ సింగ్ కమిటీ సిఫార్సులు దీనికి నేపథ్యం. 86వ సవరణ (2002) ద్వారా పిల్లల విద్యకు సంబంధించిన 11వ విధి చేర్చబడింది.

The 11 Fundamental Duties | 11 ప్రాథమిక విధులు

  1. Abide by the Constitution and respect its ideals and institutions, the National Flag and the National Anthem.
  2. Cherish and follow the noble ideals that inspired the national struggle for freedom.
  3. Uphold and protect the sovereignty, unity and integrity of India.
  4. Defend the country and render national service when called upon to do so.
  5. Promote harmony and the spirit of common brotherhood transcending religious, linguistic, regional or sectional diversities; renounce practices derogatory to the dignity of women.
  6. Value and preserve the rich heritage of India’s composite culture.
  7. Protect and improve the natural environment including forests, lakes, rivers and wildlife, and have compassion for living creatures.
  8. Develop scientific temper, humanism and the spirit of inquiry and reform.
  9. Safeguard public property and abjure violence.
  10. Strive towards excellence in all spheres of individual and collective activity so that the nation constantly rises to higher levels of endeavour and achievement.
  11. As a parent or guardian, provide opportunities for education to a child or ward between the ages of 6 and 14 years.

తెలుగు సారాంశం: రాజ్యాంగం, జాతీయ జెండా, జాతీయ గీతాన్ని గౌరవించడం; స్వాతంత్ర్య పోరాట ఆదర్శాలను పాటించడం; దేశ సార్వభౌమత్వం, ఐక్యతను కాపాడడం; దేశ రక్షణలో సేవ; సామరస్యం, మహిళల గౌరవం; సంస్కృతి పరిరక్షణ; పర్యావరణ రక్షణ; శాస్త్రీయ దృక్పథం; ప్రజా ఆస్తి రక్షణ, హింసకు దూరంగా ఉండడం; ప్రతి రంగంలో శ్రేష్ఠత; 6–14 ఏళ్ల పిల్లలకు విద్యావకాశం కల్పించడం.

10. DPSP and Fundamental Duties in governance | పాలనలో ప్రాముఖ్యత

  • DPSPs guide legislation and public policy in labour welfare, education, public health, rural local government, environment and social justice.
  • Fundamental Duties encourage responsible citizenship, constitutional culture, public-property protection and environmental stewardship.
  • Courts may refer to DPSPs and duties when interpreting constitutional provisions and assessing the purpose of legislation, but they do not automatically create an enforceable cause of action.
  • Implementation depends on democratic institutions, legislation, public resources, administrative capacity and citizen participation.

తెలుగు: కార్మిక సంక్షేమం, విద్య, ప్రజారోగ్యం, గ్రామీణ స్వపరిపాలన, పర్యావరణ పరిరక్షణ వంటి రంగాల్లో DPSPలు విధానాలకు దిశానిర్దేశం చేస్తాయి. విధులు బాధ్యతాయుత పౌరసత్వాన్ని ప్రోత్సహిస్తాయి.

11. Mains answer frameworks | మెయిన్స్ సమాధాన రూపకల్పన

Question: “DPSPs are fundamental to governance though non-justiciable.” Explain.

Introduction: Define Part IV and quote the substance of Article 37.

Body: Explain welfare-state goals; classify socialist, Gandhian and liberal-intellectual themes; show implementation through laws and programmes; discuss the complementary relationship with Fundamental Rights and the Minerva Mills principle.

Conclusion: DPSPs translate constitutional aspirations into public policy and require sustained legislative and administrative commitment.

Question: Assess the relevance of Fundamental Duties in contemporary India.

Discuss constitutional respect, social harmony, dignity of women, environmental protection, scientific temper and public-property protection. Explain that duties are not a substitute for enforceable rights and need civic education and fair implementation.

తెలుగు సూచన: Article number, amendment, తీర్పు, ప్రస్తుత పాలనా ఉదాహరణలను సరైన సందర్భంలో జోడించండి. విధులను హక్కులకు ప్రత్యామ్నాయంగా చూపవద్దు.

12. Quick revision | త్వరిత పునశ్చరణ

  • DPSP: Part IV, Articles 36–51; inspired mainly by Irish Constitution.
  • Article 37: non-justiciable, but fundamental in governance.
  • Article 39A: equal justice and free legal aid; 42nd Amendment.
  • Article 40: village panchayats; Article 43: living wage and cottage industries.
  • Article 44: Uniform Civil Code; Article 45: early childhood care up to age six.
  • Article 48A: environment; Article 50: separation of judiciary and executive.
  • Fundamental Duties: Part IVA, Article 51A; 11 duties.
  • 42nd Amendment (1976): inserted ten duties; 86th Amendment (2002): added eleventh duty.
  • Minerva Mills: harmony between FRs and DPSPs is part of the basic structure.

Answer: First Amendment. The judgment prompted the First Amendment, including Article 15(4).

Q40. The 42nd Amendment attempted to extend Article 31C protection to:

Answer: All DPSPs. The extension to all DPSPs was invalidated in Minerva Mills.

Q41. Article 31C protection is associated with laws implementing:

Answer: Article 39(b) and 39(c). The surviving scope is linked to Article 39(b) and 39(c), subject to judicial interpretation.

Q42. Which Article promotes cottage industries in rural areas?

Answer: Article 43. Article 43 includes promotion of cottage industries in rural areas.

Q43. Which one is NOT a DPSP?

Answer: Article 32. Article 32 is a Fundamental Right remedy in Part III.

Q44. Which one is NOT a Fundamental Duty?

Answer: Pay income tax as a specifically enumerated Article 51A duty. Article 51A does not enumerate a specific duty to pay income tax.

Q45. DPSPs are best described as:

Answer: Guidelines for State policy. They guide legislation and governance, but are not directly enforceable.

Q46. Which pair is correctly matched?

Answer: Article 39A – Free legal aid. Article 39A concerns equal justice and free legal aid.

Q47. Which pair is correctly matched?

Answer: Article 45 – Early childhood care. Article 45 now concerns early childhood care and education up to age six.

Q48. Which case clarified Article 39(b) and material resources of the community in 2024?

Answer: Property Owners Association v. State of Maharashtra. The 2024 Supreme Court decision clarified the scope of Article 39(b).

Q49. Fundamental Duties are generally:

Answer: Non-justiciable by themselves, though laws may give effect to them. They are civic duties in Part IVA; legislation may support enforcement.

Q50. The 73rd Amendment is connected with:

Answer: Panchayati Raj constitutional status. The 73rd Amendment inserted Part IX on Panchayats, supporting the decentralisation objective in Article 40.

Q51. The best constitutional approach to FRs and DPSPs is:

Answer: They are complementary and should be harmonised. The Supreme Court has emphasised harmony and balance between the two.
