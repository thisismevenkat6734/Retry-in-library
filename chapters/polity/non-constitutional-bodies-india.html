Overview | అవలోకనం

Non-constitutional bodies are institutions that are not directly created by the Constitution of India. They may be established by an Act of Parliament or State Legislature, or by an executive resolution or order of the government.

భారత రాజ్యాంగం ద్వారా నేరుగా ఏర్పాటు చేయబడని సంస్థలను రాజ్యాంగేతర సంస్థలు అంటారు. వీటిని పార్లమెంట్ లేదా రాష్ట్ర శాసనసభ చట్టం ద్వారా, లేదా ప్రభుత్వ కార్యనిర్వాహక ఉత్తర్వుల ద్వారా ఏర్పాటు చేయవచ్చు.

Exam Focus: Remember the establishing Act, year, composition, appointment, powers and functions of each institution. A statutory body and an executive body are both non-constitutional, but their legal foundations are different.

Types | రకాలు

  • Statutory Bodies: Established through legislation.
  • Executive Bodies: Established through executive resolutions or government orders.
  • Regulatory Bodies: Regulate specific sectors under applicable laws.
  • Investigative Agencies: Investigate specified offences and matters under applicable legal authority.

1. National Human Rights Commission (NHRC)

The National Human Rights Commission is a statutory body established under the Protection of Human Rights Act, 1993.

జాతీయ మానవ హక్కుల కమిషన్ మానవ హక్కుల పరిరక్షణ, ఉల్లంఘనలపై విచారణ మరియు అవగాహన కల్పించడం వంటి పనులు చేస్తుంది.

Composition

  • A Chairperson who has been the Chief Justice of India or a Judge of the Supreme Court.
  • One member who is, or has been, a Judge of the Supreme Court.
  • One member who is, or has been, the Chief Justice of a High Court.
  • Three members with knowledge of or practical experience in human rights, of whom at least one must be a woman.
  • Deemed members from specified national commissions under the Act for their relevant functions.

Appointment

The Chairperson and members are appointed by the President on the recommendations of a statutory committee headed by the Prime Minister.

Tenure

Under the amended law, the Chairperson and members hold office for three years or until attaining 70 years of age, whichever is earlier. They are eligible for reappointment subject to the Act.

Functions

  • Inquire into complaints of human rights violations or negligence in preventing such violations.
  • Intervene in court proceedings involving allegations of human rights violations, with the approval of the court.
  • Visit jails and other institutions where people are detained.
  • Review constitutional and legal safeguards for human rights.
  • Promote human rights research and awareness.
  • Encourage the efforts of non-governmental organisations working in this field.
NHRC recommendations are generally recommendatory, not equivalent to binding court judgments. The Act also prescribes jurisdictional and procedural limitations, including a one-year limitation for taking cognizance of alleged violations.

2. State Human Rights Commission (SHRC)

State Human Rights Commissions are established under the Protection of Human Rights Act, 1993.

  • They function at the State level.
  • The Chairperson and members are appointed by the Governor on the recommendations of the State-level committee specified in the Act.
  • The Chairperson must have been the Chief Justice or a Judge of a High Court.
  • The Commission examines human rights matters within its statutory jurisdiction.
  • It can visit certain institutions and make recommendations.
NHRC works at the national level; SHRC works at the State level. Their powers and jurisdiction are governed by the Protection of Human Rights Act.

3. Central Information Commission (CIC)

The Central Information Commission was established under the Right to Information Act, 2005.

సమాచార హక్కు చట్టం ద్వారా ప్రజలకు ప్రభుత్వ సంస్థల నుంచి సమాచారం పొందే హక్కు కల్పించబడింది. ఈ చట్టం అమలులో సమాచార కమిషన్లు ముఖ్యమైన పాత్ర పోషిస్తాయి.

Composition

  • Chief Information Commissioner.
  • Such number of Central Information Commissioners as may be deemed necessary, subject to the Act.

Appointment

The President appoints the Chief Information Commissioner and Information Commissioners on the recommendation of a committee consisting of:

  • Prime Minister – Chairperson.
  • Leader of Opposition in the Lok Sabha.
  • A Union Cabinet Minister nominated by the Prime Minister.

Tenure

The RTI Amendment Act, 2019 changed the framework for tenure and service conditions. The Central Government prescribes these by rules. Under the 2019 Rules, the tenure is three years, subject to the applicable age limit and rules.

Functions and powers

  • Hear complaints under the RTI Act.
  • Hear second appeals in matters within its jurisdiction.
  • Examine whether public authorities comply with their obligations under the Act.
  • Impose penalties on Public Information Officers in cases specified by the Act.
  • Recommend disciplinary action in appropriate cases under the Act.
The CIC is a statutory body, not a constitutional body. State Information Commissions deal with relevant State public authorities.

4. State Information Commission (SIC)

State Information Commissions are established under the Right to Information Act, 2005.

  • They consist of a State Chief Information Commissioner and State Information Commissioners.
  • They are appointed by the Governor.
  • The selection committee consists of the Chief Minister, Leader of Opposition in the Legislative Assembly, and a Cabinet Minister nominated by the Chief Minister.
  • They hear complaints and second appeals concerning State public authorities under the Act.
  • The 2019 amendment framework and applicable rules govern tenure and service conditions.

5. Central Vigilance Commission (CVC)

The Central Vigilance Commission was initially established in 1964 by an executive resolution following the recommendations of the Santhanam Committee. It received statutory status through the Central Vigilance Commission Act, 2003.

కేంద్ర ప్రభుత్వ వ్యవస్థలో అవినీతి నిరోధక చర్యలు, విజిలెన్స్ పరిపాలనకు సంబంధించి CVC ముఖ్యమైన పాత్ర పోషిస్తుంది.

Composition

  • Central Vigilance Commissioner – Chairperson.
  • Not more than two Vigilance Commissioners – Members.

Appointment

Appointed by the President on the recommendation of a committee consisting of the Prime Minister, Union Home Minister and Leader of Opposition in the Lok Sabha.

Tenure

Four years or until attaining 65 years of age, whichever is earlier, subject to the Act.

Functions

  • Exercise superintendence over the functioning of the Delhi Special Police Establishment in relation to investigations of offences under the Prevention of Corruption Act, within the statutory framework.
  • Review the progress of investigations and prosecution under relevant provisions.
  • Advise Central Government organisations on vigilance matters.
  • Exercise jurisdiction over specified categories of public servants.
  • Oversee vigilance administration in Central Government organisations within its legal powers.
CVC is a statutory body. It is not the same as the CBI. CVC exercises vigilance oversight and statutory superintendence in specified matters; CBI is an investigative agency.

6. Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI)

The Central Bureau of Investigation is India's premier central investigating agency for specified cases. It was established in 1963 by a Government of India resolution.

Its legal foundation for exercising police powers in relevant cases is primarily the Delhi Special Police Establishment Act, 1946.

Background

  • The Special Police Establishment was created during the Second World War in 1941.
  • The Delhi Special Police Establishment Act was enacted in 1946.
  • The CBI was established by an executive resolution on 1 April 1963.

Major functions

  • Investigate corruption and bribery cases involving specified public servants.
  • Investigate serious economic offences.
  • Investigate special crimes and cases referred to it by competent authorities or courts.
  • Investigate cases referred by State Governments, subject to legal requirements.

Jurisdiction

  • Under the DSPE Act, the Central Government may extend the powers and jurisdiction of the DSPE to areas in States, subject to the Act.
  • Consent of the concerned State Government is generally required for the DSPE to exercise jurisdiction in a State, unless a court order or other applicable legal basis governs the matter.
  • The Supreme Court and High Courts can direct a CBI investigation in appropriate cases under their constitutional powers.
CBI is not a constitutional body. Its establishment was through an executive resolution, while its police powers are rooted in the DSPE Act.

7. Lokpal and Lokayukta

The Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act, 2013 provides for the establishment of Lokpal at the Union level and Lokayuktas at the State level.

లోక్‌పాల్ మరియు లోకాయుక్త సంస్థలు అవినీతి ఆరోపణలపై విచారణకు సంబంధించిన వ్యవస్థలో భాగంగా పనిచేస్తాయి.

Lokpal composition

  • A Chairperson.
  • Up to eight members.
  • Fifty per cent of the members shall be judicial members.
  • At least fifty per cent of members shall be from SCs, STs, OBCs, minorities and women, as provided by the Act.

Appointment

The President appoints the Lokpal Chairperson and members on the recommendation of a Selection Committee consisting of:

  • Prime Minister – Chairperson.
  • Speaker of the Lok Sabha.
  • Leader of Opposition in the Lok Sabha.
  • Chief Justice of India or a Supreme Court Judge nominated by the CJI.
  • An eminent jurist nominated by the President on the recommendations of the other members of the Selection Committee.

Functions

  • Inquire into allegations of corruption against specified public functionaries within its jurisdiction.
  • Exercise powers under the Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act.
  • Refer matters for preliminary inquiry or investigation through agencies as provided by law.
  • Supervise or direct investigation in circumstances specified in the Act.

Lokayukta

  • Lokayukta functions at the State level.
  • The 2013 Act requires every State to establish a Lokayukta through a State law within the prescribed framework.
  • Composition, appointment and jurisdiction vary according to the relevant State law.

8. National Investigation Agency (NIA)

The National Investigation Agency was established under the National Investigation Agency Act, 2008, following the 2008 Mumbai terror attacks.

It is a central agency responsible for investigation and prosecution of offences listed in the NIA Act and its Schedule.

Major functions

  • Investigate scheduled offences affecting national security and other matters covered by the Act.
  • Investigate terrorism-related offences and specified offences under scheduled laws.
  • Coordinate with State agencies and other law enforcement bodies.
  • Prosecute cases before designated Special Courts.

Jurisdiction

  • The Central Government may direct the NIA to investigate a scheduled offence under the Act.
  • The NIA can investigate scheduled offences across India under the statutory framework.
  • The Act also provides for investigation of certain offences committed outside India, subject to its provisions and applicable law.
NIA is a statutory investigative agency. Its legal foundation is the NIA Act, 2008.

9. National Green Tribunal (NGT)

The National Green Tribunal was established under the National Green Tribunal Act, 2010.

పర్యావరణ పరిరక్షణ, అడవులు మరియు సహజ వనరులకు సంబంధించిన కొన్ని చట్టపరమైన వివాదాలను త్వరితగతిన పరిష్కరించడానికి NGT ఏర్పాటు చేయబడింది.

Composition

  • Chairperson.
  • Judicial Members.
  • Expert Members.

Functions

  • Adjudicate civil cases involving substantial questions relating to the environment under the scheduled enactments.
  • Provide relief and compensation to victims of pollution and environmental damage within its jurisdiction.
  • Order restitution of damaged property and the environment.
  • Apply principles such as sustainable development, precautionary principle and polluter pays principle.

Important points

  • It is a statutory tribunal.
  • It was established in 2010.
  • It is guided by principles of natural justice.
  • It is not bound by the procedure laid down in the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908.
  • It is required to endeavour to dispose of applications or appeals within six months of filing, subject to the Act.

10. NITI Aayog

NITI Aayog stands for National Institution for Transforming India. It was established on 1 January 2015 through an executive resolution of the Union Government.

It replaced the Planning Commission, which had been established in 1950.

Composition and structure

  • Prime Minister – Chairperson.
  • Governing Council consisting of Chief Ministers of States and Lieutenant Governors of Union Territories with legislatures, and other specified representatives.
  • Regional Councils may be formed for specific issues.
  • Vice-Chairperson, full-time members, part-time members and ex-officio members.
  • Chief Executive Officer appointed by the Prime Minister for a specified tenure.

Objectives

  • Promote cooperative federalism.
  • Encourage competitive federalism.
  • Develop strategic and long-term policy frameworks.
  • Promote innovation, entrepreneurship and knowledge sharing.
  • Monitor and evaluate development programmes.
  • Support sustainable and inclusive development.
NITI Aayog is an executive body, not a constitutional or statutory body. It does not have the same role as the former Planning Commission in allocating plan funds.

11. Competition Commission of India (CCI)

The Competition Commission of India was established under the Competition Act, 2002.

It promotes competition, prevents practices having an adverse effect on competition and protects consumer interests.

Functions

  • Prevent anti-competitive agreements.
  • Address abuse of dominant position.
  • Regulate combinations such as mergers and acquisitions under the Act.
  • Promote and sustain competition in markets.
  • Undertake competition advocacy.
CCI is a statutory regulatory body. It is not a constitutional body.

12. Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI)

SEBI was initially established in 1988 through an executive resolution. It received statutory status under the SEBI Act, 1992.

Objectives and functions

  • Protect the interests of investors in securities.
  • Promote the development of the securities market.
  • Regulate the securities market.
  • Register and regulate specified market intermediaries.
  • Prevent fraudulent and unfair trade practices.
  • Promote investor education and awareness.

13. Reserve Bank of India (RBI)

The Reserve Bank of India was established on 1 April 1935 under the Reserve Bank of India Act, 1934. It was nationalised in 1949.

Major functions

  • Formulate and implement monetary policy.
  • Issue banknotes, subject to the legal framework.
  • Act as banker to the Central and State Governments.
  • Act as banker to banks.
  • Regulate and supervise banks and specified financial institutions.
  • Manage foreign exchange reserves and foreign exchange regulation under applicable law.
  • Maintain financial stability.
The RBI is a statutory institution established under the RBI Act, 1934. It is India's central bank.

14. Other Important Statutory Bodies

National Commission for Women (NCW)

Established under the National Commission for Women Act, 1990. It reviews safeguards for women, examines complaints and advises on policy matters relating to women's rights.

National Commission for Minorities (NCM)

Established under the National Commission for Minorities Act, 1992. It monitors safeguards for notified religious minorities and makes recommendations.

National Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission (NCDRC)

Established under consumer protection legislation. It is a national-level consumer dispute redressal commission under the applicable Consumer Protection Act.

National Disaster Management Authority (NDMA)

Established under the Disaster Management Act, 2005. The Prime Minister is its Chairperson. It lays down policies, plans and guidelines for disaster management.

National Commission for Protection of Child Rights (NCPCR)

Established under the Commission for Protection of Child Rights Act, 2005. It examines safeguards for child rights and reviews laws and policies concerning children.

Insolvency and Bankruptcy Board of India (IBBI)

Established under the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016. It regulates insolvency professionals, insolvency professional agencies and information utilities under the Code.

Unique Identification Authority of India (UIDAI)

Established under the Aadhaar (Targeted Delivery of Financial and Other Subsidies, Benefits and Services) Act, 2016. It is responsible for Aadhaar-related functions under the Act.

17. Mains Answer Framework

Question: Explain the importance of statutory and executive bodies in Indian governance.

Introduction: Statutory and executive bodies support governance by performing specialised functions, improving accountability and implementing public policies.

Body:

  • Transparency – Central and State Information Commissions.
  • Anti-corruption – CVC, Lokpal and investigative agencies.
  • Human rights protection – NHRC and SHRC.
  • Environmental protection – NGT.
  • Economic regulation – RBI, SEBI and CCI.
  • Policy planning and cooperative federalism – NITI Aayog.
  • National security – NIA and other authorised agencies.

Challenges: Vacancies, resource limitations, overlapping jurisdictions, delays, questions of independence and weak enforcement of recommendations.

Way Forward: Transparent appointments, adequate funding, professional capacity, clear legal mandates, accountability and timely publication of reports.

Conclusion: Effective statutory and executive institutions strengthen the rule of law, public accountability and delivery of public services.

18. Glossary | ముఖ్య పదాలు

  • Statutory Body: A body created by legislation – చట్టం ద్వారా ఏర్పడిన సంస్థ.
  • Executive Resolution: A government decision or order – ప్రభుత్వ కార్యనిర్వాహక తీర్మానం.
  • Regulatory Body: An institution that regulates a sector – నియంత్రణ సంస్థ.
  • Investigation: Examination of facts relating to an alleged offence – దర్యాప్తు.
  • Transparency: Openness in public administration – పారదర్శకత.
  • Accountability: Responsibility for actions and decisions – జవాబుదారీతనం.
  • Jurisdiction: Legal authority to deal with a matter – అధికార పరిధి.
  • Ombudsman: An authority that examines complaints against public administration – ప్రజా ఫిర్యాదులను పరిశీలించే అధికారి / సంస్థ.
  • Vigilance: Measures to prevent corruption and misconduct – అవినీతి నిరోధక నిఘా.
  • Environmental Tribunal: A specialised forum for environmental disputes – పర్యావరణ వివాదాల ప్రత్యేక న్యాయస్థానం.
