1. Understanding the Preamble | రాజ్యాంగ ప్రవేశిక పరిచయం

The Preamble is the introductory statement of the Constitution. It expresses the source of constitutional authority, the nature of the Indian State and the objectives that the Constitution seeks to secure. It begins with the words “We, the People of India”, which signify that the Constitution derives its authority from the people.

రాజ్యాంగ ప్రవేశిక భారత రాజ్యాంగానికి పరిచయ భాగం. భారతదేశం ఎలాంటి రాజ్యంగా ఉండాలి, ప్రజలకు ఏ విలువలు–లక్ష్యాలు కల్పించాలి అనే విషయాలను సంక్షిప్తంగా తెలియజేస్తుంది.

  • Source of authority: The people of India.
  • Nature of the State: Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic, Republic (current text).
  • Objectives: Justice, Liberty, Equality and Fraternity.
  • Date mentioned: The Constitution was adopted on 26 November 1949.

2. The Preamble: Original and Present Text | అసలు–ప్రస్తుత ప్రవేశిక

The original Preamble described India as a Sovereign Democratic Republic. The 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976 added the words “Socialist” and “Secular” and changed “unity of the Nation” to “unity and integrity of the Nation”.

3. Meaning of the Preamble’s Key Ideals | ముఖ్య పదాల అర్థం

Sovereign | సార్వభౌమత్వం

India is not legally subordinate to any external power. It can make its own laws and conduct its foreign relations, subject to its Constitution and international obligations voluntarily accepted.

Socialist | సామ్యవాద దృక్పథం

The Indian constitutional idea seeks to reduce inequalities and promote welfare and social justice through democratic institutions. It does not prescribe one rigid economic model.

Secular | లౌకికత్వం

The State has no official religion. The Constitution protects freedom of conscience and religion and requires the State to treat religions with constitutional fairness.

Democratic | ప్రజాస్వామ్యం

Political democracy includes periodic elections, universal adult suffrage, representative government, accountability and the rule of law. The Constitution also aims at social and economic democracy.

Republic | గణతంత్రం

The head of the Indian Union, the President, is elected for a term under constitutional procedures. The office is not hereditary.

4. Justice, Liberty, Equality and Fraternity | నాలుగు ప్రధాన లక్ష్యాలు

  • Justice: Social justice addresses discrimination and unequal social relations; economic justice concerns fair opportunities and reduction of economic inequalities; political justice means equal political participation.
  • Liberty: Protects freedom of thought, expression, belief, faith and worship. Liberty operates within constitutional limits and is balanced with rights of others and public order.
  • Equality: Includes equality of status and opportunity. It rejects arbitrary privilege and supports equal protection of laws.
  • Fraternity: Promotes a sense of common brotherhood, individual dignity and national unity and integrity.

Exam insight: The Preamble links individual freedoms with social transformation. Liberty without equality may preserve unjust privilege; equality without liberty may suppress individual choice; fraternity helps sustain both.

5. Is the Preamble Part of the Constitution? | ప్రవేశిక రాజ్యాంగంలో భాగమా?

The Supreme Court’s position developed through important cases:

  • Berubari Union case (1960): The Court treated the Preamble as a key to understanding the Constitution but stated that it was not part of the Constitution in that decision.
  • Kesavananda Bharati case (1973): The Supreme Court held that the Preamble is part of the Constitution. It can be amended under Article 368, but the basic structure cannot be destroyed.
  • LIC of India case (1995): The Court reaffirmed that the Preamble is part of the Constitution.

Legal status: The Preamble is not independently enforceable in court and does not itself confer a separate set of legal powers. It is an important interpretive guide to constitutional provisions.

6. Amendment of the Preamble and Basic Structure | సవరణ–మౌలిక నిర్మాణం

The Preamble can be amended under Article 368, but an amendment cannot damage or destroy the Constitution’s basic structure. This principle was laid down in the Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala (1973) judgment.

  • The 42nd Amendment (1976) is the only amendment that has changed the Preamble’s text.
  • It added “Socialist” and “Secular”.
  • It added “Integrity” to the phrase “unity and integrity of the Nation”.
  • The amendment did not replace the words “Democratic Republic”.

Basic structure: The Supreme Court has identified features such as supremacy of the Constitution, republican and democratic form of government, secularism, federalism, separation of powers, judicial review, rule of law and free and fair elections in different judgments. There is no single exhaustive list.

7. Salient Features of the Indian Constitution | భారత రాజ్యాంగ ముఖ్య లక్షణాలు

  1. Written and detailed Constitution: It sets out institutions, powers, rights, procedures and Centre–State relations in detail.
  2. Drawn from multiple sources: It adapts ideas from Indian experience and other constitutional systems.
  3. Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic Republic: These describe the constitutional identity of India today.
  4. Parliamentary government: The Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha at Union level.
  5. Federal system with a strong Centre: Powers are divided, but the Union has important constitutional advantages, especially in emergencies and specified subjects.
  6. Fundamental Rights: Part III protects civil and political freedoms and equality.
  7. Directive Principles: Part IV guides the State towards social and economic welfare.
  8. Fundamental Duties: Part IVA sets out citizens’ duties; added in 1976.
  9. Independent judiciary: Courts interpret the Constitution and protect constitutional limits.
  10. Judicial review: Courts can examine the constitutional validity of laws and executive action.
  11. Universal adult franchise: Adult citizens can vote subject to constitutional and statutory qualifications.
  12. Single citizenship: India has one citizenship for the Union, unlike some federations with dual citizenship.
  13. Emergency provisions: The Constitution provides for national, State and financial emergencies.
  14. Local self-government: Panchayats and Municipalities received constitutional status through the 73rd and 74th Amendments.

8. Federalism with a Strong Centre | బలమైన కేంద్రంతో సమాఖ్య వ్యవస్థ

India has a federal constitutional structure: powers are distributed between the Union and States, and the Constitution is supreme. However, several provisions give the Union a comparatively strong position.

  • Union, State and Concurrent Lists distribute legislative subjects under the Seventh Schedule.
  • Residuary legislative powers are vested in Parliament.
  • Parliament can legislate on State List subjects in specified constitutional circumstances, such as a Rajya Sabha resolution under Article 249 or a national emergency under Article 250.
  • Emergency provisions can alter the ordinary federal balance.
  • All-India Services and an integrated judiciary support administrative and institutional unity.

Balanced view: Indian federalism is not simply unitary. States have constitutionally protected powers, elected governments and legislative fields; the system combines federal distribution with strong national institutions.

9. Parliamentary System | పార్లమెంటరీ విధానం

India follows a parliamentary form of government at the Union and State levels. The President is the constitutional head of the Union, while the Prime Minister and Council of Ministers exercise real executive authority in accordance with the Constitution.

  • The Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha (Article 75(3)).
  • The Prime Minister is the head of the Union Council of Ministers.
  • The President ordinarily acts on the aid and advice of the Council of Ministers, subject to constitutional provisions.
  • At State level, the Governor is the constitutional head and the Chief Minister leads the Council of Ministers.
  • The parliamentary system promotes executive accountability to the elected legislature.

10. Independent Judiciary and Judicial Review | స్వతంత్ర న్యాయవ్యవస్థ

The Constitution establishes an integrated judicial system with the Supreme Court at the apex, High Courts in the States and subordinate courts below them. Judicial independence is supported by security of tenure, protected service conditions and constitutional procedures for removal of judges.

  • Article 32: Right to move the Supreme Court for enforcement of Fundamental Rights.
  • Article 226: High Courts can issue writs for Fundamental Rights and for other purposes.
  • Judicial review: Courts can test laws and executive actions against the Constitution.
  • Basic structure doctrine: Constitutional amendments cannot destroy the basic structure.

న్యాయవ్యవస్థ స్వతంత్రంగా ఉండటం రాజ్యాంగ పరిపాలన, పౌర హక్కులు, అధికారాల పరిమితుల రక్షణకు అవసరం.

11. Universal Adult Franchise and Single Citizenship | సార్వత్రిక ఓటుహక్కు

Article 326 provides for elections to the Lok Sabha and State Legislative Assemblies on the basis of adult suffrage, subject to constitutional and statutory conditions. The voting age was originally 21 years and was reduced to 18 by the 61st Constitutional Amendment Act, 1988.

  • Current minimum voting age: 18 years.
  • Voting is subject to citizenship, registration and other legal qualifications.
  • India provides single citizenship for the whole country; there is no separate State citizenship.
  • Universal adult franchise gave a broad democratic foundation to the Republic from its beginning.

12. Emergency Provisions | అత్యవసర నిబంధనలు

The Constitution contains three broad types of emergency provisions:

TypeArticleBroad ground
National EmergencyArticle 352War, external aggression or armed rebellion
President’s Rule in a StateArticle 356Failure of constitutional machinery in a State
Financial EmergencyArticle 360Threat to financial stability or credit of India or any part

These provisions are exceptional and are subject to constitutional procedures and safeguards. The 44th Amendment (1978) introduced important safeguards, including replacing “internal disturbance” with “armed rebellion” as a ground for National Emergency.

13. Local Self-Government | స్థానిక స్వపరిపాలన

The original Constitution referred to village panchayats in Article 40 among the Directive Principles. Constitutional status for rural and urban local bodies was strengthened later.

  • 73rd Amendment Act, 1992: Added Part IX and the Eleventh Schedule; constitutional framework for Panchayats.
  • 74th Amendment Act, 1992: Added Part IXA and the Twelfth Schedule; constitutional framework for Municipalities.
  • These amendments provided for regular elections, reservations and State Finance Commissions, subject to constitutional provisions.

Exam caution: Panchayats and Municipalities were not part of the original 1950 constitutional structure in their present constitutional form; the 73rd and 74th Amendments came in 1992.

15. Mains Answer Frameworks | మెయిన్స్ సమాధాన రూపకల్పన

Q1. Explain the significance of the Preamble.

Introduction: Describe it as the introductory statement expressing constitutional authority, identity and objectives.

Body: Explain “We, the People”; Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic, Republic; Justice, Liberty, Equality and Fraternity; link it to interpretation and basic structure.

Conclusion: The Preamble is the philosophical compass of the Constitution, guiding interpretation without independently creating enforceable rights.

Q2. “The Indian Constitution is federal in form but has a strong Centre.” Discuss.

Explain division of powers, written and supreme Constitution, independent judiciary and State governments; then discuss residuary powers, emergency provisions, Parliament’s powers in specified circumstances and integrated institutions. Conclude that Indian federalism is a distinctive constitutional balance.

Q3. Describe the major features of the Indian Constitution.

Organise the answer under parliamentary democracy, federalism, rights, Directive Principles, duties, independent judiciary, judicial review, adult franchise, single citizenship, emergency provisions and local self-government.
