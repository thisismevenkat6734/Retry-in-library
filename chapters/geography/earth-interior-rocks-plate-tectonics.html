1. Introduction – పరిచయం

Earth is the third planet from the Sun and the only known planet that supports life. It has a solid surface, liquid water, an atmosphere and a complex internal structure.

భూమి సూర్యుడి నుంచి మూడవ గ్రహం. జీవం ఉన్నట్లు తెలిసిన ఏకైక గ్రహం. భూమిపై ఘన ఉపరితలం, ద్రవరూప నీరు, వాతావరణం మరియు సంక్లిష్టమైన అంతర్గత నిర్మాణం ఉన్నాయి.

ShapeOblate spheroid – ధ్రువాల వద్ద కొద్దిగా చదునుగా, భూమధ్యరేఖ వద్ద ఉబ్బుగా ఉంటుంది.
Mean radiusApproximately 6,371 km.

Major Earth systems

  • Lithosphere: Earth's rigid outer layer – భూమి యొక్క గట్టి బాహ్య పొర.
  • Hydrosphere: All water on Earth – భూమిపై ఉన్న నీటి సముదాయం.
  • Atmosphere: Gaseous envelope surrounding Earth – భూమిని ఆవరించే వాయు పొర.
  • Biosphere: Regions where life exists – జీవం ఉన్న ప్రాంతాలు.
Remember: These systems interact with one another. Changes in one system can influence the others.

2. Interior of the Earth – భూమి అంతర్భాగం

Earth's interior is divided into three major compositional layers: crust, mantle and core.

భూమి అంతర్భాగాన్ని ప్రధానంగా మూడు పొరలుగా విభజిస్తారు: భూపటలం, మాంటిల్, కేంద్రకం.

CRUST – భూపటలం
MANTLE – మాంటిల్
OUTER CORE – బాహ్య కేంద్రకం
INNER CORE – అంతర్గత కేంద్రకం

Schematic representation – Not to scale

A. Crust – భూపటలం

  • It is the outermost solid layer of Earth.
  • It is relatively thin compared with the mantle and core.
  • Continental crust is generally thicker than oceanic crust.
  • Continental crust is mainly granitic in composition, while oceanic crust is predominantly basaltic.
  • Oceanic crust is generally denser than continental crust.

భూపటలం భూమి యొక్క అత్యంత బాహ్య ఘన పొర. ఖండ భూపటలం సాధారణంగా మందంగా ఉంటుంది. సముద్ర భూపటలం తక్కువ మందంగా, ఎక్కువ సాంద్రతతో ఉంటుంది.

B. Mantle – మాంటిల్

  • It lies below the crust and extends to a depth of about 2,900 km.
  • It is composed mainly of silicate rocks rich in magnesium and iron.
  • Most of the mantle is solid, but it can deform and flow slowly over geological time.
  • Heat transfer and slow mantle flow are important in plate tectonics.

మాంటిల్ భూపటలం కింద ఉంటుంది. ఇది ప్రధానంగా సిలికేట్ పదార్థాలతో ఏర్పడింది. అధిక ఉష్ణోగ్రత ఉన్నప్పటికీ ఎక్కువ భాగం ఘన స్థితిలో ఉంటుంది; దీర్ఘకాలంలో నెమ్మదిగా ప్రవహించగలదు.

C. Core – కేంద్రకం

  • The core is mainly composed of iron and nickel.
  • Outer core is liquid.
  • Inner core is solid due to enormous pressure.
  • Movement of electrically conducting liquid metal in the outer core helps generate Earth's magnetic field.

కేంద్రకం ప్రధానంగా ఇనుము, నికెల్‌తో ఏర్పడింది. బాహ్య కేంద్రకం ద్రవరూపంలో ఉండగా, అంతర్గత కేంద్రకం అధిక పీడనం కారణంగా ఘనంగా ఉంటుంది.

3. Evidence for Earth's Interior – భూమి అంతర్భాగానికి ఆధారాలు

Direct sources

  • Deep mining – లోతైన గనుల తవ్వకం.
  • Deep drilling – లోతైన డ్రిల్లింగ్.
  • Volcanic materials brought to the surface – అగ్నిపర్వతాల ద్వారా పైకి వచ్చిన పదార్థాలు.

Indirect sources

  • Seismic waves: Earthquake waves provide important information about internal layers.
  • Gravity: Variations in gravity help infer the distribution of mass inside Earth.
  • Magnetic field: Earth's magnetic properties provide clues about its core.
  • Meteorites: Their composition helps scientists understand materials that formed the early Earth.
Most important: Seismic waves are among the most powerful tools for studying Earth's internal structure.

4. Seismic Waves – భూకంప తరంగాలు

Seismic waves are waves of energy released during earthquakes or other sudden movements within Earth.

Primary Waves (P-waves)

  • Fastest seismic body waves.
  • Longitudinal or compressional waves.
  • Travel through solids, liquids and gases.
  • Arrive first at seismic recording stations.

Secondary Waves (S-waves)

  • Slower than P-waves.
  • Transverse or shear waves.
  • Travel through solids but not through liquids.
  • Their absence through the liquid outer core provides evidence that it is liquid.

Surface Waves

  • Travel along or near Earth's surface.
  • Often cause significant damage during earthquakes.
  • Include Love waves and Rayleigh waves.

5. Minerals – ఖనిజాలు

A mineral is a naturally occurring inorganic solid with a characteristic chemical composition and an ordered internal structure.

ఖనిజం అనేది సహజంగా ఏర్పడే, సాధారణంగా అకర్బన ఘన పదార్థం. దీనికి నిర్దిష్ట రసాయన కూర్పు మరియు క్రమబద్ధమైన అంతర్గత స్ఫటిక నిర్మాణం ఉంటాయి.

Physical properties of minerals

  • Colour: Visible colour of a mineral.
  • Streak: Colour of the mineral in powdered form.
  • Hardness: Resistance to scratching.
  • Lustre: How a mineral reflects light.
  • Cleavage: Tendency to break along specific planes.
  • Fracture: Pattern of breakage when cleavage is absent or not followed.
  • Specific gravity: Relative density of a mineral.

6. Rocks – శిలలు

Rocks are naturally occurring solid aggregates of one or more minerals or mineraloid materials.

శిలలు అనేవి ఒకటి లేదా అంతకంటే ఎక్కువ ఖనిజాలు లేదా ఖనిజసదృశ పదార్థాలతో ఏర్పడే సహజ ఘన సముదాయాలు.

Three major types of rocks

Igneous Rocksఅగ్నిజ శిలలు – magma లేదా lava చల్లబడి ఘనీభవించడం వల్ల ఏర్పడతాయి.
Sedimentary Rocksఅవక్షేప శిలలు – అవక్షేపాలు పేరుకుపోయి, ఒత్తిడి మరియు సిమెంటేషన్ ద్వారా ఏర్పడతాయి.
Metamorphic Rocksరూపాంతర శిలలు – పూర్వపు శిలలు వేడి, పీడనం లేదా ద్రవాల ప్రభావంతో మార్పు చెందుతాయి.

A. Igneous Rocks – అగ్నిజ శిలలు

They form when molten rock cools and solidifies.

  • Intrusive: Magma cools below the surface. Usually coarse-grained. Example: Granite.
  • Extrusive: Lava cools at the surface. Usually fine-grained. Example: Basalt.
  • Other examples include obsidian and pumice.

B. Sedimentary Rocks – అవక్షేప శిలలు

They form through deposition, compaction and cementation of sediments, or through chemical and biological processes.

  • Clastic: Formed from fragments of pre-existing rocks. Example: Sandstone.
  • Chemical: Formed by precipitation of minerals from solution. Example: Rock salt.
  • Organic/Biochemical: Formed with the contribution of organisms. Example: Some limestones and coal.
  • Often occur in layers called strata.
  • May contain fossils.

C. Metamorphic Rocks – రూపాంతర శిలలు

They form when existing rocks are altered by heat, pressure and chemically active fluids without complete melting.

  • Shale → Slate.
  • Limestone → Marble.
  • Granite → Gneiss.
  • Sandstone → Quartzite.
Metamorphism does not normally involve complete melting. If a rock melts and later solidifies, it forms an igneous rock.

శిలలు శిథిలీకరణ, అపక్షయం, అవక్షేపణ, ఘనీభవనం, వేడి, పీడనం మరియు ద్రవీభవనం వంటి ప్రక్రియల ద్వారా ఒక రకం నుంచి మరొక రకంగా మారుతుంటాయి.

The rock cycle is not a fixed one-way sequence. Any rock type can be transformed through different geological processes.

8. Continental Drift Theory – ఖండ చలన సిద్ధాంతం

Alfred Wegener proposed the Continental Drift Theory in 1912. He suggested that present-day continents were once joined together in a supercontinent called Pangaea.

ఆల్ఫ్రెడ్ వెగెనర్ 1912లో ఖండ చలన సిద్ధాంతాన్ని ప్రతిపాదించాడు. ప్రస్తుత ఖండాలన్నీ ఒకప్పుడు పాంజియా అనే మహా ఖండంగా కలిసి ఉండేవని వివరించాడు.

Important terms

  • Pangaea: Ancient supercontinent containing most of Earth's continental landmass.
  • Panthalassa: Vast ocean surrounding Pangaea.
  • Laurasia: Northern landmass formed after the breakup of Pangaea.
  • Gondwanaland: Southern landmass formed after the breakup of Pangaea.

Evidence proposed for continental drift

  • Jigsaw fit of continental margins, especially South America and Africa.
  • Similar fossils found on continents now separated by oceans.
  • Matching rock formations and geological structures.
  • Palaeoclimatic evidence, including ancient glacial deposits.
Wegener could not provide a satisfactory mechanism for continental movement. Later developments in seafloor spreading and plate tectonics provided a stronger explanatory framework.

9. Sea-Floor Spreading – సముద్ర అడుగు విస్తరణ

Harry Hess developed the sea-floor spreading hypothesis in the early 1960s. New oceanic crust forms at mid-ocean ridges as magma rises, cools and solidifies.

మధ్య సముద్ర పర్వత శ్రేణుల వద్ద మాగ్మా పైకి వచ్చి చల్లబడటం వల్ల కొత్త సముద్ర భూపటలం ఏర్పడుతుంది. దీని వల్ల సముద్రపు అడుగు విస్తరిస్తుంది.

  • New crust forms at divergent plate boundaries.
  • Older oceanic crust moves away from the ridge.
  • Magnetic stripes on the ocean floor provide evidence of sea-floor spreading.
  • Oceanic crust is recycled at many subduction zones.

10. Plate Tectonics – భూఫలక సిద్ధాంతం

The lithosphere is divided into several large and small tectonic plates that move slowly over the underlying, mechanically weaker asthenosphere.

లిథోస్ఫియర్ అనేక పెద్ద మరియు చిన్న భూఫలకాలుగా విభజించబడింది. ఇవి కిందనున్న బలహీనమైన ఆస్తెనోస్ఫియర్‌పై నెమ్మదిగా కదులుతాయి.

Major tectonic plates

  • Pacific Plate.
  • North American Plate.
  • South American Plate.
  • African Plate.
  • Eurasian Plate.
  • Indo-Australian Plate (often treated as separate Indian and Australian plates in some models).
  • Antarctic Plate.

Types of plate boundaries

1. Divergent BoundaryTwo plates move away from each other. New crust forms. Example: Mid-Atlantic Ridge.
2. Convergent BoundaryTwo plates move towards each other. Subduction or mountain building may occur. Example: Himalayas.
3. Transform BoundaryTwo plates slide past each other. Crust is neither created nor destroyed overall. Example: San Andreas Fault.

Divergent boundaries – విభేదక సరిహద్దులు

  • Plates move apart.
  • Magma may rise and create new crust.
  • Mid-ocean ridges and continental rift valleys may develop.

Convergent boundaries – అభిసరణ సరిహద్దులు

  • Oceanic–Continental: Denser oceanic plate usually subducts beneath continental plate. Volcanic mountain chains may form.
  • Oceanic–Oceanic: One oceanic plate subducts beneath another; volcanic island arcs may form.
  • Continental–Continental: Collision can produce high mountain ranges. Example: Himalayas.

Transform boundaries – పరివర్తన సరిహద్దులు

Plates slide horizontally past each other. Friction and sudden movement can produce earthquakes.

Important: Plate boundaries are major zones of earthquakes, volcanoes and mountain building, but earthquakes can also occur within plates.

11. Indian Plate and Himalayas – భారతీయ భూఫలకం

The Indian Plate moved northwards and collided with the Eurasian Plate. This collision led to the formation and continuing uplift of the Himalayan mountain system.

భారతీయ భూఫలకం ఉత్తర దిశగా కదిలి యూరేషియన్ భూఫలకంతో ఢీకొంది. ఈ ఖండాల ఢీకొనడం వల్ల హిమాలయ పర్వత వ్యవస్థ ఏర్పడింది; ఈ ప్రాంతంలో భూగర్భ మార్పులు ఇప్పటికీ కొనసాగుతున్నాయి.

  • The Himalayas are young fold mountains.
  • The collision zone remains tectonically active.
  • Large earthquakes can occur in the Himalayan region.
  • The Indo-Gangetic Plain lies south of the Himalayan mountain system.

12. Earthquakes – భూకంపాలు

An earthquake is the shaking of the ground caused by a sudden release of energy within Earth's crust or lithosphere, commonly due to movement along faults.

భూమి లోపల నిల్వ ఉన్న శక్తి ఒక్కసారిగా విడుదల కావడం వల్ల భూమి కంపించడాన్ని భూకంపం అంటారు. సాధారణంగా భ్రంశాల వెంట జరిగే కదలిక దీనికి కారణం.

Important terms

  • Focus / Hypocentre: Point inside Earth where an earthquake begins.
  • Epicentre: Point on Earth's surface directly above the focus.
  • Fault: A fracture or zone of fractures along which rocks have moved.
  • Aftershocks: Smaller earthquakes following a larger earthquake in the same region.
  • Foreshocks: Smaller earthquakes that precede a larger earthquake in some sequences; they cannot reliably be identified in advance.

Magnitude and Intensity

  • Magnitude: Measures the size of an earthquake based on seismic data. Moment Magnitude Scale (Mw) is widely used for large earthquakes.
  • Intensity: Describes observed shaking and damage at a particular location. Modified Mercalli Intensity (MMI) is one intensity scale.
Magnitude and intensity are not the same. One earthquake has a magnitude value, but its intensity can vary from place to place.

Causes

  • Tectonic plate movement.
  • Volcanic activity.
  • Collapse of underground cavities or mines.
  • Human-induced seismic activity in certain circumstances.

Earthquake safety

  • Drop, Cover and Hold On during strong shaking.
  • Stay away from glass and heavy objects that may fall.
  • Do not use lifts during an earthquake.
  • After shaking stops, move carefully to a safe open area if necessary.
  • Follow official emergency instructions and avoid damaged buildings.

13. Volcanoes – అగ్నిపర్వతాలు

A volcano is an opening or landform through which magma, gases and volcanic materials reach the Earth's surface.

భూమి అంతర్భాగంలోని మాగ్మా, వాయువులు మరియు అగ్నిపర్వత పదార్థాలు భూమి ఉపరితలానికి చేరే మార్గం లేదా దాని వల్ల ఏర్పడే భౌగోళిక నిర్మాణాన్ని అగ్నిపర్వతం అంటారు.

Types based on activity

  • Active: Erupting now or showing evidence of recent activity and potential to erupt again.
  • Dormant: Not currently erupting but may erupt again.
  • Extinct: Not expected to erupt again under the prevailing scientific assessment.

Types based on form

Shield VolcanoBroad, gently sloping volcano formed mainly by fluid lava. Example: Mauna Loa.
Composite / StratovolcanoSteep-sided volcano built from alternating layers of lava and pyroclastic material. Example: Mount Fuji.
Cinder ConeUsually small, steep-sided cone formed mainly from volcanic fragments.
Lava DomeRounded mound formed by viscous lava accumulating near the vent.

Volcanic materials

  • Lava: Magma that reaches the surface.
  • Ash: Fine volcanic fragments.
  • Volcanic gases: Include water vapour, carbon dioxide and sulphur dioxide.
  • Pyroclastic flows: Fast-moving, hot mixtures of gas and volcanic fragments.

14. Distribution of Volcanoes – అగ్నిపర్వతాల విస్తరణ

Volcanoes are commonly concentrated near plate boundaries, especially subduction zones and divergent boundaries.

  • Pacific Ring of Fire: A major belt of volcanoes and earthquakes around the Pacific Ocean.
  • Mid-Ocean Ridges: Volcanic activity occurs where plates move apart.
  • Hotspots: Volcanic activity can occur within plates, such as the Hawaiian Islands.

Volcanoes in India

  • Barren Island: Located in the Andaman Sea; recognised as India's active volcano.
  • Narcondam Island: Volcanic island in the Andaman Sea, generally classified as dormant.
Barren Island is an important Geography fact for Indian competitive examinations.

15. Earthquake and Volcano Zones

Earthquakes and volcanoes are commonly found around tectonic plate boundaries, but their patterns are not identical.

  • Subduction zones commonly produce powerful earthquakes and volcanic arcs.
  • Transform boundaries commonly produce earthquakes but usually do not create volcanic arcs.
  • Divergent boundaries produce shallow earthquakes and volcanic activity.
  • Hotspots can produce volcanoes away from plate boundaries.
