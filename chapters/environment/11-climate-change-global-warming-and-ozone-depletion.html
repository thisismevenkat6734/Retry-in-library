1. Weather and Climate | వాతావరణ స్థితి మరియు శీతోష్ణస్థితి

Weather: The short-term condition of the atmosphere at a particular place and time. It includes temperature, rainfall, humidity, wind and cloud cover.

వాతావరణ స్థితి (Weather): ఒక ప్రాంతంలో ఒక నిర్దిష్ట సమయంలో ఉండే ఉష్ణోగ్రత, వర్షపాతం, గాలి, తేమ వంటి వాతావరణ పరిస్థితులు.

Climate: The long-term pattern of weather conditions in a region, generally assessed over periods of about 30 years or more.

శీతోష్ణస్థితి (Climate): ఒక ప్రాంతంలోని దీర్ఘకాలిక వాతావరణ పరిస్థితుల సగటు మరియు వాటి మార్పుల ధోరణి.

WeatherClimate
Short-term atmospheric conditions.Long-term weather patterns.
May change within hours or days.Describes patterns over decades.
Example: Rain today.Example: A region's typical monsoon pattern.

1.1 Components of climate

  • Temperature: Degree of hotness or coldness of the atmosphere.
  • Precipitation: Water falling from the atmosphere as rain, snow, sleet or hail.
  • Humidity: Amount of water vapour present in air.
  • Atmospheric pressure: Force exerted by the weight of air.
  • Wind: Movement of air from higher-pressure areas towards lower-pressure areas.
  • Cloud cover: Portion of the sky covered by clouds.

1.2 Climate system

The climate system consists of five major interacting components:

  1. Atmosphere: The gaseous envelope surrounding Earth.
  2. Hydrosphere: Earth's water bodies, including oceans, rivers and lakes.
  3. Cryosphere: Frozen water, including glaciers, ice sheets, sea ice and snow.
  4. Lithosphere: Earth's solid outer part, including rocks and land surfaces.
  5. Biosphere: All living organisms and ecosystems.

వాతావరణ వ్యవస్థలో వాయుమండలం, జలావరణం, మంచు ప్రాంతాలు, భూభాగం మరియు జీవావరణం పరస్పరం ప్రభావితం చేసుకుంటాయి.

1.3 Climate variability and climate change

  • Climate variability: Natural variations in climate conditions over different time periods.
  • Climate change: A long-term shift in climate patterns, caused by natural processes, human activities or both.
  • Anthropogenic climate change: Climate change resulting from human activities such as fossil-fuel combustion and deforestation.

2. Greenhouse Effect and Global Warming | హరితగృహ ప్రభావం మరియు భూతాపం

2.1 Greenhouse effect

The greenhouse effect is a natural process in which certain atmospheric gases absorb and re-emit outgoing infrared radiation, warming Earth's surface and lower atmosphere.

భూమి నుంచి వెలువడే ఇన్‌ఫ్రారెడ్ వికిరణాన్ని కొన్ని వాయువులు గ్రహించి తిరిగి విడుదల చేయడం వల్ల భూమి ఉపరితలం మరియు దిగువ వాయుమండలం వేడిగా ఉంటాయి. దీనిని హరితగృహ ప్రభావం అంటారు.

How the greenhouse effect works

  1. Sunlight reaches Earth, mainly as shortwave radiation.
  2. Some incoming sunlight is reflected by clouds, atmosphere and Earth's surface.
  3. Earth absorbs part of the energy and warms up.
  4. The warm surface emits energy as infrared radiation.
  5. Greenhouse gases absorb and re-emit some of this outgoing infrared radiation.
  6. This natural process keeps Earth warmer than it would otherwise be.

2.2 Major greenhouse gases

GasImportant sources or characteristics
Carbon dioxide (CO₂)Fossil-fuel burning, deforestation, cement production and other industrial activities.
Methane (CH₄)Livestock, rice cultivation, landfills, wetlands and fossil-fuel production.
Nitrous oxide (N₂O)Agricultural soils, nitrogen fertilisers, industrial processes and combustion.
Water vapourMajor natural greenhouse gas; atmospheric concentration is strongly influenced by temperature and the water cycle.
Ozone (O₃)Acts as a greenhouse gas in the troposphere and protects life from UV radiation in the stratosphere.
Fluorinated gasesIncludes HFCs, PFCs, SF₆ and NF₃; many have high global warming potential.
Important: Carbon dioxide is a major long-lived greenhouse gas emitted by human activities. Water vapour is important in the natural greenhouse effect and acts mainly as a climate feedback.

2.3 Global warming

Global warming refers specifically to the long-term increase in Earth's average surface temperature, primarily due to increased concentrations of greenhouse gases from human activities.

మానవ కార్యకలాపాల వల్ల గ్రీన్‌హౌస్ వాయువుల సాంద్రత పెరిగి భూమి సగటు ఉష్ణోగ్రత దీర్ఘకాలంగా పెరగడాన్ని గ్లోబల్ వార్మింగ్ అంటారు.

2.4 Natural and human causes

Natural factors:

  • Volcanic eruptions.
  • Changes in solar output.
  • Natural variations in ocean-atmosphere systems.
  • Long-term changes in Earth's orbit and axial orientation.

Human factors:

  • Burning coal, petroleum and natural gas.
  • Deforestation and land-use changes.
  • Industrial production and cement manufacturing.
  • Agriculture and livestock emissions.
  • Waste disposal and landfill methane emissions.
  • Use and leakage of certain fluorinated gases.

2.5 Carbon dioxide and the carbon cycle

Carbon naturally moves among the atmosphere, oceans, living organisms, soils and rocks. Human activities transfer additional carbon into the atmosphere, particularly through fossil-fuel combustion and land-use changes.

కార్బన్ వాయుమండలం, సముద్రాలు, మొక్కలు, జంతువులు, నేల మరియు శిలల మధ్య చలనం చేస్తుంది. శిలాజ ఇంధనాల దహనం వల్ల వాయుమండలంలో అదనపు కార్బన్ డయాక్సైడ్ చేరుతుంది.

2.6 Global Warming Potential (GWP)

Global Warming Potential compares the warming effect of a greenhouse gas with that of carbon dioxide over a specified time horizon.

  • Carbon dioxide is assigned a GWP of 1 for the selected assessment framework.
  • Methane and many fluorinated gases have higher GWP values per unit mass than CO₂.
  • GWP values depend on the assessment and time horizon used.

CO₂ equivalent (CO₂e): A common unit used to express the combined climate impact of different greenhouse gases.

3. Impacts of Climate Change | వాతావరణ మార్పుల ప్రభావాలు

3.1 Rising temperatures

  • More frequent or intense heat extremes in many regions.
  • Increased heat stress among humans and animals.
  • Changes in crop growth and agricultural productivity.
  • Higher cooling demand and pressure on electricity systems.

3.2 Melting glaciers and ice sheets

Warming causes glaciers and ice sheets to lose mass. This contributes to sea-level rise and can affect water availability in glacier-fed river systems.

ఉష్ణోగ్రత పెరగడం వల్ల హిమానీనదాలు మరియు మంచు పలకలు కరుగుతాయి. దీనివల్ల సముద్ర మట్టం పెరగడమే కాకుండా కొన్ని నదుల నీటి లభ్యతపై ప్రభావం పడవచ్చు.

3.3 Sea-level rise

Major causes include:

  • Thermal expansion: Seawater expands as it warms.
  • Melting land ice: Water from glaciers and ice sheets enters oceans.

Sea-level rise increases risks of coastal flooding, erosion, saltwater intrusion and damage to coastal ecosystems.

3.4 Changes in rainfall patterns

  • Some regions may experience increased rainfall or intense rainfall events.
  • Other regions may experience prolonged dry conditions.
  • Monsoon timing and distribution may be affected.
  • Water availability may become less predictable.

3.5 Extreme weather events

Climate change can influence the frequency or intensity of certain extremes, including heatwaves, heavy rainfall and agricultural or ecological droughts. The effect varies by region and event type.

3.6 Ocean changes

  • Ocean warming: Increase in ocean heat content and sea-surface temperatures.
  • Ocean acidification: Ocean water absorbs CO₂, which changes seawater chemistry and lowers pH.
  • Coral bleaching: Heat stress can cause corals to lose symbiotic algae or their pigments.
  • Deoxygenation: Some ocean regions experience declining dissolved oxygen.

3.7 Impacts on biodiversity

  • Species may shift their geographical ranges or move to higher elevations.
  • Changes in seasonal timing can disturb breeding and migration.
  • Coral reefs, wetlands and mountain ecosystems face serious risks.
  • Species with limited ability to adapt or move may face increased extinction risk.

3.8 Impacts on agriculture and food security

  • Heat stress can reduce crop yields.
  • Changes in rainfall can affect irrigation and soil moisture.
  • Pests and crop diseases may change their distribution.
  • Extreme weather can damage crops, livestock and infrastructure.

3.9 Impacts on human health

  • Heat-related illness and mortality.
  • Changes in the distribution of some vector-borne diseases.
  • Health risks associated with floods and droughts.
  • Food and water insecurity.
  • Respiratory and cardiovascular stress during certain extreme conditions.
Exam Point: Climate change is not only an environmental issue. It also affects agriculture, health, water resources, biodiversity, infrastructure and economic development.

4. Climate Feedbacks and Tipping Elements | వాతావరణ ప్రతిచర్యలు

4.1 Climate feedback

A climate feedback is a process that either amplifies or reduces an initial climate change.

FeedbackExplanation
Ice-albedo feedbackMelting ice exposes darker surfaces that absorb more sunlight, causing additional warming.
Water vapour feedbackWarmer air can hold more water vapour, which strengthens the greenhouse effect.
Cloud feedbackChanges in clouds can alter how much sunlight is reflected and how much heat is retained.
Carbon-cycle feedbackWarming can alter carbon storage and release from ecosystems and oceans.

4.2 Positive and negative feedback

  • Positive feedback: Amplifies the initial change.
  • Negative feedback: Reduces or counteracts the initial change.

పాజిటివ్ ఫీడ్‌బ్యాక్ ప్రారంభ మార్పును పెంచుతుంది. నెగటివ్ ఫీడ్‌బ్యాక్ ప్రారంభ మార్పును తగ్గించే దిశగా పనిచేస్తుంది.

4.3 Tipping elements

A tipping element is a part of the climate system that may undergo a large or potentially abrupt change after crossing a critical threshold.

Examples often discussed in climate science include major ice sheets, certain ocean circulation systems, coral reef ecosystems and parts of the Amazon rainforest.

5. Mitigation and Adaptation | తగ్గింపు మరియు అనుకూలనం

5.1 Climate change mitigation

Mitigation means reducing greenhouse gas emissions or increasing the removal of greenhouse gases from the atmosphere.

గ్రీన్‌హౌస్ వాయువుల విడుదలను తగ్గించడం లేదా వాటిని వాతావరణం నుంచి తొలగించే సామర్థ్యాన్ని పెంచడం మిటిగేషన్ అంటారు.

  • Expanding renewable energy such as solar and wind.
  • Improving energy efficiency in buildings and industries.
  • Promoting public transport and cleaner mobility.
  • Protecting forests and restoring degraded ecosystems.
  • Reducing methane emissions from waste and agriculture.
  • Improving industrial processes and low-carbon technologies.
  • Encouraging sustainable consumption and production.

5.2 Climate change adaptation

Adaptation means adjusting natural or human systems to actual or expected climate impacts in order to reduce harm or make use of possible opportunities.

ప్రస్తుత లేదా భవిష్యత్తులో ఎదురయ్యే వాతావరణ మార్పుల ప్రభావాలకు అనుగుణంగా మారి నష్టాలను తగ్గించుకోవడాన్ని అడాప్టేషన్ అంటారు.

  • Developing drought-resistant crop varieties.
  • Improving water conservation and rainwater harvesting.
  • Building flood-resilient infrastructure.
  • Creating heat action plans and early warning systems.
  • Restoring mangroves and coastal ecosystems.
  • Improving disaster preparedness and public health systems.
MitigationAdaptation
Addresses the causes of climate change.Addresses the impacts of climate change.

5.3 Carbon sinks

A carbon sink absorbs more carbon than it releases over a given period.

  • Forests and other terrestrial ecosystems.
  • Oceans.
  • Soils and certain geological formations.

Carbon sequestration: The process of capturing and storing atmospheric carbon dioxide or carbon in long-lived reservoirs.

6. Ozone Layer and Ozone Depletion | ఓజోన్ పొర మరియు దాని క్షీణత

6.1 What is ozone?

Ozone (O₃) is a molecule consisting of three oxygen atoms.

It is present in different parts of the atmosphere and has different effects depending on its location.

Stratospheric ozoneTropospheric ozone
Located mainly in the stratosphere.Located in the lower atmosphere.
Absorbs much of the Sun's harmful ultraviolet radiation.Air pollutant harmful to human health and vegetation at elevated levels.
Essential for protecting life.Can contribute to photochemical smog.

6.2 Ozone layer

The ozone layer is a region of the stratosphere with relatively high ozone concentration. It absorbs much of the Sun's harmful UV-B radiation and nearly all UV-C radiation reaching the atmosphere.

సమతాప మండలంలో అధికంగా ఓజోన్ ఉండే ప్రాంతాన్ని ఓజోన్ పొర అంటారు. ఇది సూర్యుడి నుంచి వచ్చే హానికరమైన అతినీలలోహిత కిరణాలను గ్రహిస్తుంది.

6.3 Ultraviolet radiation

TypeCharacteristics
UV-ALonger wavelength; reaches Earth's surface and contributes to skin ageing and other effects.
UV-BPartly absorbed by ozone; excessive exposure can cause skin cancer, cataracts and other harm.
UV-CVery harmful; absorbed by oxygen and ozone in the atmosphere under natural conditions.

6.4 Ozone depletion

Ozone depletion refers to the reduction of stratospheric ozone concentration due to chemical reactions involving ozone-depleting substances.

ఓజోన్‌ను విచ్ఛిన్నం చేసే రసాయన పదార్థాల ప్రభావంతో సమతాప మండలంలోని ఓజోన్ పరిమాణం తగ్గడాన్ని ఓజోన్ క్షీణత అంటారు.

6.5 Major ozone-depleting substances (ODS)

  • Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs).
  • Halons.
  • Carbon tetrachloride.
  • Methyl chloroform.
  • Hydrochlorofluorocarbons (HCFCs).
  • Methyl bromide.

6.6 How CFCs destroy ozone

  1. CFCs can reach the stratosphere because they are relatively stable in the lower atmosphere.
  2. Ultraviolet radiation breaks down CFC molecules.
  3. Chlorine atoms are released.
  4. Chlorine participates in catalytic reactions that convert ozone into oxygen.
  5. A chlorine atom can take part in many ozone-destroying reaction cycles before being removed from the active cycle.

అతినీలలోహిత కిరణాల ప్రభావంతో CFCలు విచ్ఛిన్నమై క్లోరిన్ అణువులను విడుదల చేస్తాయి. ఈ క్లోరిన్ రసాయన చర్యల ద్వారా ఓజోన్‌ను ఆక్సిజన్‌గా మార్చడంలో పాల్గొంటుంది.

6.7 Antarctic ozone hole

The Antarctic ozone hole is a seasonal, severe depletion of stratospheric ozone over Antarctica, especially during Southern Hemisphere spring.

  • Extremely low temperatures in the polar stratosphere support the formation of polar stratospheric clouds.
  • Chemical reactions on these clouds make chlorine compounds more reactive.
  • When sunlight returns in spring, rapid ozone destruction occurs.
  • The Antarctic ozone hole is strongly associated with human-made ozone-depleting substances.

6.8 Effects of ozone depletion

  • Increased UV-B exposure at Earth's surface.
  • Higher risk of skin cancer and sun-related skin damage.
  • Increased risk of cataracts and other eye damage.
  • Potential suppression of parts of the immune system.
  • Damage to phytoplankton and some aquatic organisms.
  • Adverse effects on certain crops and terrestrial plants.
Remember: Ozone depletion and global warming are different environmental problems. Some ozone-depleting substances are also powerful greenhouse gases, so the issues can be connected.

7. Ozone Protection and Montreal Protocol | ఓజోన్ పరిరక్షణ

7.1 Vienna Convention, 1985

The Vienna Convention for the Protection of the Ozone Layer was adopted in 1985. It established a framework for international cooperation on ozone protection, scientific research and information exchange.

7.2 Montreal Protocol, 1987

The Montreal Protocol on Substances that Deplete the Ozone Layer was adopted in 1987. It controls the production and consumption of ozone-depleting substances through agreed phase-out schedules.

  • It is an international environmental agreement.
  • It targets substances responsible for ozone depletion.
  • It includes different control schedules for developed and developing countries.
  • It has been strengthened through later amendments.

7.3 Kigali Amendment, 2016

The Kigali Amendment to the Montreal Protocol was adopted in 2016. It provides for the phasedown of hydrofluorocarbons (HFCs).

Important: HFCs generally do not deplete the ozone layer, but many have high global warming potential. Their phasedown is therefore important for climate protection.

7.4 Alternatives and responsible cooling

  • Use refrigerants with lower climate impact where technically and safely suitable.
  • Prevent refrigerant leakage from air conditioners and refrigeration systems.
  • Recover and recycle refrigerants through trained technicians.
  • Improve energy efficiency of cooling appliances.
  • Ensure safe disposal of old refrigeration equipment.

8. International Climate Agreements and Institutions | అంతర్జాతీయ వాతావరణ ఒప్పందాలు

8.1 United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change (UNFCCC)

The UNFCCC was adopted in 1992 at the Rio Earth Summit and entered into force in 1994. It provides the international framework for cooperation on climate change.

8.2 Kyoto Protocol, 1997

The Kyoto Protocol was adopted in 1997 under the UNFCCC. It established legally binding emission-reduction targets for developed countries that were parties to its commitments during the relevant commitment periods.

8.3 Paris Agreement, 2015

The Paris Agreement was adopted in 2015 under the UNFCCC.

  • It aims to hold the increase in global average temperature to well below 2°C above pre-industrial levels.
  • It seeks efforts to limit the temperature increase to 1.5°C above pre-industrial levels.
  • Countries submit Nationally Determined Contributions (NDCs).
  • It includes a global stocktake process to assess collective progress.
  • It addresses mitigation, adaptation and support for developing countries.

8.4 IPCC

The Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC) was established in 1988 by the World Meteorological Organization (WMO) and the United Nations Environment Programme (UNEP).

It assesses scientific knowledge about climate change, its impacts, risks, adaptation and mitigation. It does not conduct climate research itself as its primary function.

8.5 COP

COP means Conference of the Parties. It is the meeting of parties to an international convention, such as the UNFCCC COP for climate negotiations.

9. Climate Change in India | భారతదేశంలో వాతావరణ మార్పులు

9.1 Major vulnerabilities

  • Dependence of agriculture on monsoon rainfall.
  • Heatwaves in several inland and urban regions.
  • Flooding associated with intense rainfall and river systems.
  • Coastal erosion, cyclones and sea-level rise risks.
  • Himalayan glacier and mountain ecosystem changes.
  • Water stress and groundwater pressure.

9.3 State-level action

States prepare and implement climate-related strategies and action plans suited to local vulnerabilities, development priorities and national policy frameworks.

9.4 Andhra Pradesh and Telangana: Important concerns

Andhra Pradesh:

  • Coastal vulnerability to cyclones, storm surges and sea-level rise.
  • Risks to agriculture from changing rainfall and temperature.
  • Water-resource management challenges.
  • Importance of mangroves and coastal ecosystems as natural protective systems.

Telangana:

  • Heat stress and urban heat-island effects.
  • Rainfall variability and drought risks.
  • Water conservation and groundwater management.
  • Climate-resilient agriculture and urban planning.

9.5 Local climate action

  • Rainwater harvesting and watershed development.
  • Urban tree cover and green spaces.
  • Efficient irrigation and soil-moisture conservation.
  • Early warning systems for cyclones, floods and heatwaves.
  • Energy-efficient buildings and appliances.
  • Protection of wetlands, forests and mangroves.
