1. Constitutional Provisions for Environment | పర్యావరణానికి సంబంధించిన రాజ్యాంగ నిబంధనలు

The Constitution of India provides an important foundation for environmental protection through Directive Principles of State Policy, Fundamental Duties and judicial interpretation of Fundamental Rights.

భారత రాజ్యాంగం ఆదేశిక సూత్రాలు, ప్రాథమిక విధులు మరియు ప్రాథమిక హక్కుల ద్వారా పర్యావరణ పరిరక్షణకు పునాది కల్పిస్తుంది.

1.1 Article 48A – Protection and improvement of environment

Article 48A directs the State to endeavour to protect and improve the environment and safeguard the forests and wildlife of the country.

ఆర్టికల్ 48A ప్రకారం పర్యావరణాన్ని రక్షించడం, మెరుగుపరచడం మరియు అడవులు, వన్యప్రాణులను సంరక్షించడం రాష్ట్ర బాధ్యత.

It was inserted by the 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976.

1.2 Article 51A(g) – Fundamental Duty

Article 51A(g) makes it a fundamental duty of every citizen of India to protect and improve the natural environment, including forests, lakes, rivers and wildlife, and to have compassion for living creatures.

అడవులు, సరస్సులు, నదులు, వన్యప్రాణులు సహా సహజ పర్యావరణాన్ని కాపాడటం మరియు జీవుల పట్ల కరుణ చూపడం ప్రతి భారత పౌరుడి ప్రాథమిక విధి.

It was also introduced through the 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976.

1.3 Article 21 – Right to Life

Article 21 guarantees the right to life and personal liberty. The Supreme Court has interpreted the right to life to include aspects of a clean and healthy environment.

ఆర్టికల్ 21 జీవించే హక్కు మరియు వ్యక్తిగత స్వేచ్ఛను కల్పిస్తుంది. న్యాయస్థానాలు ఆరోగ్యకరమైన పర్యావరణాన్ని కూడా జీవించే హక్కుతో అనుసంధానించాయి.

1.5 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976

  • Added Article 48A to the Directive Principles of State Policy.
  • Added Article 51A(g) to Fundamental Duties.
  • Moved forests and protection of wild animals and birds from the State List to the Concurrent List.
Exam Point: Article 48A = State's responsibility. Article 51A(g) = Citizens' fundamental duty. Article 21 = Right to life, interpreted to include environmental dimensions.

2. Major Environmental Laws in India | భారతదేశంలోని ముఖ్యమైన పర్యావరణ చట్టాలు

2.1 Indian Forest Act, 1927

The Indian Forest Act, 1927 is an important law dealing with forests, forest produce and transit of forest produce. It provides a framework for classifying and regulating certain categories of forests.

అటవీ ప్రాంతాలు, అటవీ ఉత్పత్తులు మరియు వాటి రవాణాకు సంబంధించిన నియంత్రణలకు ఇండియన్ ఫారెస్ట్ యాక్ట్, 1927 ఒక ముఖ్యమైన చట్టం.

2.2 Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972

This Act provides legal protection for wild animals, birds and plants and establishes a framework for protected areas and wildlife conservation.

వన్యప్రాణులు, పక్షులు మరియు కొన్ని వృక్షజాతుల సంరక్షణకు ఈ చట్టం చట్టపరమైన రక్షణ కల్పిస్తుంది.

Important provisions and concepts include:

  • Protection of specified wild animals and plants.
  • Establishment and management of protected areas such as national parks and wildlife sanctuaries.
  • Regulation of hunting and trade in wildlife and wildlife products.
  • Wildlife conservation authorities and advisory bodies.

The Wildlife (Protection) Amendment Act, 2022 introduced changes including provisions relating to CITES implementation and rationalisation of schedules.

2.3 Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1974

The main objective is to prevent and control water pollution and maintain or restore the wholesomeness of water.

నీటి కాలుష్యాన్ని నివారించడం, నియంత్రించడం మరియు నీటి నాణ్యతను కాపాడటం ఈ చట్టం ప్రధాన ఉద్దేశం.

  • Established the Central Pollution Control Board framework.
  • Provides for State Pollution Control Boards.
  • Regulates discharge of pollutants into streams and wells under the Act.
  • Provides for standards, monitoring and enforcement mechanisms.

The Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Amendment Act, 2024 introduced changes to the regulatory framework. Its application and commencement provisions should be read with relevant notifications and state applicability.

2.4 Forest (Conservation) Act, 1980

The law was originally enacted to restrict the dereservation of forests and use of forest land for non-forest purposes without prior approval of the Central Government, subject to its provisions.

అటవీ భూమిని అటవీయేతర అవసరాలకు మళ్లించడాన్ని నియంత్రించడానికి ఈ చట్టం రూపొందించబడింది.

Current name: Following the 2023 amendment, the law is titled the Van (Sanrakshan Evam Samvardhan) Adhiniyam, 1980.

  • Regulates specified diversion of forest land for non-forest purposes.
  • Provides a framework for prior Central Government approval in covered cases.
  • Supports forest conservation and management.
  • Includes provisions amended in 2023.

2.5 Air (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1981

The objective is to prevent, control and reduce air pollution.

గాలి కాలుష్యాన్ని నివారించడం, నియంత్రించడం మరియు తగ్గించడం ఈ చట్టం ప్రధాన లక్ష్యం.

  • Provides for Central and State Pollution Control Board functions.
  • Enables declaration of air pollution control areas.
  • Provides for emission standards and regulatory measures.
  • Supports monitoring and enforcement against air pollution.

2.6 Environment (Protection) Act, 1986

This is commonly described as an umbrella legislation for environmental protection in India.

భారతదేశంలో పర్యావరణ పరిరక్షణకు విస్తృతమైన చట్టపరమైన ఆధారాన్ని అందించే చట్టం Environment (Protection) Act, 1986.

It was enacted following the 1972 Stockholm Conference and came into force on 19 November 1986.

  • Empowers the Central Government to take measures to protect and improve the environment.
  • Provides a broad framework for environmental standards.
  • Supports regulation of hazardous substances.
  • Provides a basis for rules and notifications on waste, environmental impact assessment and other matters.
  • Contains powers relating to inspection, directions and enforcement.
Important: The Jan Vishwas (Amendment of Provisions) Act, 2023 amended provisions of the Environment (Protection) Act, with relevant changes brought into force from 1 April 2024. Do not rely on outdated penalty descriptions when studying current law.

2.7 Public Liability Insurance Act, 1991

This Act provides for immediate relief to persons affected by accidents involving hazardous substances, on the basis of no-fault liability as provided by the law.

ప్రమాదకర పదార్థాల ప్రమాదాల వల్ల నష్టపోయిన వ్యక్తులకు తక్షణ ఉపశమనం కల్పించడానికి ఈ చట్టం రూపొందించబడింది.

2.8 Biological Diversity Act, 2002

The Act aims at:

  1. Conservation of biological diversity.
  2. Sustainable use of its components.
  3. Fair and equitable sharing of benefits arising from the use of biological resources and associated knowledge.

జీవ వైవిధ్య పరిరక్షణ, వనరుల సుస్థిర వినియోగం మరియు వాటి ద్వారా లభించే ప్రయోజనాల సమానమైన భాగస్వామ్యం ఈ చట్టం ముఖ్య లక్ష్యాలు.

Important institutions created or recognised under this framework:

  • National Biodiversity Authority (NBA).
  • State Biodiversity Boards (SBBs).
  • Biodiversity Management Committees (BMCs).

The Biological Diversity (Amendment) Act, 2023 amended the original law.

2.9 National Green Tribunal Act, 2010

The Act established the National Green Tribunal (NGT) for effective and expeditious disposal of environmental cases and related matters.

పర్యావరణానికి సంబంధించిన వివాదాలను వేగంగా పరిష్కరించడానికి నేషనల్ గ్రీన్ ట్రిబ్యునల్ చట్టం, 2010 ద్వారా NGT ఏర్పాటైంది.

  • Deals with civil cases involving substantial environmental questions under the scheduled enactments.
  • Provides for relief and compensation in appropriate cases.
  • Can order restitution of damaged property and the environment.
  • Applies principles such as sustainable development, precautionary principle and polluter pays principle.

2.10 Compensatory Afforestation Fund Act, 2016

This Act provides a framework for the collection and utilisation of funds related to compensatory afforestation and associated activities.

అటవీ భూమి మళ్లింపుకు సంబంధించిన పరిహార అటవీకరణ నిధుల సేకరణ మరియు వినియోగానికి ఈ చట్టం వ్యవస్థను కల్పిస్తుంది.

2.11 Forest Rights Act, 2006

The Scheduled Tribes and Other Traditional Forest Dwellers (Recognition of Forest Rights) Act, 2006 recognises certain forest rights of eligible Scheduled Tribes and other traditional forest dwellers.

అర్హులైన షెడ్యూల్డ్ తెగలు మరియు ఇతర సంప్రదాయ అటవీ నివాసుల అటవీ హక్కులను గుర్తించడానికి ఈ చట్టం రూపొందించబడింది.

  • Recognises individual and community forest rights subject to legal conditions.
  • Recognises community rights over certain forest resources.
  • Includes responsibilities relating to conservation and protection of forests and biodiversity.

3. Environmental Institutions in India | భారతదేశ పర్యావరణ సంస్థలు

3.1 Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change (MoEFCC)

MoEFCC is the principal Union Government ministry responsible for environmental and forest policies, conservation programmes, climate-related policy and environmental legislation.

పర్యావరణం, అటవీ సంరక్షణ, వాతావరణ విధానాలు మరియు సంబంధిత చట్టాల అమలులో కేంద్ర ప్రభుత్వ ప్రధాన మంత్రిత్వ శాఖ MoEFCC.

3.2 Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB)

CPCB is a statutory organisation established under the Water Act, 1974. It also performs functions under the Air Act, 1981 and Environment (Protection) Act, 1986.

  • Advises the Central Government on pollution control matters.
  • Coordinates activities of State Pollution Control Boards.
  • Provides technical guidance and conducts or supports monitoring.
  • Develops or recommends standards under applicable laws.
  • Collects and disseminates pollution-related information.

కేంద్ర కాలుష్య నియంత్రణ మండలి దేశవ్యాప్తంగా కాలుష్య నియంత్రణకు సాంకేతిక మార్గదర్శకత్వం, సమన్వయం మరియు పర్యవేక్షణలో ముఖ్యపాత్ర పోషిస్తుంది.

3.3 State Pollution Control Boards (SPCBs)

  • Implement pollution-control laws at the state level.
  • Monitor industrial emissions and effluents.
  • Grant or regulate consents under applicable laws.
  • Inspect facilities and take enforcement action within legal powers.
  • Advise state governments on pollution-control matters.

రాష్ట్ర కాలుష్య నియంత్రణ మండళ్లు రాష్ట్రంలో పరిశ్రమలు, కాలుష్య వనరుల పర్యవేక్షణ మరియు చట్టాల అమలులో పనిచేస్తాయి.

3.4 National Green Tribunal (NGT)

NGT is a specialised environmental judicial body established under the National Green Tribunal Act, 2010.

  • Provides specialised adjudication of specified environmental disputes.
  • Can grant relief and compensation in appropriate cases.
  • Can order restoration of damaged environments.
  • Is guided by principles of natural justice and statutory provisions.

Principal Bench: New Delhi.

3.5 National Biodiversity Authority (NBA)

The NBA was established under the Biological Diversity Act, 2002.

  • Regulates specified access to biological resources and associated knowledge under the Act.
  • Advises the Central Government on biodiversity conservation matters.
  • Supports fair and equitable benefit sharing.
  • Performs functions relating to biodiversity governance.

3.6 State Biodiversity Boards (SBBs)

State Biodiversity Boards advise state governments and perform regulatory and other functions under the Biological Diversity Act within their jurisdiction.

3.7 Biodiversity Management Committees (BMCs)

BMCs are constituted by local bodies under the Biological Diversity Act framework.

  • Promote conservation and sustainable use of biological resources.
  • Document local biodiversity-related knowledge.
  • Prepare People's Biodiversity Registers (PBRs) as prescribed.
  • Support local participation in biodiversity governance.

3.8 National Tiger Conservation Authority (NTCA)

NTCA is a statutory body constituted under the Wildlife (Protection) Act framework.

  • Provides oversight and guidance for tiger conservation.
  • Supports implementation of Project Tiger.
  • Issues guidelines and monitors tiger reserves under the law.

3.9 Wildlife Crime Control Bureau (WCCB)

WCCB is a statutory multi-disciplinary body under the Wildlife (Protection) Act framework. It helps combat organised wildlife crime and illegal trade in wildlife products.

3.10 National Board for Wildlife (NBWL)

NBWL is a statutory body under the Wildlife (Protection) Act. It advises the Central Government on wildlife conservation and related policy matters.

4.1 National Forest Policy, 1988

The National Forest Policy, 1988 emphasises environmental stability, ecological balance, conservation of natural heritage and people's participation in forest protection.

  • Maintaining environmental stability.
  • Conserving natural forests and biodiversity.
  • Increasing tree cover and restoring degraded forests.
  • Meeting the needs of forest-dependent communities.
  • Encouraging people's participation in forest management.

4.2 National Environment Policy, 2006

The National Environment Policy, 2006 provides a broad policy framework for environmental protection and sustainable development.

Important themes include:

  • Conservation of critical environmental resources.
  • Livelihood security for the poor.
  • Inter-generational equity.
  • Integration of environmental concerns into development planning.
  • Efficiency in environmental resource use.

4.3 National Water Policy, 2012

The National Water Policy, 2012 addresses integrated water-resource planning and management.

  • Integrated water-resource management.
  • Water conservation and efficiency.
  • Protection of water quality.
  • Groundwater management.
  • Consideration of climate change and water security.

5. Important Environmental Legal Principles | పర్యావరణ న్యాయ సూత్రాలు

5.1 Polluter Pays Principle

The polluter should bear the cost of preventing, controlling and remedying pollution and environmental damage, as applicable.

కాలుష్యానికి కారణమైన వ్యక్తి లేదా సంస్థ కాలుష్య నియంత్రణ మరియు పర్యావరణ నష్ట పరిహార ఖర్చులను భరించాలి.

5.2 Precautionary Principle

Where there is a threat of serious or irreversible environmental damage, lack of full scientific certainty should not be used as a reason to postpone appropriate preventive measures.

తీవ్రమైన లేదా తిరిగి సరిచేయలేని పర్యావరణ నష్టం సంభవించే ప్రమాదం ఉన్నప్పుడు పూర్తి శాస్త్రీయ నిర్ధారణ లేదనే కారణంతో ముందస్తు జాగ్రత్త చర్యలను వాయిదా వేయకూడదు.

5.3 Sustainable Development

Development should meet present needs without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs.

భవిష్యత్ తరాల అవసరాలను తీర్చుకునే సామర్థ్యాన్ని దెబ్బతీయకుండా ప్రస్తుత తరాల అవసరాలను తీర్చే అభివృద్ధి సుస్థిర అభివృద్ధి.

5.4 Public Trust Doctrine

Natural resources such as rivers, forests and certain public lands are held by the State in trust for the public. The State has duties to protect these resources for public use and future generations.

5.5 Inter-generational Equity

Present generations should use natural resources responsibly so that future generations also have access to them.

5.6 Environmental Justice

Environmental benefits and burdens should be distributed fairly. No community should bear a disproportionate share of pollution or environmental harm.

5.7 Absolute Liability

In Indian environmental jurisprudence, the absolute liability principle developed in the Oleum Gas Leak case requires enterprises engaged in hazardous or inherently dangerous activities to bear absolute and non-delegable liability for harm caused by accidents arising from such activities, subject to the applicable legal framework.

Exam Point: Polluter Pays = Polluter bears the cost. Precautionary = Prevent possible serious harm. Public Trust = State protects resources for the public. Sustainable Development = Balance present and future needs.

6. Important Environmental Judgments | ముఖ్యమైన పర్యావరణ తీర్పులు

6.1 Rural Litigation and Entitlement Kendra v. State of Uttar Pradesh (Dehradun Quarrying Case)

One of India's early environmental cases involving ecological damage from limestone quarrying in the Mussoorie-Dehradun region. It highlighted the need to balance development with environmental protection.

6.2 M.C. Mehta v. Union of India – Oleum Gas Leak Case

The Supreme Court developed the principle of absolute liability for enterprises engaged in hazardous or inherently dangerous activities.

6.3 Subhash Kumar v. State of Bihar (1991)

The Supreme Court recognised that the right to life under Article 21 includes the right to enjoy pollution-free water and air for full enjoyment of life.

6.4 Vellore Citizens Welfare Forum v. Union of India (1996)

The Supreme Court recognised the precautionary principle and polluter pays principle as essential features of sustainable development in Indian environmental law.

6.5 Indian Council for Enviro-Legal Action v. Union of India (1996)

The case reinforced the polluter pays principle in relation to environmental damage caused by industrial pollution.

6.6 T.N. Godavarman Thirumulpad v. Union of India

A major series of cases concerning forest conservation and the interpretation and protection of forests under Indian law.

6.7 M.C. Mehta v. Union of India – Ganga Pollution Case

Important proceedings concerning industrial pollution and the protection of the River Ganga.

Revision: Oleum Gas Leak – Absolute Liability. Vellore Citizens – Precautionary and Polluter Pays. Subhash Kumar – Pollution-free water and air under Article 21.

7.1 Convention on Biological Diversity (CBD)

CBD was adopted at the 1992 Rio Earth Summit. Its three main objectives are:

  1. Conservation of biological diversity.
  2. Sustainable use of its components.
  3. Fair and equitable sharing of benefits arising from genetic resources.

7.2 Ramsar Convention

The Ramsar Convention is an international treaty focused on wetlands. It was adopted in 1971 in Ramsar, Iran.

Ramsar Site: A wetland designated as internationally important under the Convention.

7.3 CITES

CITES stands for Convention on International Trade in Endangered Species of Wild Fauna and Flora. It regulates international trade in listed species to ensure that trade does not threaten their survival.

7.4 Basel Convention

The Basel Convention controls transboundary movements of hazardous wastes and their disposal, with particular attention to protecting human health and the environment.

7.5 Stockholm Convention

The Stockholm Convention addresses persistent organic pollutants (POPs), which can persist in the environment, accumulate in living organisms and pose risks to health and ecosystems.

8. Environmental Governance in Andhra Pradesh and Telangana | ఆంధ్రప్రదేశ్ మరియు తెలంగాణలో పర్యావరణ పాలన

8.1 Andhra Pradesh

  • Andhra Pradesh Pollution Control Board (APPCB) performs state-level pollution control functions.
  • State forest and wildlife departments implement forest and wildlife conservation measures.
  • Coastal regulation and wetland conservation are important because of the state's coastline and river systems.
  • Environmental clearances and pollution-control consents apply to specified projects and industries under relevant laws.

8.2 Telangana

  • Telangana State Pollution Control Board (TSPCB) performs pollution-control functions in the state.
  • State forest and wildlife departments support conservation and protected-area management.
  • Urban air quality, industrial pollution, water quality and waste management are important governance issues.
  • Local bodies and state agencies participate in environmental planning and implementation.

8.3 Role of local bodies

  • Municipalities and municipal corporations manage local solid waste and sanitation functions.
  • Gram Panchayats can participate in local environmental protection and resource management.
  • Biodiversity Management Committees support local biodiversity governance.
  • Public participation and environmental awareness help improve implementation.
