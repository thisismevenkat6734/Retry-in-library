1. Meaning and Classification of Waste | వ్యర్థాల అర్థం మరియు వర్గీకరణ

Waste: Any material or substance that its holder discards, intends to discard, or is required to discard.

వ్యర్థం: ఉపయోగం లేదని భావించి పారవేసే లేదా పారవేయాల్సిన పదార్థాన్ని వ్యర్థం అంటారు.

Waste is generated by households, industries, agriculture, hospitals, commercial establishments and construction activities.

గృహాలు, పరిశ్రమలు, వ్యవసాయం, ఆసుపత్రులు, వ్యాపార సంస్థలు, నిర్మాణ పనుల వల్ల వ్యర్థాలు ఏర్పడతాయి.

1.1 Classification by physical state | భౌతిక స్థితి ఆధారంగా

  • Solid waste: Paper, food scraps, plastic, metal, glass and discarded materials.
  • Liquid waste: Domestic sewage, industrial effluents and wastewater.
  • Gaseous waste: Harmful gases and emissions released into the atmosphere.

1.2 Classification by biodegradability | కుళ్లిపోయే లక్షణం ఆధారంగా

TypeMeaning and examples
BiodegradableCan be broken down by microorganisms under suitable conditions. Examples: food scraps, leaves and some paper products.
Non-biodegradableDoes not readily decompose through natural biological processes. Examples: many plastics, glass and certain metals.

సూక్ష్మజీవుల ద్వారా సహజంగా కుళ్లిపోయేవి బయోడీగ్రేడబుల్ వ్యర్థాలు. సులభంగా కుళ్లిపోనివి నాన్-బయోడీగ్రేడబుల్ వ్యర్థాలు.

1.3 Classification by risk and source

  • Hazardous waste: Waste that may harm human health or the environment because of properties such as toxicity, corrosivity, flammability or reactivity.
  • Non-hazardous waste: Waste that does not exhibit hazardous characteristics under applicable rules.
  • Municipal solid waste: Waste generated by households, shops, offices and similar urban activities.
  • Industrial waste: Waste produced during manufacturing and industrial processes.
  • Biomedical waste: Waste generated during diagnosis, treatment or immunisation of humans or animals, and related research or healthcare activities.
  • E-waste: Discarded electrical and electronic equipment and covered components.
  • Construction and demolition waste: Concrete, bricks, tiles, wood, metals and other debris from construction or demolition.
  • Agricultural waste: Crop residues, husk, animal waste and other farm-related residues.
  • Radioactive waste: Waste containing radioactive substances that require specialised handling and disposal.
Exam Point: Not every industrial or electronic waste item is automatically hazardous. Its characteristics and applicable legal classification determine how it must be handled.

2. Waste Management Hierarchy | వ్యర్థాల నిర్వహణ ప్రాధాన్యత క్రమం

Waste management aims to prevent waste generation, reduce resource consumption, recover useful materials and minimise environmental damage.

వ్యర్థాలు ఏర్పడకుండా చూడటం, తగ్గించడం, తిరిగి ఉపయోగించడం, రీసైక్లింగ్ చేయడం మరియు చివరగా సురక్షితంగా పారవేయడం వ్యర్థాల నిర్వహణ లక్ష్యం.

Waste Hierarchy – Preferred order

  1. Refuse: Avoid unnecessary products and single-use items.
  2. Reduce: Consume fewer resources and generate less waste.
  3. Reuse: Use a product again without major processing.
  4. Repair: Fix damaged products instead of discarding them.
  5. Refurbish: Restore an old product to a usable condition.
  6. Recycle: Process waste into new materials or products.
  7. Recover: Recover useful materials or energy from waste.
  8. Dispose: Safely dispose of residual waste that cannot be practically recovered.

Important concepts

  • 5R approach: Commonly expressed as Refuse, Reduce, Reuse, Repurpose and Recycle. Different educational frameworks may use different R-terms.
  • Circular economy: An economic system that keeps products and materials in use for as long as possible and reduces waste.
  • Linear economy: Traditional take-make-use-dispose model.
  • Resource efficiency: Achieving more value with fewer natural resources.
  • Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR): A policy approach that assigns producers responsibility for managing the post-consumer stage of specified products or packaging under applicable rules.
Remember: Prevention and reduction are generally preferred over recycling and disposal because they avoid waste generation in the first place.

3. Municipal Solid Waste Management | పురపాలక ఘన వ్యర్థాల నిర్వహణ

Municipal solid waste management includes segregation, collection, transportation, processing, recovery and safe disposal.

పట్టణ ప్రాంతాల్లో ఏర్పడే ఘన వ్యర్థాలను వేరు చేయడం నుంచి సేకరణ, రవాణా, ప్రాసెసింగ్, రీసైక్లింగ్ మరియు సురక్షిత పారవేత వరకు జరిగే ప్రక్రియను మున్సిపల్ వ్యర్థాల నిర్వహణ అంటారు.

3.1 Segregation at source | ఉత్పత్తి స్థలంలోనే వేరు చేయడం

Waste should be separated where it is generated. A common household system includes:

CategoryExamplesTypical management
Wet / biodegradableFood scraps, vegetable peels, garden wasteComposting or biomethanation where suitable
Dry / recyclablePaper, cardboard, clean plastic, glass, metalSorting and material recovery
Domestic hazardousBatteries, certain chemicals, paint containers, some lampsSeparate collection and authorised handling
Sanitary wasteUsed sanitary productsSafe wrapping and separate handling as required by local rules

తడి వ్యర్థాలు, పొడి వ్యర్థాలు, ఇంటి ప్రమాదకర వ్యర్థాలను కలపకుండా వేర్వేరుగా ఉంచాలి.

3.2 Collection and transportation

  • Door-to-door collection reduces uncontrolled dumping.
  • Separate collection helps prevent recyclable materials from becoming contaminated.
  • Covered vehicles reduce littering and leakage during transportation.
  • Transfer stations may be used when waste must be moved from small collection vehicles to larger vehicles.

3.3 Material Recovery Facility (MRF)

An MRF is a facility where collected dry waste is sorted into recyclable material streams such as paper, metals, plastics and glass.

పేపర్, ప్లాస్టిక్, లోహాలు, గాజు వంటి పునర్వినియోగ పదార్థాలను వేరు చేసే కేంద్రాన్ని Material Recovery Facility అంటారు.

3.4 Composting

Composting is the controlled biological decomposition of organic waste into a stable, soil-like product called compost.

  • Requires suitable moisture, oxygen and a balanced mix of organic materials.
  • Produces compost that can improve soil structure.
  • Proper management helps control odour, pests and unwanted contamination.

సేంద్రియ వ్యర్థాలను సూక్ష్మజీవుల సహాయంతో కుళ్లబెట్టి కంపోస్ట్ తయారు చేసే ప్రక్రియ కంపోస్టింగ్.

3.5 Vermicomposting

Vermicomposting uses earthworms and microorganisms to convert suitable biodegradable organic waste into nutrient-rich vermicompost.

వానపాములు మరియు సూక్ష్మజీవుల సహాయంతో సేంద్రియ వ్యర్థాలను ఎరువుగా మార్చే ప్రక్రియ వర్మీకంపోస్టింగ్.

3.6 Anaerobic digestion and biomethanation

Anaerobic digestion is the breakdown of organic matter by microorganisms in the absence of oxygen.

  • Produces biogas, mainly methane and carbon dioxide.
  • Biogas can be used as a fuel after appropriate handling.
  • The remaining digestate may be used as a soil amendment when it meets quality and safety requirements.

ఆక్సిజన్ లేని పరిస్థితుల్లో సూక్ష్మజీవులు సేంద్రియ పదార్థాలను విచ్ఛిన్నం చేసి బయోగ్యాస్ ఉత్పత్తి చేసే ప్రక్రియను అనారోబిక్ డైజెషన్ అంటారు.

3.7 Waste-to-Energy

Waste-to-energy technologies recover usable energy from suitable waste streams. They may include controlled combustion, refuse-derived fuel and other thermal or biological processes.

Limitations: Mixed wet waste, high moisture, poor segregation, air emissions, high capital cost and ash disposal can reduce performance. Waste-to-energy should not replace waste prevention, segregation and recycling.

Landfill gas: Gas produced by decomposition of organic matter, often containing methane and carbon dioxide.

వ్యర్థాల గుండా నీరు ప్రవహించి కలుషితమైన ద్రవాన్ని లీచేట్ అంటారు. సేంద్రియ వ్యర్థాల కుళ్ళడం వల్ల మీథేన్ వంటి వాయువులు ఏర్పడతాయి.

4. Plastic Pollution | ప్లాస్టిక్ కాలుష్యం

Plastic pollution occurs when plastic materials accumulate in the environment and cause harm to ecosystems, wildlife and human well-being.

ప్లాస్టిక్ పదార్థాలు పర్యావరణంలో పేరుకుపోయి జీవులకు, పర్యావరణ వ్యవస్థలకు హాని కలిగించడాన్ని ప్లాస్టిక్ కాలుష్యం అంటారు.

4.1 Types of plastic waste

  • Single-use plastics: Products designed primarily for one use before disposal.
  • Macroplastics: Larger visible plastic pieces.
  • Microplastics: Plastic particles generally smaller than 5 millimetres.
  • Primary microplastics: Manufactured or released directly in very small sizes.
  • Secondary microplastics: Formed when larger plastic items fragment due to sunlight, weathering, abrasion and other processes.

4.2 Sources of microplastics

  • Fragmentation of plastic bags, bottles and packaging.
  • Synthetic textile fibres released during washing.
  • Tyre and road-wear particles.
  • Paint and coating particles.
  • Some industrial processes and product uses.

4.3 Environmental impacts

  • Animals may swallow plastic, mistaking it for food.
  • Plastic waste can entangle marine animals and birds.
  • Plastic fragments can enter rivers, lakes, soil and oceans.
  • Microplastics have been detected in many environmental compartments and food chains.
  • Plastic waste can block drains and contribute to urban flooding.
  • Burning plastics in the open can release harmful pollutants.
  • Some plastic products contain additives that may create additional environmental or health concerns.

4.4 Marine plastic pollution

Plastic reaches the ocean through rivers, coastal activities, littering, fishing-related sources and inadequate waste management. Marine organisms may ingest plastic or become entangled in discarded nets and packaging.

నదులు, తీర ప్రాంతాలు, చేపల వేట పరికరాలు మరియు సరైన వ్యర్థాల నిర్వహణ లేకపోవడం వల్ల ప్లాస్టిక్ సముద్రాల్లోకి చేరుతుంది.

4.5 Plastic pollution prevention

  1. Avoid unnecessary single-use products.
  2. Use durable reusable bags, bottles and containers.
  3. Segregate plastic waste at source.
  4. Support collection and recycling systems.
  5. Prevent littering and open burning.
  6. Promote reusable and responsibly designed alternatives.
  7. Follow applicable plastic waste management rules.
Exam Point: Recycling is useful, but not all plastics are equally recyclable. Prevention, reduction, reuse and better product design are also essential.

5. Electronic Waste (E-Waste) | ఎలక్ట్రానిక్ వ్యర్థాలు

E-waste consists of discarded electrical and electronic equipment and relevant components covered by applicable rules.

పనిచేయని లేదా ఉపయోగం ముగిసిన మొబైల్ ఫోన్లు, కంప్యూటర్లు, టీవీలు, ఛార్జర్లు మరియు ఇతర ఎలక్ట్రానిక్ పరికరాల వ్యర్థాలను ఈ-వేస్ట్ అంటారు.

5.1 Examples

  • Mobile phones, tablets and computers.
  • Televisions and monitors.
  • Printers and electronic accessories.
  • Electrical and electronic appliances covered by applicable rules.
  • Printed circuit boards, cables and certain batteries or components, depending on the applicable regulatory category.

5.2 Environmental concerns

Some electronic products contain lead, mercury, cadmium, certain flame retardants and other substances of concern. Unsafe dismantling, acid leaching and open burning can expose workers and communities to hazardous materials.

సరైన పద్ధతిలో రీసైక్లింగ్ చేయకపోతే సీసం, పాదరసం, కాడ్మియం వంటి హానికర పదార్థాలు పర్యావరణంలోకి చేరే ప్రమాదం ఉంది.

5.3 Safe e-waste management

  • Use authorised collection and recycling channels.
  • Do not burn wires or circuit boards to recover metals.
  • Do not dismantle hazardous components without appropriate facilities.
  • Encourage repair, refurbishment and reuse where safe.
  • Follow data-security practices before handing over devices.

6. Biomedical and Hazardous Waste | బయోమెడికల్ మరియు ప్రమాదకర వ్యర్థాలు

6.1 Biomedical waste

Biomedical waste is generated during healthcare, diagnosis, treatment, immunisation, medical research and related activities.

ఆసుపత్రులు, క్లినిక్‌లు, ప్రయోగశాలలు మరియు వైద్య సేవల ద్వారా ఏర్పడే ప్రత్యేక వ్యర్థాలను బయోమెడికల్ వ్యర్థాలు అంటారు.

Examples

  • Contaminated dressings and materials.
  • Used needles and other sharps.
  • Discarded blood-contaminated materials.
  • Certain laboratory waste and pathological waste.
  • Some pharmaceutical and chemical waste from healthcare activities.

6.2 Safe biomedical waste handling

  • Segregate waste at the point of generation.
  • Use appropriate labelled, colour-coded containers and bags under the applicable rules.
  • Keep sharps in puncture-resistant containers.
  • Ensure safe storage and timely collection.
  • Use authorised treatment and disposal facilities.
  • Train healthcare workers and provide suitable protective equipment.
Important: Biomedical waste colour categories depend on the applicable rules and waste type. Never mix sharps, infectious waste and ordinary household waste.

6.3 Hazardous waste

Hazardous waste may be toxic, corrosive, reactive, flammable or otherwise dangerous.

Examples include certain industrial chemicals, solvents, contaminated containers, some metal-bearing residues and certain process wastes.

విషపూరిత, మండే, తినివేసే లేదా ప్రతిచర్య కలిగించే లక్షణాలున్న వ్యర్థాలను ప్రమాదకర వ్యర్థాలు అంటారు.

Management principles: Identify and classify the waste, label it, store it safely, prevent leaks, use trained handlers, transport through authorised channels and treat or dispose of it in approved facilities.

7. Other Waste Streams | ఇతర వ్యర్థాల రకాలు

7.1 Construction and Demolition Waste

Includes concrete, bricks, tiles, wood, metals, glass, soil and rubble produced by construction, renovation and demolition.

  • Separate reusable materials at the site.
  • Recover metals, wood and other usable materials.
  • Recycle suitable concrete and masonry into aggregates where standards permit.
  • Transport and dispose of residual waste through authorised systems.

7.2 Agricultural waste

Includes crop residues, straw, husk, animal manure and packaging from agricultural activities.

Suitable residues may be composted, used for biogas, converted into useful products or returned to soil through appropriate practices. Uncontrolled burning causes air pollution.

7.3 Wastewater and sludge

Wastewater treatment removes physical, chemical and biological contaminants. Treatment may include primary, secondary and tertiary stages.

  • Primary treatment: Physical removal of settleable and floating solids.
  • Secondary treatment: Biological processes that reduce biodegradable organic matter.
  • Tertiary treatment: Additional treatment to remove nutrients, pathogens or other remaining contaminants as required.
  • Sludge: Semi-solid material separated during wastewater treatment; it requires appropriate treatment and safe management.

8. Waste Management Laws and Institutions in India | భారతదేశంలోని చట్టాలు మరియు సంస్థలు

India has different rules for different waste streams. The following are important for competitive examinations.

8.1 Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR)

EPR makes producers responsible for meeting prescribed obligations relating to collection, recycling, processing or environmentally sound management of specified products or packaging after consumer use.

ఉత్పత్తి చేసిన వస్తువులు వినియోగం తర్వాత వ్యర్థాలుగా మారినప్పుడు వాటి నిర్వహణలో ఉత్పత్తిదారులకు బాధ్యత కల్పించే విధానాన్ని EPR అంటారు.

8.2 PIBOs

In the plastic packaging framework, PIBOs refers to Producers, Importers and Brand Owners. They have specified obligations under applicable plastic waste management provisions.

8.3 Important institutions

  • Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change (MoEFCC): Central ministry responsible for environmental policy and related regulatory frameworks.
  • Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB): National-level pollution control body with monitoring, coordination and technical functions.
  • State Pollution Control Boards (SPCBs): State-level pollution control and regulatory bodies.
  • Urban Local Bodies (ULBs): Important authorities for municipal solid waste management.
  • Authorised recyclers and treatment facilities: Handle specified waste streams according to applicable approvals and standards.
Exam Reminder: The Environment (Protection) Act was enacted in 1986. The year 2016 is important for several waste-management rules. The E-Waste Management Rules, 2022 form a separate regulatory framework.

9. Sustainable Waste Management | సుస్థిర వ్యర్థాల నిర్వహణ

  • Promote waste prevention and responsible consumption.
  • Segregate waste at source.
  • Improve collection and recycling infrastructure.
  • Support composting and biomethanation of suitable organic waste.
  • Prevent open dumping and open burning.
  • Reduce unnecessary packaging and single-use products.
  • Encourage repair, reuse and product-life extension.
  • Protect sanitation workers through training and protective equipment.
  • Include informal waste workers in safer and fairer systems.
  • Build public awareness and community participation.

వ్యర్థాల నిర్వహణలో ప్రభుత్వ సంస్థలతో పాటు ప్రజలు, పరిశ్రమలు, ఉత్పత్తిదారులు మరియు స్థానిక సంస్థల భాగస్వామ్యం అవసరం.
