1. Introduction to Environmental Pollution

Environmental pollution is the introduction of substances or forms of energy into the environment in quantities or conditions that cause harmful changes to living organisms, ecosystems or human well-being.

తెలుగు: గాలి, నీరు, నేల వంటి పర్యావరణ భాగాల్లో హానికర పదార్థాలు లేదా శక్తి చేరి జీవులకు, పర్యావరణ వ్యవస్థలకు నష్టం కలిగించడాన్ని పర్యావరణ కాలుష్యం అంటారు.

Pollutant – కాలుష్య కారకం

A pollutant is a substance or form of energy that causes pollution or contributes to harmful environmental changes.

Examples: Particulate matter, toxic chemicals, sewage, pesticides, heavy metals, excessive heat and unwanted sound.

Sources of Pollution

  • Natural sources: Volcanic eruptions, dust storms, forest fires, pollen and natural decomposition.
  • Human-made sources: Industries, vehicles, power plants, agriculture, mining, sewage and waste disposal.

Classification by Source

  • Point-source pollution: Pollution released from an identifiable source, such as a factory discharge pipe.
  • Non-point-source pollution: Pollution from diffuse sources, such as agricultural runoff from many fields.

Primary and Secondary Pollutants

  • Primary pollutants: Emitted directly into the environment. Examples: Carbon monoxide, sulphur dioxide and some particulate matter.
  • Secondary pollutants: Form in the environment through chemical or physical reactions involving primary pollutants. Examples: Ground-level ozone and some components of photochemical smog.

2. Air Pollution – వాయు కాలుష్యం

Air pollution occurs when harmful gases, particles or biological materials are present in the atmosphere at concentrations that can harm human health, other organisms or the environment.

గాలిలో హానికర వాయువులు, ధూళి కణాలు లేదా ఇతర పదార్థాలు ప్రమాదకర స్థాయిలో చేరడాన్ని వాయు కాలుష్యం అంటారు.

Major Sources of Air Pollution

  • Motor vehicles and road transport.
  • Thermal power plants and industrial emissions.
  • Burning of coal, petroleum and other fossil fuels.
  • Construction, demolition and road dust.
  • Open burning of waste and crop residues.
  • Household solid fuels and inefficient cooking systems.
  • Mining, quarrying and certain industrial processes.
  • Natural events such as dust storms and wildfires.

Particulate Matter – PM10 and PM2.5

  • PM10: Particles with aerodynamic diameter generally 10 micrometres or less.
  • PM2.5: Fine particles with aerodynamic diameter generally 2.5 micrometres or less.
  • PM2.5 can penetrate deep into the respiratory system and may enter the bloodstream indirectly through biological processes.
  • Both are important indicators in air-quality monitoring.

PM2.5 అనేవి చాలా సూక్ష్మమైన ధూళి కణాలు. ఇవి ఊపిరితిత్తుల లోతైన భాగాలకు చేరగలవు.

Smog – పొగమంచు కాలుష్యం

Smog is a polluted atmospheric condition involving smoke, fog or chemical pollutants.

  • Classical or sulphurous smog: Historically associated with coal combustion, smoke, sulphur dioxide and fog in cool, humid conditions.
  • Photochemical smog: Forms when nitrogen oxides and VOCs react in sunlight, producing ozone and other oxidants.

Acid Rain – ఆమ్ల వర్షం

Acid deposition occurs when sulphur dioxide and nitrogen oxides are transformed in the atmosphere into acidic compounds that reach the ground through rain, snow, fog or dry deposition.

  • Can acidify lakes and soils.
  • May damage sensitive aquatic organisms and forests.
  • Can corrode buildings and monuments, especially those made of limestone or marble.
  • Can contribute to nutrient loss from soils.

SO₂ మరియు NOx వాయువులు వాతావరణంలో మార్పులకు గురై ఆమ్ల పదార్థాలుగా ఏర్పడి వర్షం లేదా పొడి నిక్షేపణ ద్వారా భూమికి చేరవచ్చు. దీనిని Acid Deposition అంటారు.

Photochemical Smog Formation

  • Vehicle and industrial emissions release nitrogen oxides and VOCs.
  • Sunlight drives chemical reactions between these substances.
  • Ground-level ozone and other oxidants can form.
  • These pollutants irritate the respiratory system and damage vegetation.

Effects of Air Pollution

  • Asthma attacks and respiratory irritation.
  • Reduced lung function and increased respiratory disease risk.
  • Cardiovascular impacts associated with fine particulate exposure.
  • Reduced visibility due to haze.
  • Damage to crops, forests and natural vegetation.
  • Acid deposition and damage to buildings.
  • Contribution of some air pollutants to climate change.

Air Pollution Control Measures

  • Use cleaner fuels and cleaner energy technologies.
  • Promote public transport, walking and cycling.
  • Maintain vehicles and enforce emission standards.
  • Install suitable pollution-control equipment in industries.
  • Control construction and road dust.
  • Prevent open burning of waste and crop residues.
  • Improve household cooking energy and ventilation.
  • Increase green cover using appropriate native vegetation.
  • Monitor air quality and enforce pollution-control rules.

3. Water Pollution – జల కాలుష్యం

Water pollution occurs when physical, chemical or biological substances change water quality and make it harmful or unsuitable for its intended uses or aquatic life.

నీటిలో హానికర రసాయనాలు, సూక్ష్మజీవులు లేదా వ్యర్థాలు చేరి నీటి నాణ్యత దెబ్బతినడాన్ని జల కాలుష్యం అంటారు.

Sources of Water Pollution

  • Untreated domestic sewage.
  • Industrial effluents and chemicals.
  • Agricultural runoff containing fertilisers and pesticides.
  • Oil spills and petroleum leakage.
  • Plastic waste and microplastics.
  • Mining and heavy-metal contamination.
  • Thermal discharges from power plants and industries.
  • Pathogens from human and animal waste.

Biochemical Oxygen Demand (BOD)

BOD is the amount of dissolved oxygen required by microorganisms to break down biodegradable organic matter in water under specified test conditions.

  • Higher BOD often indicates greater biodegradable organic pollution.
  • Microorganisms consume oxygen while decomposing organic matter.
  • Excessive organic pollution can reduce dissolved oxygen available to aquatic organisms.

నీటిలోని కుళ్లే సేంద్రియ పదార్థాలను సూక్ష్మజీవులు విచ్ఛిన్నం చేయడానికి అవసరమైన కరిగిన ఆక్సిజన్ పరిమాణాన్ని BOD అంటారు. సాధారణంగా BOD ఎక్కువగా ఉంటే సేంద్రియ కాలుష్యం ఎక్కువగా ఉండే అవకాశం ఉంది.

Chemical Oxygen Demand (COD)

COD measures the oxygen equivalent required to chemically oxidise substances in a water sample using a specified test method.

  • It can indicate chemically oxidisable pollution.
  • COD is not identical to BOD.
  • COD results are often available faster than standard BOD test results.

Dissolved Oxygen (DO)

DO is the amount of oxygen dissolved in water and available to aquatic organisms.

  • Many aquatic organisms require adequate DO to survive.
  • Organic pollution can reduce DO because microorganisms consume oxygen.
  • Low DO can cause stress or death of fish and other aquatic organisms.

Eutrophication – పోషక అధిక్యత

Eutrophication is the enrichment of a water body with nutrients, particularly nitrogen and phosphorus, which can stimulate excessive algal or aquatic plant growth.

నత్రజని, భాస్వరం వంటి పోషకాలు నీటిలో అధికంగా చేరి ఆల్గీ మరియు నీటి మొక్కలు విపరీతంగా పెరగడాన్ని Eutrophication అంటారు.

Process of Eutrophication

  1. Fertilisers or sewage add excess nutrients to water.
  2. Algae and aquatic plants grow rapidly.
  3. Algal blooms may block sunlight and affect aquatic plants.
  4. Dead organic matter is decomposed by microorganisms.
  5. Decomposition consumes dissolved oxygen.
  6. Low oxygen can cause fish kills and damage aquatic ecosystems.

Biomagnification – జీవ విస్తరణ

Biomagnification is the increase in concentration of certain persistent pollutants in organisms at successively higher trophic levels of a food chain.

ఆహార గొలుసులో పై స్థాయి జీవుల శరీరాల్లో కొన్ని నిలకడైన విషపదార్థాల సాంద్రత పెరుగుతూ ఉండే ప్రక్రియను Biomagnification అంటారు.

Example: Certain persistent pollutants such as methylmercury can biomagnify in aquatic food webs.

Bioaccumulation

Bioaccumulation is the build-up of a substance in an organism over time when uptake exceeds elimination.

Biomagnification concerns increasing concentration across trophic levels; bioaccumulation concerns build-up within an organism.

Water Pollution Control Measures

  • Treat domestic sewage before discharge.
  • Use effluent treatment plants in industries.
  • Reduce excessive fertiliser and pesticide use.
  • Prevent dumping of solid waste into water bodies.
  • Protect wetlands and riverbank vegetation.
  • Monitor groundwater and surface-water quality.
  • Promote safe sanitation and wastewater reuse.
  • Control industrial chemicals and heavy-metal discharge.

4. Soil Pollution – నేల కాలుష్యం

Soil pollution occurs when harmful chemicals, wastes or other contaminants accumulate in soil at levels that adversely affect organisms, soil functions or human health.

నేలలో హానికర రసాయనాలు, పురుగుమందులు, భారీ లోహాలు లేదా వ్యర్థాలు పేరుకుపోయి నేల నాణ్యతను దెబ్బతీయడాన్ని Soil Pollution అంటారు.

Major Sources of Soil Pollution

  • Excessive use of chemical fertilisers and pesticides.
  • Industrial waste and chemical spills.
  • Improper disposal of municipal solid waste.
  • Mining waste and metal contamination.
  • Oil and petroleum leakage.
  • Untreated sewage sludge.
  • Electronic waste and battery waste.
  • Plastic waste and microplastics.

Major Soil Contaminants

ContaminantPossible Source
LeadBattery recycling, some industrial activities and contaminated waste
MercuryIndustrial processes and certain waste streams
CadmiumSome fertilisers, batteries and industrial wastes
Pesticide residuesAgricultural pesticide use
Petroleum hydrocarbonsOil spills and fuel leakage
Excess nutrientsOveruse of fertilisers and manure

Effects of Soil Pollution

  • Reduces soil fertility and biological activity.
  • Can harm earthworms, soil microbes and other organisms.
  • May contaminate crops and enter food chains.
  • Can contaminate groundwater through leaching.
  • May reduce agricultural productivity.
  • Some toxic substances can cause long-term health effects.

Bioaccumulation in Food Chains

Some contaminants enter plants from soil and can pass to herbivores and predators through food chains. The degree of transfer depends on the chemical, organism and environmental conditions.

Soil Pollution Prevention

  • Use fertilisers according to soil tests and crop needs.
  • Adopt integrated pest management.
  • Dispose of hazardous waste safely.
  • Prevent untreated industrial discharge onto land.
  • Promote composting of suitable organic waste.
  • Control mining waste and restore mined land.
  • Use safe methods for e-waste and battery recycling.
  • Monitor contaminated land and groundwater.

Soil Remediation Techniques

  • Bioremediation: Use microorganisms to break down or transform certain contaminants.
  • Phytoremediation: Use plants to remove, stabilise or contain certain contaminants.
  • Soil washing: Separate contaminants from soil using suitable physical or chemical processes.
  • Containment: Isolate contaminated soil to reduce exposure and spread.

5. Noise Pollution – శబ్ద కాలుష్యం

Noise pollution is unwanted or excessive sound that interferes with normal activities or can harm human health and well-being.

అవసరానికి మించిన లేదా ఇబ్బంది కలిగించే శబ్దం వల్ల మనుషులు, జంతువులు మరియు పర్యావరణానికి కలిగే హానిని శబ్ద కాలుష్యం అంటారు.

Sources of Noise Pollution

  • Road traffic and vehicle horns.
  • Aircraft and railway operations.
  • Industrial machinery and generators.
  • Construction activities.
  • Loudspeakers and high-volume music.
  • Firecrackers and public events.
  • Household appliances and commercial activities.

Measurement of Sound

  • Sound level is commonly expressed in decibels (dB).
  • Environmental noise is often measured using the A-weighted scale, written as dB(A).
  • Decibels use a logarithmic scale; a small numerical increase can represent a substantial change in sound intensity.
  • Sound exposure depends on both intensity and duration.

Effects of Noise Pollution

  • Hearing impairment from excessive sound exposure.
  • Sleep disturbance and fatigue.
  • Stress, annoyance and reduced concentration.
  • Interference with communication and learning.
  • Possible cardiovascular effects associated with long-term exposure.
  • Disturbance of wildlife communication, breeding and movement.

Noise Pollution Control

  • Maintain vehicles and machines properly.
  • Use silencers and sound barriers where appropriate.
  • Restrict unnecessary honking.
  • Control loudspeaker use and observe legal time restrictions.
  • Use acoustic insulation in buildings.
  • Plan industrial zones away from sensitive residential areas where feasible.
  • Develop green belts as one supporting measure.
  • Use hearing protection in high-noise workplaces.

Noise Pollution Rules in India

The Noise Pollution (Regulation and Control) Rules, 2000 provide a framework for ambient noise standards and control of noise-producing sources.

Vulnerable Groups

  • Children and older adults.
  • People with respiratory or cardiovascular conditions.
  • Pregnant women and developing foetuses.
  • Outdoor workers and people living near major pollution sources.
  • Communities dependent on polluted rivers, lakes or contaminated land.

Environmental Justice

Environmental justice concerns fair treatment and meaningful participation of all people in environmental decisions, regardless of income or social background. Pollution burdens often affect vulnerable communities disproportionately.

7. Pollution Monitoring and Control Technologies

Air Pollution Monitoring

  • Air-quality monitoring stations measure pollutants such as PM2.5, PM10, SO₂, NO₂, CO and ozone.
  • Continuous monitoring helps identify pollution trends and episodes.
  • Emission monitoring checks pollution released from industrial sources.

Air Pollution Control Equipment

  • Electrostatic Precipitator: Removes particulate matter from industrial exhaust using electrical charges.
  • Bag Filter: Captures dust particles using fabric filter bags.
  • Scrubber: Uses liquid or other media to remove selected gases or particles from exhaust streams.
  • Catalytic Converter: Helps reduce harmful vehicle exhaust pollutants such as carbon monoxide, hydrocarbons and nitrogen oxides in suitable systems.

Wastewater Treatment

  1. Primary treatment: Physical removal of large solids and settleable suspended matter.
  2. Secondary treatment: Biological treatment to reduce biodegradable organic matter.
  3. Tertiary or advanced treatment: Additional removal of nutrients, pathogens, salts or other pollutants depending on the treatment design.

Soil Remediation

  • Bioremediation uses microorganisms.
  • Phytoremediation uses plants.
  • Containment prevents further spread of contamination.
  • Excavation and safe treatment may be used for severely contaminated soil.

8. Pollution Control in India – Laws and Institutions

Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1974

  • Provides a legal framework to prevent and control water pollution.
  • Provides for Central and State Pollution Control Boards.
  • Regulates certain discharges and pollution-control requirements.

Air (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1981

  • Provides a framework for prevention, control and abatement of air pollution.
  • Empowers pollution control boards to perform functions under the Act.
  • Supports the regulation of industrial emissions and air pollution control areas.

Environment (Protection) Act, 1986

  • Provides a broad framework for protection and improvement of the environment.
  • Empowers the Central Government to take measures to protect environmental quality.
  • Supports environmental standards and rules.

Noise Pollution (Regulation and Control) Rules, 2000

  • Provide ambient noise standards for different area categories.
  • Regulate the use of loudspeakers and public-address systems.
  • Provide a framework for control of noise sources.

Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB)

  • Established in 1974 under the Water Act.
  • Provides technical services and national coordination in pollution control.
  • Advises the Central Government on matters relating to pollution prevention and control.
  • Coordinates with State Pollution Control Boards.
  • Supports pollution monitoring and environmental standards.

State Pollution Control Boards (SPCBs)

  • Operate at the state level under relevant laws.
  • Monitor and regulate pollution sources within their jurisdiction.
  • Implement consent and compliance requirements under applicable laws.
  • Conduct inspections, monitoring and enforcement functions.

National Green Tribunal (NGT)

  • Established under the National Green Tribunal Act, 2010.
  • Provides specialised adjudication of environmental disputes within its jurisdiction.
  • Deals with cases involving environmental protection and compensation for environmental damage as provided by law.

Andhra Pradesh and Telangana

  • Andhra Pradesh Pollution Control Board (APPCB) carries out pollution-control functions in Andhra Pradesh.
  • Telangana State Pollution Control Board (TSPCB) performs pollution-control functions in Telangana.
  • Both boards deal with monitoring, industrial compliance, water and air pollution control and related environmental functions.

9. Important Terms for Competitive Exams

  • AQI: Air Quality Index; communicates air-quality conditions through a simplified index.
  • BOD: Biochemical Oxygen Demand; oxygen required by microorganisms to break down biodegradable organic matter.
  • COD: Chemical Oxygen Demand; oxygen equivalent used to chemically oxidise substances in a water sample.
  • DO: Dissolved Oxygen; oxygen present in water.
  • Eutrophication: Nutrient enrichment of water leading to excessive plant or algal growth.
  • Biomagnification: Increase of certain pollutant concentrations at higher trophic levels.
  • Bioaccumulation: Build-up of a substance within an organism over time.
  • PM2.5: Fine particulate matter of 2.5 micrometres or less.
  • PM10: Particulate matter of 10 micrometres or less.
  • Smog: Polluted atmospheric haze involving smoke and chemical pollutants.
  • Acid Deposition: Deposition of acidic substances through wet or dry processes.
  • Bioremediation: Use of microorganisms to treat contamination.
  • Phytoremediation: Use of plants to remove, stabilise or contain contaminants.
  • Leq: Equivalent continuous sound level over a specified period.
