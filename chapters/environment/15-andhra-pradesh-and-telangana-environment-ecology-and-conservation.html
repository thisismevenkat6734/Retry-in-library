1. Introduction to Regional Ecology | ప్రాంతీయ జీవావరణ పరిచయం

Andhra Pradesh and Telangana are located in the south-eastern part of India. Their environments include plateaus, hills, forests, rivers, wetlands, agricultural landscapes and settlements.

ఆంధ్రప్రదేశ్ మరియు తెలంగాణ భారతదేశ దక్షిణ-తూర్పు ప్రాంతంలో ఉన్నాయి. ఈ రాష్ట్రాల్లో పీఠభూములు, కొండలు, అడవులు, నదులు, చిత్తడి నేలలు, వ్యవసాయ భూములు మరియు పట్టణ ప్రాంతాలు ఉన్నాయి.

1.1 Major ecological features

  • Eastern Ghats: Important hill ranges and forest ecosystems, especially in Andhra Pradesh.
  • Deccan Plateau: A major geographical feature of Telangana and parts of Andhra Pradesh.
  • Coastal ecosystems: Beaches, estuaries, mangroves and coastal wetlands of Andhra Pradesh.
  • River ecosystems: Godavari, Krishna, Penna and their tributaries.
  • Forest ecosystems: Dry deciduous, moist deciduous, scrub and other forest types.
  • Wetlands: Lakes, reservoirs, tanks, marshes and riverine wetlands.

1.2 Eastern Ghats

The Eastern Ghats are discontinuous hill ranges along India's eastern side. They pass through parts of Andhra Pradesh and extend into neighbouring states.

తూర్పు కనుమలు భారతదేశ తూర్పు ప్రాంతంలో విస్తరించి ఉన్న విడివిడిగా కనిపించే కొండ శ్రేణులు. ఇవి ఆంధ్రప్రదేశ్‌లోని అనేక ప్రాంతాల జీవవైవిధ్యానికి ముఖ్యమైనవి.

  • Support diverse forest ecosystems.
  • Provide habitats for endemic and threatened species.
  • Influence local climate and water systems.
  • Support tribal livelihoods and forest-dependent communities.
  • Contain important biodiversity landscapes such as Nallamala and Seshachalam.

1.3 Deccan Plateau

The Deccan Plateau is characterised by extensive uplands, rocky terrain, seasonal streams and different types of dry forest and scrub ecosystems.

  • Important for dry deciduous vegetation.
  • Supports grasslands, scrublands and wildlife habitats.
  • Contains several river basins and reservoirs.
  • Faces pressures from land-use change, mining and urban expansion in some areas.

2. Environment of Andhra Pradesh | ఆంధ్రప్రదేశ్ పర్యావరణం

2.1 Geographical and ecological features

Andhra Pradesh has a long coastline along the Bay of Bengal and diverse inland landscapes.

  • Eastern Ghats hill ranges.
  • Coastal plains and estuaries.
  • Godavari and Krishna river deltas.
  • Dry and moist deciduous forests.
  • Red sanders habitats in the Seshachalam region.
  • Coastal mangroves and marine ecosystems.

2.3 Red Sanders (Red Sandalwood)

Scientific name: Pterocarpus santalinus.

Red Sanders is a valuable tree species native to parts of the southern Eastern Ghats, particularly associated with the Seshachalam region of Andhra Pradesh.

  • It is valued for its distinctive red-coloured heartwood.
  • It is threatened by illegal harvesting and smuggling.
  • Its conservation requires habitat protection and control of illegal trade.
  • It is included in international trade-control arrangements under CITES.

2.5 Nagarjunasagar-Srisailam Tiger Reserve landscape

The Nagarjunasagar-Srisailam Tiger Reserve landscape is an important tiger conservation area associated with the Nallamala forests.

  • Located across parts of Andhra Pradesh and the wider Nallamala landscape.
  • Known for dry deciduous and mixed forest habitats.
  • Supports tigers, leopards, sloth bears, deer and other wildlife.
  • Includes important riverine and forest ecosystems.
  • Conservation involves habitat protection, anti-poaching measures and community participation.

2.6 Seshachalam Biosphere Reserve

The Seshachalam Biosphere Reserve is located in the Eastern Ghats of Andhra Pradesh.

  • Known for red sanders and diverse plant life.
  • Includes forested hills and unique ecological habitats.
  • Supports birds, mammals, reptiles and many plant species.
  • Important for conservation, research and sustainable resource management.

3. Environment of Telangana | తెలంగాణ పర్యావరణం

3.1 Geographical features

Telangana is primarily located on the Deccan Plateau. Its environmental systems include dry and moist deciduous forests, scrublands, rivers, reservoirs and wetlands.

  • Godavari and Krishna river basins.
  • Deccan Plateau and rocky landscapes.
  • Nallamala and other forest landscapes.
  • Seasonal streams and tanks.
  • Urban lakes and wetlands.

3.2 Forest ecosystems

  • Southern tropical dry deciduous forests.
  • Mixed deciduous forests.
  • Teak-dominated forest patches.
  • Thorn scrub and dry woodland ecosystems.
  • Riverine vegetation in suitable locations.

3.4 Kawal Tiger Reserve

Kawal Tiger Reserve is an important conservation landscape in northern Telangana.

  • Contains dry deciduous forest habitats.
  • Supports tigers, leopards, sloth bears, deer and other wildlife.
  • Forms part of a wider forest landscape with ecological connections to neighbouring regions.
  • Habitat connectivity is important for long-term wildlife conservation.

3.5 Amrabad Tiger Reserve

Amrabad Tiger Reserve is located in the Nallamala forest landscape.

  • Contains rugged hills, valleys and forest ecosystems.
  • Supports tiger habitat and other wildlife.
  • Includes dry deciduous and mixed forest vegetation.
  • Community participation is important for conservation.

3.6 Urban biodiversity in Hyderabad

  • KBR National Park: Important urban forest and biodiversity area.
  • Mrugavani National Park: Protects forest and wildlife habitats near the city.
  • Mahavir Harina Vanasthali: Associated with blackbuck conservation.
  • Urban lakes: Provide water storage and habitat for birds and aquatic organisms.

4. Rivers, Wetlands and Mangroves | నదులు, చిత్తడి నేలలు మరియు మడ అడవులు

4.1 Godavari River

The Godavari is one of India's major peninsular rivers. It flows through parts of Maharashtra, Telangana and Andhra Pradesh before reaching the Bay of Bengal.

  • Supports agriculture and drinking-water systems.
  • Provides habitats for fish and other aquatic organisms.
  • Creates riverine wetlands and delta ecosystems.
  • Supports mangrove habitats in its delta region.

4.2 Krishna River

The Krishna is another major peninsular river flowing through the Deccan region and Andhra Pradesh before reaching the Bay of Bengal.

  • Supports irrigation and drinking-water supply.
  • Important for reservoirs and riverine ecosystems.
  • Its delta supports wetlands and coastal biodiversity.
  • Water allocation and pollution management are important challenges.

4.3 Penna River

The Penna River is an important river system of southern Andhra Pradesh.

  • Supports regional agriculture and water supply.
  • Flows through relatively dry regions.
  • Water availability can vary significantly with rainfall.

4.4 Kolleru Lake

Kolleru Lake is a major freshwater lake located between the Krishna and Godavari delta regions.

  • Important habitat for resident and migratory birds.
  • Supports fish and other aquatic biodiversity.
  • Plays an important role in regional water storage.
  • Faces pressures from pollution, encroachment and changes in land use.

కొల్లేరు సరస్సు కృష్ణా మరియు గోదావరి డెల్టాల మధ్య ఉన్న ముఖ్యమైన మంచినీటి సరస్సు. ఇది వలస పక్షులు మరియు స్థానిక పక్షులకు ముఖ్యమైన ఆవాసం.

4.5 Pulicat Lake

Pulicat is a large brackish-water lagoon located along the Andhra Pradesh–Tamil Nadu coastal region.

  • Supports migratory birds, including flamingos.
  • Provides habitat for fish and aquatic organisms.
  • Supports local fishing communities.
  • Requires management of pollution, sedimentation and habitat disturbance.

4.6 Mangrove ecosystems

Mangroves are salt-tolerant trees and shrubs found in intertidal coastal environments.

Important mangrove regions in Andhra Pradesh include:

  • Godavari delta and Coringa region.
  • Krishna delta.
  • Other suitable estuarine and coastal locations.

Functions of mangroves

  • Reduce coastal erosion.
  • Provide natural protection against waves and storm impacts.
  • Serve as nursery habitats for fish and crustaceans.
  • Store carbon in vegetation and sediments.
  • Support coastal biodiversity and livelihoods.

5.2 Olive Ridley Turtle conservation

Olive Ridley turtles are marine reptiles known for mass nesting behaviour called Arribada, observed at certain nesting beaches.

  • Some beaches along Andhra Pradesh's coast are nesting habitats.
  • Fishing activity can cause accidental capture in fishing gear.
  • Artificial lighting and disturbance can affect nesting and hatchlings.
  • Beach protection and community awareness support conservation.

5.3 Great Indian Bustard

The Great Indian Bustard is a critically endangered bird associated with open grassland and semi-arid landscapes.

  • Threats include habitat loss and fragmentation.
  • Collisions with overhead power lines are a serious threat.
  • Grassland protection is important for its survival.

5.4 Endemic and threatened plant species

  • Red Sanders is an important endemic tree of the southern Eastern Ghats.
  • Eastern Ghats forests support several regionally important plant species.
  • Habitat loss and illegal extraction threaten some rare plants.
  • Botanical research and habitat conservation support plant diversity.

6. Biodiversity Hotspots and Ecological Regions | జీవవైవిధ్య హాట్‌స్పాట్లు

6.1 What is a biodiversity hotspot?

A biodiversity hotspot is a biogeographic region with exceptional levels of endemic plant diversity that has experienced significant habitat loss.

To qualify under the widely used Conservation International criteria, a region must contain at least 1,500 endemic vascular plant species and have lost at least 70% of its original natural vegetation.

6.2 Eastern Himalaya and Western Ghats

India contains parts of several global biodiversity hotspots. The Western Ghats–Sri Lanka hotspot is relevant to southern India's broader biodiversity geography. The Eastern Ghats themselves are an important biodiversity region, but should not automatically be described as a separate global hotspot.

6.3 Local biodiversity importance

  • Eastern Ghats forests support diverse and specialised habitats.
  • Nallamala forests are important for large mammals and forest biodiversity.
  • Seshachalam supports red sanders and distinctive plant diversity.
  • Godavari and Krishna deltas support mangroves and aquatic biodiversity.
  • Kolleru and Pulicat support important wetland bird communities.

7. Environmental Problems in Andhra Pradesh and Telangana | పర్యావరణ సమస్యలు

7.1 Deforestation and forest degradation

  • Expansion of agriculture and settlements.
  • Roads and infrastructure development.
  • Illegal tree cutting and forest fires.
  • Mining and quarrying in sensitive landscapes.
  • Fragmentation of wildlife habitats.

7.2 Water pollution

  • Untreated sewage entering rivers and lakes.
  • Industrial wastewater discharge.
  • Agricultural runoff containing fertilisers and pesticides.
  • Plastic and solid waste dumping.
  • Changes in water quality affecting fish and aquatic organisms.

7.3 Air pollution

  • Vehicle emissions in urban areas.
  • Industrial and power-sector emissions.
  • Construction dust.
  • Open burning of waste.
  • Road dust and other particulate sources.

7.4 Soil degradation

  • Soil erosion due to vegetation loss.
  • Excessive chemical input in agriculture.
  • Salinity and waterlogging in some irrigated areas.
  • Mining-related land degradation.
  • Improper disposal of industrial and municipal waste.

7.5 Coastal environmental problems

  • Coastal erosion and shoreline changes.
  • Mangrove degradation.
  • Plastic pollution and marine litter.
  • Habitat disturbance from unplanned development.
  • Impacts of sea-level rise and extreme coastal events.

7.6 Human-wildlife conflict

Human-wildlife conflict occurs when interactions between people and wild animals lead to negative consequences for people, animals or their environment.

  • Crop damage by wild herbivores.
  • Livestock losses caused by predators.
  • Human injury or loss of life.
  • Retaliatory harm to wildlife.
  • Habitat fragmentation and loss of corridors.

7.7 Climate change impacts

  • Heat stress and more intense heat risks.
  • Rainfall variability and drought conditions.
  • Flood risks from intense rainfall.
  • Coastal vulnerability and sea-level rise.
  • Effects on agriculture, water resources and ecosystems.

8. Conservation Programmes and Institutions | సంరక్షణ కార్యక్రమాలు మరియు సంస్థలు

8.1 Forest Department

State Forest Departments are responsible for forest protection, wildlife management, protected-area administration, afforestation and related conservation activities under applicable laws.

8.2 National Tiger Conservation Authority (NTCA)

NTCA provides statutory oversight and guidance for Project Tiger and tiger reserve management in India.

8.3 Wildlife Institute of India (WII)

WII supports wildlife research, training, ecological studies and conservation planning.

8.4 Central Zoo Authority (CZA)

CZA regulates zoos and supports standards for zoo management and conservation breeding in India.

8.5 State Biodiversity Boards

State Biodiversity Boards support implementation of biodiversity conservation provisions, advise state governments and perform functions under the Biological Diversity Act and applicable rules.

8.6 Biodiversity Management Committees (BMCs)

BMCs are local bodies established under the biodiversity law framework to promote conservation, sustainable use and documentation of local biological resources and associated knowledge.

8.7 People's Biodiversity Register (PBR)

A People's Biodiversity Register documents local biological resources, their uses and associated traditional knowledge, following applicable legal procedures.

8.9 Telangana – Haritha Haram

Telanganaku Haritha Haram is a major state plantation and green-cover initiative aimed at increasing vegetation and supporting environmental improvement.

  • Encourages plantation and tree cover.
  • Promotes community participation.
  • Supports urban and rural greenery.
  • Requires suitable species selection and long-term survival monitoring.

8.10 Andhra Pradesh – Forest and wildlife conservation

  • Protection of red sanders and its natural habitats.
  • Management of tiger reserve landscapes.
  • Conservation of mangroves in the Godavari and Krishna deltas.
  • Protection of wetland birds and nesting beaches.
  • Community involvement in forest and wildlife protection.
