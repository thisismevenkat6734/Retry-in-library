1. Food Chain | ఆహార గొలుసు

A food chain is a linear sequence of organisms through which food, nutrients and energy pass as one organism consumes another.

ఒక జీవి మరొక జీవిని ఆహారంగా తీసుకోవడం ద్వారా ఆహారం మరియు శక్తి ఒక జీవి నుంచి మరొక జీవికి క్రమంగా బదిలీ అయ్యే వరుసను ఆహార గొలుసు అంటారు.

Basic components

  1. Producers: Green plants, algae and other autotrophs that manufacture organic matter.
  2. Primary consumers: Organisms that feed directly on producers, generally herbivores.
  3. Secondary consumers: Organisms that feed on primary consumers.
  4. Tertiary consumers: Organisms that feed on secondary consumers.
  5. Top consumers: Organisms occupying the highest feeding position in a particular food chain.
Sunlight → Grass → Grasshopper → Frog → Snake → Eagle

సూర్యకాంతి → గడ్డి → మిడత → కప్ప → పాము → డేగ

The arrows in a food chain indicate the direction of energy transfer: from the organism being eaten to the organism that eats it.

Important characteristics

  • A food chain represents a single feeding pathway.
  • Most food chains begin with photosynthetic producers.
  • Each feeding position represents a trophic level.
  • Energy available generally decreases at higher trophic levels.
  • Food chains are simplified representations of natural feeding relationships.

2. Types of Food Chains | ఆహార గొలుసుల రకాలు

A. Grazing Food Chain (GFC) | మేత ఆహార గొలుసు

A grazing food chain begins with living green plants or other photosynthetic producers. Herbivores feed on producers, followed by carnivores.

జీవించి ఉన్న ఆకుపచ్చ మొక్కలు లేదా ఉత్పాదకులతో ప్రారంభమయ్యే ఆహార గొలుసును Grazing Food Chain అంటారు.

Grass → Grasshopper → Frog → Snake → Hawk
  • First trophic level: Grass (producer).
  • Second trophic level: Grasshopper (primary consumer).
  • Third trophic level: Frog (secondary consumer).
  • Fourth trophic level: Snake (tertiary consumer).
  • Higher trophic level: Hawk (top consumer in this example).

B. Detritus Food Chain (DFC) | శిథిల పదార్థ ఆహార గొలుసు

A detritus food chain begins with dead organic matter such as fallen leaves, dead plants and animal remains.

ఎండిన ఆకులు, చనిపోయిన మొక్కలు, జంతువుల అవశేషాలు వంటి సేంద్రియ పదార్థాలతో ప్రారంభమయ్యే ఆహార గొలుసును Detritus Food Chain అంటారు.

Dead leaves → Earthworm → Bird → Hawk
  • Detritus is the starting material.
  • Detritivores consume and fragment dead organic matter.
  • Predators may feed on detritivores.
  • Decomposers such as bacteria and fungi further break down organic matter.

C. Parasitic Food Chain | పరాన్నజీవి ఆహార గొలుసు

In a parasitic food chain, energy passes from a larger host organism to smaller parasites and sometimes to hyperparasites.

Tree → Bird → Louse → Hyperparasite

ఒక పెద్ద ఆతిథ్య జీవి నుంచి చిన్న పరాన్నజీవులకు, అక్కడి నుంచి హైపర్ పరాన్నజీవులకు శక్తి బదిలీ అవుతుంది.

Comparison

FeatureGrazing Food ChainDetritus Food Chain
Starting pointLiving producersDead organic matter
First consumersUsually herbivoresDetritivores and decomposer-associated organisms
Energy sourceEnergy fixed by producersEnergy stored in detritus
ExampleGrass → Deer → TigerDead leaves → Earthworm → Bird

3. Food Web | ఆహార జాలం

A food web is a network of interconnected food chains in an ecosystem. It represents the complex feeding relationships among organisms.

ఒక పర్యావరణ వ్యవస్థలో అనేక ఆహార గొలుసులు పరస్పరం అనుసంధానమై ఏర్పడే సంక్లిష్ట ఆహార సంబంధాల వ్యవస్థను ఆహార జాలం అంటారు.

Illustrative food web

Grass → Grasshopper → Frog → Snake → Eagle
Grass → Rabbit → Fox
Seeds → Mouse → Snake → Eagle
Plants → Deer → Tiger

This is a simplified illustration. Actual food webs contain many more feeding links.

Characteristics of food webs

  • An organism may consume multiple food sources.
  • A single prey species may be eaten by several predators.
  • Omnivores may feed at more than one trophic level.
  • Food webs show multiple pathways of energy transfer.
  • They provide a more realistic representation of ecosystem feeding relationships than a single food chain.

Ecological importance

  • Food webs connect different populations in an ecosystem.
  • They help explain the effects of changes in one species on other species.
  • Alternative food sources may help some consumers survive when one food source declines.
  • Loss of a species can change feeding relationships and ecosystem structure.
  • They help ecologists understand predator-prey relationships and ecosystem stability.

4. Food Chain vs Food Web | తేడాలు

Food ChainFood Web
Linear feeding pathway.Network of interconnected feeding pathways.
Usually shows one route of energy transfer.Shows several possible routes of energy transfer.
Relatively simple representation.More complex representation.
Less representative of complex natural relationships.Often more realistic for natural ecosystems.
Usually fewer feeding links are shown.Many feeding links are shown.
Exam Tip: A food chain is linear; a food web is interconnected. Do not confuse a food web with an energy pyramid.

5. Trophic Levels | పోషక స్థాయులు

A trophic level is the feeding position occupied by an organism in a food chain.

ఆహార గొలుసులో ఒక జీవి ఆక్రమించే ఆహార లేదా పోషక స్థానాన్ని Trophic Level అంటారు.

LevelCategoryExample
FirstProducersGrass, algae, phytoplankton
SecondPrimary consumersGrasshopper, deer, zooplankton
ThirdSecondary consumersFrog, small carnivorous fish
FourthTertiary consumersSnake, larger predatory fish
HigherTop consumersEagle or tiger in particular food chains

Important points

  • Producers occupy the first trophic level in a typical grazing food chain.
  • Primary consumers occupy the second trophic level.
  • Secondary consumers occupy the third trophic level.
  • Decomposers act on organic matter originating from all trophic levels; they are not restricted to one simple level.
  • Omnivores can occupy more than one trophic level depending on their diet.
The same organism may occupy different trophic positions in different food chains. Trophic position depends on what it eats.

6. Ecological Pyramids | పర్యావరణ పిరమిడ్లు

Ecological pyramids are graphical representations of trophic relationships in an ecosystem. They represent the number, biomass or energy at different trophic levels.

పర్యావరణ వ్యవస్థలో వివిధ పోషక స్థాయుల వద్ద జీవుల సంఖ్య, జీవద్రవ్యరాశి లేదా శక్తిని పిరమిడ్ రూపంలో చూపించడాన్ని Ecological Pyramids అంటారు.

Types of ecological pyramids

  1. Pyramid of Numbers
  2. Pyramid of Biomass
  3. Pyramid of Energy

7. Pyramid of Numbers | సంఖ్యల పిరమిడ్

A pyramid of numbers represents the number of individual organisms present at each trophic level.

A. Upright pyramid of numbers

Commonly illustrated by a grassland ecosystem. Producers are numerous, primary consumers are fewer and higher-level consumers are progressively fewer.

Many grasses
↓
Fewer grasshoppers
↓
Still fewer frogs
↓
Very few snakes

B. Inverted pyramid of numbers

May occur in a tree ecosystem where one large producer supports numerous herbivorous insects and parasites.

One large tree
↓
Many insects
↓
More numerous parasites in some examples

C. Spindle-shaped representation

Some ecosystems show a spindle-shaped pattern in which the number of organisms increases at an intermediate trophic level and decreases at higher levels.

A pyramid of numbers counts individual organisms. It does not measure their size, mass or energy content.

8. Pyramid of Biomass | జీవద్రవ్యరాశి పిరమిడ్

Biomass is the total mass of living biological material present in a specified area or volume at a particular time.

A pyramid of biomass represents the standing biomass at each trophic level.

A. Upright biomass pyramid

Common in many terrestrial ecosystems. Producers such as trees and grasses may have greater standing biomass than herbivores and carnivores.

Large producer biomass
↓
Lower herbivore biomass
↓
Still lower carnivore biomass

B. Inverted biomass pyramid

Can occur in some aquatic ecosystems. Phytoplankton may have lower standing biomass than the zooplankton they support because phytoplankton reproduce and turn over rapidly.

Lower standing biomass of phytoplankton
↓
Higher standing biomass of zooplankton at a given time

Why can aquatic biomass pyramids be inverted?

  • Phytoplankton have rapid reproduction and high turnover.
  • They may be consumed quickly by zooplankton.
  • Standing biomass is a snapshot, not a measure of total production over time.
  • A small standing stock can support a larger consumer stock when production and turnover are rapid.
Biomass is commonly measured as dry mass per unit area or volume. It should not be confused with productivity, which is a rate over time.

9. Pyramid of Energy | శక్తి పిరమిడ్

A pyramid of energy represents the rate of energy flow through each trophic level over a specified period.

Characteristics

  • It is always upright when trophic levels and energy flow are measured consistently.
  • Energy available decreases at successive trophic levels.
  • It accounts for energy transfer over time.
  • It is useful for comparing energy availability across trophic levels.
  • Energy is generally expressed per unit area per unit time.
Producers: 10,000 units
↓
Primary consumers: 1,000 units
↓
Secondary consumers: 100 units
↓
Tertiary consumers: 10 units
The energy pyramid is always upright. Pyramids of numbers and biomass can be upright or inverted.

11. Lindeman's 10% Law | లిండెమాన్ 10% నియమం

Raymond Lindeman developed the trophic-dynamic concept in 1942. The commonly taught 10% law states that, on average, approximately 10% of energy at one trophic level is transferred to the next trophic level.

ఒక పోషక స్థాయిలో ఉన్న శక్తిలో సగటున సుమారు 10 శాతం మాత్రమే తదుపరి పోషక స్థాయికి బదిలీ అవుతుందని 10% నియమం వివరిస్తుంది.

Example calculation

Trophic levelEnergy available
Producers10,000 units
Primary consumers1,000 units
Secondary consumers100 units
Tertiary consumers10 units

Why is energy not transferred completely?

  • Energy is used for respiration and metabolism.
  • Energy is used for movement, growth and other life activities.
  • Not all consumed food is digested or assimilated.
  • Some organic matter is not eaten.
  • Some energy leaves the organism in waste.
  • Energy is ultimately dissipated as heat.
Important: The 10% value is a simplified approximation, not an exact universal rate. Actual trophic transfer efficiency varies with organisms and ecosystems.

12. Ecological Efficiency | పర్యావరణ సామర్థ్యం

Ecological efficiency describes how efficiently energy or production is transferred between trophic levels.

Major types

  • Consumption efficiency: Fraction of production at one trophic level that is consumed by the next level.
  • Assimilation efficiency: Fraction of consumed energy that is absorbed rather than egested.
  • Production efficiency: Fraction of assimilated energy converted into new biomass.
  • Trophic transfer efficiency: Fraction of production at one trophic level that becomes production at the next trophic level.

ఆహార వినియోగం, జీర్ణక్రియ, శరీర జీవక్రియ మరియు కొత్త జీవద్రవ్యరాశి తయారీ వంటి అంశాలు శక్తి బదిలీ సామర్థ్యాన్ని ప్రభావితం చేస్తాయి.

13. Ecological Significance of Food Relationships

  • Population regulation: Predators can influence prey population sizes.
  • Energy transfer: Food relationships move energy through ecosystems.
  • Nutrient movement: Feeding and decomposition move nutrients among organisms and the environment.
  • Community structure: Feeding interactions influence species abundance and distribution.
  • Indirect effects: Changes in one species may affect other species through food-web connections.
  • Conservation: Protecting species and habitats can maintain ecological relationships.

Example: Trophic cascade

A trophic cascade occurs when changes in one trophic level cause indirect effects at other trophic levels.

For example, a change in predator abundance may alter herbivore numbers and consequently influence vegetation.

Trophic cascades show that predators can affect ecosystems indirectly, not only by consuming prey but also by changing prey behaviour and abundance.
