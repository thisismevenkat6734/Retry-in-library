1. Introduction to Disasters | విపత్తుల పరిచయం

Disaster: A serious disruption of the functioning of a community or society, causing widespread human, material, economic or environmental losses and impacts that exceed the ability of the affected community to cope using its own resources.

విపత్తు: ఒక సమాజం లేదా ప్రాంతం సాధారణ కార్యకలాపాలకు తీవ్ర అంతరాయం కలిగించి, ప్రాణనష్టం, ఆస్తి నష్టం, ఆర్థిక నష్టం లేదా పర్యావరణ నష్టాన్ని కలిగించే సంఘటన. ఆ ప్రాంత ప్రజలు తమ సాధారణ వనరులతోనే దాని ప్రభావాలను ఎదుర్కోలేని పరిస్థితి ఏర్పడవచ్చు.

1.1 Hazard, Disaster and Risk

  • Hazard: A potentially damaging event, phenomenon or human activity.
  • Disaster: A serious disruption resulting from the interaction of hazards with exposed and vulnerable people or assets.
  • Disaster risk: The potential losses that may occur due to the interaction of hazard, exposure, vulnerability and capacity.

Hazard అంటే ప్రమాదాన్ని కలిగించే అవకాశం ఉన్న సంఘటన లేదా పరిస్థితి. ప్రతి Hazard తప్పనిసరిగా Disasterగా మారదు. ప్రజలు, ఆస్తులు ప్రమాదానికి గురై, వాటి బలహీనత ఎక్కువగా ఉన్నప్పుడు నష్టం సంభవించే అవకాశం పెరుగుతుంది.

1.2 Disaster Management

Disaster management is the organisation, planning and application of measures for preparing for, responding to and recovering from disasters.

విపత్తులకు ముందస్తు సిద్ధత, విపత్తు సమయంలో స్పందన, సహాయక చర్యలు, పునరావాసం మరియు పునర్నిర్మాణం వంటి చర్యలను ప్రణాళికాబద్ధంగా నిర్వహించడాన్ని విపత్తు నిర్వహణ అంటారు.

1.3 Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR)

Disaster Risk Reduction means preventing new disaster risks, reducing existing risks and managing residual risks through appropriate policies, strategies and actions.

కొత్త విపత్తు ప్రమాదాలు ఏర్పడకుండా నిరోధించడం, ఇప్పటికే ఉన్న ప్రమాదాలను తగ్గించడం మరియు మిగిలిన ప్రమాదాలను సమర్థంగా నిర్వహించడమే DRR లక్ష్యం.

2. Types of Disasters | విపత్తుల రకాలు

2.1 Natural Disasters

Natural disasters are associated with natural hazards. Human exposure, vulnerability and preparedness influence the scale of their consequences.

CategoryExamples
GeologicalEarthquakes, landslides, volcanic eruptions, tsunamis.
HydrologicalFloods, flash floods, avalanches.
MeteorologicalCyclones, storms, lightning, hailstorms.
ClimatologicalDroughts, heatwaves, cold waves, wildfires.
BiologicalEpidemics, pandemics, pest attacks and disease outbreaks.

2.2 Geological Disasters

  • Earthquake: Sudden shaking of the ground caused by the release of energy within the Earth's crust.
  • Landslide: Movement of rock, soil or debris down a slope under gravity.
  • Tsunami: A series of large sea waves commonly generated by undersea earthquakes, volcanic activity or submarine landslides.

2.3 Hydrological Disasters

  • Flood: Overflow of water onto normally dry land.
  • Flash flood: Rapid flooding that develops in a short period, often after intense rainfall or sudden water release.
  • Urban flood: Flooding in urban areas due to intense rainfall, inadequate drainage and other contributing factors.

2.4 Meteorological and Climatological Disasters

  • Cyclone: A rotating low-pressure weather system. Tropical cyclones can produce strong winds, heavy rainfall and storm surges.
  • Drought: A prolonged period of inadequate water availability compared with normal conditions.
  • Heatwave: A period of unusually high temperatures according to the applicable regional criteria.
  • Cold wave: A period of unusually low temperatures that may affect health, agriculture and infrastructure.
  • Lightning: An electrical discharge in the atmosphere that can cause death, injuries, fires and infrastructure damage.

2.5 Biological Disasters

  • Epidemic: Occurrence of disease cases above what is normally expected in a particular community or region.
  • Pandemic: An epidemic that spreads across countries or continents and affects large populations.
  • Pest outbreak: Rapid increase of harmful organisms that damage crops, forests or ecosystems.

2.6 Human-induced Disasters

TypeExamples
IndustrialChemical leaks, factory explosions and industrial fires.
TransportRoad, rail, air and maritime accidents.
FireUrban fires, building fires and major forest fires caused by human activity.
StructuralBuilding collapse, bridge failure and dam-related accidents.
EnvironmentalOil spills, toxic contamination and hazardous waste incidents.
Conflict-relatedWar, displacement and violence-related emergencies.
Remember: A disaster may have natural and human-related contributing factors. Poor land-use planning, unsafe construction and environmental degradation can increase the consequences of natural hazards.

3. Disaster Risk Concepts | విపత్తు ప్రమాదానికి సంబంధించిన భావనలు

3.1 Hazard

A hazard is a potentially damaging physical event, phenomenon or human activity that may cause loss of life, injury, property damage, social and economic disruption or environmental degradation.

3.2 Exposure

Exposure refers to people, infrastructure, housing, economic assets and other resources located in areas that could be affected by hazards.

వరదలు, తుఫానులు లేదా భూకంపాలు సంభవించే ప్రాంతాల్లో ఉన్న ప్రజలు, ఇళ్లు, రహదారులు, పరిశ్రమలు మరియు ఇతర ఆస్తులను Exposure అంటారు.

3.3 Vulnerability

Vulnerability refers to the conditions determined by physical, social, economic and environmental factors that increase the susceptibility of people or assets to the impacts of hazards.

ఒక ప్రమాదం సంభవించినప్పుడు ప్రజలు లేదా ఆస్తులు ఎక్కువ నష్టానికి గురయ్యేలా చేసే పరిస్థితులను Vulnerability అంటారు.

3.4 Capacity

Capacity refers to the strengths, resources, skills and abilities available within a community or organisation to manage and reduce disaster risks.

విపత్తులను ఎదుర్కోవడానికి ఒక సమాజం వద్ద ఉన్న నైపుణ్యాలు, వనరులు, సాంకేతికత, సంస్థాగత బలం మరియు సహాయక వ్యవస్థలను Capacity అంటారు.

3.5 Simple disaster risk relationship

Conceptual relationship:

Disaster Risk increases with Hazard, Exposure and Vulnerability, and decreases as Capacity improves.

ప్రమాదం, Exposure మరియు Vulnerability పెరిగితే విపత్తు ప్రమాదం పెరుగుతుంది. సిద్ధత మరియు సామర్థ్యం పెరిగితే నష్టాలను తగ్గించవచ్చు.

This is a simplified learning relationship, not a universal numerical risk-calculation formula.

3.6 Resilience

Resilience is the ability of a community, system or society exposed to hazards to resist, absorb, accommodate, adapt to, transform and recover from their effects in a timely and efficient manner.

విపత్తు ప్రభావాలను తట్టుకుని, అవసరమైన మార్పులు చేసుకుని, తిరిగి సాధారణ స్థితికి చేరుకునే సమాజం లేదా వ్యవస్థ సామర్థ్యాన్ని Resilience అంటారు.

3.7 Risk assessment

Disaster risk assessment identifies hazards, analyses exposure and vulnerability, evaluates existing capacities and estimates potential consequences.

Common activities include:

  • Hazard identification and mapping.
  • Exposure assessment.
  • Vulnerability analysis.
  • Capacity assessment.
  • Risk evaluation and prioritisation.
  • Preparation of risk-reduction plans.

3.8 Early Warning System (EWS)

An early warning system provides timely and useful information about an impending hazard so that people and authorities can take protective action.

విపత్తు సంభవించే ముందు ప్రజలకు సమయానికి హెచ్చరికలు అందించి ప్రాణ, ఆస్తి నష్టాలను తగ్గించడమే ముందస్తు హెచ్చరిక వ్యవస్థ లక్ష్యం.

Important components:

  1. Disaster risk knowledge.
  2. Hazard monitoring and forecasting.
  3. Communication and dissemination of warnings.
  4. Preparedness and response capability.

4. Disaster Management Cycle | విపత్తు నిర్వహణ చక్రం

Disaster management is commonly explained through interconnected phases. These phases are not always strictly separate; recovery and risk reduction can overlap.

4.1 Prevention

Prevention aims to avoid the creation of disaster risks or prevent hazards from causing disaster impacts where possible.

విపత్తు ప్రమాదాలు ఏర్పడకుండా లేదా వాటి ప్రభావాలు సంభవించకుండా ముందుగానే చర్యలు తీసుకోవడం Prevention.

Examples:

  • Restricting construction in highly hazardous areas.
  • Protecting natural floodplains and wetlands.
  • Enforcing safe land-use planning.
  • Preventing unsafe industrial practices.

4.2 Mitigation

Mitigation reduces the severity or consequences of disasters.

Examples:

  • Earthquake-resistant construction.
  • Flood embankments and improved drainage where appropriate.
  • Cyclone shelters.
  • Retrofitting vulnerable buildings.
  • Coastal ecosystem protection.

4.3 Preparedness

Preparedness develops the knowledge, plans, resources and abilities required to respond effectively.

  • Mock drills and evacuation exercises.
  • Emergency response plans.
  • Training of community volunteers.
  • Emergency communication systems.
  • Stocking essential relief materials.
  • Identifying safe shelters and evacuation routes.

4.4 Response

Response includes actions taken during or immediately after a disaster to save lives, reduce health impacts, ensure public safety and meet the basic needs of affected people.

  • Search and rescue.
  • First aid and emergency medical care.
  • Evacuation and shelter.
  • Food, safe drinking water and sanitation.
  • Emergency communication and coordination.

4.5 Recovery

Recovery restores and improves the livelihoods, health, economic, physical, social, cultural and environmental assets of affected communities.

4.6 Rehabilitation

Rehabilitation restores essential services, livelihoods and community functions after the immediate emergency phase.

4.7 Reconstruction

Reconstruction involves rebuilding damaged infrastructure, housing and public facilities. It should incorporate improved safety standards and risk reduction.

PhaseMain objectiveExample
PreventionAvoid creating riskRestricting unsafe development in flood-prone areas.
MitigationReduce impactRetrofitting buildings.
PreparednessBe readyMock drills and evacuation plans.
ResponseSave lives immediatelySearch and rescue.
RecoveryRestore and improveRebuilding safer infrastructure.
Key exam point: Preparedness happens before a disaster; response occurs during and immediately after it. Recovery includes rehabilitation and reconstruction.

5. Structural and Non-Structural Measures | నిర్మాణాత్మక మరియు నిర్మాణేతర చర్యలు

5.1 Structural measures

Structural measures involve physical construction or engineering works to reduce disaster risk.

  • Flood protection embankments.
  • Stormwater drainage systems.
  • Cyclone shelters.
  • Earthquake-resistant buildings.
  • Retaining walls and slope stabilisation.
  • Coastal protection structures where environmentally appropriate.

5.2 Non-structural measures

Non-structural measures reduce risk through policies, planning, education, information and institutional arrangements.

  • Building codes and land-use regulations.
  • Hazard maps and risk assessments.
  • Public awareness campaigns.
  • Insurance and financial risk-transfer mechanisms.
  • Early warning systems.
  • Training and community preparedness.

6. Disaster Preparedness and Safety | విపత్తు సిద్ధత మరియు భద్రత

6.1 Before a disaster

  • Understand local hazards and official warning systems.
  • Prepare an emergency contact list.
  • Identify evacuation routes and safe shelters.
  • Keep essential medicines and first-aid supplies.
  • Store safe drinking water and essential food.
  • Secure important documents in waterproof protection.
  • Follow official advisories and participate in mock drills.

6.2 During a flood

  • Move to higher ground when instructed.
  • Never walk or drive through moving floodwater.
  • Stay away from electric poles and fallen power lines.
  • Follow evacuation instructions.
  • Drink safe or treated water.
  • Do not enter floodwater to rescue someone without appropriate training and equipment.

6.3 During an earthquake

  • Drop, Cover and Hold On when shaking begins.
  • Stay away from windows and heavy objects that may fall.
  • If indoors, avoid using lifts.
  • If outdoors, move away from buildings, trees and power lines.
  • After shaking stops, be alert for aftershocks.
  • Follow official instructions before re-entering damaged buildings.

6.4 During a cyclone

  • Follow official cyclone warnings and evacuation orders.
  • Move to designated shelters when required.
  • Stay indoors away from windows during dangerous winds.
  • Keep emergency supplies ready.
  • Do not go outside during a temporary lull or apparent calm unless authorities confirm it is safe.
  • Avoid coastal areas affected by storm surge.

6.5 During a heatwave

  • Drink water regularly.
  • Avoid strenuous outdoor activity during the hottest hours.
  • Wear light, loose-fitting clothing.
  • Stay in cool or shaded places.
  • Check on elderly people, children and those needing assistance.
  • Seek urgent medical help for signs of heatstroke, such as confusion or collapse.
Safety reminder: Always follow official warnings and instructions from competent authorities. Local conditions can change quickly.

7. Disaster Management in India | భారతదేశంలో విపత్తు నిర్వహణ

7.1 Disaster Management Act, 2005

The Disaster Management Act, 2005 provides the legal and institutional framework for disaster management in India.

  • Provides for the establishment of national, state and district-level disaster management institutions.
  • Defines roles and responsibilities for disaster management planning and coordination.
  • Provides a framework for disaster response and preparedness.
  • Supports prevention, mitigation, preparedness, response and recovery activities.

7.2 National Disaster Management Authority (NDMA)

NDMA is the apex national-level body for disaster management policy, plans and guidelines in India. It is chaired by the Prime Minister of India.

NDMA భారతదేశంలో జాతీయ స్థాయిలో విపత్తు నిర్వహణ విధానాలు, ప్రణాళికలు మరియు మార్గదర్శకాలకు సంబంధించిన ప్రధాన సంస్థ. దీనికి భారత ప్రధానమంత్రి అధ్యక్షత వహిస్తారు.

7.3 National Executive Committee (NEC)

The NEC assists in implementing and coordinating the national disaster management framework and performs functions assigned under the Disaster Management Act.

7.4 National Disaster Response Force (NDRF)

NDRF is a specialised force for disaster response, including search and rescue, evacuation and emergency response operations.

NDRF విపత్తుల సమయంలో శోధన, రక్షణ, తరలింపు మరియు అత్యవసర సహాయక చర్యల్లో ప్రత్యేకంగా పనిచేస్తుంది.

7.5 National Institute of Disaster Management (NIDM)

NIDM supports disaster management through training, research, capacity development, documentation and knowledge sharing.

7.6 State Disaster Management Authority (SDMA)

Each state has a State Disaster Management Authority under the legal framework. It is chaired by the Chief Minister of the state.

7.7 District Disaster Management Authority (DDMA)

DDMA coordinates disaster management at the district level. The District Collector or District Magistrate generally chairs the authority, with the prescribed co-chairing arrangement under the Act.

7.9 Role of major technical agencies

  • India Meteorological Department (IMD): Weather forecasts, cyclone warnings, heatwave and other meteorological information.
  • Central Water Commission (CWC): Flood forecasting and hydrological information.
  • Indian National Centre for Ocean Information Services (INCOIS): Ocean information and tsunami early warning services.
  • Geological Survey of India (GSI): Geological information and landslide-related studies.
  • National Remote Sensing Centre (NRSC): Satellite-based information and remote sensing support.

8. Sendai Framework for Disaster Risk Reduction | సెండాయ్ ఫ్రేమ్‌వర్క్

The Sendai Framework for Disaster Risk Reduction 2015–2030 was adopted at the Third UN World Conference on Disaster Risk Reduction in Sendai, Japan, in March 2015.

Sendai Framework 2015–2030ను 2015 మార్చిలో జపాన్‌లోని Sendai నగరంలో జరిగిన మూడవ ఐక్యరాజ్యసమితి విపత్తు ప్రమాద తగ్గింపు ప్రపంచ సదస్సులో ఆమోదించారు.

8.1 Four priorities for action

  1. Understanding disaster risk: Improve knowledge of hazards, exposure, vulnerability and capacity.
  2. Strengthening disaster risk governance: Improve institutions, coordination and accountability.
  3. Investing in disaster risk reduction for resilience: Invest in structural and non-structural risk reduction.
  4. Enhancing disaster preparedness for effective response and to Build Back Better in recovery, rehabilitation and reconstruction: Strengthen preparedness and rebuild more safely after disasters.

8.2 Seven global targets

  • Substantially reduce global disaster mortality.
  • Substantially reduce the number of affected people.
  • Reduce direct disaster economic loss in relation to global GDP.
  • Reduce disaster damage to critical infrastructure and disruption of basic services.
  • Increase the number of countries with national and local disaster risk reduction strategies.
  • Enhance international cooperation to developing countries.
  • Increase the availability of and access to multi-hazard early warning systems and disaster risk information.
Exam point: The Sendai Framework covers 2015–2030 and has four priorities for action and seven global targets.

10. Disaster Risks in Andhra Pradesh and Telangana | ఆంధ్రప్రదేశ్ మరియు తెలంగాణలో విపత్తు ప్రమాదాలు

10.1 Andhra Pradesh

Andhra Pradesh has a long coastline along the Bay of Bengal. Several districts and coastal communities may face multiple hazards.

  • Cyclones: Coastal areas can be affected by strong winds, heavy rainfall and storm surges.
  • Floods: River basins and low-lying areas may experience flooding.
  • Coastal erosion: Some coastal stretches face erosion and shoreline changes.
  • Heatwaves: High temperatures can affect public health, water availability and agriculture.
  • Drought: Rainfall variability can affect agriculture and drinking-water availability.
  • Lightning: Thunderstorms can cause casualties and damage.

10.2 Important rivers and flood risk

  • Godavari River basin.
  • Krishna River basin.
  • Penna River basin.
  • Other local streams and drainage systems.

Heavy rainfall, upstream water releases, river conditions and local drainage capacity can influence flood risk.

10.3 Telangana

  • Urban flooding: Intense rainfall and inadequate drainage can cause flooding in urban areas.
  • River floods: Godavari and Krishna river systems and their tributaries can affect vulnerable locations.
  • Heatwaves: High temperatures create risks for workers, older people and people with health vulnerabilities.
  • Drought: Rainfall variability can affect crops, groundwater and rural livelihoods.
  • Lightning and storms: Seasonal thunderstorms may cause damage and casualties.

10.4 Risk reduction measures for both states

  • Strengthen district disaster management plans.
  • Improve local early warning communication.
  • Protect wetlands, lakes, natural drainage channels and floodplains.
  • Improve cyclone shelters and evacuation arrangements in coastal areas.
  • Promote heat action plans and public health awareness.
  • Improve dam and reservoir operation coordination.
  • Conduct community training and regular mock drills.
  • Use hazard maps and land-use planning to reduce exposure.

11. Community-Based Disaster Risk Management | సమాజ ఆధారిత విపత్తు నిర్వహణ

Community-based disaster risk management involves local people in identifying risks, planning preparedness measures and participating in response and recovery.

11.1 Role of communities

  • Identify vulnerable households and local hazards.
  • Prepare village and neighbourhood disaster plans.
  • Identify safe routes and evacuation centres.
  • Participate in mock drills.
  • Support elderly people, children and persons with disabilities during evacuation.
  • Share verified warnings through trusted communication channels.
  • Protect local water bodies, forests and other natural buffers.

11.2 Inclusive disaster management

Disaster planning should consider the different needs of women, children, older persons, persons with disabilities, low-income households and other vulnerable groups.

  • Accessible shelters and evacuation routes.
  • Safe sanitation and privacy arrangements.
  • Accessible warning messages.
  • Continuity of essential medicines and healthcare.
  • Protection against exploitation and discrimination.

13. Quick Revision Points | త్వరిత పునశ్చరణ

  • Hazard does not automatically become a disaster.
  • Exposure means people and assets located in hazard-prone areas.
