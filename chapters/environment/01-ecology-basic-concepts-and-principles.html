Chapter 01: Ecology – Basic Concepts and Principles

Useful for: APPSC, TSPSC, UPSC, SSC, Railway and other competitive examinations.

ఈ అధ్యాయంలో జీవులు, వాటి పరిసరాలు, జీవులు–పర్యావరణం మధ్య సంబంధాలు, పర్యావరణ స్థాయులు, అనుకూలనాలు మరియు ముఖ్యమైన పర్యావరణ సూత్రాలను అధ్యయనం చేస్తాము.

1. Meaning and Definition of Ecology | ఆవరణ శాస్త్రం అర్థం

Ecology is the scientific study of interactions among organisms and between organisms and their physical environment.

ఆవరణ శాస్త్రం (Ecology) అనేది జీవుల మధ్య పరస్పర సంబంధాలను, జీవులకు మరియు వాటి భౌతిక పరిసరాలకు మధ్య ఉండే సంబంధాలను శాస్త్రీయంగా అధ్యయనం చేసే శాస్త్రం.

  • The term Ecology was coined by German biologist Ernst Haeckel in 1866.
  • It is derived from Greek words: Oikos = house or dwelling; Logos = study.
  • Ecology examines distribution, abundance, behaviour and relationships of organisms.
  • It includes both living (biotic) and non-living (abiotic) components.
Exam Point: Ernst Haeckel coined the term Ecology in 1866. Do not confuse Ecology with Ecosystem; an ecosystem is a functional unit studied in ecology.

Related branches | సంబంధిత శాఖలు

  • Autecology: Study of an individual organism or a single species in relation to its environment.
  • Synecology: Study of groups or communities of organisms in relation to their environment.
  • Population Ecology: Study of population size, density, growth and distribution.
  • Community Ecology: Study of interactions among different populations living together.
  • Landscape Ecology: Study of ecological patterns and processes across large areas.
  • Global Ecology: Study of ecological processes at the planetary scale.

2. Environment | పర్యావరణం

Environment refers to the total surroundings and conditions in which an organism lives and functions.

ఒక జీవి నివసించే, జీవించే మరియు అభివృద్ధి చెందే అన్ని పరిసరాలు, పరిస్థితులు కలిపి పర్యావరణం అంటారు.

Components of Environment

A. Abiotic Components | నిర్జీవ అంశాలు

Non-living physical and chemical factors that influence organisms.

  • Climatic factors: Temperature, rainfall, humidity, sunlight and wind.
  • Edaphic factors: Soil texture, soil moisture, soil pH and mineral content.
  • Topographic factors: Altitude, slope and direction of land surface.
  • Water factors: Salinity, dissolved oxygen, pH and water availability.

B. Biotic Components | జీవ అంశాలు

All living organisms present in an environment.

  • Producers: Organisms that manufacture organic food, usually through photosynthesis. Examples: green plants, algae and some bacteria.
  • Consumers: Organisms that obtain energy by eating other organisms. Examples: deer, insects, birds and humans.
  • Decomposers: Organisms that break down dead organic matter and release nutrients. Examples: many bacteria and fungi.
Plants are generally producers, animals are consumers, and many bacteria and fungi are decomposers. Ecological roles depend on how an organism obtains energy and nutrients.

3. Levels of Ecological Organisation | పర్యావరణ సంస్థీకరణ స్థాయులు

Ecological organisation is studied from an individual organism to the entire biosphere.

LevelMeaningExample
OrganismIndividual living beingOne tiger
PopulationIndividuals of the same species living in a particular areaDeer population in a forest
CommunityDifferent populations living and interacting in an areaPlants, animals and microbes in a forest
EcosystemCommunity interacting with abiotic environmentPond ecosystem
BiomeLarge ecological region characterised by climate and typical vegetationDesert, tundra, tropical rainforest
BiosphereAll regions of Earth where life existsGlobal life-supporting zone

Important distinctions

  • Population: Same species; same defined area; same period.
  • Community: Multiple species populations interacting in an area.
  • Ecosystem: Community plus abiotic environment and their interactions.
  • Biome: Large regional ecological unit, often identified by climate and dominant vegetation.
  • Biosphere: Global sum of Earth's ecosystems.
Remember the sequence: Organism → Population → Community → Ecosystem → Biome → Biosphere.

4. Habitat and Ecological Niche | నివాస స్థలం మరియు జీవావరణ పాత్ర

Habitat

A habitat is the natural place or environment where an organism lives.

ఒక జీవి సహజంగా నివసించే ప్రదేశాన్ని నివాస స్థలం (Habitat) అంటారు.

Examples: A pond is a habitat for fish; a forest is a habitat for elephants; a tree may be a habitat for birds and insects.

Ecological Niche

An ecological niche refers to the functional role of a species in an ecosystem, including its resource use, environmental requirements and interactions.

పర్యావరణ వ్యవస్థలో ఒక జీవి పోషించే పాత్ర, ఆహార వినియోగం, నివాస అవసరాలు, ఇతర జీవులతో సంబంధాలు కలిపి దాని పర్యావరణ స్థానం లేదా Niche అంటారు.

  • Habitat describes where an organism lives.
  • Niche describes how an organism lives and functions.
  • Two species may share a habitat but use different resources or occupy different niches.
  • Strong competition can occur when species have substantially overlapping resource requirements.
Example: Different bird species may live in the same forest but feed at different heights or on different foods. Their habitats overlap, while their niches may differ.

5. Biotic Interactions | జీవుల మధ్య సంబంధాలు

Organisms interact in many ways. These relationships influence survival, reproduction and population size.

InteractionEffectExplanation and example
Mutualism+/+Both species benefit. Example: pollinating insects and flowering plants.
Commensalism+/0One benefits; the other is not significantly affected. Example: epiphytic orchids growing on trees.
Parasitism+/-Parasite benefits while host is harmed. Example: tapeworm in humans.
Predation+/-Predator kills and consumes prey. Example: tiger hunting deer.
Competition-/-Both competitors experience reduced access to limited resources. Example: plants competing for sunlight.
Amensalism-/0One is harmed while the other is not significantly affected. Example: certain fungi releasing substances that inhibit bacteria.
Neutralism0/0No significant direct effect on either species; strict neutralism is difficult to establish in nature.

Important concepts

  • Predation: Helps regulate prey populations and can influence community structure.
  • Competition: Occurs when organisms require the same limited resources.
  • Resource partitioning: Species reduce competition by using resources in different ways, places or times.
  • Coevolution: Two or more interacting species exert reciprocal evolutionary influences on each other.
  • Symbiosis: Close and often long-term association between different species; it can include mutualism, commensalism or parasitism.

6. Adaptation | అనుకూలనం

Adaptation is an inherited characteristic that improves an organism's ability to survive and reproduce in a particular environment.

ఒక నిర్దిష్ట పర్యావరణంలో జీవి మనుగడ మరియు పునరుత్పత్తికి సహాయపడే వారసత్వ లక్షణాన్ని అనుకూలనం అంటారు.

Types of Adaptations

  • Morphological / Structural: Physical features. Example: thick fur in polar mammals; spines in cactus.
  • Physiological: Internal functional processes. Example: water conservation mechanisms in desert animals.
  • Behavioural: Actions that improve survival. Example: seasonal migration of birds.

Examples

  • Cactus has spines, a thick water-storing stem and a waxy surface that reduce water loss.
  • Polar bears have thick fur and a layer of insulating fat.
  • Camels show several adaptations that help them survive in arid environments.
  • Mangroves possess specialised features that help them tolerate waterlogged and often saline conditions.
An individual organism may acclimatise during its lifetime. Evolutionary adaptation, in contrast, involves inherited population-level changes across generations.

7. Ecological Principles and Laws | పర్యావరణ సూత్రాలు

A. Liebig's Law of the Minimum

Proposed by Justus von Liebig. Growth is limited by the essential resource or nutrient that is in shortest supply relative to the organism's requirement.

ఒక జీవి ఎదుగుదలకు అవసరమైన పోషకాలలో తగినంతగా లేని అంశం ఎదుగుదలను పరిమితం చేస్తుంది.

Example: If a plant has sufficient nitrogen and potassium but insufficient phosphorus, phosphorus may limit its growth.

B. Shelford's Law of Tolerance

Proposed by Victor Ernest Shelford. The distribution and success of an organism depend on its tolerance limits for environmental factors.

ఉష్ణోగ్రత, తేమ, లవణీయత వంటి పర్యావరణ అంశాలకు ప్రతి జీవికి కనిష్ఠ మరియు గరిష్ఠ సహన పరిమితులు ఉంటాయి.

  • Minimum limit: Lowest level tolerated.
  • Maximum limit: Highest level tolerated.
  • Optimum range: Conditions under which the organism performs best.
  • Zone of stress: Survival may be possible, but growth or reproduction is reduced.

C. Ecological Succession

Ecological succession is the gradual change in species composition and community structure over time.

  • Primary succession: Begins in an area without developed soil, such as newly exposed bare rock.
  • Secondary succession: Begins where a community has been disturbed but soil remains, such as an abandoned field.
  • Pioneer species: Early colonisers of a newly available habitat.
  • Seral stages: Intermediate communities during succession.
  • Climax community: A relatively stable late-successional community in the traditional model; actual ecosystems can remain dynamic.

D. Ecological Stability

  • Resistance: Ability to remain relatively unchanged during disturbance.
  • Resilience: Ability to recover after disturbance.
  • Stability: A broad term describing the capacity of an ecosystem to maintain or recover its functions and structure.

8. Population Ecology | జనాభా ఆవరణ శాస్త్రం

A population is a group of individuals of the same species living in a particular geographical area at a given time.

Population attributes

  • Population density: Number of individuals per unit area or volume.
  • Birth rate (Natality): Rate at which new individuals are added through reproduction.
  • Death rate (Mortality): Rate at which individuals die.
  • Immigration: Movement of individuals into a population.
  • Emigration: Movement of individuals out of a population.
  • Age structure: Distribution of individuals among age groups.
  • Sex ratio: Relative proportion of males and females.

Population growth

Population size changes due to births, deaths, immigration and emigration.

Population change = (Births + Immigration) − (Deaths + Emigration)

Exponential Growth

Occurs when resources are effectively unlimited and population growth is proportional to population size. It produces a J-shaped growth curve.

Equation: dN/dt = rN

Logistic Growth

Occurs when resources are limited and population growth slows as the population approaches the carrying capacity. It produces an S-shaped or sigmoid curve.

Equation: dN/dt = rN(1 − N/K)

  • N: Population size.
  • r: Intrinsic rate of natural increase.
  • K: Carrying capacity of the environment.

Carrying Capacity

Carrying capacity is the population size that an environment can support over time under prevailing conditions and resource availability.

ఒక పర్యావరణం అందుబాటులో ఉన్న వనరులతో దీర్ఘకాలం నిలబెట్టగల జనాభా పరిమాణాన్ని Carrying Capacity అంటారు.

10. Ecology and Related Sciences | సంబంధిత శాస్త్రాలు

  • Environmental Science: Interdisciplinary study of environmental systems and human interactions with them.
  • Conservation Biology: Science focused on protecting biodiversity and preventing extinction.
  • Ethology: Scientific study of animal behaviour.
  • Biogeography: Study of the geographical distribution of organisms.
  • Evolutionary Biology: Study of how populations and species change over generations.
  • Climatology: Study of climate and long-term atmospheric patterns.
  • Limnology: Study of inland aquatic ecosystems such as lakes and ponds.
