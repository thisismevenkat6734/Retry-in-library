Chapter 04: Biogeochemical Cycles

Useful for: APPSC, TSPSC, UPSC, SSC, Railway and other competitive examinations.

ఈ అధ్యాయంలో నీరు, కార్బన్, నైట్రోజన్, ఆక్సిజన్, ఫాస్ఫరస్ మరియు సల్ఫర్ వంటి మూలకాలు ప్రకృతిలో ఎలా చక్రాకారంగా ప్రయాణిస్తాయో తెలుసుకుంటాము.

1. Meaning of Biogeochemical Cycles | జీవ భూ రసాయనిక చక్రాల అర్థం

Biogeochemical cycles describe the movement and recycling of chemical elements and compounds between living organisms and the non-living environment.

జీవులు మరియు నిర్జీవ పర్యావరణం మధ్య నీరు, కార్బన్, నైట్రోజన్, ఫాస్ఫరస్ వంటి పదార్థాలు నిరంతరం మార్పిడి చెంది తిరిగి వినియోగంలోకి వచ్చే ప్రక్రియలను జీవ భూ రసాయనిక చక్రాలు అంటారు.

Word meaning

  • Bio: Life or living organisms.
  • Geo: Earth, rocks, soil and other geological components.
  • Chemical: Elements and compounds involved in natural processes.

Why are these cycles important?

  • Maintain the availability of essential nutrients.
  • Support plant growth and ecosystem productivity.
  • Connect atmosphere, hydrosphere, lithosphere and biosphere.
  • Help regulate climate and atmospheric composition.
  • Maintain ecological balance and long-term ecosystem functioning.
  • Prevent essential elements from remaining permanently locked in one compartment.
Important: Energy generally flows through ecosystems and is dissipated as heat, whereas matter and nutrients are recycled through biogeochemical cycles.

2. Types of Biogeochemical Cycles | చక్రాల రకాలు

A. Gaseous cycles

The major reservoir is generally the atmosphere or ocean, and the elements can move relatively quickly between reservoirs.

Examples: Carbon, nitrogen and oxygen cycles.

B. Sedimentary cycles

The major reservoirs are generally rocks, soil and sediments. Weathering and geological processes play important roles.

Example: Phosphorus cycle.

C. Hydrological cycle

The continuous circulation of water between the atmosphere, land, oceans and living organisms.

Example: Evaporation, condensation and precipitation.

3. Hydrological Cycle / Water Cycle | జల చక్రం

The water cycle is the continuous movement of water between the oceans, atmosphere, land and living organisms.

సముద్రాలు, వాతావరణం, భూమి మరియు జీవుల మధ్య నీరు నిరంతరం ప్రయాణించే ప్రక్రియను జల చక్రం అంటారు.

Oceans and Water Bodies → Evaporation → Condensation → Clouds → Precipitation → Runoff / Infiltration → Rivers and Groundwater → Oceans

Major processes

1. Evaporation | ఆవిరీభవనం

Conversion of liquid water into water vapour due to energy, mainly from sunlight.

సూర్యుని శక్తి వల్ల ద్రవరూపంలోని నీరు నీటి ఆవిరిగా మారడాన్ని ఆవిరీభవనం అంటారు.

2. Transpiration | బాష్పోత్సర్జనం

Release of water vapour from plants, mainly through stomata in leaves.

మొక్కల ఆకులలోని రంధ్రాల ద్వారా నీటి ఆవిరి వాతావరణంలోకి విడుదల కావడాన్ని బాష్పోత్సర్జనం అంటారు.

3. Evapotranspiration

The combined water loss through evaporation from surfaces and transpiration from plants.

4. Condensation | ఘనీభవనం

Conversion of water vapour into tiny liquid droplets or ice crystals, contributing to cloud formation.

5. Precipitation | అవపాతం

Water falling from the atmosphere to Earth's surface as rain, snow, sleet or hail.

6. Infiltration | భూమిలోకి నీరు చొరబడటం

Entry of water into the soil surface.

7. Percolation | భూగర్భంలోకి ఇంకిపోవడం

Downward movement of water through soil and permeable rock layers.

8. Surface runoff | ఉపరితల ప్రవాహం

Water flowing over land into streams, rivers, lakes and oceans.

Importance of the water cycle

  • Replenishes freshwater resources.
  • Supports agriculture and natural vegetation.
  • Influences weather and climate.
  • Helps recharge groundwater.
  • Transports nutrients and dissolved substances.
Groundwater recharge depends on rainfall, soil properties, land cover, geology and human activities. Excessive paving and urbanisation can reduce infiltration.

4. Carbon Cycle | కార్బన్ చక్రం

The carbon cycle describes the movement of carbon among the atmosphere, oceans, soil, rocks and living organisms.

వాతావరణం, సముద్రాలు, నేల, రాళ్లు మరియు జీవుల మధ్య కార్బన్ మార్పిడి జరిగే ప్రక్రియను కార్బన్ చక్రం అంటారు.

Major carbon reservoirs

  • Atmosphere: Carbon dioxide (CO₂) and small amounts of other carbon-containing gases.
  • Oceans: Dissolved inorganic carbon, including bicarbonate and carbonate ions.
  • Living organisms: Carbon in carbohydrates, proteins, fats and other organic compounds.
  • Soil: Organic matter and soil carbon.
  • Rocks and sediments: Carbonate rocks and long-term geological carbon stores.
  • Fossil fuels: Coal, petroleum and natural gas formed from ancient organic matter.

Major processes

1. Photosynthesis

Green plants and other photosynthetic organisms absorb atmospheric or dissolved CO₂ and convert carbon into organic compounds using light energy.

CO₂ + Water + Light → Organic Matter + Oxygen

2. Respiration

Organisms break down organic compounds to release energy, returning carbon dioxide to the atmosphere or water under aerobic conditions.

3. Decomposition

Decomposers break down dead organic matter, returning carbon to soil, water and atmosphere.

4. Ocean-atmosphere exchange

Carbon dioxide continuously moves between the atmosphere and ocean through dissolution and release.

5. Sedimentation and burial

Some carbon becomes stored in sediments and carbonate rocks over long geological periods.

6. Combustion

Burning fossil fuels and biomass releases carbon dioxide. Incomplete combustion can also produce carbon monoxide and other pollutants.

7. Volcanic activity and weathering

Geological processes exchange carbon between Earth's interior, rocks, oceans and atmosphere.

Short-term and long-term carbon cycles

Short-term cycleLong-term cycle
Photosynthesis, respiration and decomposition.Rock weathering, sedimentation and geological processes.
Usually operates over days to centuries.Can operate over thousands to millions of years.
Involves living organisms and nearby reservoirs.Involves geological carbon storage and release.

Human impact on carbon cycle

  • Burning coal, petroleum and natural gas increases atmospheric CO₂.
  • Deforestation reduces carbon storage in vegetation and soils.
  • Cement production releases CO₂ through chemical processes and energy use.
  • Land-use changes alter carbon storage and exchange.
  • Ocean absorption of additional CO₂ contributes to ocean acidification.
Carbon dioxide is a major greenhouse gas. Human-driven increases in atmospheric greenhouse gases contribute to global warming and climate change.

5. Nitrogen Cycle | నైట్రోజన్ చక్రం

The nitrogen cycle describes the conversion and movement of nitrogen among the atmosphere, soil, water and living organisms.

వాతావరణం, నేల, నీరు మరియు జీవుల మధ్య నైట్రోజన్ వివిధ రసాయన రూపాల్లో మార్పిడి చెందే ప్రక్రియను నైట్రోజన్ చక్రం అంటారు.

Importance of nitrogen

  • Essential component of amino acids and proteins.
  • Present in DNA and RNA.
  • Required for chlorophyll formation and plant growth.
  • Important for enzymes and many biological molecules.

Major steps of nitrogen cycle

1. Nitrogen fixation | నైట్రోజన్ స్థిరీకరణ

Conversion of atmospheric nitrogen (N₂) into biologically usable nitrogen compounds, such as ammonia or ammonium.

Biological nitrogen fixation: Certain bacteria and archaea perform this process.

  • Rhizobium: Symbiotic nitrogen-fixing bacteria associated with root nodules of many legumes.
  • Azotobacter: Free-living nitrogen-fixing bacterium.
  • Clostridium: Some species can fix nitrogen under anaerobic conditions.
  • Anabaena: Nitrogen-fixing cyanobacterium; some species occur in association with Azolla.

Non-biological fixation: Lightning can convert atmospheric nitrogen into nitrogen oxides, which may enter soil through precipitation. Industrial processes also fix nitrogen.

2. Nitrification | నైట్రిఫికేషన్

Conversion of ammonia or ammonium into nitrite and then nitrate by microorganisms.

NH₄⁺ → NO₂⁻ → NO₃⁻
  • Nitrosomonas: Traditionally associated with ammonia oxidation to nitrite.
  • Nitrobacter: Traditionally associated with nitrite oxidation to nitrate.
  • Other microorganisms, including archaea and different bacterial groups, also perform nitrification.

3. Assimilation | శోషణ

Plants absorb nitrate or ammonium from soil and use nitrogen to make amino acids and other organic compounds. Animals obtain nitrogen by eating plants or other animals.

4. Ammonification | అమ్మోనిఫికేషన్

Decomposers convert organic nitrogen in dead organisms and waste into ammonia or ammonium.

5. Denitrification | డీనైట్రిఫికేషన్

Microorganisms reduce nitrate or nitrite to gaseous nitrogen forms, returning nitrogen to the atmosphere under suitable conditions.

NO₃⁻ → NO₂⁻ → NO → N₂O → N₂

Many denitrifying bacteria perform this process, often in oxygen-poor environments.

Importance of nitrogen-fixing organisms

  • Increase the availability of usable nitrogen.
  • Support soil fertility and plant growth.
  • Reduce dependence on some synthetic nitrogen fertilisers in agricultural systems.
  • Maintain nitrogen cycling in natural ecosystems.
Atmospheric nitrogen (N₂) is abundant, but most plants cannot directly use it. Nitrogen fixation converts it into usable chemical forms.

6. Oxygen Cycle | ఆక్సిజన్ చక్రం

The oxygen cycle describes the movement and exchange of oxygen among the atmosphere, hydrosphere, lithosphere and biosphere.

వాతావరణం, నీరు, భూమి మరియు జీవుల మధ్య ఆక్సిజన్ మార్పిడి జరిగే ప్రక్రియను ఆక్సిజన్ చక్రం అంటారు.

Major sources of oxygen

  • Photosynthesis by terrestrial plants.
  • Photosynthesis by algae and aquatic plants.
  • Photosynthesis by marine phytoplankton and cyanobacteria.

Major oxygen-consuming processes

  • Respiration: Organisms use oxygen during aerobic respiration.
  • Decomposition: Aerobic microbes consume oxygen while breaking down organic matter.
  • Combustion: Burning of fuels and biomass uses oxygen.
  • Oxidation: Chemical reactions involving oxidation consume oxygen in many environments.

Photosynthesis and respiration

Photosynthesis: CO₂ + H₂O + Light → Organic Matter + O₂
Respiration: Organic Matter + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O + Energy

Importance

  • Oxygen is essential for aerobic respiration.
  • Dissolved oxygen supports aquatic organisms.
  • Atmospheric oxygen supports many biological and chemical processes.
  • Ozone in the stratosphere absorbs much of the Sun's harmful ultraviolet radiation.
Photosynthesis is a major biological source of free oxygen. The oxygen released during oxygenic photosynthesis originates from water molecules.

7. Phosphorus Cycle | ఫాస్ఫరస్ చక్రం

The phosphorus cycle describes the movement of phosphorus through rocks, soil, water and living organisms.

రాళ్లు, నేల, నీరు మరియు జీవుల మధ్య ఫాస్ఫరస్ మార్పిడి జరిగే ప్రక్రియను ఫాస్ఫరస్ చక్రం అంటారు.

Importance of phosphorus

  • Component of DNA and RNA.
  • Essential part of ATP, which transfers energy in cells.
  • Present in phospholipids of cell membranes.
  • Important for bones and teeth in vertebrates.
  • Required for plant growth and root development.

Major steps

  1. Weathering: Weathering of phosphate-containing rocks releases phosphate into soil and water.
  2. Plant uptake: Plants absorb dissolved phosphate ions through roots.
  3. Food-chain transfer: Animals obtain phosphorus by eating plants or other animals.
  4. Decomposition: Dead organisms and waste return phosphorus to soil and water.
  5. Runoff: Phosphates can be transported into rivers, lakes and oceans.
  6. Sedimentation: Some phosphorus settles and becomes part of sediments.
  7. Geological uplift: Geological processes can eventually expose phosphate-rich rocks again.
Phosphate Rocks → Weathering → Soil / Water Phosphate → Plants → Animals → Decomposition → Soil / Water

Important characteristics

  • The phosphorus cycle is mainly sedimentary.
  • It has no major gaseous phase under ordinary ecological conditions.
  • Rocks and sediments are important long-term reservoirs.
  • Weathering is an important natural source of available phosphate.
  • The cycle is generally slower than many gaseous nutrient cycles.

Human impacts

  • Excessive fertiliser use increases phosphate runoff.
  • Untreated sewage adds phosphorus to water bodies.
  • Phosphate-rich detergents can contribute to nutrient pollution where their use and discharge occur.
  • Mining of phosphate rock alters landscapes and natural phosphorus stores.
Excess phosphorus entering lakes can promote eutrophication, algal blooms and oxygen depletion.

8. Sulphur Cycle | సల్ఫర్ చక్రం

The sulphur cycle describes the movement of sulphur among rocks, soil, water, atmosphere and living organisms.

రాళ్లు, నేల, నీరు, వాతావరణం మరియు జీవుల మధ్య సల్ఫర్ మార్పిడి జరిగే ప్రక్రియను సల్ఫర్ చక్రం అంటారు.

Importance of sulphur

  • Present in amino acids such as cysteine and methionine.
  • Important in the structure of certain proteins.
  • Participates in enzyme function and metabolic processes.
  • Essential nutrient for plants and microorganisms.

Major sources and processes

  • Weathering: Sulphur-containing minerals release sulphate into soil and water.
  • Volcanic activity: Releases sulphur gases, including sulphur dioxide and hydrogen sulphide.
  • Decomposition: Organic sulphur compounds are broken down and returned to the environment.
  • Plant uptake: Plants commonly absorb sulphur in the form of sulphate ions.
  • Microbial transformations: Microorganisms convert sulphur between different chemical forms.
  • Combustion: Burning sulphur-containing fossil fuels releases sulphur dioxide.

Human impacts

  • Coal and petroleum combustion can release sulphur dioxide.
  • Sulphur dioxide can form sulphuric acid and contribute to acid deposition.
  • Mining and industrial processing can alter sulphur movement.
  • Acid deposition can acidify soil and freshwater and harm vegetation and aquatic organisms.

DeforestationCarbon and waterReduced carbon storage and changes in evapotranspiration.
Excess fertiliser useNitrogen and phosphorusNutrient runoff, eutrophication and water pollution.
UrbanisationWaterReduced infiltration and increased surface runoff.

MiningPhosphorus and sulphurChanges to geological stores and possible water contamination.

Environmental consequences

  • Climate change: Increased greenhouse gases alter Earth's energy balance.
  • Eutrophication: Excess nitrogen and phosphorus promote excessive aquatic plant and algal growth.
  • Acid deposition: Sulphur dioxide and nitrogen oxides contribute to acid-forming reactions in the atmosphere.
  • Groundwater stress: Excessive extraction can reduce groundwater storage.
  • Soil degradation: Nutrient imbalance and erosion reduce soil quality.

11. Conservation and Sustainable Management

  • Use fertilisers according to soil testing and crop requirements.
  • Prevent untreated sewage and industrial effluents from entering water bodies.
  • Protect forests, wetlands and natural vegetation.
  • Improve wastewater treatment and nutrient removal.
  • Adopt sustainable agriculture and efficient irrigation.
  • Reduce fossil fuel use and improve energy efficiency.
  • Promote renewable energy and cleaner industrial technologies.
  • Protect soil through vegetation cover and erosion control.
  • Restore degraded ecosystems and watersheds.
  • Monitor water quality, nutrient loads and greenhouse gas emissions.
Integrated watershed management, pollution prevention and sustainable nutrient management help protect biogeochemical cycles.
