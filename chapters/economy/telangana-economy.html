1. Introduction – పరిచయం

Telangana is a state in south-central India. It was formed on 2 June 2014 as the 29th state of India at the time, following the Andhra Pradesh Reorganisation Act, 2014. Hyderabad became the capital of Telangana.

తెలంగాణ భారతదేశంలోని దక్షిణ-మధ్య ప్రాంతంలో ఉన్న రాష్ట్రం. ఆంధ్రప్రదేశ్ పునర్వ్యవస్థీకరణ చట్టం, 2014 ప్రకారం 2 జూన్ 2014న తెలంగాణ రాష్ట్రం ఏర్పడింది. హైదరాబాద్ తెలంగాణ రాజధానిగా కొనసాగుతోంది.

Historical Background – చారిత్రక నేపథ్యం

  • Telangana was part of the former Hyderabad State before the linguistic reorganisation of states.
  • On 1 November 1956, Telangana region of Hyderabad State was merged with Andhra State to form Andhra Pradesh, subject to the political and administrative arrangements of that period.
  • The Telangana movement sought a separate state, with issues including regional development, employment, water and political representation.
  • Telangana became a separate state on 2 June 2014.
Exam Point: Telangana Formation Day is observed on 2 June every year.

Geographical and Economic Context

Telangana is largely a plateau region. The Godavari and Krishna river systems are important to its water resources. Agriculture, industry, information technology, trade, construction and public services contribute to its economy.

2. Structure of Telangana Economy – ఆర్థిక వ్యవస్థ నిర్మాణం

The state economy is commonly classified into three sectors:

SectorActivitiesExamples
PrimaryNatural-resource basedAgriculture, livestock, forestry, mining
SecondaryManufacturing and constructionPharmaceuticals, textiles, food processing, construction
TertiaryServicesIT, banking, trade, transport, tourism, education

GSDP – రాష్ట్ర స్థూల దేశీయోత్పత్తి

Gross State Domestic Product is the value of final goods and services produced within the geographical boundaries of a state during a specified period.

NSDP – నికర రాష్ట్ర దేశీయోత్పత్తి

Net State Domestic Product is obtained after deducting consumption of fixed capital (depreciation) from GSDP.

Per Capita Income – తలసరి ఆదాయం

Per capita income is an average measure calculated by dividing income by population. It does not show how income is distributed among people.

State economic performance should be examined using real and nominal growth, sector-wise contribution, employment, poverty and human-development indicators—not a single figure alone.

3. Agriculture and Allied Activities – వ్యవసాయం

Agriculture is important for rural employment, food security, raw materials and allied livelihoods in Telangana. Rainfall, soil, irrigation access and market conditions influence crop patterns.

Major Crops – ప్రధాన పంటలు

  • Rice (వరి): An important food crop, grown in irrigated and suitable rain-fed areas.
  • Cotton (పత్తి): Major commercial crop in several districts.
  • Maize (మొక్కజొన్న): Used for food, feed and industrial purposes.
  • Red gram (కందులు): Important pulse crop.
  • Soybean (సోయాబీన్): Oilseed and protein crop grown in suitable areas.
  • Turmeric (పసుపు): Commercial spice crop.
  • Horticulture: Mango, citrus, vegetables, flowers and other crops.

Crop Seasons

  • Kharif: Sown mainly with the onset of the southwest monsoon.
  • Rabi: Sown after the monsoon, generally using residual soil moisture or irrigation.

Factors Affecting Agriculture

Monsoon variability, groundwater availability, irrigation infrastructure, input costs, farm size, crop prices, storage and access to markets influence agricultural outcomes.

Allied Activities

Dairy, sheep and goat rearing, poultry, fisheries, sericulture and other activities supplement farm incomes and diversify rural livelihoods.

Exam Point: Crop diversification, soil health, efficient irrigation, storage and market access are important for sustainable agricultural growth.

4. Irrigation and Water Resources – నీటిపారుదల

The Godavari and Krishna are the two major river systems associated with Telangana. Irrigation projects, reservoirs, tanks and groundwater contribute to water availability.

Important Projects

  • Kaleshwaram Lift Irrigation Project: A major lift-irrigation project planned to utilise Godavari water for irrigation and other water needs. Its design, performance, costs and current operational status should be studied using recent official and technical reports.
  • Nagarjuna Sagar Project: A major multipurpose project on the Krishna River, serving areas in Telangana and Andhra Pradesh.
  • Srisailam Project: A major project on the Krishna River with power-generation and water-management functions.
  • Jurala Project: A project on the Krishna River in Telangana.
  • Mission Kakatiya: A programme focused on restoration and improvement of minor irrigation tanks.

Tank Irrigation

Traditional tanks store rainwater and support irrigation, groundwater recharge, livestock and local ecosystems. Desilting, feeder-channel restoration and maintenance can improve their performance.

Water Management Challenges

  • Uneven rainfall and drought risk.
  • Groundwater depletion in stressed areas.
  • Inter-state river-water sharing and coordination.
  • Maintenance and efficiency of irrigation infrastructure.
  • Balancing irrigation, drinking water, industry and ecological needs.

5. Agriculture Support and Rural Institutions

  • Rythu Vedika: A platform intended to support farmer interaction, information and collective discussion.
  • Primary Agricultural Credit Societies: Cooperative institutions providing agricultural credit and related services.
  • NABARD: National Bank for Agriculture and Rural Development; supports rural development and agricultural finance.
  • FCI: Food Corporation of India; involved in foodgrain procurement, storage and distribution.
  • Market infrastructure: Regulated markets, warehouses, cold chains and processing facilities help farmers reach buyers.

Government agricultural assistance programmes and their names, eligibility conditions and benefits can change. Always refer to current state and Union Government notifications for up-to-date scheme details.

6. Industrial Development – పారిశ్రామిక అభివృద్ధి

Industry creates jobs, adds value to raw materials, supports exports and strengthens the economic base. Telangana has developed industrial clusters, investment-support institutions and sector-specific infrastructure.

Major Industrial Sectors

  • Pharmaceuticals: Hyderabad and surrounding industrial clusters have a major pharmaceutical and life-sciences presence.
  • Biotechnology: Research, development and production activities.
  • Textiles: Cotton-based textiles, garments and technical textiles.
  • Food processing: Processing of agricultural and horticultural produce.
  • Engineering: Machinery, components and industrial equipment.
  • Defence and aerospace: Manufacturing, research and related supply chains.
  • Mineral-based industries: Cement and other industries using mineral resources.

Industrial Policy and Institutions

TG-iPASS: Telangana State Industrial Project Approval and Self-Certification System. It was introduced to simplify industrial approvals and provide a time-bound framework for processing applications.

TSIIC: Telangana State Industrial Infrastructure Corporation, associated with industrial infrastructure and industrial park development.

Industrial Development Needs

Reliable electricity, water, transport, skilled labour, finance, technology, industrial land, logistics and predictable regulations support industrial growth.

7. IT, Services and Urban Economy – ఐటీ మరియు సేవారంగం

Hyderabad is a major centre for information technology, IT-enabled services, pharmaceuticals, education, healthcare, financial services and research-related activities.

Information Technology

  • Software development and IT services.
  • Business process management and IT-enabled services.
  • Data centres, cloud services and digital infrastructure.
  • Start-ups, innovation and technology entrepreneurship.
  • Cybersecurity, artificial intelligence and other emerging technology areas.

HITEC City and Related Clusters

HITEC City and nearby technology districts helped strengthen Hyderabad's IT ecosystem. The wider ecosystem includes technology firms, start-ups, research institutions, skilled workers and supporting services.

Other Services

Trade, transport, tourism, banking, insurance, education, healthcare, hospitality and real estate are important service activities.

Tourism

Telangana's tourism assets include heritage monuments, forts, temples, lakes, forests, wildlife areas and cultural destinations. Tourism supports accommodation, transport, restaurants, guides and local businesses.

8. MSMEs and Entrepreneurship – సూక్ష్మ, చిన్న, మధ్యతరహా పరిశ్రమలు

Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises contribute to employment, entrepreneurship, local supply chains and balanced regional development.

  • Manufacturing enterprises produce goods and industrial components.
  • Service enterprises operate in repair, transport, hospitality, technology and professional services.
  • Food-processing enterprises connect farmers with markets.
  • Local enterprises can support women entrepreneurs and rural livelihoods.

Common challenges include access to credit, technology, skilled labour, market information, infrastructure and timely payments. Udyam Registration is the Union Government's MSME registration system. Classification thresholds should be checked against current official rules.

9. Minerals and Natural Resources – ఖనిజ వనరులు

Mineral resources support construction, power, cement, manufacturing and other industries. Telangana has deposits of coal, limestone and several other minerals.

  • Coal: Important for energy and industrial uses. Singareni Collieries Company Limited is associated with coal mining in the state.
  • Limestone: A major raw material for cement production.
  • Granite and other minerals: Used in construction and stone industries.

Mining can generate employment and revenue, but it must be managed with environmental safeguards, worker safety, land restoration and responsible resource use.

10. Telangana State Budget and Public Finance – రాష్ట్ర బడ్జెట్

The state budget is an annual financial statement of estimated receipts and expenditure for a financial year.

Revenue Receipts

These normally do not create liabilities or reduce assets. Examples include state taxes, fees, user charges, the state's share of central taxes and grants-in-aid.

Capital Receipts

These create liabilities or reduce assets. Examples include borrowings, recovery of loans and certain disinvestment proceeds.

Revenue Expenditure

Routine expenditure such as salaries, pensions, interest payments and many subsidies is generally classified as revenue expenditure.

Capital Expenditure

Expenditure on assets such as roads, irrigation infrastructure, public buildings and certain investments is generally classified as capital expenditure.

Deficit Concepts

  • Revenue Deficit: Revenue expenditure minus revenue receipts.
  • Fiscal Deficit: Total expenditure minus revenue receipts and non-debt capital receipts.
  • Primary Deficit: Fiscal deficit minus interest payments.
Formula:
Fiscal Deficit = Total Expenditure − (Revenue Receipts + Non-debt Capital Receipts)

State finances are influenced by tax revenue, central transfers, grants, welfare commitments, salaries, pensions, subsidies, capital investment and debt servicing. Fiscal responsibility frameworks aim to promote prudent public finance management.

11. Revenue Sources of Telangana – ఆదాయ వనరులు

  • State GST: State component of GST on intra-state supplies.
  • State excise: Revenue under applicable state excise laws.
  • Stamp duty and registration: Receipts linked to property-related instruments and registration.
  • Motor vehicle tax: Tax revenue related to motor vehicles.
  • Non-tax revenue: Fees, user charges, royalties and other receipts.
  • Central tax devolution: State's share of the divisible pool of central taxes.
  • Grants-in-aid: Transfers from the Union Government under applicable provisions.

12. Welfare, Human Development and Inclusion – సంక్షేమం

Human development is assessed through health, education, income, nutrition, housing, sanitation, drinking water and access to opportunities.

Human Development Index

UNDP's Human Development Index combines three dimensions:

  1. Health, measured through life expectancy.
  2. Education, measured through schooling indicators.
  3. Standard of living, measured through GNI per capita.

Welfare Policy Areas

  • Food security and nutrition.
  • Education and scholarships.
  • Public health and healthcare access.
  • Farmer support and rural livelihoods.
  • Women and child development.
  • Social security for vulnerable groups.
  • Housing, drinking water and sanitation.

Effective welfare delivery requires transparent beneficiary identification, adequate funding, accountability, grievance redressal and outcome evaluation.

13. Urbanisation and Infrastructure

Urban centres support industry, services, education, healthcare, trade and innovation. Hyderabad is the largest urban economic centre in Telangana.

  • Transport: Roads, railways, public transport and logistics networks.
  • Urban services: Drinking water, sanitation, drainage and solid-waste management.
  • Digital infrastructure: Broadband, data connectivity and e-governance.
  • Industrial infrastructure: Industrial parks, power, water and logistics.
  • Housing: Affordable housing and planned urban development.

Urban growth should be supported by sustainable planning, public transport, environmental protection and resilient infrastructure.

14. Economic Challenges and Sustainable Development

Major Challenges

  • Agricultural risk: Monsoon variability, price fluctuations and farm input costs.
  • Water management: Groundwater stress, irrigation efficiency and inter-state river issues.
  • Regional imbalance: Differences in infrastructure, investment and employment opportunities between regions.
  • Employment: Need for productive, secure and skilled employment opportunities.
  • Urban pressure: Congestion, pollution, housing and service-delivery challenges.
  • Fiscal pressure: Balancing welfare commitments, debt servicing and development expenditure.
  • Environmental concerns: Mining impacts, pollution, forest conservation and climate risks.

Way Forward

  1. Improve agricultural productivity and diversify rural livelihoods.
  2. Strengthen irrigation efficiency and water conservation.
  3. Promote responsible manufacturing and MSMEs.
  4. Expand skills, research, innovation and quality employment.
  5. Improve logistics and infrastructure beyond major urban centres.
  6. Strengthen public health, education and human development.
  7. Maintain fiscal transparency and sustainable public finance.
  8. Protect forests, water bodies and natural resources.

16. Mains Answer Framework – మెయిన్స్ సమాధాన రూపకల్పన

Question: Explain the major drivers and challenges of Telangana's economy.

Introduction: Introduce Telangana's formation, economic structure and the role of Hyderabad as a major services and technology centre.

Drivers: IT and services, pharmaceuticals, manufacturing, agriculture, irrigation, trade, infrastructure and entrepreneurship.

Challenges: Agricultural uncertainty, water management, regional disparities, employment quality, urban pressure, environmental concerns and fiscal commitments.

Way Forward: Promote diversified agriculture, efficient irrigation, MSMEs, industrial clusters, skills, balanced regional investment and sustainable public finance.

Conclusion: Inclusive and sustainable growth can improve productivity, employment and living standards across Telangana.

Answer Writing Tip: Use the latest Telangana Socio-Economic Outlook, state budget documents and official department statistics for current numerical data. Clearly mention the year and whether a figure is an advance estimate, revised estimate or actual.
