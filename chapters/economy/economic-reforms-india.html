1. Meaning of Economic Reforms | ఆర్థిక సంస్కరణల అర్థం

English: Economic reforms are policy changes introduced by a government to improve the efficiency, productivity, competitiveness and performance of an economy.

తెలుగు: ఆర్థిక వ్యవస్థ సామర్థ్యం, ఉత్పాదకత, పోటీతత్వం మరియు పనితీరును మెరుగుపరచడానికి ప్రభుత్వం చేపట్టే విధాన మార్పులను ఆర్థిక సంస్కరణలు అంటారు.

Objectives | లక్ష్యాలు

  • Accelerate economic growth. / ఆర్థిక వృద్ధిని వేగవంతం చేయడం.
  • Improve productivity and efficiency. / ఉత్పాదకత, సామర్థ్యాన్ని పెంచడం.
  • Encourage private investment. / ప్రైవేట్ పెట్టుబడులను ప్రోత్సహించడం.
  • Promote international trade. / అంతర్జాతీయ వాణిజ్యాన్ని ప్రోత్సహించడం.
  • Attract foreign investment and technology. / విదేశీ పెట్టుబడులు, సాంకేతికతను ఆకర్షించడం.
  • Improve public-sector performance. / ప్రభుత్వ రంగ పనితీరును మెరుగుపరచడం.
  • Strengthen financial institutions. / ఆర్థిక సంస్థలను బలోపేతం చేయడం.

2. Indian Economy Before 1991 | 1991కు ముందు భారత ఆర్థిక వ్యవస్థ

After Independence, India adopted a planned development strategy with an important role for the public sector, industrial licensing and import substitution.

స్వాతంత్ర్యం తరువాత భారతదేశం ప్రణాళికాబద్ధ అభివృద్ధి విధానాన్ని అనుసరించింది. ప్రభుత్వ రంగం, పారిశ్రామిక లైసెన్సులు మరియు దిగుమతుల ప్రత్యామ్నాయ ఉత్పత్తికి ప్రాధాన్యత ఇచ్చింది.

Major characteristics

  • Planning: Five-Year Plans guided economic development.
  • Public Sector: Government enterprises played a major role in strategic and basic industries.
  • Industrial Licensing: Many industries required government licences for establishment or expansion.
  • Import Substitution: Domestic production was encouraged to reduce dependence on imports.
  • Trade Restrictions: High tariffs and quantitative restrictions were used to regulate imports.
  • Regulated Economy: Government controls influenced investment, production and trade decisions.

Limitations

  • Limited competition in several sectors.
  • Low productivity in some industries.
  • Administrative delays and licensing complexity.
  • Limited access to modern technology in certain sectors.
  • Pressure on government finances.
  • External-sector vulnerability.

3. Economic Crisis of 1991 | 1991 ఆర్థిక సంక్షోభం

India faced a severe balance-of-payments crisis in 1991. Foreign exchange reserves had fallen to a critically low level, creating difficulty in financing essential imports and meeting external payment obligations.

1991లో భారతదేశం తీవ్రమైన చెల్లింపుల సమతుల్యత సంక్షోభాన్ని ఎదుర్కొంది. విదేశీ మారక నిల్వలు చాలా తక్కువ స్థాయికి పడిపోవడంతో అవసరమైన దిగుమతులు, విదేశీ చెల్లింపులు చేయడం కష్టమైంది.

Major causes

  • Fiscal Deficit: High government expenditure relative to revenue contributed to macroeconomic imbalances.
  • External Debt: Growing external payment obligations increased vulnerability.
  • Oil Price Shock: The Gulf crisis of 1990–91 contributed to higher oil import costs.
  • Political Instability: Political uncertainty affected economic confidence.
  • Low Export Growth: Export earnings were insufficient to comfortably meet external payment needs.
  • High Imports: Import requirements placed pressure on foreign exchange reserves.
  • Inflation: Inflationary pressures weakened macroeconomic stability.

Consequences

  • Severe foreign exchange shortage.
  • Pressure on the Indian rupee.
  • Difficulty in financing imports.
  • Need for external financial assistance.
  • Urgent macroeconomic stabilisation measures.
The 1991 crisis became a turning point in India's economic policy and accelerated major structural reforms.

4. New Economic Policy, 1991 | నూతన ఆర్థిక విధానం

In 1991, India introduced a major package of economic reforms commonly associated with the New Economic Policy.

1991లో భారతదేశం నూతన ఆర్థిక విధానం కింద కీలకమైన ఆర్థిక సంస్కరణలను ప్రవేశపెట్టింది.

Three pillars: LPG

  • Liberalisation: Reduction of unnecessary government controls and restrictions.
  • Privatisation: Increasing private-sector participation and, in some cases, transferring ownership or control of public enterprises.
  • Globalisation: Greater integration of the Indian economy with the world economy.

Two broad dimensions

  • Stabilisation: Short-term measures to restore macroeconomic stability, control inflation and address the balance-of-payments crisis.
  • Structural Reforms: Long-term changes to improve efficiency, competition and productivity.

5. Liberalisation | సరళీకరణ

Liberalisation means reducing excessive government regulations and allowing greater freedom for businesses and markets, subject to necessary safeguards.

అనవసరమైన ప్రభుత్వ నియంత్రణలను తగ్గించి, వ్యాపారాలు మరియు మార్కెట్లకు ఎక్కువ స్వేచ్ఛ కల్పించడాన్ని సరళీకరణ అంటారు.

Major measures

  • Removal of industrial licensing for most industries, subject to specified exceptions.
  • Reduction of restrictions on private investment.
  • Relaxation of import controls and trade barriers.
  • Reforms in financial and capital markets.
  • Greater competition in several sectors.
  • Changes in foreign investment regulations.

Advantages

  • Encourages competition.
  • Improves efficiency and productivity.
  • Expands consumer choice.
  • Encourages innovation.
  • Can attract domestic and foreign investment.

Concerns

  • Small firms may face stronger competition.
  • Some sectors may experience job displacement.
  • Market concentration can develop without effective regulation.
  • Benefits may not be distributed equally.

6. Privatisation | ప్రైవేటీకరణ

Privatisation refers to increasing private-sector ownership, management or participation in economic activities and public enterprises.

ఆర్థిక కార్యకలాపాలు మరియు ప్రభుత్వ రంగ సంస్థల్లో ప్రైవేట్ రంగ యాజమాన్యం, నిర్వహణ లేదా భాగస్వామ్యాన్ని పెంచడాన్ని ప్రైవేటీకరణ అంటారు.

Methods

  • Disinvestment: Government sells part of its shareholding in a public enterprise.
  • Strategic Disinvestment: Sale of a substantial government stake along with transfer of management control, where applicable.
  • Public-Private Partnership: Government and private entities cooperate in projects and services.
  • Private Management: Private entities may be involved in operating or managing certain services under contracts.

Potential benefits

  • Greater operational efficiency.
  • Access to private capital and technology.
  • Competition and innovation.
  • Reduced financial burden on government in some cases.

Concerns

  • Possibility of private monopolies.
  • Employment and labour concerns.
  • Public interest and affordability issues.
  • Need for transparent valuation and regulation.

7. Disinvestment in India | భారతదేశంలో పెట్టుబడుల ఉపసంహరణ

Disinvestment occurs when the government sells some or all of its ownership stake in a public-sector enterprise.

ప్రభుత్వం ప్రభుత్వ రంగ సంస్థలో తన వాటాలో కొంత భాగాన్ని లేదా మొత్తం వాటాను విక్రయించడాన్ని Disinvestment అంటారు.

Objectives

  • Mobilise government resources.
  • Improve enterprise efficiency.
  • Encourage wider public ownership.
  • Reduce the government's direct financial exposure in selected enterprises.
  • Support strategic restructuring where appropriate.

Types

  • Minority Stake Sale: Government sells a portion of its shareholding but retains control.
  • Strategic Disinvestment: Substantial stake sale with transfer of management control, as applicable.
  • Privatisation: Transfer of ownership or control to private entities.
Disinvestment and privatisation are related but not identical. Every disinvestment does not necessarily transfer management control.

8. Globalisation | ప్రపంచీకరణ

Globalisation refers to the increasing integration of economies through international trade, investment, technology, finance and movement of information.

వాణిజ్యం, పెట్టుబడులు, సాంకేతికత, ఆర్థిక ప్రవాహాలు మరియు సమాచార మార్పిడి ద్వారా దేశాల ఆర్థిక వ్యవస్థలు పరస్పరం అనుసంధానమవడాన్ని ప్రపంచీకరణ అంటారు.

Features

  • Expansion of international trade.
  • Cross-border investment.
  • Technology transfer.
  • Growth of multinational companies.
  • Integration of financial markets.
  • Global production and supply chains.

Benefits

  • Access to international markets.
  • Greater investment opportunities.
  • Access to technology and knowledge.
  • More consumer choices.
  • Export opportunities for domestic businesses.

Challenges

  • Exposure to global economic shocks.
  • Pressure on domestic small producers.
  • Unequal distribution of benefits.
  • Dependence on international supply chains.
  • Concerns about cultural and environmental impacts.

9. Industrial Reforms | పారిశ్రామిక సంస్కరణలు

Industrial reforms aimed to make Indian industries more competitive, productive and responsive to market conditions.

Major measures

  • Industrial licensing was abolished for most industries, with specified exceptions.
  • The list of industries reserved exclusively for the public sector was reduced.
  • Foreign investment was permitted in several sectors under specified conditions.
  • Technology agreements were liberalised in specified cases.
  • Competition and private-sector participation were encouraged.
  • Public-sector enterprises were subjected to restructuring and disinvestment measures.

Impact

  • Greater competition in many industries.
  • Expansion of consumer goods markets.
  • Growth of selected export-oriented industries.
  • Greater access to technology and investment.
  • Uneven progress across sectors and regions.

10. Trade Reforms | వాణిజ్య సంస్కరణలు

Trade reforms aim to make international trade more efficient and competitive by changing tariffs, import restrictions, export procedures and trade regulations.

Major measures

  • Reduction of many import licensing restrictions.
  • Gradual reduction of customs tariffs over time.
  • Changes in quantitative restrictions.
  • Promotion of exports.
  • Greater integration with the global trading system.
  • Foreign exchange and trade policy reforms.

Expected outcomes

  • Greater competition.
  • Access to imported capital goods and technology.
  • Export market expansion.
  • Improved resource allocation.

11. Financial Sector Reforms | ఆర్థిక రంగ సంస్కరణలు

Financial sector reforms aim to strengthen banks, financial institutions, capital markets and the overall financial system.

Narasimham Committee I (1991)

  • Recommended reforms in the banking sector.
  • Emphasised prudential norms.
  • Recommended strengthening capital adequacy.
  • Supported greater operational flexibility for banks.
  • Recommended reducing excessive directed credit requirements over time.

Narasimham Committee II (1998)

  • Focused on strengthening the banking system.
  • Emphasised better asset quality and risk management.
  • Recommended consolidation and stronger banking supervision.
  • Highlighted the importance of capital adequacy.

Major reforms

  • Introduction and strengthening of prudential norms.
  • Income recognition and asset classification rules.
  • Capital adequacy requirements.
  • Greater competition in banking.
  • Development of financial markets.

12. Tax Reforms | పన్ను సంస్కరణలు

Tax reforms aim to create a simpler, fairer and more efficient tax system while improving revenue collection.

Major objectives

  • Broaden the tax base.
  • Reduce unnecessary complexity.
  • Improve compliance.
  • Reduce tax evasion.
  • Encourage investment and economic activity.
  • Improve transparency and administration.

Important developments

  • Changes in direct tax rates and structures over time.
  • Modernisation of tax administration.
  • Expansion of electronic filing and digital payments.
  • Introduction of the Goods and Services Tax in 2017.

13. Goods and Services Tax (GST) | వస్తు సేవల పన్ను

GST is a destination-based indirect tax on the supply of goods and services. It replaced several indirect taxes and created a more unified national indirect tax framework.

వస్తువులు మరియు సేవల సరఫరాపై విధించే గమ్యస్థాన ఆధారిత పరోక్ష పన్ను GST. ఇది పలు పరోక్ష పన్నులను ఒక సమగ్ర వ్యవస్థలోకి తీసుకువచ్చింది.

Important facts

  • Implemented in India on 1 July 2017.
  • Constitutional framework introduced through the 101st Constitutional Amendment Act, 2016.
  • GST Council is provided under Article 279A.
  • GST is destination-based.
  • Input Tax Credit helps reduce cascading taxation, subject to rules.

Types of GST

  • CGST: Central Goods and Services Tax.
  • SGST: State Goods and Services Tax.
  • UTGST: Union Territory Goods and Services Tax.
  • IGST: Integrated Goods and Services Tax, generally applicable to inter-State supplies and imports under the GST framework.

GST Council

The GST Council is a constitutional body that makes recommendations on important GST matters. It includes the Union Finance Minister as Chairperson and representatives of States and Union Territories as specified by the Constitution.

14. Public Sector Reforms | ప్రభుత్వ రంగ సంస్కరణలు

Public-sector reforms seek to improve the efficiency, accountability and performance of government-owned enterprises.

Major measures

  • Disinvestment in selected public enterprises.
  • Greater operational autonomy for eligible enterprises.
  • Professional management and corporate governance.
  • Performance monitoring.
  • Strategic restructuring of selected enterprises.

Maharatna, Navratna and Miniratna

These categories provide different levels of financial and operational autonomy to eligible Central Public Sector Enterprises, subject to government guidelines.

15. NITI Aayog | నీతి ఆయోగ్

NITI Aayog is the Government of India's public policy think tank. It replaced the Planning Commission.

భారత ప్రభుత్వానికి విధానపరమైన సలహాలు, పరిశోధన మరియు సహకార సమాఖ్య విధానంలో సహాయం చేసే సంస్థ NITI Aayog.

Important facts

  • Established on 1 January 2015.
  • Replaced the Planning Commission.
  • Chairperson: Prime Minister of India.
  • Promotes cooperative federalism and competitive federalism.
  • Provides policy advice and supports innovation.
  • Encourages evidence-based policymaking.

Planning Commission vs NITI Aayog

  • Planning Commission was established in 1950.
  • NITI Aayog was established in 2015.
  • Planning Commission had a centralised planning and resource-allocation role.
  • NITI Aayog functions primarily as a policy think tank and does not allocate plan funds in the former Planning Commission manner.

16. Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC), 2016 | దివాలా, దివాలా పరిష్కార చట్టం

The Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 provides a consolidated legal framework for time-bound insolvency resolution and bankruptcy proceedings for eligible entities and individuals.

కంపెనీలు మరియు అర్హులైన వ్యక్తుల దివాలా సమస్యలను నిర్ణీత ప్రక్రియలో పరిష్కరించేందుకు IBC 2016 చట్టం రూపొందించబడింది.

Objectives

  • Time-bound insolvency resolution.
  • Improve credit discipline.
  • Maximise the value of assets.
  • Promote availability of credit.
  • Balance the interests of stakeholders.

Important institutions

  • Insolvency and Bankruptcy Board of India (IBBI): Regulates insolvency professionals, agencies and information utilities under the Code.
  • National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT): Adjudicating authority for corporate insolvency matters.
  • Debt Recovery Tribunal (DRT): Adjudicating authority for specified individual and partnership matters under the Code.
  • Insolvency Professionals: Manage insolvency resolution processes under applicable rules.

17. Competition Policy and Competition Commission of India | పోటీ విధానం

Competition policy aims to promote fair competition and prevent practices that harm markets and consumers.

Competition Act, 2002

  • Provides the legal framework for competition regulation in India.
  • Addresses anti-competitive agreements.
  • Prohibits abuse of dominant position.
  • Regulates combinations such as mergers and acquisitions under specified conditions.

Competition Commission of India (CCI)

CCI is the statutory authority responsible for enforcing competition law and promoting competition in Indian markets.

Importance

  • Protects the competitive process.
  • Discourages cartels and anti-competitive conduct.
  • Promotes consumer welfare.
  • Encourages innovation and efficiency.

18. Make in India, Startup India and Other Initiatives | ముఖ్య కార్యక్రమాలు

Make in India

Launched in September 2014 to promote manufacturing, investment, innovation and employment.

Startup India

Launched in 2016 to encourage entrepreneurship, innovation and startup development.

Digital India

Promotes digital infrastructure, digital services and digital empowerment.

Production Linked Incentive (PLI)

Provides incentives linked to incremental production or sales in selected sectors under scheme conditions.

Atmanirbhar Bharat Abhiyan

Announced in 2020, it focused on strengthening domestic capabilities, resilience and economic recovery through multiple policy measures.

PM Gati Shakti

Promotes integrated and coordinated planning of multimodal infrastructure.

19. Positive Outcomes and Limitations of Reforms | సంస్కరణల ఫలితాలు, పరిమితులు

Positive outcomes

  • Greater integration with the global economy.
  • Expansion of private-sector participation.
  • Growth of telecommunications and information technology services.
  • Greater availability of consumer goods and services.
  • Increased foreign investment in several sectors.
  • Development of financial and capital markets.
  • Improved competition in many industries.

Limitations and concerns

  • Regional inequalities remain.
  • Employment growth has not always matched economic growth.
  • Agriculture continues to face structural challenges.
  • Small enterprises may struggle with competition and finance.
  • Income and wealth inequalities remain concerns.
  • External shocks can affect the domestic economy.
  • Access to quality education and healthcare remains uneven.

20. Future Priorities for Economic Reforms | భవిష్యత్ సంస్కరణల ప్రాధాన్యతలు

  • Employment: Promote labour-intensive manufacturing and services.
  • Education: Improve learning outcomes and employable skills.
  • Agriculture: Strengthen productivity, storage, processing and market access.
  • Infrastructure: Improve transport, energy, logistics and digital connectivity.
  • MSMEs: Improve access to finance, technology and markets.
  • Health: Strengthen affordable public healthcare.
  • Innovation: Encourage research and development.
  • Environment: Promote sustainable and low-carbon growth.
  • Governance: Improve transparency, accountability and ease of doing business.
  • Inclusion: Ensure that growth benefits disadvantaged groups and regions.
