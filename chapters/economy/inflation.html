ద్రవ్యోల్బణం – English + తెలుగు

Detailed Notes · Types · Causes · Effects · RBI Policy · 50 Practice MCQs

1. Meaning of Inflation – ద్రవ్యోల్బణం అర్థం

Inflation is a sustained increase in the general price level of goods and services in an economy over a period of time. As the general price level rises, the purchasing power of money falls.

తెలుగు: ఒక ఆర్థిక వ్యవస్థలో వస్తువులు, సేవల సాధారణ ధరల స్థాయి నిరంతరంగా పెరగడాన్ని ద్రవ్యోల్బణం అంటారు. ధరలు పెరిగినప్పుడు డబ్బు కొనుగోలు శక్తి తగ్గుతుంది.

  • Inflation refers to a general rise in prices, not merely an increase in the price of one product.
  • It is measured over a period of time.
  • It reduces the purchasing power of money when income does not rise at the same pace.
  • Moderate and stable inflation is generally preferred over very high or volatile inflation.
Example: If a basket of goods costs ₹1,000 this year and ₹1,060 next year, the price increase is 6%, assuming the basket and measurement method remain comparable.

2. Purchasing Power of Money – డబ్బు కొనుగోలు శక్తి

Purchasing power refers to the quantity of goods and services that a unit of money can buy.

  • When the general price level rises, purchasing power generally falls.
  • When the general price level falls, purchasing power generally rises.
  • People with fixed incomes may face hardship during high inflation.

తెలుగు: ఒక రూపాయితో ఎంత వస్తువులు, సేవలు కొనగలమో దానిని డబ్బు కొనుగోలు శక్తి అంటారు. ధరలు పెరిగితే కొనుగోలు శక్తి తగ్గుతుంది.

3. Measurement of Inflation – ద్రవ్యోల్బణం కొలత

Inflation is measured using price indices that track changes in the prices of a selected basket of goods and services.

Inflation Rate Formula

Inflation Rate = [(Current Price Index − Previous Price Index) ÷ Previous Price Index] × 100

Example: Previous CPI = 150; Current CPI = 159. Inflation = [(159 − 150) ÷ 150] × 100 = 6%.

4. Consumer Price Index (CPI) – వినియోగదారుల ధరల సూచీ

CPI measures changes over time in the prices paid by consumers for a selected basket of goods and services.

  • It reflects changes in consumer-level prices.
  • It is relevant to the cost of living and household purchasing power.
  • India's CPI is compiled and released by the National Statistical Office (NSO), under MoSPI.
  • Different CPI series may cover different population groups and use different weights.
  • India's monetary policy inflation target is expressed in terms of CPI inflation.

Major CPI categories historically used in India

  • CPI for Industrial Workers (CPI-IW).
  • CPI for Agricultural Labourers (CPI-AL).
  • CPI for Rural Labourers (CPI-RL).
  • Combined CPI for Rural and Urban areas.
Exam Point: CPI Combined is the key headline inflation measure used for India's flexible inflation-targeting framework.

5. Wholesale Price Index (WPI) – టోకు ధరల సూచీ

WPI tracks changes in prices of goods at the wholesale level. It does not directly measure the prices of services consumed by households.

  • It measures price movements at the wholesale stage.
  • It covers primary articles, fuel and power, and manufactured products under its established classification.
  • It is compiled and released by the Office of the Economic Adviser, Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade (DPIIT), Ministry of Commerce and Industry.
  • It is useful for studying price changes in goods at the wholesale level.

6. GDP Deflator – GDP డిఫ్లేటర్

The GDP deflator measures price changes in all domestically produced final goods and services included in GDP.

Formula: GDP Deflator = (Nominal GDP ÷ Real GDP) × 100.

  • It covers domestically produced final output.
  • Its coverage changes with the composition of production.
  • It is not based on a fixed consumer basket in the same way as CPI.
  • It includes price changes in investment and government output as well as consumption.

7. Types of Inflation Based on Rate – వేగం ఆధారంగా రకాలు

1. Creeping Inflation

A slow and relatively mild rise in the general price level.

స్వల్పంగా, నెమ్మదిగా ధరలు పెరగడం.

2. Walking Inflation

A more noticeable and moderate pace of price increase.

3. Running Inflation

A rapid increase in the general price level that creates serious economic pressure.

4. Galloping Inflation

Very high and accelerating inflation that can seriously damage economic stability.

5. Hyperinflation

Extremely rapid and uncontrolled inflation, in which money may rapidly lose its purchasing power.

There is no single universally applied numerical threshold for every informal label such as creeping, walking or running inflation. Hyperinflation is often associated with the widely cited Cagan criterion of inflation exceeding 50% per month.

8. Types Based on Causes

Demand-Pull Inflation

Demand-pull inflation occurs when aggregate demand rises faster than the economy's ability to produce goods and services.

  • Increase in household consumption.
  • Increase in government expenditure.
  • Rapid expansion of credit.
  • Increase in investment or exports.

తెలుగు: వస్తువులు, సేవల సరఫరా కంటే మొత్తం డిమాండ్ వేగంగా పెరిగినప్పుడు ధరలు పెరుగుతాయి.

Cost-Push Inflation

Cost-push inflation occurs when production costs rise and firms pass some of the increase to consumers through higher prices.

  • Increase in wages not matched by productivity.
  • Higher crude oil and energy prices.
  • Increase in raw material costs.
  • Higher transport and logistics costs.
  • Supply disruptions and crop failures.

తెలుగు: ఉత్పత్తి ఖర్చులు పెరగడం వల్ల వస్తువుల ధరలు పెరగడాన్ని Cost-Push Inflation అంటారు.

9. Other Types of Inflation

  • Built-in Inflation: Inflation sustained through expectations and wage-price adjustments.
  • Imported Inflation: Domestic prices rise due to higher import prices or exchange-rate depreciation.
  • Structural Inflation: Inflation arising from structural bottlenecks such as supply constraints and market rigidities.
  • Core Inflation: A measure that excludes selected volatile components, commonly food and fuel in many Indian discussions.
  • Headline Inflation: Inflation measured using the full index basket.
  • Food Inflation: Increase in prices of food items.

11. Effects of Inflation – ద్రవ్యోల్బణం ప్రభావాలు

Effects on Consumers

  • Reduces purchasing power.
  • Raises household expenditure.
  • Can reduce real savings if interest earned is below inflation.
  • Can affect low-income families more severely.

Effects on Producers

  • Higher input costs can reduce profit margins.
  • Uncertainty makes business planning difficult.
  • Some firms may benefit if selling prices rise faster than costs.

Effects on Borrowers and Lenders

  • Unexpected inflation may benefit borrowers with fixed-rate debt.
  • Lenders may lose purchasing power if interest received does not compensate for inflation.
  • Expected inflation is often reflected in nominal interest rates.

Effects on Government

  • Raises the cost of public procurement.
  • Can increase subsidy and welfare expenditure pressures.
  • May raise nominal tax collections, depending on tax design and economic conditions.

12. Inflation and Income Distribution

  • People with fixed pensions or fixed salaries may lose purchasing power.
  • Workers whose wages adjust slowly may experience a decline in real wages.
  • People holding assets may be affected differently depending on asset prices.
  • Unexpected inflation can redistribute wealth between borrowers and lenders.
  • High inflation can increase uncertainty and inequality.

Real Income: Income adjusted for changes in the price level.

Real Income approximately changes according to nominal income growth minus inflation, when rates are relatively small.

13. Inflation and Interest Rates

The Fisher relationship connects nominal interest rates, real interest rates and expected inflation.

Approximate Fisher Equation: Nominal Interest Rate ≈ Real Interest Rate + Expected Inflation.

తెలుగు: నామమాత్ర వడ్డీ రేటు అనేది వాస్తవ వడ్డీ రేటు మరియు ఆశించిన ద్రవ్యోల్బణం కలయికగా సుమారుగా అర్థం చేసుకోవచ్చు.

Example: If the real interest rate is 2% and expected inflation is 4%, the approximate nominal interest rate is 6%.

14. Deflation – ప్రతి ద్రవ్యోల్బణం

Deflation is a sustained fall in the general price level.

  • Purchasing power of money rises in terms of the goods and services whose prices fall.
  • Consumers may postpone purchases if they expect further price declines.
  • Firms may face falling revenues and profits.
  • Debt burdens can rise in real terms.
  • Persistent deflation can discourage investment and economic activity.

తెలుగు: వస్తువులు, సేవల సాధారణ ధరల స్థాయి నిరంతరంగా తగ్గడాన్ని Deflation అంటారు.

15. Disinflation – ద్రవ్యోల్బణ వేగం తగ్గడం

Disinflation means a decline in the rate of inflation. Prices are still rising, but at a slower rate.

Example:
Year 1 inflation = 8%.
Year 2 inflation = 5%.
This is disinflation, not deflation, because the general price level is still increasing.

తెలుగు: ధరలు పెరుగుతూనే ఉన్నా, పెరుగుదల వేగం తగ్గితే Disinflation అంటారు.

16. Stagflation – స్తబ్ద ద్రవ్యోల్బణం

Stagflation is a situation in which high inflation occurs together with weak economic growth and high unemployment.

  • Rising prices.
  • Weak or stagnant economic growth.
  • High unemployment or underemployment.
  • Often associated with adverse supply shocks.

తెలుగు: అధిక ద్రవ్యోల్బణం, తక్కువ ఆర్థిక వృద్ధి, అధిక నిరుద్యోగం ఒకేసారి ఉండే పరిస్థితిని Stagflation అంటారు.

17. Reflation – ఆర్థిక చైతన్య పునరుద్ధరణ

Reflation refers to policies or developments intended to raise aggregate demand and prices when an economy is experiencing weak demand or deflationary pressure.

  • May involve expansionary fiscal policy.
  • May involve accommodative monetary policy.
  • May support investment and employment during a downturn.
  • It differs from uncontrolled inflation.

18. RBI and Inflation Targeting in India

India follows a flexible inflation-targeting framework under the RBI Act.

  • The framework was formally introduced through amendments to the RBI Act in 2016.
  • The inflation target is set by the Central Government in consultation with the RBI.
  • The target is based on CPI inflation.
  • The Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) determines the policy repo rate to achieve the target.
  • The target is reviewed periodically under the statutory framework.

Inflation Target for 2021–2026

4% CPI inflation with a tolerance band of 2% to 6%.

The target for the next five-year period beginning April 2026 must be checked against the latest official government notification. Do not assume that the 2021–2026 target automatically continues unchanged.

19. Monetary Measures to Control Inflation

The RBI can use monetary policy tools to influence demand, credit and liquidity.

  • Increase in Repo Rate: Can make borrowing costlier and moderate demand.
  • Increase in CRR: Can reduce lendable resources of banks.
  • Increase in SLR: Can affect banks' funds available for additional lending.
  • Open Market Sales: RBI sells government securities to absorb liquidity.
  • Liquidity Management: RBI uses liquidity facilities to manage money-market conditions.
Monetary policy works with time lags and may be less effective against inflation caused mainly by temporary supply shortages.

20. Fiscal Measures to Control Inflation

Fiscal policy is the use of government taxation and expenditure to influence economic activity.

  • Reduce or rationalise non-essential public expenditure when demand is excessive.
  • Increase taxes where appropriate to moderate aggregate demand.
  • Improve public distribution and targeted assistance to vulnerable groups.
  • Invest in storage, transport and supply-chain infrastructure.
  • Improve agricultural productivity and market access.
  • Use targeted subsidies to reduce hardship without creating excessive fiscal pressure.

21. Supply-Side Measures

  • Increase domestic production of essential commodities.
  • Improve storage and cold-chain infrastructure.
  • Reduce transport bottlenecks.
  • Improve market information and distribution.
  • Use imports when necessary to address shortages, subject to trade policy.
  • Prevent hoarding and anti-competitive practices under applicable law.

Supply-side action is particularly important when inflation is caused by food shortages, energy prices or supply-chain disruptions.

22. Inflation in India – Important Features

  • Food prices can have a significant influence on headline CPI inflation.
  • Monsoon conditions can affect agricultural production and food prices.
  • International crude oil prices can affect transport and production costs.
  • Exchange-rate movements can affect the domestic cost of imports.
  • Supply-chain disruptions can increase the prices of essential goods.
  • RBI monetary policy aims to maintain price stability while keeping growth in mind.

25. Mains Answer Framework

Question: Explain the causes and effects of inflation in India and suggest measures to control it.

Introduction: Inflation is a sustained rise in the general price level that reduces the purchasing power of money.

Causes:

  1. Demand-pull pressures from higher consumption and investment.
  2. Cost-push pressures due to fuel, transport and input costs.
  3. Food supply disruptions caused by weather and agricultural conditions.
  4. Imported inflation through international commodity prices and exchange-rate movements.
  5. Supply-chain bottlenecks and structural constraints.

Effects:

  1. Reduces real income and purchasing power.
  2. Affects low-income households disproportionately.
  3. Creates uncertainty for businesses and investors.
  4. Can redistribute income between borrowers and lenders.
  5. May increase fiscal pressure on government.

Measures:

  1. Appropriate RBI monetary policy and liquidity management.
  2. Fiscal discipline and targeted expenditure.
  3. Improved agricultural productivity and storage.
  4. Efficient transport, logistics and supply chains.
  5. Targeted protection for vulnerable households.

Conclusion: A balanced combination of monetary, fiscal and supply-side policies is essential to maintain price stability while supporting inclusive economic growth.
