1. Meaning of External Sector | బాహ్య రంగం అర్థం

English

The external sector refers to the economic transactions of a country with the rest of the world. It includes exports, imports, foreign investment, international borrowing, remittances, foreign exchange transactions and other cross-border financial flows.

తెలుగు

ఒక దేశం ప్రపంచంలోని ఇతర దేశాలతో జరిపే ఆర్థిక లావాదేవీలన్నింటినీ బాహ్య రంగం అంటారు. ఎగుమతులు, దిగుమతులు, విదేశీ పెట్టుబడులు, అంతర్జాతీయ రుణాలు, విదేశీ మారక ద్రవ్య లావాదేవీలు ఇందులో భాగం.

Importance | ప్రాముఖ్యత

  • Expands markets for domestic producers. / దేశీయ ఉత్పత్తిదారులకు మార్కెట్లను విస్తరిస్తుంది.
  • Provides access to imported technology and goods. / విదేశీ సాంకేతికత, వస్తువులు అందుబాటులోకి వస్తాయి.
  • Encourages foreign investment. / విదేశీ పెట్టుబడులను ప్రోత్సహిస్తుంది.
  • Influences foreign exchange reserves. / విదేశీ మారక నిల్వలపై ప్రభావం చూపుతుంది.
  • Connects the domestic economy with global markets. / దేశీయ ఆర్థిక వ్యవస్థను ప్రపంచ మార్కెట్లతో అనుసంధానిస్తుంది.

2. International Trade | అంతర్జాతీయ వాణిజ్యం

International trade means the exchange of goods and services between countries.

రెండు లేదా అంతకంటే ఎక్కువ దేశాల మధ్య వస్తువులు, సేవల మార్పిడిని అంతర్జాతీయ వాణిజ్యం అంటారు.

Exports – ఎగుమతులు

Goods and services sold by domestic residents to buyers in other countries are exports.

మన దేశం నుంచి ఇతర దేశాలకు విక్రయించే వస్తువులు, సేవలను ఎగుమతులు అంటారు.

Imports – దిగుమతులు

Goods and services purchased from other countries are imports.

ఇతర దేశాల నుంచి మన దేశం కొనుగోలు చేసే వస్తువులు, సేవలను దిగుమతులు అంటారు.

Advantages of trade

  • Specialisation and efficient resource use.
  • Access to a wider variety of goods.
  • Competition and productivity improvements.
  • Employment in export-oriented sectors.
  • Technology transfer and international cooperation.

Challenges

  • Dependence on imported critical goods.
  • Global price fluctuations.
  • Trade deficits and external vulnerabilities.
  • Competition for domestic producers.
  • Global economic and geopolitical shocks.

3. Balance of Trade (BOT) | వాణిజ్య సమతుల్యత

Balance of Trade refers to the difference between the value of merchandise exports and merchandise imports during a specified period.

ఒక నిర్దిష్ట కాలంలో వస్తువుల ఎగుమతులు మరియు దిగుమతుల విలువ మధ్య వ్యత్యాసాన్ని Balance of Trade అంటారు.

Formula

Balance of Trade = Merchandise Exports − Merchandise Imports
  • Trade Surplus: Exports of goods exceed imports of goods.
  • Trade Deficit: Imports of goods exceed exports of goods.
  • Balanced Trade: Merchandise exports equal merchandise imports.

Example: If merchandise exports are ₹100 crore and imports are ₹120 crore, the trade balance is a deficit of ₹20 crore.

ఉదాహరణ: ఎగుమతులు ₹100 కోట్లు, దిగుమతులు ₹120 కోట్లు అయితే వాణిజ్య లోటు ₹20 కోట్లు.

4. Balance of Payments (BOP) | చెల్లింపుల శేషం

Balance of Payments is a systematic record of economic transactions between residents of a country and the rest of the world during a specified period.

ఒక దేశ నివాసితులు మరియు ప్రపంచంలోని ఇతర దేశాల మధ్య నిర్దిష్ట కాలంలో జరిగిన ఆర్థిక లావాదేవీల క్రమబద్ధమైన రికార్డును Balance of Payments అంటారు.

Main components

  • Current Account.
  • Capital Account.
  • Financial Account.
  • Errors and Omissions.
  • Reserve assets and related financing entries.
In common Indian exam discussions, capital-account flows are often discussed together with financial flows. In the IMF's detailed presentation, the financial account is distinguished from the capital account.

5. Current Account | కరెంట్ అకౌంట్

The Current Account records transactions involving goods, services, primary income and secondary income between residents and non-residents.

వస్తువులు, సేవలు, ప్రాథమిక ఆదాయం మరియు ద్వితీయ ఆదాయానికి సంబంధించిన అంతర్జాతీయ లావాదేవీలను Current Account నమోదు చేస్తుంది.

Components

  • Goods: Merchandise exports and imports.
  • Services: IT services, tourism, transport, insurance and financial services.
  • Primary Income: Interest, dividends and compensation of employees.
  • Secondary Income: Personal transfers, remittances and certain transfers without a direct exchange.

Current Account Deficit (CAD)

A current account deficit occurs when current-account payments exceed current-account receipts.

Current Accountలో చెల్లింపులు, ఆదాయాల కంటే ఎక్కువగా ఉన్నప్పుడు CAD ఏర్పడుతుంది.

Current Account Surplus

A surplus occurs when current-account receipts exceed payments.

6. Capital Account and Financial Account | క్యాపిటల్, ఫైనాన్షియల్ అకౌంట్లు

Capital Account

In the IMF framework, the capital account records capital transfers and transactions in non-produced, non-financial assets.

Financial Account

The financial account records cross-border transactions in financial assets and liabilities.

  • Foreign Direct Investment (FDI).
  • Foreign Portfolio Investment (FPI).
  • Loans and other investment.
  • Banking capital and deposits.
  • Reserve assets.
Exam distinction: FDI and FPI are financial-account transactions in the detailed international accounts framework, not ordinary merchandise trade.

7. Foreign Exchange | విదేశీ మారక ద్రవ్యం

Foreign exchange refers to foreign currencies and financial instruments used for international payments.

అంతర్జాతీయ చెల్లింపుల కోసం ఉపయోగించే విదేశీ కరెన్సీలు మరియు సంబంధిత ఆర్థిక సాధనాలను Foreign Exchange అంటారు.

Foreign Exchange Market

The foreign exchange market is where currencies are bought and sold. Exchange rates are influenced by demand, supply, monetary policy, trade flows, investment flows and market expectations.

Foreign Exchange Reserves

Foreign exchange reserves are external assets held and managed by a country's monetary authority.

India's reserve components

  • Foreign Currency Assets.
  • Gold.
  • Special Drawing Rights (SDRs).
  • Reserve Tranche Position in the IMF.

The Reserve Bank of India manages India's foreign exchange reserves.

8. Exchange Rate | మారకపు విలువ

An exchange rate is the price of one currency expressed in terms of another currency.

ఒక దేశ కరెన్సీ విలువను మరొక దేశ కరెన్సీలో తెలిపే విలువను Exchange Rate అంటారు.

Appreciation

Currency appreciation means an increase in the value of a currency relative to another currency under a given exchange-rate comparison.

Depreciation

Currency depreciation means a fall in the value of a currency relative to another currency.

Devaluation

Devaluation is an official reduction in the value of a currency under a fixed or managed exchange-rate arrangement.

Revaluation

Revaluation is an official increase in a currency's value under a fixed or managed exchange-rate arrangement.

Appreciation and depreciation are commonly market-driven terms; devaluation and revaluation refer to official changes under fixed or managed systems.

9. Types of Exchange Rate Systems | మారకపు రేటు విధానాలు

Fixed Exchange Rate

The monetary authority maintains the currency at a declared value or within a specified range against another currency or basket.

Floating Exchange Rate

The exchange rate is primarily determined by market demand and supply.

Managed Float

The exchange rate is mainly market-determined, but the central bank may intervene to address disorderly market conditions or excessive volatility.

India's exchange rate system

India follows a market-determined exchange-rate system with intervention by the Reserve Bank of India to manage excessive volatility and disorderly conditions.

10. Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) | విదేశీ ప్రత్యక్ష పెట్టుబడి

FDI is a cross-border investment in which an investor from one country obtains a lasting interest and significant influence in an enterprise in another country.

ఒక విదేశీ పెట్టుబడిదారు మరో దేశంలోని సంస్థలో దీర్ఘకాలిక ప్రయోజనం మరియు గణనీయమైన ప్రభావాన్ని పొందే పెట్టుబడిని FDI అంటారు.

Features

  • Usually has a long-term investment perspective.
  • May involve ownership, management participation or control.
  • Can bring technology, skills and capital.
  • May create employment and production capacity.

Routes in India

  • Automatic Route: Investment permitted without prior government approval, subject to applicable laws and conditions.
  • Government Route: Prior government approval is required.

11. Foreign Portfolio Investment (FPI) | విదేశీ పోర్ట్‌ఫోలియో పెట్టుబడి

FPI refers to investment by foreign investors in financial securities such as shares and bonds without obtaining the same kind of lasting management control associated with FDI.

విదేశీ పెట్టుబడిదారులు భారతీయ షేర్లు, బాండ్లు వంటి ఆర్థిక సెక్యూరిటీలలో చేసే పెట్టుబడులను FPI అంటారు.

FDI vs FPI

FDIFPI
Long-term interest in an enterpriseInvestment in financial securities
Can involve management influenceUsually no management control
Often less liquidUsually more liquid
May bring technology and productive capacityProvides financial market capital

12. Foreign Institutional Investors and Investment Flows | విదేశీ పెట్టుబడి ప్రవాహాలు

Foreign Portfolio Investors invest in financial markets subject to applicable regulations. Their buying and selling activity can influence stock markets, bond markets and currency demand.

Important effects

  • FPI inflows can increase demand for domestic financial assets.
  • Large outflows can create market volatility.
  • Capital flows can affect the exchange rate.
  • Long-term investment and short-term portfolio flows have different risk characteristics.
FPI is not identical to FDI. FPI flows can be more sensitive to global interest rates, risk sentiment and market conditions.

13. Liberalisation of India's External Sector | బాహ్య రంగ సంస్కరణలు

India introduced major external-sector reforms in 1991 following a severe balance-of-payments crisis.

Major reforms

  • Reduction of many import licensing restrictions.
  • Changes in import tariffs.
  • Exchange-rate adjustments and reforms.
  • Greater foreign investment access in several sectors.
  • Expansion of export-oriented activities.
  • Greater integration with global trade and finance.

Objectives

  • Improve external competitiveness.
  • Attract foreign capital and technology.
  • Increase exports.
  • Improve efficiency and productivity.
  • Strengthen external-sector resilience.

14. Foreign Trade Policy | విదేశీ వాణిజ్య విధానం

Foreign Trade Policy provides the framework for regulating and promoting India's exports and imports.

భారతదేశ ఎగుమతులు, దిగుమతులను నియంత్రించడం మరియు ప్రోత్సహించేందుకు రూపొందించే విధానాన్ని Foreign Trade Policy అంటారు.

Important objectives

  • Promote exports of goods and services.
  • Improve trade facilitation.
  • Enhance global competitiveness.
  • Support exporters and employment generation.
  • Reduce unnecessary procedural barriers.

Foreign Trade Policy 2023

India's Foreign Trade Policy 2023 came into effect on 1 April 2023. It focuses on export promotion, ease of doing business, process simplification and emerging areas such as e-commerce exports.

Directorate General of Foreign Trade (DGFT)

DGFT functions under the Ministry of Commerce and Industry and implements the Foreign Trade Policy framework.

15. Special Economic Zones (SEZs) | ప్రత్యేక ఆర్థిక మండలాలు

Special Economic Zones are designated areas with a special economic and regulatory framework to promote exports, investment and economic activity.

ఎగుమతులు, పెట్టుబడులు మరియు ఆర్థిక కార్యకలాపాలను ప్రోత్సహించేందుకు ప్రత్యేక విధానాలు కలిగిన ప్రాంతాలను SEZs అంటారు.

Objectives

  • Promote exports.
  • Attract domestic and foreign investment.
  • Generate employment.
  • Develop infrastructure.
  • Encourage manufacturing and services.

SEZ Act, 2005

The Special Economic Zones Act, 2005 provides the statutory framework for the development, operation and regulation of SEZs in India. The Act came into force in 2006.

16. World Trade Organization (WTO) | ప్రపంచ వాణిజ్య సంస్థ

The WTO is an international organisation that deals with global trade rules and provides a forum for trade negotiations and dispute settlement.

ప్రపంచ వాణిజ్య నియమాలు, చర్చలు మరియు వివాద పరిష్కారాలకు వేదికగా పనిచేసే అంతర్జాతీయ సంస్థ WTO.

Important facts

  • Established on 1 January 1995.
  • Headquarters: Geneva, Switzerland.
  • Replaced the GATT institutional framework.
  • Promotes rules-based international trade.
  • Provides a platform for trade negotiations.
  • Has a dispute settlement framework.

Important principles

  • Most-Favoured-Nation (MFN): Generally requires equal trade treatment among WTO members, subject to permitted exceptions.
  • National Treatment: Imported products should generally receive treatment no less favourable than like domestic products after entry into the market, subject to applicable rules.
  • Transparency: Members are expected to make trade rules and measures transparent.

17. General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade (GATT) | గాట్ ఒప్పందం

GATT was an international agreement aimed at reducing tariffs and other trade barriers. It came into force in 1948.

అంతర్జాతీయ వాణిజ్యంలో సుంకాలు మరియు ఇతర అడ్డంకులను తగ్గించేందుకు రూపొందిన ఒప్పందం GATT.

Important points

  • GATT came into force in 1948.
  • It focused mainly on trade in goods.
  • Several rounds of trade negotiations took place under GATT.
  • The Uruguay Round led to the creation of the WTO.
  • The WTO began functioning in 1995.

18. International Monetary Fund (IMF) | అంతర్జాతీయ ద్రవ్య నిధి

The IMF is an international organisation that promotes international monetary cooperation and global financial stability.

అంతర్జాతీయ ద్రవ్య సహకారం మరియు ప్రపంచ ఆర్థిక స్థిరత్వాన్ని ప్రోత్సహించే సంస్థ IMF.

Important facts

  • Established in 1944 at the Bretton Woods Conference.
  • Began operations in 1945.
  • Headquarters: Washington, D.C., USA.
  • Provides policy advice and financial assistance to member countries.
  • Monitors global economic and financial developments.
  • Issues Special Drawing Rights (SDRs).

Special Drawing Rights (SDR)

SDR is an international reserve asset created by the IMF. It is not a currency, but it can supplement the official reserves of member countries.

19. World Bank Group | ప్రపంచ బ్యాంకు సమూహం

The World Bank Group provides financing, knowledge and assistance for development and poverty reduction.

Important institutions

  • IBRD: International Bank for Reconstruction and Development.
  • IDA: International Development Association.
  • IFC: International Finance Corporation.
  • MIGA: Multilateral Investment Guarantee Agency.
  • ICSID: International Centre for Settlement of Investment Disputes.

Important distinction

The IMF focuses on international monetary cooperation and macroeconomic stability, while the World Bank Group focuses mainly on long-term development and poverty reduction.

20. Regional Trade Agreements | ప్రాంతీయ వాణిజ్య ఒప్పందాలు

Regional Trade Agreements are agreements between two or more countries to reduce trade barriers and promote economic cooperation.

Types

  • Free Trade Area: Members reduce or eliminate tariffs among themselves while maintaining separate external trade policies.
  • Customs Union: Members have free trade among themselves and a common external tariff.
  • Common Market: Includes freer movement of goods, services, capital and labour, subject to the arrangement.
  • Economic Union: Deeper economic integration, potentially including coordination of economic policies.

Important examples

  • ASEAN – Association of Southeast Asian Nations.
  • SAFTA – South Asian Free Trade Area.
  • RCEP – Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership.
  • European Union – A highly integrated regional grouping.

21. India's Major Trade Partners and Trade Composition | వాణిజ్య భాగస్వాములు

India trades with many countries across Asia, Europe, North America, Africa and other regions. The ranking of trade partners changes over time and depends on the year and whether exports, imports or total trade are considered.

Major export categories

  • Engineering goods.
  • Petroleum products.
  • Pharmaceuticals.
  • Gems and jewellery.
  • Textiles and garments.
  • Agricultural and food products.
  • IT and other services.

Major import categories

  • Crude petroleum and petroleum products.
  • Gold and precious metals.
  • Electronics and machinery.
  • Chemicals.
  • Fertilisers.
  • Coal and other energy resources.
Exam tip: Do not memorise a trade partner ranking without checking the financial year and data source.

22. Remittances and International Services | విదేశీ చెల్లింపులు

Remittances are transfers of money by individuals, often migrants or overseas workers, to recipients in their home countries.

విదేశాల్లో పనిచేసే వ్యక్తులు తమ స్వదేశంలోని కుటుంబాలకు లేదా ఇతరులకు పంపే డబ్బును Remittances అంటారు.

Importance to India

  • Support household consumption.
  • Help finance education and healthcare.
  • Provide foreign exchange inflows.
  • Support the current account through secondary income receipts.

Services exports

India exports services such as information technology, business services, financial services, tourism and professional services.

23. Tariffs and Non-Tariff Barriers | సుంకాలు, ఇతర వాణిజ్య అడ్డంకులు

Tariff

A tariff is a tax imposed on imported or exported goods. Import tariffs are commonly used to regulate imports and raise revenue.

Non-Tariff Barriers

  • Import quotas.
  • Licensing requirements.
  • Technical standards.
  • Sanitary and phytosanitary measures.
  • Rules of origin.
  • Administrative restrictions.

Anti-Dumping Duty

An anti-dumping duty may be imposed when imported goods are dumped and cause material injury to the domestic industry, subject to investigation and legal conditions.

Countervailing Duty

A countervailing duty may be imposed to offset certain subsidies provided by a foreign government to exported goods, subject to applicable rules.

Safeguard Measures

Safeguard measures may temporarily restrict imports when increased imports cause or threaten serious injury to a domestic industry, subject to WTO rules.

24. India's Foreign Exchange Management Act (FEMA) | ఫెమా చట్టం

The Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999 provides the legal framework for managing foreign exchange transactions in India.

భారతదేశంలో విదేశీ మారక ద్రవ్య లావాదేవీల నిర్వహణకు FEMA, 1999 చట్టం చట్టపరమైన వ్యవస్థను అందిస్తుంది.

  • Enacted in 1999.
  • Came into force on 1 June 2000.
  • Replaced the Foreign Exchange Regulation Act (FERA), 1973.
  • Aims to facilitate external trade and payments.
  • Supports orderly development and maintenance of the foreign exchange market.
  • RBI and authorised persons have important roles under the Act.

FERA vs FEMA

  • FERA was a stricter foreign exchange regulation law.
  • FEMA provides a management-oriented framework.
  • FEMA violations are generally treated as civil contraventions, subject to statutory provisions.

25. Current Account Convertibility and Capital Account Convertibility | కరెన్సీ మార్పిడి స్వేచ్ఛ

Current Account Convertibility

It refers to the freedom to convert domestic currency into foreign currency for permitted current international transactions, such as trade in goods and services and certain transfers.

Capital Account Convertibility

It refers to freedom to convert domestic financial assets into foreign financial assets and vice versa, subject to the applicable framework.

India's position

India accepted current account convertibility in 1994 under the IMF framework. Capital account convertibility remains subject to regulations and restrictions.

India does not have full capital account convertibility. This distinction is important for competitive examinations.
