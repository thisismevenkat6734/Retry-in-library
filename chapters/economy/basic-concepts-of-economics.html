1. Meaning of Economics – ఆర్థిక శాస్త్రం అర్థం

Economics is a social science that studies how individuals, businesses, governments and societies use limited resources to satisfy unlimited or multiple wants.

ఆర్థిక శాస్త్రం: మనుషుల అవసరాలు అనేకం. కానీ వాటిని తీర్చడానికి అందుబాటులో ఉన్న వనరులు పరిమితం. ఈ పరిమిత వనరులను ఎలా ఉపయోగించుకోవాలి అనే విషయాన్ని అధ్యయనం చేసేదే ఆర్థిక శాస్త్రం.

Core economic problem: Scarcity of resources and unlimited wants.
ప్రధాన ఆర్థిక సమస్య: వనరుల కొరత – అవసరాల అపరిమితత్వం.

Important definitions

  • Adam Smith: Often called the Father of Economics. His famous book The Wealth of Nations was published in 1776.
  • Alfred Marshall: Economics as a study of mankind in the ordinary business of life; associated with the welfare definition.
  • Lionel Robbins: Economics studies human behaviour as a relationship between ends and scarce means which have alternative uses.
  • Paul Samuelson: Emphasised choice and allocation of scarce resources over time, including production and distribution.

2. Nature and Scope of Economics – స్వభావం మరియు పరిధి

  • Economics is a social science. / ఇది సామాజిక శాస్త్రం.
  • It studies production, consumption, exchange and distribution.
  • It examines how resources are allocated among competing uses.
  • It helps analyse economic growth, employment, inflation and poverty.
  • It is both theoretical and practical in application.

Central economic questions

  1. What to produce? ఏ వస్తువులు, సేవలు ఉత్పత్తి చేయాలి?
  2. How to produce? ఏ సాంకేతికత లేదా పద్ధతితో ఉత్పత్తి చేయాలి?
  3. For whom to produce? ఎవరికి ఉత్పత్తి చేయాలి, ఎలా పంపిణీ చేయాలి?

ఈ మూడు ప్రశ్నలు ప్రతి ఆర్థిక వ్యవస్థలో కీలకమైనవి.

3. Scarcity, Choice and Opportunity Cost

Scarcity – కొరత

Scarcity means resources are limited in relation to human wants. Scarcity does not always mean absolute shortage; it means resources cannot satisfy every possible want at the same time.

Choice – ఎంపిక

Because resources are limited, individuals and governments must choose between alternative uses.

Opportunity Cost – అవకాశ వ్యయం

Opportunity cost is the value of the next best alternative sacrificed when a choice is made.

Example: You have ₹100 and choose a book instead of a meal. The opportunity cost is the value of the next best alternative you gave up, not necessarily the total of all alternatives.

తెలుగు: ఒక ఎంపిక చేసినప్పుడు వదులుకున్న తదుపరి ఉత్తమ ప్రత్యామ్నాయం విలువను అవకాశ వ్యయం అంటారు.

4. Microeconomics and Macroeconomics

Microeconomics – సూక్ష్మ ఆర్థిక శాస్త్రంMacroeconomics – స్థూల ఆర్థిక శాస్త్రం
Studies individual economic units.Studies the economy as a whole.
Individual consumer and firm behaviour.National income, inflation and unemployment.
Demand for a particular product.Aggregate demand and aggregate supply.
Price of a specific commodity.General price level.

తెలుగు: ఒక వినియోగదారుడు లేదా ఒక సంస్థ ప్రవర్తనను అధ్యయనం చేయడం Microeconomics. దేశ ఆర్థిక వ్యవస్థ మొత్తాన్ని అధ్యయనం చేయడం Macroeconomics.

5. Positive and Normative Economics

  • Positive Economics: Describes what is, using testable statements and evidence. Example: A rise in price may reduce quantity demanded, other things being equal.
  • Normative Economics: Deals with what ought to be, involving value judgements. Example: The government should increase spending on education.

తెలుగు: వాస్తవాలు, ఆధారాలపై ఆధారపడిన విశ్లేషణ Positive Economics. ఏది జరగాలి అనే అభిప్రాయాలు, విలువలతో కూడిన విశ్లేషణ Normative Economics.

7. Goods and Services – వస్తువులు మరియు సేవలు

  • Goods: Physical or tangible products, such as books, food and furniture.
  • Services: Activities or benefits that are generally intangible, such as teaching, banking and transport.
  • Consumer goods: Goods used directly by consumers.
  • Capital goods: Goods used to produce other goods and services, such as machinery.
  • Intermediate goods: Goods used as inputs in producing other goods.
  • Final goods: Goods ready for final use or investment and not intended for further processing in the current accounting period.

తెలుగు: నేరుగా వినియోగించే వస్తువులు Consumer Goods. ఇతర వస్తువుల ఉత్పత్తికి ఉపయోగించే యంత్రాలు వంటి వాటిని Capital Goods అంటారు.

8. Utility, Value and Wealth

  • Utility: The want-satisfying power of a good or service. / అవసరాన్ని తీర్చే సామర్థ్యం.
  • Value: In economics, value may refer to usefulness or exchange value depending on context.
  • Wealth: Economic assets having value and capable of satisfying wants, subject to the definition used.
  • Economic goods: Goods that are scarce relative to demand and therefore have an opportunity cost.
  • Free goods: Goods available in abundance relative to demand in a particular context, with no opportunity cost in that context.
A good can have utility without having a market price. Utility and price are not identical concepts.

9. Demand – డిమాండ్

Demand refers to the quantity of a good that consumers are willing and able to buy at different prices during a given period, other things remaining constant.

Law of Demand

Other things remaining constant, when price rises, quantity demanded generally falls; when price falls, quantity demanded generally rises.

తెలుగు: ఇతర అంశాలు మారకుండా ఉంటే ధర పెరిగినప్పుడు డిమాండ్ పరిమాణం సాధారణంగా తగ్గుతుంది; ధర తగ్గితే పెరుగుతుంది.

Factors affecting demand

  • Price of the commodity
  • Consumer income
  • Prices of related goods
  • Tastes and preferences
  • Population and number of buyers
  • Expectations about future prices and income

Substitute and Complementary goods

  • Substitutes: Goods that can replace each other, such as tea and coffee in some uses.
  • Complements: Goods used together, such as cars and fuel.

10. Supply – సరఫరా

Supply is the quantity of a good that producers are willing and able to offer for sale at different prices during a given period.

Law of Supply

Other things remaining constant, a higher price generally leads to a higher quantity supplied, while a lower price generally leads to a lower quantity supplied.

తెలుగు: ఇతర అంశాలు మారకుండా ఉంటే ధర పెరిగినప్పుడు సరఫరా పరిమాణం సాధారణంగా పెరుగుతుంది.

Factors affecting supply

  • Price of the product
  • Cost of inputs
  • Technology
  • Taxes and subsidies
  • Number of sellers
  • Producer expectations
  • Natural conditions

11. Market and Market Structures

  • Perfect Competition: Many buyers and sellers, homogeneous products, and free entry and exit in the ideal model.
  • Monopoly: A single seller with no close substitute and significant barriers to entry.
  • Monopolistic Competition: Many firms selling differentiated products.
  • Oligopoly: A market dominated by a small number of large firms whose decisions are interdependent.

తెలుగు: మార్కెట్ నిర్మాణం అనేది మార్కెట్‌లో సంస్థల సంఖ్య, పోటీ స్థాయి, ఉత్పత్తుల స్వభావం మరియు కొత్త సంస్థల ప్రవేశంపై ఆధారపడి ఉంటుంది.

12. Production Possibility Curve (PPC)

A Production Possibility Curve shows the maximum possible combinations of two goods that an economy can produce with given resources and technology, assuming efficient use of resources.

  • On the curve: Productively efficient combinations.
  • Inside the curve: Underutilisation or inefficiency of resources.
  • Outside the curve: Unattainable with current resources and technology.
  • Outward shift: Economic growth due to more resources or better technology.
PPC is generally concave to the origin because of increasing opportunity cost, under standard assumptions.

14. Economic Growth and Economic Development

  • Economic Growth: Increase in real output or real national income over time.
  • Economic Development: A broader process involving growth and improvements in living standards, health, education, opportunities and structural change.
  • Per Capita Income: National income divided by population, subject to the income measure used.
  • Human Development: Focuses on expanding people's capabilities and opportunities.

తెలుగు: దేశంలో ఉత్పత్తి లేదా వాస్తవ ఆదాయం పెరగడం Economic Growth. ప్రజల జీవన ప్రమాణాలు, ఆరోగ్యం, విద్య, అవకాశాలు కూడా మెరుగుపడటం Economic Development.

17. Mains Answer Framework – సమాధానం రాయడానికి

Question: Explain the basic economic problem of scarcity and choice.

Introduction: Economics studies the allocation of scarce resources among competing human wants.

Body:

  1. Human wants are numerous and continuously changing.
  2. Resources such as land, labour, capital and time are limited.
  3. Resources have alternative uses, creating the need for choice.
  4. Every choice involves an opportunity cost.
  5. Societies must decide what, how and for whom to produce.

Conclusion: Efficient allocation of scarce resources is essential for economic welfare, inclusive growth and sustainable development.

తెలుగు సూచన: పరిచయం, కొరత కారణాలు, ఎంపిక అవసరం, అవకాశ వ్యయం, మూడు ప్రధాన ఆర్థిక ప్రశ్నలు, ముగింపు అనే క్రమంలో సమాధానం రాయండి.
