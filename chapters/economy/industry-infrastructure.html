1. Meaning and Importance of Industry | పరిశ్రమల అర్థం, ప్రాముఖ్యత

English

Industry refers to economic activities involving the production, processing and manufacturing of goods, and certain services. Industrial development transforms raw materials into finished products and contributes to economic growth.

తెలుగు

ముడి పదార్థాలను ప్రాసెస్ చేసి తుది వస్తువులుగా తయారు చేయడం మరియు సంబంధిత ఉత్పత్తి కార్యకలాపాలను పరిశ్రమలు అంటారు. పారిశ్రామిక అభివృద్ధి ఆర్థిక వృద్ధికి కీలకం.

Importance | ప్రాముఖ్యత

  • Employment: Creates direct and indirect jobs. / ప్రత్యక్ష, పరోక్ష ఉపాధి కల్పిస్తుంది.
  • GDP Growth: Increases value addition and national output. / జాతీయ ఉత్పత్తిని పెంచుతుంది.
  • Modernisation: Encourages technology and innovation. / సాంకేతిక అభివృద్ధికి తోడ్పడుతుంది.
  • Exports: Helps earn foreign exchange. / విదేశీ మారక ద్రవ్య ఆదాయాన్ని పెంచుతుంది.
  • Infrastructure: Supports transport, power and communication development. / మౌలిక సదుపాయాల అభివృద్ధికి దోహదపడుతుంది.
  • Regional Development: Creates economic opportunities outside major cities. / ప్రాంతీయ అభివృద్ధికి తోడ్పడుతుంది.

2. Classification of Industries | పరిశ్రమల వర్గీకరణ

Based on economic activity

  • Primary Industries: Activities based on natural resources, such as mining, fishing and forestry.
  • Secondary Industries: Manufacturing and construction that transform raw materials into products.
  • Tertiary Industries: Services such as banking, transport, trade and communication.

Based on ownership

  • Public Sector: Owned or controlled by government.
  • Private Sector: Owned by private individuals or companies.
  • Joint Sector: Ownership or participation shared by government and private entities.
  • Cooperative Sector: Owned and managed by members for their common benefit.

Based on size

  • Micro enterprises.
  • Small enterprises.
  • Medium enterprises.
  • Large-scale industries.
A service activity can belong to the tertiary sector even when it is closely connected with manufacturing.

3. Industrial Development in India | భారత పారిశ్రామిక అభివృద్ధి

India's industrial policy evolved from a strong public-sector role after independence towards a more liberalised and globally integrated industrial economy after the reforms of 1991.

స్వాతంత్ర్యం తరువాత ప్రభుత్వ రంగానికి అధిక ప్రాధాన్యత ఇచ్చిన భారత పారిశ్రామిక విధానం, 1991 ఆర్థిక సంస్కరణల తరువాత ప్రైవేట్ పెట్టుబడులు మరియు ప్రపంచ మార్కెట్లకు మరింతగా తెరుచుకుంది.

  • Before Independence: Traditional industries, textiles, mining and limited modern manufacturing developed under colonial economic conditions.
  • 1948 Industrial Policy: Recognised the importance of both public and private sectors.
  • 1956 Industrial Policy: Emphasised public-sector leadership and industrial development.
  • 1977 Industrial Policy: Gave attention to small-scale and cottage industries.
  • 1980 Industrial Policy: Emphasised modernisation, productivity and competition.
  • 1991 New Industrial Policy: Introduced major liberalisation, delicensing and greater private-sector participation.

4. Industrial Policy Resolution, 1956 | పారిశ్రామిక విధాన తీర్మానం 1956

The Industrial Policy Resolution of 1956 was a landmark policy that expanded the role of the public sector in India's industrial development.

1956 పారిశ్రామిక విధాన తీర్మానం భారతదేశ పారిశ్రామిక అభివృద్ధిలో ప్రభుత్వ రంగానికి ముఖ్యమైన పాత్రను కల్పించింది.

  • Emphasised a socialist pattern of society.
  • Placed strategic and basic industries under strong public-sector leadership.
  • Encouraged industrialisation and capital goods production.
  • Recognised the role of small-scale industries.
  • Promoted balanced regional development.

Three broad schedules

  • Schedule A: Industries exclusively reserved for the State.
  • Schedule B: Industries in which the State would progressively expand its role, with private participation also possible.
  • Schedule C: Remaining industries generally left to private initiative, subject to regulation.
The 1956 policy is associated with public-sector expansion and the socialist pattern of society.

5. New Industrial Policy, 1991 | నూతన పారిశ్రామిక విధానం

The New Industrial Policy of 1991 was introduced during a serious balance-of-payments crisis. It transformed India's industrial framework through liberalisation, privatisation and globalisation.

1991లో చెల్లింపుల సమతుల్యత సంక్షోభం నేపథ్యంలో కొత్త పారిశ్రామిక విధానం ప్రవేశపెట్టబడింది. ఇది సరళీకరణ, ప్రైవేటీకరణ, ప్రపంచీకరణకు మార్గం సుగమం చేసింది.

  • Industrial Delicensing: Licensing was abolished for most industries, subject to specified exceptions.
  • Public Sector Reforms: Reduced the number of industries reserved exclusively for the public sector.
  • Foreign Investment: Liberalised foreign direct investment in several sectors.
  • Foreign Technology: Simplified access to foreign technology agreements in specified cases.
  • Competition: Encouraged greater competition and private enterprise.
  • Disinvestment: Allowed government to reduce its stake in selected public enterprises.

Three key terms

  • Liberalisation: Reducing unnecessary government controls and restrictions.
  • Privatisation: Increasing private ownership or participation in economic activities.
  • Globalisation: Greater integration with the world economy through trade, investment and technology.

6. Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises (MSMEs) | సూక్ష్మ, చిన్న, మధ్యతరహా పరిశ్రమలు

MSMEs are important for employment, entrepreneurship, exports, local production and inclusive industrial growth.

ఉపాధి, వ్యాపారవేత్తల అభివృద్ధి, ఎగుమతులు మరియు స్థానిక ఉత్పత్తిలో MSME రంగం కీలక పాత్ర పోషిస్తుంది.

Classification effective from 1 April 2025

CategoryInvestment limitAnnual turnover limit
MicroUp to ₹2.5 croreUp to ₹10 crore
SmallUp to ₹25 croreUp to ₹100 crore
MediumUp to ₹125 croreUp to ₹500 crore

Both investment and turnover criteria are used for classification. An enterprise must satisfy the applicable limits for its category under the rules.

Importance of MSMEs

  • Generate employment with relatively lower capital requirements.
  • Promote entrepreneurship and innovation.
  • Support large industries through supply chains.
  • Encourage rural and semi-urban industrialisation.
  • Contribute to exports and local manufacturing.

Challenges

  • Limited access to affordable credit.
  • Delayed payments from buyers.
  • Technology and skill gaps.
  • Marketing and competition challenges.
  • High compliance and operating costs.

7. Important Industrial Schemes | ముఖ్య పారిశ్రామిక పథకాలు

Make in India

Launched in September 2014, Make in India aims to promote manufacturing, investment, innovation, skill development and ease of doing business.

Production Linked Incentive (PLI) Scheme

PLI schemes provide incentives linked to incremental production or sales in selected sectors, subject to scheme conditions.

  • Promotes domestic manufacturing.
  • Encourages investment and scale.
  • Supports selected strategic and technology-intensive sectors.

Startup India

Launched in 2016 to promote innovation, entrepreneurship and startup development.

Stand-Up India

Promotes bank loans for eligible SC/ST borrowers and women entrepreneurs for setting up greenfield enterprises.

PM Vishwakarma

Supports traditional artisans and craftspeople through skill training, tools, credit and market-related support under scheme guidelines.

PMEGP

Prime Minister's Employment Generation Programme supports eligible micro-enterprise creation through credit-linked subsidy arrangements.

8. Public Sector Enterprises | ప్రభుత్వ రంగ సంస్థలు

Public Sector Enterprises (PSEs) are enterprises owned or controlled by the Central Government, State Governments or both.

కేంద్ర లేదా రాష్ట్ర ప్రభుత్వాల యాజమాన్యంలో లేదా నియంత్రణలో పనిచేసే సంస్థలను ప్రభుత్వ రంగ సంస్థలు అంటారు.

Importance

  • Development of strategic sectors.
  • Infrastructure investment.
  • Regional development and employment.
  • Supply of essential goods and services.
  • Support for industrial growth.

Maharatna, Navratna and Miniratna

These are categories of Central Public Sector Enterprises that receive different levels of financial and operational autonomy, subject to eligibility criteria and government guidelines.

  • Maharatna: Highest category among these recognised CPSE status groups.
  • Navratna: Provides substantial operational and investment autonomy to eligible CPSEs.
  • Miniratna: Includes Category I and Category II CPSEs meeting prescribed conditions.

Disinvestment and Privatisation

  • Disinvestment: Government sells part or all of its shareholding in a public enterprise.
  • Strategic Disinvestment: Sale of a substantial government stake along with transfer of management control, as applicable.
  • Privatisation: Transfer of ownership or control to private entities.

9. Industrial Corridors | పారిశ్రామిక కారిడార్లు

Industrial corridors are planned geographical zones that connect industrial centres with transport networks, logistics facilities, urban centres and supporting infrastructure.

పరిశ్రమలను రవాణా, లాజిస్టిక్స్, నగరాలు మరియు మౌలిక సదుపాయాలతో అనుసంధానించే ప్రణాళికాబద్ధ ప్రాంతాలను పారిశ్రామిక కారిడార్లు అంటారు.

Objectives

  • Attract industrial investment.
  • Improve logistics and transport efficiency.
  • Create manufacturing clusters.
  • Generate employment.
  • Develop industrial townships and infrastructure.

Important examples

  • Delhi–Mumbai Industrial Corridor (DMIC).
  • Chennai–Bengaluru Industrial Corridor (CBIC).
  • Amritsar–Kolkata Industrial Corridor (AKIC).
  • East Coast Industrial Corridor (ECIC).
  • Bengaluru–Mumbai Economic Corridor (BMEC).
The East Coast Industrial Corridor is especially relevant to eastern coastal States, including Andhra Pradesh.

10. Infrastructure: Meaning and Importance | మౌలిక సదుపాయాలు

Infrastructure refers to the basic physical and organisational facilities required for economic and social activities.

ఆర్థిక, సామాజిక కార్యకలాపాలు సజావుగా జరగడానికి అవసరమైన ప్రాథమిక భౌతిక మరియు సంస్థాగత సదుపాయాలను మౌలిక సదుపాయాలు అంటారు.

Types

  • Economic Infrastructure: Roads, railways, ports, airports, electricity, irrigation, telecommunications and logistics.
  • Social Infrastructure: Schools, colleges, hospitals, sanitation, drinking water and housing.

Importance

  • Reduces transportation and production costs.
  • Improves productivity.
  • Encourages private investment.
  • Connects markets and regions.
  • Improves living standards.
  • Supports inclusive growth.

11. Transport Infrastructure | రవాణా మౌలిక సదుపాయాలు

Road Transport

Roads connect villages, towns, cities, markets and industrial centres. They are important for passenger and freight movement.

  • National Highways connect major cities, economic centres and regions.
  • State Highways connect important locations within States.
  • Rural roads improve village connectivity.
  • Expressways provide controlled-access, high-speed road connections.

Important road programmes

  • National Highways Development Project: Major programme for highway development.
  • Golden Quadrilateral: Connects Delhi, Mumbai, Chennai and Kolkata.
  • North–South and East–West Corridors: Improve long-distance road connectivity.
  • Bharatmala Pariyojana: Aims to improve road connectivity, freight efficiency and economic corridors.
  • PMGSY: Rural road connectivity programme.

Railways

Indian Railways is a major transport system for passengers and freight.

  • Supports movement of bulk goods.
  • Connects industrial and agricultural markets.
  • Provides mass passenger transport.
  • Dedicated Freight Corridors improve freight movement.
  • Electrification and modernisation improve efficiency.

Water Transport

Includes inland waterways, coastal shipping and maritime transport. It can be cost-effective for transporting large volumes of goods.

Air Transport

Supports fast passenger movement, high-value cargo and regional connectivity.

12. Ports and Shipping | ఓడరేవులు, సముద్ర రవాణా

Ports are gateways for international trade and coastal transport. They support imports, exports, industrial development and logistics.

  • Deendayal Port, Gujarat.

  • Mormugao Port, Goa.
  • New Mangalore Port, Karnataka.

Sagarmala Programme

Sagarmala aims to promote port-led development through port modernisation, port connectivity, coastal economic zones and coastal community development.

13. Energy Infrastructure | ఇంధన మౌలిక సదుపాయాలు

Energy infrastructure includes electricity generation, transmission, distribution, fuel supply and renewable energy systems.

Major energy sources

  • Thermal: Electricity generated mainly using coal, gas or other fuels.
  • Hydropower: Electricity generated from flowing or stored water.
  • Nuclear: Electricity produced through nuclear reactions.
  • Solar: Energy from sunlight.
  • Wind: Energy from moving air.
  • Biomass: Energy from organic material.

Renewable Energy

Renewable sources are naturally replenished. Solar and wind energy are important components of India's energy transition.

Important programmes

  • National Solar Mission: Promotes solar energy development.
  • PM-KUSUM: Supports solar energy solutions in agriculture.
  • UJALA: Promotes energy-efficient LED lighting.
  • Saubhagya: Household electrification programme.
  • Green Energy Corridor: Supports transmission infrastructure for renewable energy integration.

14. Telecommunications and Digital Infrastructure | టెలికమ్యూనికేషన్

Telecommunication infrastructure includes mobile networks, broadband, optical fibre, satellites, data centres and digital communication systems.

Importance

  • Connects people and businesses.
  • Supports digital payments and e-commerce.
  • Enables online education and healthcare.
  • Improves government service delivery.
  • Supports digital entrepreneurship.

Important initiatives

  • Digital India: Promotes digital infrastructure, digital services and digital empowerment.
  • BharatNet: Aims to provide broadband connectivity to Gram Panchayats and rural areas.
  • 5G: Supports high-speed connectivity and applications such as industrial automation.
  • Common Service Centres: Provide access to digital and government services in rural areas.

15. Logistics and Warehousing | లాజిస్టిక్స్, గిడ్డంగులు

Logistics involves planning, storing, handling and transporting goods from producers to consumers.

ఉత్పత్తిదారుల నుంచి వినియోగదారుల వరకు వస్తువుల నిల్వ, నిర్వహణ మరియు రవాణా ప్రక్రియను లాజిస్టిక్స్ అంటారు.

Components

  • Warehousing and storage.
  • Road, rail, air and water freight.
  • Cold-chain facilities.
  • Inventory management.
  • Packaging and distribution.
  • Digital tracking and supply-chain management.

National Logistics Policy

Launched in 2022, the National Logistics Policy aims to improve logistics efficiency, reduce logistics costs and strengthen the competitiveness of Indian products.

PM Gati Shakti

PM Gati Shakti National Master Plan, launched in 2021, promotes integrated planning and coordinated development of multimodal infrastructure.

16. Public-Private Partnership (PPP) | ప్రభుత్వ-ప్రైవేట్ భాగస్వామ్యం

PPP is a framework in which government and private entities cooperate to finance, build, operate or maintain infrastructure and public services under agreed arrangements.

ప్రభుత్వం మరియు ప్రైవేట్ సంస్థలు కలిసి మౌలిక సదుపాయాలను నిర్మించడం, నిర్వహించడం లేదా సేవలు అందించే విధానాన్ని PPP అంటారు.

Common models

  • BOT: Build–Operate–Transfer.
  • BOOT: Build–Own–Operate–Transfer.
  • DBFOT: Design–Build–Finance–Operate–Transfer.
  • HAM: Hybrid Annuity Model, used in certain infrastructure projects.

Advantages

  • Mobilises private investment.
  • Can improve project management and efficiency.
  • Shares project risks under contract arrangements.
  • May accelerate infrastructure creation.

Challenges

  • Contract disputes.
  • Demand and revenue uncertainty.
  • Land acquisition delays.
  • Regulatory and approval issues.
  • Need for transparent risk allocation.

17. Infrastructure Financing | మౌలిక సదుపాయాల ఆర్థిక వనరులు

Sources

  • Government budgetary support.
  • Public-sector investment.
  • Private-sector investment.
  • Bank and financial institution loans.
  • Infrastructure bonds.
  • Multilateral development finance.
  • Public-Private Partnerships.

National Infrastructure Pipeline (NIP)

The National Infrastructure Pipeline was announced in 2019 and provides a framework for infrastructure investment planning across sectors.

National Investment and Infrastructure Fund (NIIF)

NIIF is an investment platform established to mobilise capital for infrastructure and other strategic investment opportunities in India.

Infrastructure Investment Trusts (InvITs)

InvITs are investment vehicles that allow investors to invest in income-generating infrastructure assets, subject to regulatory requirements.

18. Industrial Challenges in India | భారత పరిశ్రమల సమస్యలు

  • Infrastructure Gaps: Inadequate power, transport and logistics in some regions.
  • Credit Constraints: Difficulties in accessing affordable finance, especially for smaller firms.
  • Technology Gaps: Need for modern machinery, research and innovation.
  • Skill Shortages: Mismatch between industry requirements and available skills.
  • Regulatory Complexity: Multiple approvals and compliance requirements.
  • Import Dependence: Reliance on imported components and critical materials in certain sectors.
  • Regional Imbalance: Industrial investment is concentrated in certain locations.
  • Environmental Concerns: Pollution, resource use and waste management challenges.

Possible solutions

  • Improve infrastructure and logistics.
  • Strengthen vocational education and industry-linked skills.
  • Improve access to finance for MSMEs.
  • Promote research, innovation and technology adoption.
  • Encourage sustainable industrial development.
  • Improve ease of doing business and policy stability.

19. Industrial Development in Andhra Pradesh | ఆంధ్రప్రదేశ్ పారిశ్రామిక అభివృద్ధి

Andhra Pradesh has opportunities in manufacturing, food processing, pharmaceuticals, electronics, textiles, ports, logistics and renewable energy.

Important industrial and economic features

  • Visakhapatnam is an important port and industrial centre.
  • Krishnapatnam and other port-linked locations support trade and logistics.
  • Food processing is linked to the State's agricultural and horticultural production.
  • Pharmaceuticals, chemicals and petrochemical-related activities are important industrial areas.
  • Electronics and manufacturing investments contribute to industrial diversification.
  • Industrial parks and corridors support investment and employment.

Key development needs

  • Reliable power and water supply.
  • Efficient road, rail and port connectivity.
  • Skill development and technical education.
  • Support for MSMEs and local suppliers.
  • Environmental safeguards and sustainable industrialisation.
