1. Meaning of Poverty | పేదరికం అర్థం

English: Poverty is a condition in which individuals or households lack sufficient resources to meet basic needs and maintain an acceptable standard of living.

తెలుగు: వ్యక్తులు లేదా కుటుంబాలు కనీస అవసరాలను తీర్చుకోవడానికి, గౌరవప్రదమైన జీవన ప్రమాణాన్ని కొనసాగించడానికి తగిన వనరులు లేని పరిస్థితిని పేదరికం అంటారు.

Basic needs | ప్రాథమిక అవసరాలు

  • Food and adequate nutrition / ఆహారం, పోషకాహారం.
  • Clothing and shelter / దుస్తులు, నివాసం.
  • Education / విద్య.
  • Healthcare / ఆరోగ్య సంరక్షణ.
  • Safe drinking water and sanitation / సురక్షిత తాగునీరు, పారిశుద్ధ్యం.
  • Access to livelihood and essential services / ఉపాధి, ప్రాథమిక సేవలు.

Types of Poverty

  • Absolute Poverty: Inability to meet a minimum standard of basic needs.
  • Relative Poverty: Poverty measured in comparison with the living standards of other people in a society.
  • Chronic Poverty: Poverty that persists for a long period.
  • Transient Poverty: Temporary poverty caused by shocks such as illness, job loss or crop failure.
  • Rural Poverty: Poverty experienced by people in rural areas.
  • Urban Poverty: Poverty experienced by people in urban areas.

2. Causes of Poverty in India | భారతదేశంలో పేదరికానికి కారణాలు

  • Unemployment: Lack of stable employment reduces household income. / స్థిరమైన ఉపాధి లేకపోవడం.
  • Low Productivity: Low agricultural and industrial productivity limits earnings. / తక్కువ ఉత్పాదకత.
  • Population Pressure: Rapid population growth can increase pressure on resources and services. / జనాభా ఒత్తిడి.
  • Unequal Distribution: Unequal distribution of income, assets and opportunities. / ఆదాయం, ఆస్తుల అసమాన పంపిణీ.
  • Low Education: Limited education and skills restrict employment opportunities. / విద్య, నైపుణ్యాల కొరత.
  • Health Problems: Illness and high healthcare costs can push families into poverty. / అనారోగ్యం, వైద్య ఖర్చులు.
  • Regional Imbalances: Unequal development across regions. / ప్రాంతీయ అభివృద్ధిలో అసమానతలు.
  • Social Discrimination: Barriers based on social and economic circumstances. / సామాజిక వివక్ష.
  • Indebtedness: Excessive debt can reduce disposable household income. / అధిక అప్పుల భారం.

3. Poverty Measurement | పేదరికం కొలత

Poverty measurement attempts to identify people whose income or consumption is below a specified poverty threshold.

నిర్దేశించిన కనీస ఆదాయం లేదా వినియోగ స్థాయికి దిగువన ఉన్నవారిని గుర్తించే విధానాన్ని పేదరికం కొలత అంటారు.

Poverty Line

A poverty line is a threshold used to identify individuals or households unable to afford a specified minimum level of consumption or resources.

Consumption Expenditure

Household consumption expenditure has historically been an important basis for estimating poverty in India. It includes spending on food and non-food items.

Head Count Ratio (HCR)

Head Count Ratio = (Number of people below poverty line ÷ Total population) × 100

It measures the percentage of the population below the poverty line. It does not show how far below the poverty line poor people are.

Poverty Gap

The poverty gap measures the average shortfall of poor people's consumption or income from the poverty line. It indicates the depth of poverty.

Limitations of income-based measurement

  • May not capture health and education deprivation.
  • May not reflect differences in cost of living.
  • May overlook access to public services.
  • Income and consumption can vary over time.

4. Important Poverty Estimation Committees | పేదరిక అంచనా కమిటీలు

Alagh Committee (1979)

Formulated a poverty line approach based on minimum calorie requirements and consumption expenditure.

Lakdawala Expert Group (1993)

  • Recommended poverty estimation based on consumption expenditure.
  • Used state-specific poverty lines and price indices.
  • Linked poverty estimates to calorie-based consumption norms used in the methodology.

Tendulkar Committee (2009)

  • Recommended a broader consumption-based poverty estimation methodology.
  • Moved away from direct reliance on calorie norms as the sole basis.
  • Considered expenditure on health and education more explicitly.
  • Used a common reference poverty line basket with adjustments for price differences.

Rangarajan Committee (2014)

  • Suggested a different poverty estimation methodology from Tendulkar.
  • Used separate normative food and non-food expenditure considerations.
  • Proposed higher poverty thresholds than the Tendulkar approach for the reference period.
Exam caution: Committee estimates refer to specific historical methodologies and reference years. Do not treat an old poverty line as a current universal income threshold.

5. Multidimensional Poverty Index (MPI) | బహుముఖ పేదరిక సూచీ

Multidimensional poverty recognises that poverty is not only a lack of income. People may also experience deprivation in health, education and living standards.

ఆదాయం మాత్రమే కాకుండా ఆరోగ్యం, విద్య, జీవన ప్రమాణాల్లో కూడా లోపాలు ఉంటే దానిని బహుముఖ పేదరికంగా పరిగణిస్తారు.

Three dimensions

  • Health: Nutrition and child mortality indicators.
  • Education: Years of schooling and school attendance.
  • Standard of Living: Cooking fuel, sanitation, drinking water, electricity, housing, assets and related indicators.

Global MPI

The Global Multidimensional Poverty Index is developed by the United Nations Development Programme (UNDP) and Oxford Poverty and Human Development Initiative (OPHI).

National MPI

NITI Aayog has published national multidimensional poverty-related reports for India using an adapted indicator framework.

MPI identifies overlapping deprivations experienced by the same person or household, rather than measuring income alone.

6. Poverty Alleviation Programmes | పేదరిక నిర్మూలన పథకాలు

Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Act (MGNREGA)

  • Enacted in 2005.
  • Provides a statutory guarantee of up to 100 days of wage employment in a financial year to eligible rural households whose adult members volunteer for unskilled manual work.
  • Promotes livelihood security and creation of rural assets.
  • Gram Panchayats have an important implementation role.

National Rural Livelihoods Mission (NRLM)

Also known as DAY-NRLM, it promotes self-help groups, financial inclusion, livelihoods and community institutions among rural poor households.

National Urban Livelihoods Mission (NULM)

DAY-NULM focuses on urban livelihoods, skill development, self-employment and support for the urban poor.

Pradhan Mantri Awas Yojana (PMAY)

PMAY supports access to affordable housing through its rural and urban components, subject to scheme guidelines.

National Food Security Act (NFSA), 2013

Provides a legal framework for subsidised food grains to eligible persons and households.

Antyodaya Anna Yojana (AAY)

Targets the poorest of the poor households for highly subsidised food grains under the public distribution system.

7. Meaning of Unemployment | నిరుద్యోగం అర్థం

English: Unemployment refers to a situation in which people who are willing and able to work and are seeking work cannot find employment.

తెలుగు: పని చేయడానికి సిద్ధంగా ఉండి, పని చేసే సామర్థ్యం కలిగి, ఉద్యోగం కోసం ప్రయత్నిస్తున్నప్పటికీ ఉపాధి లభించని పరిస్థితిని నిరుద్యోగం అంటారు.

Important distinction

Not every person without a job is counted as unemployed. A person who is not seeking or available for work may be classified outside the labour force under standard labour-statistics definitions.

8. Types of Unemployment | నిరుద్యోగ రకాలు

  • Open Unemployment: People willing and able to work cannot find any job. / పని చేయాలనుకున్నా ఉద్యోగం దొరకకపోవడం.
  • Disguised Unemployment: More workers are engaged than actually required; some workers can be removed without reducing output. / అవసరానికి మించి కార్మికులు ఉండటం.
  • Seasonal Unemployment: Employment is available only during certain seasons. / కొన్ని కాలాల్లో మాత్రమే పని లభించడం.
  • Structural Unemployment: A mismatch between workers' skills or locations and available jobs. / నైపుణ్యాలు, ఉద్యోగ అవసరాల మధ్య అసమతుల్యత.
  • Frictional Unemployment: Temporary unemployment while changing jobs or entering the labour market. / ఉద్యోగ మార్పు సమయంలో తాత్కాలిక నిరుద్యోగం.
  • Cyclical Unemployment: Unemployment caused by a fall in aggregate demand during an economic downturn. / ఆర్థిక మాంద్యం కారణంగా ఉపాధి తగ్గడం.
  • Technological Unemployment: Jobs lost or transformed because of technological change and automation. / ఆటోమేషన్ వల్ల ఉద్యోగాల స్వరూపం మారడం లేదా తగ్గడం.
  • Educated Unemployment: Educated people are unable to find jobs matching their qualifications or expectations. / చదువుకున్న వారికి తగిన ఉద్యోగాలు లభించకపోవడం.
  • Underemployment: People work fewer hours than they want or in jobs that do not fully use their skills. / సామర్థ్యానికి లేదా అవసరానికి తగినంత పని లేకపోవడం.

9. Disguised Unemployment in Agriculture | వ్యవసాయంలో దాగి ఉన్న నిరుద్యోగం

Disguised unemployment is common in situations where too many people work on a farm or family enterprise relative to the output produced.

ఒక వ్యవసాయ క్షేత్రంలో అవసరానికి మించి వ్యక్తులు పనిచేస్తూ, కొంతమంది తొలగించినా మొత్తం ఉత్పత్తి తగ్గని పరిస్థితిని దాగి ఉన్న నిరుద్యోగం అంటారు.

Example

If five workers are sufficient to produce the same output but eight people are working, the additional three may represent disguised unemployment.

Reasons

  • Small and fragmented landholdings.
  • Dependence on agriculture.
  • Limited non-farm employment.
  • Seasonal nature of agricultural work.

Solutions

  • Rural industrialisation.
  • Agro-processing and food industries.
  • Skill development.
  • Improved irrigation and productivity.
  • Non-farm rural employment opportunities.

10. Measurement of Unemployment in India | భారతదేశంలో నిరుద్యోగ కొలత

The Periodic Labour Force Survey (PLFS) is an important official source of labour-force statistics in India.

Periodic Labour Force Survey (PLFS) భారతదేశంలో ఉపాధి, నిరుద్యోగం మరియు కార్మిక శక్తికి సంబంధించిన ముఖ్యమైన అధికారిక గణాంక సర్వే.

Conducting agency

National Statistical Office (NSO), under the Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation (MoSPI), conducts PLFS.

Important concepts

  • Labour Force: Employed persons plus unemployed persons who are seeking and/or available for work under the applicable measure.
  • Worker Population Ratio (WPR): Percentage of the population that is employed.
  • Labour Force Participation Rate (LFPR): Percentage of the population that is in the labour force.
  • Unemployment Rate (UR): Percentage of the labour force that is unemployed.
Unemployment Rate = (Unemployed persons ÷ Labour force) × 100

Usual Status

Usual status considers a relatively long reference period, generally 365 days preceding the survey date, with principal and subsidiary activity status.

Current Weekly Status (CWS)

CWS considers the activity status during the seven days preceding the survey date.

11. Employment Structure in India | భారత ఉపాధి నిర్మాణం

Employment in India is spread across agriculture, industry and services. The share of workers in each sector changes as the economy develops.

Primary Sector

  • Agriculture and allied activities.
  • Forestry and fishing.
  • Mining and quarrying.

Secondary Sector

  • Manufacturing.
  • Construction.
  • Electricity, gas and related utilities.

Tertiary Sector

  • Trade and transport.
  • Banking and insurance.
  • Education and healthcare.
  • IT and communication.
  • Public administration and other services.

Organised and Unorganised Employment

  • Organised Sector: Generally operates under formal registration, regulation and employment arrangements.
  • Unorganised Sector: Often includes smaller, less formally regulated economic activities and workers with limited social security.

12. Formal and Informal Employment | అధికారిక, అనధికారిక ఉపాధి

Formal employment generally provides documented employment relationships and access to statutory protections where applicable.

Informal employment often involves limited written contracts, uncertain earnings and weaker access to social security.

Challenges faced by informal workers

  • Income insecurity.
  • Limited health and pension protection.
  • Unsafe working conditions.
  • Irregular working hours.
  • Limited access to formal credit.

Measures to improve formalisation

  • Worker registration and social-security access.
  • Skill development.
  • Better enforcement of labour protections.
  • Support for small enterprises.
  • Digital access to employment and welfare services.

13. Important Employment and Social Security Programmes | ఉపాధి, సామాజిక భద్రత పథకాలు

MGNREGA

Provides a statutory rural wage-employment guarantee of up to 100 days per financial year to eligible rural households that demand unskilled manual work.

Deen Dayal Upadhyaya Grameen Kaushalya Yojana (DDU-GKY)

Promotes placement-linked skill training for eligible rural youth.

Pradhan Mantri Kaushal Vikas Yojana (PMKVY)

Supports skill training, certification and employability through approved programmes.

Pradhan Mantri Shram Yogi Maandhan (PM-SYM)

A contributory pension scheme for eligible unorganised workers, subject to entry conditions and scheme rules.

e-Shram

A national database initiative for unorganised workers intended to facilitate access to social-security and welfare programmes.

Employees' Provident Fund (EPF)

A contributory social-security savings arrangement for eligible employees under applicable law.

Employees' State Insurance (ESI)

Provides specified social-security and medical benefits to eligible insured employees and their families.

14. Inequality | అసమానతలు

Inequality refers to unequal distribution of income, wealth, assets, opportunities and access to essential services among individuals or groups.

వ్యక్తులు లేదా సమూహాల మధ్య ఆదాయం, సంపద, ఆస్తులు, అవకాశాలు మరియు ప్రాథమిక సేవల పంపిణీలో తేడాలను అసమానతలు అంటారు.

Types of inequality

  • Income Inequality: Unequal distribution of income.
  • Wealth Inequality: Unequal ownership of assets and wealth.
  • Gender Inequality: Unequal opportunities and outcomes based on gender.
  • Regional Inequality: Differences in development across regions.
  • Educational Inequality: Unequal access to quality education.
  • Health Inequality: Unequal access to healthcare and health outcomes.
  • Digital Inequality: Unequal access to digital devices, internet and digital skills.

15. Gini Coefficient | గిని గుణకం

The Gini coefficient is a statistical measure of inequality, commonly used to measure income or wealth distribution inequality.

ఆదాయం లేదా సంపద పంపిణీలో అసమానతలను కొలవడానికి ఉపయోగించే గణాంక సూచీ Gini Coefficient.

  • Gini value of 0 represents perfect equality.
  • Gini value of 1 represents perfect inequality in the standard mathematical formulation.
  • Higher Gini values indicate greater inequality.
  • It is based on the Lorenz Curve.

Lorenz Curve

The Lorenz Curve graphically represents the distribution of income or wealth across a population.

జనాభాలో ఆదాయం లేదా సంపద ఎలా పంపిణీ అయిందో గ్రాఫ్ రూపంలో చూపించే వక్రరేఖ Lorenz Curve.

A Lorenz Curve farther away from the line of perfect equality indicates greater inequality.

16. Inclusive Growth | సమ్మిళిత వృద్ధి

Inclusive growth means economic growth whose benefits are broadly shared across society, including disadvantaged groups and weaker regions.

సమాజంలోని అన్ని వర్గాలు, ముఖ్యంగా వెనుకబడిన వర్గాలు మరియు ప్రాంతాలు కూడా ఆర్థిక వృద్ధి ప్రయోజనాలు పొందే విధానాన్ని సమ్మిళిత వృద్ధి అంటారు.

Key features

  • Employment opportunities.
  • Equal access to education and healthcare.
  • Financial inclusion.
  • Rural and regional development.
  • Gender equality.
  • Social protection.
  • Access to infrastructure and technology.

Why is it important?

Growth without broad participation may leave disadvantaged groups behind. Inclusive growth seeks to combine higher productivity with fairness of opportunity.

17. Financial Inclusion and Poverty Reduction | ఆర్థిక చేరిక

Financial inclusion means providing affordable and useful financial services to individuals and businesses.

Important initiatives

  • Pradhan Mantri Jan Dhan Yojana: Promotes access to basic bank accounts and financial services.
  • Direct Benefit Transfer (DBT): Transfers eligible benefits directly to beneficiaries' bank accounts.
  • Microfinance: Provides small-scale financial services to underserved groups.
  • Self-Help Groups: Promote savings, credit and collective economic activity.
  • Digital Payments: Improve access to convenient financial transactions.

Role in poverty reduction

  • Improves access to savings and credit.
  • Supports small businesses.
  • Reduces dependence on informal high-cost borrowing.
  • Facilitates delivery of welfare benefits.

18. Social Protection and Food Security | సామాజిక రక్షణ, ఆహార భద్రత

Public Distribution System (PDS)

PDS distributes food grains and certain essential commodities to eligible households through fair price shops under applicable rules.

National Food Security Act (NFSA), 2013

  • Provides a legal framework for food security.
  • Covers eligible households under priority and Antyodaya categories.
  • Provides for nutritional support to specified groups under the Act.

Social protection

Social protection includes measures that protect individuals and households against economic and social risks.

  • Old-age pensions.
  • Health protection.
  • Food support.
  • Employment support.
  • Disability assistance.
  • Maternity benefits.

19. Poverty, Unemployment and Inequality: Relationship | పరస్పర సంబంధం

Poverty, unemployment and inequality are interconnected but are not identical concepts.

  • Unemployment can reduce household income and increase poverty.
  • Low education can contribute to unemployment and persistent poverty.
  • Income and wealth inequality can restrict access to opportunities.
  • Poor health can reduce productivity and increase household expenses.
  • Regional inequality can limit employment opportunities in underdeveloped areas.
  • Social protection can reduce vulnerability to economic shocks.

Integrated policy approach

  • Quality education and skill development.
  • Labour-intensive manufacturing and services.
  • Rural non-farm employment.
  • Better public healthcare.
  • Affordable housing and basic services.
  • Financial inclusion and social security.
  • Equal opportunities for women and disadvantaged groups.
