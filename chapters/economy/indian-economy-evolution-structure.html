1. Introduction to Indian Economy – భారత ఆర్థిక వ్యవస్థ పరిచయం

The Indian economy is one of the world's major economies. It includes agriculture, industry and services, with both public and private sector participation.

భారత ఆర్థిక వ్యవస్థలో వ్యవసాయం, పరిశ్రమలు, సేవారంగం ప్రధాన భాగాలు. ప్రభుత్వ రంగం మరియు ప్రైవేట్ రంగం రెండూ ఇందులో కీలక పాత్ర పోషిస్తాయి.

  • India is generally described as a mixed economy.
  • It is a developing economy with a large domestic market.
  • Economic activities include production, consumption, investment, trade and distribution.
  • Economic policy aims include growth, employment, stability and inclusive development.
Exam Point: India follows a mixed economic model in which public and private sectors coexist.

2. Indian Economy before Independence – స్వాతంత్ర్యానికి ముందు భారత ఆర్థిక వ్యవస్థ

During colonial rule, India's economy was shaped by British imperial interests. Several traditional industries declined, agriculture faced structural problems and India became an important supplier of raw materials and a market for manufactured goods.

Major characteristics

  • Agricultural dependence: A large proportion of the population depended on agriculture.
  • Low productivity: Traditional methods, limited irrigation and inadequate investment affected farm output.
  • Deindustrialisation: Many traditional handicrafts and indigenous industries faced decline.
  • Export pattern: Raw materials were exported and manufactured goods were imported in significant quantities.
  • Poverty: Low income and poor living conditions were widespread.
  • Infrastructure: Railways, ports and communication facilities expanded, but much infrastructure was designed primarily to serve colonial economic and administrative interests.
  • Limited industrial base: Modern industrial development was uneven and restricted.

Drain of Wealth Theory

Dadabhai Naoroji explained how wealth was transferred from India to Britain without an equivalent economic return to India. This was called the Drain of Wealth.

దాదాభాయ్ నౌరోజీ: భారతదేశ సంపద బ్రిటన్‌కు తరలిపోవడాన్ని Drain of Wealth సిద్ధాంతం ద్వారా వివరించారు.

Important Book: Dadabhai Naoroji's Poverty and Un-British Rule in India discussed the economic drain under British rule.

3. Indian Economy at Independence – 1947లో ఆర్థిక పరిస్థితి

At independence, India inherited a weak economic structure with widespread poverty, low literacy, low industrialisation and agricultural dependence.

AreaCondition around Independence
AgricultureLow productivity, dependence on monsoon and traditional methods
IndustryLimited industrial base and weak capital-goods capacity
EducationLow literacy and limited access
HealthWeak public health infrastructure and low life expectancy
InfrastructureUneven development and inadequate facilities
EmploymentHigh dependence on agriculture and widespread underemployment

The new government faced the challenge of reducing poverty, building infrastructure, improving agriculture and developing modern industries.

4. Planning in India – భారతదేశంలో ప్రణాళికా వ్యవస్థ

After independence, India adopted economic planning to direct investment, build productive capacity and promote balanced development.

Planning Commission

  • Established by a Government of India resolution on 15 March 1950.
  • It was a non-constitutional and non-statutory body.
  • It prepared Five-Year Plans and advised on development priorities.
  • It was replaced by NITI Aayog in 2015.

National Development Council (NDC)

  • Established in 1952.
  • It provided a platform for coordination between the Union and States on development planning.
  • It was associated with approval and review of Five-Year Plans.

5. Mahalanobis Model – మహలనోబిస్ నమూనా

The Second Five-Year Plan (1956–1961) was strongly influenced by the strategy associated with statistician P. C. Mahalanobis.

  • Emphasised heavy industries and capital goods.
  • Aimed to build long-term industrial capacity.
  • Favoured a larger role for the public sector in strategic industries.
  • Focused on reducing dependence on imported machinery over time.

తెలుగు: రెండవ పంచవర్ష ప్రణాళికలో భారీ పరిశ్రమలు, యంత్రాల తయారీ, మూలధన వస్తువుల రంగానికి ప్రాధాన్యత ఇచ్చారు.

6. Green Revolution – హరిత విప్లవం

The Green Revolution refers to the introduction and expansion of high-yielding variety seeds, chemical fertilisers, irrigation and improved farming practices to increase foodgrain production.

  • Expanded particularly from the mid-1960s.
  • Initially had a major impact on wheat production in Punjab, Haryana and western Uttar Pradesh.
  • Associated with agricultural scientists and institutions, including M. S. Swaminathan's important contribution in India.
  • Helped improve foodgrain production and food security.

Limitations and concerns

  • Regional inequalities in agricultural growth.
  • Benefits were initially concentrated in selected crops and regions.
  • Heavy irrigation use contributed to groundwater stress in some areas.
  • Excessive chemical inputs raised environmental concerns.

7. Nationalisation and Public Sector – జాతీయీకరణ మరియు ప్రభుత్వ రంగం

After independence, India expanded the public sector to build infrastructure, develop basic industries and control strategic sectors.

Bank Nationalisation

  • 14 major commercial banks were nationalised in 1969.
  • Six more banks were nationalised in 1980.
  • Objectives included wider banking access, mobilisation of savings and priority-sector lending.

Public Sector Enterprises

Public sector enterprises are organisations in which government ownership or control plays a major role. They operate in areas such as energy, transport, infrastructure and strategic industries.

Disinvestment

Disinvestment refers to the sale of a part or all of the government's stake in a public sector enterprise.

Privatisation generally involves transfer of ownership or management control to private entities. Disinvestment does not always mean complete privatisation.

8. Economic Crisis of 1991 – 1991 ఆర్థిక సంక్షోభం

India faced a severe balance of payments crisis in 1991. Foreign exchange reserves had fallen to very low levels, making it difficult to finance essential imports and meet external payment obligations.

Major causes

  • Fiscal imbalances and high government borrowing.
  • External debt and debt-servicing pressures.
  • Oil price shock associated with the Gulf crisis.
  • Political and economic uncertainty.
  • Weakness in the balance of payments.

Major policy response

India introduced a major reform programme in 1991, associated with Prime Minister P. V. Narasimha Rao and Finance Minister Dr. Manmohan Singh.

Exam Point: The 1991 reforms are commonly described using the terms LPG – Liberalisation, Privatisation and Globalisation.

9. LPG Reforms – Liberalisation, Privatisation, Globalisation

Liberalisation – సరళీకరణ

  • Reduction of unnecessary government controls and restrictions.
  • Industrial licensing was substantially dismantled, subject to specified exceptions.
  • Greater freedom for businesses and investment.
  • Financial and trade-sector reforms.

Privatisation – ప్రైవేటీకరణ

  • Greater participation of private enterprises.
  • Disinvestment in public sector enterprises.
  • Competition and efficiency as policy objectives.

Globalisation – ప్రపంచీకరణ

  • Greater integration with the world economy.
  • Expansion of international trade and investment.
  • Greater exposure to global competition and technology.
  • Participation in global production and supply chains.

LiberalisationReducing restrictions
PrivatisationIncreasing private ownership/participation

10. Structure of Indian Economy – భారత ఆర్థిక వ్యవస్థ నిర్మాణం

The Indian economy can be studied through different sectoral classifications.

Primary Sector

  • Agriculture and allied activities.
  • Fishing, forestry, mining and extraction of natural resources.
  • Provides food, raw materials and livelihoods.

Secondary Sector

  • Manufacturing and processing.
  • Construction, electricity and certain industrial activities.
  • Transforms raw materials into finished or semi-finished products.

Tertiary Sector

  • Banking, insurance and transport.
  • Education, healthcare, tourism and communication.
  • Trade, information technology and other services.
In India's economy, services account for a large share of output. Agriculture continues to support a large section of the population and remains important for livelihoods and food security.

12. Public Sector and Private Sector

  • Public Sector: Government ownership or control plays a major role.
  • Private Sector: Ownership and management are primarily in private hands.
  • Joint Sector: Public and private entities participate together in ownership or management arrangements.
  • Cooperative Sector: Enterprises owned and managed by members for their mutual benefit.

ప్రభుత్వ రంగం ప్రజా ప్రయోజనాలు, మౌలిక సదుపాయాలు మరియు వ్యూహాత్మక రంగాల్లో ముఖ్యమైన పాత్ర పోషిస్తుంది. ప్రైవేట్ రంగం పెట్టుబడులు, ఉత్పత్తి, సేవలు మరియు ఉపాధి కల్పనలో పాత్ర వహిస్తుంది.

13. Planning Commission to NITI Aayog

  • NITI Aayog was established on 1 January 2015.
  • It replaced the Planning Commission.
  • NITI stands for National Institution for Transforming India.
  • It is a policy think tank of the Government of India.
  • It promotes cooperative federalism and competitive federalism.
  • It provides strategic and technical advice and supports policy innovation.
  • It does not allocate plan funds to States in the same manner as the former Planning Commission.

15. Challenges of Indian Economy

  • Poverty: Inadequate income and limited access to essential services.
  • Unemployment: Lack of suitable work opportunities.
  • Underemployment: People work below their potential or for fewer hours than desired.
  • Income inequality: Unequal distribution of income and wealth.
  • Regional imbalance: Uneven development across States and regions.
  • Agricultural distress: Low and uncertain farm incomes, climate risks and input costs.
  • Infrastructure gaps: Need for better transport, power, logistics and digital connectivity.
  • Environmental sustainability: Balancing growth with conservation of natural resources.

18. Mains Answer Framework

Question: Explain the evolution of the Indian economy since Independence.

Introduction: At independence, India inherited an economy characterised by poverty, agricultural dependence and a limited industrial base.

Body:

  1. Planning phase: Five-Year Plans aimed at agricultural development, industrialisation and self-reliance.
  2. Public sector expansion: Government investment developed basic and strategic industries.
  3. Agricultural transformation: Green Revolution increased foodgrain production, although benefits were regionally uneven.
  4. 1991 reforms: Liberalisation, privatisation and globalisation changed the role of markets and private investment.
  5. Recent policy direction: Greater focus on infrastructure, digital services, manufacturing, financial inclusion and cooperative federalism.

Conclusion: India's economy has transformed from a largely agrarian and planned system into a diversified mixed economy. Inclusive and sustainable development remain important priorities.
